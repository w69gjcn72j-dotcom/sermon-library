When Jesus Confronts Us

When Jesus confronts us

Mark 7:1-37

Hypocrites can’t wash away spiritual sickness

Hypocrites worship in vain

An enlightening parable about food

We are the source of spiritual sickness

The Humble comes to Jesus for healing

An outsider comes to Jesus

A confronting parable about food scraps

Replying to Jesus’ parable

As we come to the end of the year, many of us would find different ways to celebrate.

This year, we have to be more creative than other years because of the restrictions on us at the moment.

A few years ago, in China, a group of foreign teachers were invited to join in the celebrating the end of term.

They would play games in teams as part of the fun.

There were four teams: one made up of teenage students, a team of foreign English teachers, a parents team, and a team of Chinese teachers.

They played different games and different teams won the various games.

It was all great fun until they played one game that involves chopsticks.

The aim of the game was to use chopsticks to carry table-tennis balls from one player to another.

The other team members all laughed at the English teachers.

There was no way that they could win against the Chinese.

The English teachers knew that it’s not likely that they would win but they would try anyway.

They started playing and almost from the start, the English teachers were winning.

All the excitement around them began to die down when it was obvious that the English teachers would win.

The teachers eventually won but there was no laughter.

The parents and the Chinese teachers wouldn’t even look at the English teachers.

When the English teachers asked what happened, the students replied, “The Chinese adults lost face.”

We don’t like it when we’re shown to be wrong in public, don’t we?

We get defensive and explain why we’re not wrong.

Sometimes we can even get aggressive and tell the other person that they’re wrong,

even if they’re right in pointing out that we’re wrong.

We don’t want to lose face.

It’s not just Chinese people who are afraid to lose face.

People from other cultures too, are afraid to lose face.

We’re afraid to be confronted and told that we’re wrong.

But more than that, sometimes we’re afraid to confront people because we don’t want to make them lose face.

We don’t want to upset them and so we would rather them continue in their error rather than correcting them.

Some preachers and churches do that.

In order to attract as many people as possible, they only speak positive messages.

They are very selective and will only say things that won’t upset anybody.

However, you don’t need to read too far into the Bible before you realise that there are confronting teachings in there.

There are things that are uncomfortable for people to hear.

In today’s passage, Jesus would say things that would upset people.

There’s no way to go around it.

If Jesus didn’t want to upset people, he would not have said what he said.

How do I respond when what Jesus confronts me with what he’s said?

What if what Jesus says makes me lose my face?

In today’s passage, we’ll see Jesus confront people on two occasions.

Firstly, he’ll confront hypocrites who can’t wash away their spiritual sickness.

Second, he’ll confront a humble woman who comes to him for healing.

If you have your Bible there, please open up with me to Mark chapter seven, beginning from verse 1.

Hypocrites can’t wash away spiritual sickness

In the first part of Mark chapter 7, we’ll see that hypocrites can’t wash away spiritual sickness.

Mark 7:1, “The Pharisees and some of the scribes who had come from Jerusalem gathered around him. 2 They observed that some of his disciples were eating bread with unclean—that is, unwashed—hands.”

The Pharisees and the scribes were religious leaders in Jesus’ days who were preoccupied with washing hands.

And two thousand years later, we have a renewed preoccupation with washing our hands.

If there’s one thing that 2020 has taught us, it’s the importance of keeping our hands clean.

This year, no matter where you went, you see sanitisers everywhere.

There are sanitisers in the Church, there are sanitisers in restaurants, there are sanitisers and shopping centres.

And even if they don’t have any, we bring our own portable hand sanitisers.

We would’ve made the religious leaders in Jesus’ days proud with our pre-occupation with handwashing!

However, the Pharisees and the scribes in Jesus’ day had another reason for keeping their hands clean.

They didn’t do it for hygienic reasons.

Rather, they did it for religious reasons.

They believed that they were ceremonially clean, and they worked hard to keep themselves ceremonially clean.

The idea is that is they somehow got in touch with people who were Gentiles, people who weren’t Jews, who wouldn’t have kept themselves ceremonially clean,

The Jews, by touching them, would become unclean.

And so, to get rid of any potential ceremonial uncleanliness, they would wash their hands.

They would sanitise their hands to be ceremonially clean, so to speak.

That’s why they were shocked to see some of Jesus’ disciples eating food with unwashed hands.

They weren’t concerned about their hygiene; they were concerned about the disciples being ceremonially unclean!

And so, they confronted Jesus’ about their disciples’ practices.

In reply, Jesus confronted their hypocrisy.

Listen to what Jesus says in verses 6-7, “He answered them, “Isaiah prophesied correctly about you hypocrites, as it is written:

This people honours me with their lips, but their heart is far from me. They worship me in vain, teaching as doctrines human commands.”

Jesus called them hypocrites.

The Chinese translates this nicely, Fake men of honour.

They were fakes; they pretended to worship God but their hearts are far from God.

They pretended to love God by continuously washing their hands, but their hearts were far from God.

In what way?

It’s because their pre-occupation with handwashing was not something that God commanded.

Rather it was something that was handed down to them by human tradition.

The priests were told to clean themselves every once in a while, as they prepare to sacrifice to God,

But God didn’t command the people in general to keep washing their hands.

The Pharisees and the Scribe would say that they’re doing it to honour God, as part of their worship to God,

But it was in vain because that’s not what God commanded.

Why would they do that?

Why would they keep the traditions of men rather than obeying God’s command?

Because it’s easier and be seen among fellow men to follow the traditions of men, rather than to follow the commands of God.

It’s far easier to follow religious practices, which you do and show on the outside,

Than to actually obey and follow what God says.

Jesus explains this with a one sentence parable in verse 15.

“Nothing that goes into a person from outside can defile him but the things that come out of a person are what defile him.”

What does Jesus mean?

Jesus explains it to his disciples like this,

“Don’t you realize that nothing going into a person from the outside can defile him?  For it doesn’t go into his heart but into the stomach and is eliminated” (thus he declared all foods clean).“

Jesus says it’s not what’s on the outside that makes you unclean.

No food can make you unclean before God.

They go through your mouth and they, eventually, get eliminated.

Literally, Jesus says that they go to the toilet. (I love how clear Jesus makes this!)

Jesus says that eating unclean food, or touching people who eat unclean food, won’t in and of themselves make you unclean.

What makes us unclean then?

Jesus says what comes out of a person is what defiles him.

Verses 21-22, “21 For from within, out of people’s hearts, come evil thoughts, sexual immoralities, thefts, murders, 22 adulteries, greed, evil actions, deceit, self-indulgence, envy, slander, pride, and foolishness. 23 All these evil things come from within and defile a person.”

The problem with our uncleanliness doesn’t come from the outside.

The problem with our uncleanliness comes from the inside; it comes from us.

We are the source of our own uncleanliness before God.

It’s like having COVID then using bottles of hand sanitisers to try to keep ourselves healthy.

It’s pointless! No amount of hand sanitisers is going to help you if you already have COVID.

If you have COVID, you need to have urgent treatment in the emergency ward, not washing your hands with bottles of hand sanitisers.

In the same way, trying to keep external human traditions like handwashing won’t treat the problem of our uncleanliness,

Since the source of our uncleanliness, the source of defilement, is our heart.

Like the Pharisees and the Scribes, we can blame the problem of our uncleanliness on other people.

We can blame it on the society, we can blame it on our background, we can blame it even on our families,

And we can try to remedy it by keeping ourselves away from them or by trying to keep as many human religious traditions as we can.

But just like trying to heal a COVID infested patient with sanitisers,

Trying to fix our spiritual sickness with human traditions, human wisdom, is utterly futile.

That’s how Jesus confronted the Pharisees, the Scribes and even the Disciples of his days: You can’t get rid of your spiritual sickness by your human commands, your human traditions.

You’re wasting your time in trying to fix yourself by your own method.

It’s a confronting message because Jesus was saying that the Pharisees and the Scribes were wrong, their traditions, which had been handed down to them for generations, were wrong.

Jesus did not hold back.

Jesus would’ve made them lose face in front of the people.

Jesus says that we can’t get rid of our uncleanliness in and of ourselves since we’re the source of uncleanliness.

The teachings of the Pharisees and the Scribes about cleanliness were completely wrong and ineffective.

What then is the right and effective way to get cleaned before God?

We see Jesus’ answer in the second part of today’s passage.

We’ll see how the humble comes to Jesus for healing.

Look with me at verses 24-25, “24 He got up and departed from there to the region of Tyre.[k] He entered a house and did not want anyone to know it, but he could not escape notice. 25 Instead, immediately after hearing about him, a woman whose little daughter had an unclean spirit came and fell at his feet.”

Jesus went to the far region of Tyre.

He didn’t want anyone to know about where he was.

It would be like a Doctor who went away for a holiday break, trying to get some rest.

He went to a rural town where nobody would recognise him when suddenly, a local woman in the town who recognises him turns up and asks for his help.

He doesn’t know her, but she’s heard of him, and so she asks for his help.

Jesus went to the region of Tyre, hoping not to be recognised but a local woman who was Gentile and Syrophoenician by birth came to see him.

Her daughter has an unclean spirit and she asks Jesus to get rid of the unclean spirit for her daughter.

You would expect Jesus, who’s kind and gentle, will grant the mother’s request.

After all, he’s done it before for other people.

And this woman was sincere; she came and fell at Jesus’ feet, asking for a favour for her daughter.

That’s why what Jesus said next was so confronting.

““Let the children be fed first, because it isn’t right to take the children’s bread and throw it to the dogs.”

It’s another one-sentence parable.

Except this time, it’s even more confronting that the last one.

In the last parable, it was about food having zero effect on your cleanliness before God.

In this parable, it’s about identifying the woman as a dog who’s not qualified to eat the food offered to children, that is, the Jews.

It’s almost as if Jesus became like the Pharisee and Scribes that he himself had confronted earlier.

From the Jews’ point of view, Gentiles, like dogs, were unclean.

They don’t deserve to be treated with special privileges, like the Jews.

It’s a very confronting message.

Being called a hypocrite would be pretty bad, but being called a dog?

That would seem very harsh.

Why has Jesus suddenly become so concerned about cleanliness?

Why did he bring up the divide between Jews and the Gentiles and be so confronting, even bordering rude, to the Gentile mother?

If we step back and take a look at the passage as a whole, we’ll see that Jesus’ parable was actually a summary of what’s been happening so far.

The Jews, represented by the children in the story, were offered the teaching and the miracles of Jesus, represented by the bread in the story.

Jesus has come to the Jews first and brought blessings with him.

He had come to bring the good news and he gave them to the Jews first.

However, the Jews, as represented by the Pharisees and the scribes, were rejecting Jesus.

They were rejecting the bread offered to them, so to speak.

How would the Gentiles receive Jesus?

Listen to the Mother’s response, “Lord, even the dogs under the table eat the children’s crumbs.”

Not only did she respond positively to Jesus; she responded him according to his parable.

Yes, what Jesus said to her might sound confronting but she was an intelligent woman who understood what Jesus was saying to her.

Yes, there was a priority for Jesus to go to the Jews first, it’s right for the Jews to meet Jesus and receive good things from him first.

But that doesn’t mean that other people won’t ever get to hear it.

After Jesus and the blessings, he brings goes to the Jews, surely other people can have some of Jesus’ blessing as well, even it’s a tiny amount.

There’s still hope for Gentiles like her.

As well as being perceptive, she was humble.

Rather than being offended by Jesus, she identified herself as a dog who would receive some benefits from Jesus.

She hadn’t come to demand Jesus to heal her daughter.

Jesus wasn’t obliged to heal her daughter.

And she certainly was not entitled to have Jesus heal her daughter.

She came begging; she didn’t deserve it.

She was only asking for some blessings, any blessings, that Jesus can generously spare.

She didn’t presume on Jesus; rather she begs Jesus and appeals to his grace, to his generosity.

It’s like someone who has been diagnosed with COVID.

It’s no use trying to fight it off on your own because you can’t.

Instead, you need to admit that you’ve got the virus and ask, humbly, for someone to treat you.

The Gentile Mother understood that she was not in a position to demand Jesus to heal her daughter from her unclean spirit.

And so, after being confronted by Jesus’ parable, she humbly begs Jesus to be gracious to her.

In response, Jesus says, “Because of this reply, you may go. The demon has left your daughter.”

When she went back to her home, her daughter was lying in bed and the demon was gone.

Both the religious leaders in Jesus’ day and the Gentile woman were confronted by Jesus.

As far as we know, the religious leaders were offended by Jesus and in time, they would plot to have him killed.

On the other hand, the Gentile woman responded in humility and her request was granted; her daughter was cleansed from the unclean spirit.

When we’re confronted by Jesus, how do we respond?

Like the religious leaders, offended because we lost face?

Or like the Gentile woman, humbly accepting Jesus even when we lose face?

If you go to a Church that’s faithful in preaching Jesus, you will be confronted.

If you are honest and read God’s word carefully, whether in Bible studies or in your personal quiet time, you will be confronted.

In fact, every time we come before God’s word, whether you’re at Church or at home, you should expect to be confronted.

If you come to Church to hear God’s word, and you’re not confronted, then it’s either because the preacher hasn’t been preaching faithfully.

If the preacher has been preaching faithfully and you’re not confronted, then you haven’t been listening.

The Bible says that God’s word is living and effective, sharper than any double-edged sword.

If we listen to what God’s word says in the Bible, it will penetrate us, it will show us who we are, it will confront us.

It will tell us of our moral failings, it will tell us of our spiritual sickness.

It will reveal the greed, the envy, the pride, the foolishness that we have deep within.

It will show that we’re unclean.

When that happens, don’t be like the Pharisees and scribe, hypocrites who blame it on other people, hypocrites who try to wash themselves by human wisdom and human tradition.

Instead, be like the Gentile mother, who humbles herself before Jesus, willing to lose face, not demanding but begging for Jesus to be gracious.

Be like the Gentile mother, who comes to Jesus, who alone can save her.

What does this mean for us?

Be humble when Jesus lovingly confronts us.

Just like a mother who gently points out that we have something on our face and wipes it away,

Allow Jesus to show the dark spots in our hearts and let him wipe them away.

On Christmas Day, I received a message from my good friend, who is also called David.

David has an auntie who was a devoted Buddhist.

For a long time, she was against anyone in her family who became a Christian.

Being the older mother of the family, she had a lot of influence over the rest of the family.

In 1999, when she was 81 years old, she became a Christian and got baptised.

She would not let her pride or the fear of losing her face stop her from coming to Jesus!

She would read the Bible every day and pray for her children and grandchildren.

She’s now over 100 years old.

Earlier this year, she was unwell with a cough.

A few weeks later, the nursing home that she was staying at had an outbreak of COVID-19.

25% of the people there had contracted the disease.

Being an elderly person with a cough with so many people around her contracting COVID, she was in a dangerous situation.

But God was gracious to her.

Not only did she not get COVID, but her cough also eventually left her.

But more importantly, she was spiritually healed.

When David’s auntie was confronted with Jesus, she humbled herself and accepted Jesus.

How about us?

When Jesus confronts us, will we humble ourselves before him?

Here are the three questions for this week:

How has Jesus confronted me in his word today?

In what way am I in danger of being a hypocrite, honouring God with my lips and not my heart?

In what way do I humble myself when he confronts me in his words?

Friends, be humble when Jesus lovingly confronts us.

Let’s pray, “Father, we thank you for sending Jesus to us. We thank you that he’s the one who shows us your holiness and your glory. Father, please forgive us when we trivialise our sins and minimise your glory when we try to make ourselves clean by our own methods. Please humble us before you when you confront us in your words. By your Spirit, transform us so that we will praise you from our heart in our words, thoughts and deeds. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”
