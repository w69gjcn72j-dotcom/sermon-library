Stand for the Glorious Jesus, before whom we all will stand

On 17 June 1966, two black men entered the Layfayette Bar and Grill in Paterson, New Jersey and killed three white people, and seriously wounding another.

Paterson was a predominantly black neighborhood.

At around that time, John Artis and his friend the boxer Rubin “Hurricane” Carter were in a car with a third friend.

Even though a lot of evidence didn’t match, both Artis and Carter were charged with murder.

Though both Artis and Carter said that they were innocent,

They were sentenced to three life sentences based on the testimony of two thieves who later recanted (backtracked) their testimony.

Artis was given a lot of opportunities to have a reduced prison sentence if he would say that his friend Carter was a killer,

However, Artis said, “I’m not lying. We didn’t do it, we weren’t there.”

After spending fifteen years in jail, Artis was paroled in 1981 and the case was thrown out by US District judge H Lee Sarokin because the prosecution was predicated upon an appeal to racism rather than reason.”

Imagine being wrongfully charged and spending fifteen years in prison for it.

And imagine how tempting it could’ve been for Artis to not stand up for but to give up his friend Carter, in order to have a lighter sentence.

Two weeks ago, we met someone else who was also wrongfully charged.

The man was Stephen and in Acts 7, Stephen was responding to the charges made against him.

They were saying that Stephen was speaking against the holy place, that is, the temple in Jerusalem,

As well as the law of Moses.

Stephen began his response by saying that as a people, the Jews, of which he himself was one, had been resisting God’s chosen leaders for almost two thousand years.

One of these leaders was Moses.

When Moses tried to help his fellow Israelites, they rejected him and asked who put Moses in charge.

Moses fled Egypt but eventually God told him to go back to lead the Israelites out of Egypt, which Moses did, by God’s mighty power.

Moses led them for forty years.

You would expect that after all those years in the desert, seeing God’s miracles every day, hearing God’s laws taught by Moses, that everything is going to be OK.

But no, things are far from OK.

In fact, how they treated Moses, God’s chosen leader, gives us a glimpse of how they would treat Jesus, as well as Jesus’ followers, of which Stephen is now one.

The way they respond to Jesus and to Jesus’ message as given by Stephen in turn helps us to see how we are to respond where we are under pressure to disobey.

What helps Christians to keep standing for Jesus when under pressure to disobey?

Please open your Bible to Acts 7 beginning from verse 37.

We’ll see three things:

The danger of disobeying God’s word in his church

The disobedient resists God and kills his messengers

Stand for the Glorious Jesus, before whom we all will stand

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Father, please forgive us our cowardice in the face of pressure and our stiff-neckedness in your call for obedience. By your Spirit, please open our ears and our hearts, not only to listen but to obey your word. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

The danger of disobeying God’s word in his church

Look with me at Acts chapter 7 beginning from verse 37, “This is the Moses who said to the Israelites: God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among your brothers.”

Moses, the powerful leader chosen by God to lead the Israelites out of Egypt said that God would raise up another prophet like him among the Israelites.

The Israelite were to expect God to send a powerful prophet like Moses to lead them once more.

What did Moses do?

Verse 38, “He is the one who was in the assembly in the wilderness, with the angel who spoke to him on Mount Sinai, and with our ancestors. He received living oracles to give to us.”

This Moses was one of them and yet at the same time, not like anyone else.

He was in the assembly in the wilderness.

The word translated ‘assembly’ is the same word as Church.

That’s what the word Church means: it’s an assembly, a gathering.

And so, you can translate it like this: He (Moses) is the one who was in the Church in the wilderness.

Moses was one of God’s people, but at the same time he was the only one chosen by God to be spoken to by the angel.

God had revealed his word, his oracles to Moses in a way that he hasn’t anybody else.

Moses was like the person between God and man.

Moses heard from God and gave God’s oracles to the Israelites, the fathers of both Stephen and his accusers.

Moses had both the authority of God’s chosen leader as well as being able to relate to them as fellow men.

But did they listen to Moses? No!

Verse 39, “Our ancestors were unwilling to obey him. Instead, they pushed him aside, and in their hearts turned back to Egypt.”

Even though they had Moses in their Church, even though they had the guy whom God had spoke to as their minister, they pushed him away, they refused to listen.

They have the one person who will teach them to live the kind of life that God wants them to live, but they refuse to listen.

Though their bodies were in the Church, their hearts were far away in Egypt.

It’s a little like this.

A few years ago, our family car of many years broke down and we had to get a new one.

Here she is, our Kia Carnival. We even give her a name: Deb.

Deb is a great car; she’s spacious, modern and can travel from here to Surfers’ Paradise on less than one full tank.

Deb is an automatic but what I didn’t know was that I can actually change the gears manually as well.

I only had old cars before Deb and they weren’t that advanced, but apparently many of the newer automatic cars give that option now.

You can put it in “D” generally or you can manually change the gears if you want to.

It’s not like the car company Kia was trying to hide it from me; it’s all in the manual.

But since I didn’t read it, I didn’t know how much fun driving Deb could really be!

That’s what’s happened to the Israelites.

They had Moses and they had God’s instructions on what it means to live.

They live in God’s assembly, God’s Church but having all these won’t save them, if their hearts are still not right with God.

Though God had saved them from Egypt, the land of slavery, their hearts turned back away from God and toward Egypt.

They might be physically saved, but they remain spiritually unsaved.

Instead of following the one true God, they made and followed gods they made with their own hands.

They wanted a god that they are happy with, a god that they can control, a god that obeys them when they call out to him.

That’s what most people do when they think about god.

They use their imagination to think about what gods should look like.

We see this in many cultures throughout history, as men and women think of the gods to behave like humans, only more powerful and more extreme.

These are gods of our imagination, gods we make with our hands.

That’s what an idol is, they are gods we make up and worship instead of the one true God.

Any god based solely on our imagination, our reasoning, our feelings are but idols.

The Israelites had God’s servant Moses with them and they had the laws given by the one true God but they chose to disobey God.

Instead, they made and worshipped their own god.

How did God respond? Look with me at verse 42, “God turned away and gave them up to worship the stars of heaven.”

God gave them away, God gave up and let them follow their own gods.

Listen to God’s question to them, “House of Israel, did you bring me offerings and sacrifices for forty years in the wilderness?”

Though the Israelites had been in God’s assembly, God’s Church in the wilderness, though they had been offering sacrifices according to Moses’ instructions, were they really worshipping God?

Though it might have looked like they were worshipping God, God says that all these forty years, they haven’t been worshipping him.

Instead, they were worshipping Moloch and Raphan, foreign, horrible gods.

Moloch was a Canaanite god whom people worship by sacrificing children to it.

That was the god that the Israelites were worshipping in their hearts, not the true God himself.

They had God’s laws, they were in God’s Church, they had Moses the prophet as their Minister, but they still worshipped an idol instead of the one true God.

Sadly, that can still happen today.

There are people who read the bible, who come to God’s Church, who sing songs of praise, but their hearts still worship an idol instead of the one true God.

Over this past week, I heard a prayer uttered in a church where neither God nor Jesus are not mentioned at all.

It sounded like a prayer, but the person was praying to some vague power, not the God of the Bible.

They were supposed to be a Church, but they were worshipping something other than God; it was basically idol worship.

Over this past week, I also went to another church where they had a lot of activities – craft classes, exercise classes, English classes – but there was nothing about Jesus.

While some of these community classes might have started with good intention, it looks like they have crowded out the message about Jesus.

Just like the Israelites of old, they might have the Bible in the Church, but if they don’t read it, if they don’t obey God’s word, if their hearts are not in worship, then they are in danger of serving a god of their own making.

As a church, we are not immune to falling into the same trap.

We must ask ourselves what it is that we have come here to do.

Our service leaders can repeat our vision statement every Sunday, we can say the Lord’s prayer every Sunday, but where are our hearts at?

To rephrase God’s question to the Israelites in verse 42, how would you answer this question?

Men and women, boys and girls at St Paul’s Kogarah, did you bring God your worship and your service, for all the months and years you’ve been coming to Church?

Is it really God that you are worshipping from your hearts? Or are you worshipping something else?

The disobedient resists God and kills his messengers

In defence of the people’s charge against Stephen changing the customs of Moses, Stephen answers by saying that their fathers (including Stephen’s) had been disobeying Moses’s instructions since the time of Moses.

Stephen now turns to the second charge about him destroying the temple.

Look with me at verse 44, “Our ancestors had the tabernacle of the testimony in the wilderness, just as he who spoke to Moses commanded him to make it according to the pattern he had seen”

Stephen reminded his accusers that it was God who told Moses how to build the tabernacle, the tent, of testimony.

It was built according to a pattern that God’s shown Moses.

This tent of testimony is symbolically the meeting place between God and man.

Inside this tent is the Holy of Holies, which represents God’s presence.

The Israelites carried the tent from the wilderness into Canaan, the Promised Land, where God cleared the nations for the Israelites.

After many years, King David took the initiative and asked to build a proper temple for God because God’s presence was still represented by the tent, but David was living in a palace.

However, God said that it wouldn’t be David but his son Solomon who would build the temple.

The temple that Solomon built was magnificent, but even Solomon himself prayed to God, “But will God indeed live on earth? Even heaven, the highest heaven, cannot contain you, much less this temple I have built.”

As magnificent as the temple was, it was not worthy, even remotely, for God to live in.

Heaven is God’s throne, the earth is God’s footstool, what can we ever build for God?

One of the most famous Church building in the world right now is the Sagrada Familia in Barcelona.

The church building project started in 1882, over 142 years ago, and it still hasn’t finished yet.

The art critic Rainer Zerbst said, “it is probably impossible to find a church building anything like it in the entire history of art”.

And you’ll be glad to know it’s due to finish in two years’ time!

But even the magnificent Sagrada Familia is not worthy of God.

In God’s eyes, it’s only a tiny centre, built for tiny people.

And so, as important and as significant as the temple was, in God’s eyes, it was only a small thing.

And as important and significant as the law and Moses himself were in the history of God’s people, merely having these were not the heart of the issue.

The heart of the issue was whether they were obeying God or not.

It’s pointless to claim to have God’s law and it’s pointless to have the most beautiful temple, if you disobey God.

Look with me at verse 51, “You stiff-necked people with uncircumcised hearts and ears! You are always resisting the Holy Spirit. As your ancestors did, you do also.”

In conclusion, Stephen calls his accusers stiff-necked and uncircumcised in both hearts and ears.

They are stiff-necked because they refuse to bow down before the Lord; they refuse to obey.

They are stiff-necked because they resist to obey God and his chosen leaders.

Together with their fathers, Stephen says that they are always resisting the Holy Spirit.

More than that, they were uncircumcised in both hearts and ears.

Circumcision was a sign that they belonged to God.

Though the men might be physically circumcised, their hearts and their ears weren’t.

Their heart still longed to sin; their heart longed for what the world longs for.

They might had been longing for money, pleasure and power.

Just like the rest of the world, their happiness depends on these, rather than God.

Their longing shows that their hearts are still uncircumcised.

They are from a long line of stiff-necked people.

Their fathers persecuted the prophets and killed those who foretold the coming of Jesus.

And now, as their sons, they betrayed and murdered the Righteous One.

Though they might claim to be God’s, their hatred of God’s chosen leaders, and ultimately their hatred of Jesus, show the true nature of their uncircumcised hearts.

There’s a myth that says that belief in God is a private matter, that it must only remain private at home.

But here the Bible tells us that it’s not a private but a communal matter.

How did these stiff-necked people disobey God?

They disobey God by disobeying the leaders whom God’s sent.

When they disobeyed Moses, they were disobeying God.

And when they were disobeying Jesus, and nailed him to the cross, they were disobeying God.

How do they disobey Jesus now?

By hating, persecuting and condemning those sent by Jesus to preach his word.

And when they show contempt to the ones Jesus sent, it’s not a light but a serious matter, for you are showing contempt for Jesus, which in turn means you’re showing contempt for God.

It might not seem like a big deal at the time – it might be a gossip, a rumour or just plain disrespect.

Moses’ own brother and sister didn’t think much of it when they complained about how God had put Moses in charge.

The High Priests and the Roman soldiers didn’t think much of it when they flogged Jesus and nailed Jesus to the cross.

People today don’t think much of it when they disrespect, tease and mock Christian leaders.

But who are they really teasing at the end? Who are they disrespecting?

The One whom God has promised to be the Saviour and Ruler of world.

For Jesus says, “If the world hates you, understand that it hated me before it hated you.” John 15:18

Stephen concludes his defence with this in verse 53, “You received the law under the direction of angels and yet have not kept it.”

That’s a question we have to ask ourselves as well.

Jesus has shown us all the promises that he’s come to fulfil.

We might read them in the Bible, we might sing about them, we might be able to teach them, but do we keep them?

Have the words of Jesus taken hold of our hearts and our lives?

For it’s before Jesus that we all must stand.

Stand for the Glorious Jesus, before whom we all will stand

Look with me at verse 54 to see the response of Stephen’s accusers, “When they heard these things, they were enraged and gnashed their teeth at him.”

They were filled with rage and hated Stephen even more.

In the midst of their rage, God opened up heaven briefly for Stephen.

Look with me at verses 55, “Stephen, full of the Holy Spirit, gazed into heaven. He saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing at the right hand of God.”

He even points his accusers to look at the vision he is seeing.

Stephen’s defence started with a reference to the God of glory appearing to Abraham.

It now ends with the God of glory appearing to Stephen, with Jesus standing next to him.

This vision looks a lot like what’s described in Psalm 110:1, “This is the declaration of the LORD to my Lord: “Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies your footstool.”

However, there’s one big difference.

Can you see it?

In Psalm the Lord sits but in Acts 7, Jesus stands.

Why is Jesus standing?

When a king sits down on his throne in his palace, it sends a clear message that he’s in charge.

This is especially the case after a battle.

Once the battle is won, he can sit down.

However, if the king stands, there’s something that he still has to do.

His mission is not quite over yet.

Jesus is standing because Stephen’s case is not over for Jesus will come to judge against Stephen’s accusers and against Stephen’s murderers.

Stephen’s accusers stoned Stephen to death.

It was a gruesome, intense, and personal form of execution.

When you’re executing someone by stoning, you’re not throwing a stone from far away, like shooting a someone with a bullet.

No, you get right up to the person when you’re stoning the person.

Stephen was unfairly trialled and unfairly executed for standing up for Jesus and the first disciple in history to die for Jesus.

And Jesus stands, ready to judge those who executed Stephen.

Here is a glimpse of what it looks like for Jesus to judge, in Revelation chapter 20 verse 11-12, “Then I saw a great white throne and one seated on it. Earth and heaven fled from his presence, and no place was found for them. 12 I also saw the dead, the great and the small, standing before the throne, and books were opened. Another book was opened, which is the book of life, and the dead were judged according to their works by what was written in the books.

All of us will stand before God’s throne.

On that day, we won’t be judging God, we won’t be deciding whether we should obey God or not.

On that day, Jesus will be judge us for disobeying him.

For all those who have been standing up for Jesus, that day will be glorious for Jesus will be glorious.

But for all those who have lived in darkness and disobedience, that day will be blinding and they would try in vain to hide from Jesus’ glory.

Jesus is the One and Only Saviour; he is the only one who can save us.

Jesus is also the ultimate Judge; we will stand before his throne.

He will condemn us for our disobedience if we remain disobedient, stiff-necked, uncircumcised in both hearts and ears.

While the mob was stoning Stephen, there was a man standing, watching and witnessing the whole thing.

He was a young man named Saul and the mob took their garments off and laid them at his feet.

Saul hated the Christians and was a willing party to see Stephen stoned to death.

Will all of them be damned to hell for what they did to Stephen?

Not if Stephen could help it.

As he was dying, Stephen used a prayer that was like the one the Lord Jesus prayed as Jesus himself was dying.

Stephen prayed, “Lord Jesus receive my spirit!”

And with loud voice Stephen prayed, “Do not hold this sin against them!”

Even as he was dying, Stephen was praying that God would save those who were stoning him, that they would repent, turn to Jesus and find forgiveness in him.

And God in his mercy answered Stephen’s prayer and saved Saul, who changed his name to Paul.

Paul himself would go on preaching Stephen’s message about Jesus to thousands upon thousands of people.

It is this same Paul who wrote many letters of the New Testament of the Bible, whose ministry God still uses today to save people two thousand years afterwards.

Stephen stood up for Jesus and God used his faithfulness to bless untold number of people.

Will you stand up for Jesus?

If we stand up for Jesus, not only in the safety of our Church but in our families, in our workplaces, in our community, then we will look forward to the day when we see Jesus face to face.

We can look forward to standing in his glory.

If we don’t stand up for Jesus, if we disobey, then it will be dreadful day, for we will be numbered with those who stoned Stephen, and all those who remain stiff-necked against Jesus.

How will you stand on the Judgement day?

May our God help us to stand up for Jesus now so that we can stand with gratitude and joy on the Day Jesus comes back.

What helps Christians to keep standing for Jesus when under pressure to disobey?

By remembering how Jesus walks to the cross in obedience to his Father’s will.

By remembering how Jesus’ resurrection means He’s the Judge of the Living and the Dead.

By remembering and loving those who are in God’s Church, seeing them not as the world does but as God would have us.

By remembering that the Day is coming when all of us have to stand before Jesus, longing to be joyful and not be shameful in his presence.

And by remembering that we’re not strong, and that we will need to constantly pray to hold on to Jesus.

And so, friends, remember this: The glorious Jesus, for whom we stand, stands against those who hear him in his church and yet still disobey

Don’t just know God’s word superficially but ingest it, understand it and do what God says!

When you come to Church, ask yourselves: are we worshipping God or something else?

We can’t make anything worthy of God, but what does what we give tell us about what we think of God?

Do our lives reflect that we’re obeying God and glorifying Jesus?

Reflect on the way we live and pray that God will give us a heart to worship him truthfully.

Here are the three questions for this week:

In what way can I fall into the danger of hearing God’s word at Church and not obeying?

In what way can I fall into the danger of worshipping a god I made instead of worshipping the one true God?

Do my words and my deeds point people to Jesus, especially when I’m under pressure?

Friends, we serve a glorious Jesus, for whom we stand.

This is the Jesus who will stand against all those who hear in his church and not obey.

And so, Church, obey!

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you for the glorious Lord Jesus, for whom we stand, for whom we live. Father please forgive us for our stiff-neckedness, for our unbelieving hearts and for our unbelieving ears. Please be merciful and renew our hearts by your Holy Spirit, that we may truly know and love you, and that we may love your people for whom Christ died. We pray this in Jesus’ name, Amen.”
