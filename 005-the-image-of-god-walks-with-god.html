The Image of God Walks with God

Genesis 5:1-32 · 2023
Big IdeaAs the image of God, fulfil who you are by walking with Jesus, the true image of God.

Sermon Title: The image of God walks with God

Passage: Genesis 5.1_32

BIG IDEA: AS THE IMAGE OF GOD, FULFIL WHO YOU ARE BY WALKING WITH JESUS, THE TRUE IMAGE OF GOD.

We fail to live up to our identity as God’s image.

The Seventh Son walked with God

Walk with Jesus, the perfect image of God

One of the great things about living here in Kogarah is how close we live to the shops and the train station.

I love the fact that I can just walk down Regent Street and before I know it, I’m at the train station.

Sometimes, I walk with my family to Kogarah to get groceries from Woolworth’s.

It’s great!

But one thing that my family tells me every time we walk is this, “Dad, why are you in such a hurry? You’re walking too fast!”

I thought I was just walking at my normal speed, but they would often say that I was walking too fast!

Does anyone else get that? Does your family say that you walk too fast too?

Oh, it’s just me.

What does my walking speed says about me?

Maybe I walk fast because I’m always running late.

Maybe I walk fast because that’s the normal walking speed back in Hong Kong!

Maybe I’m just thinking of a song with very fast tempo, and I was just trying to keep up with the song!

The way I walk says a lot about who I am.

In fact, studies have shown that each one of us have a unique way that we walk.

It’s almost as unique as the fingerprint.

In fact, some security companies are now trying to measure the way you walk by mobile phone and use your walk as an added security to your phone.

In the future the way you walk may be how your mobile phone verifies that you are you!

But even without using the way you walk as a security measure, the way you walk says a lot about you.

Not just your physical walk, but the way you live your life.

The way you roll (go about everyday business), the way you walk!

How does the way you walk, the way you live, say about your identity?

That’s what we’re going to see in today’s passage.

So please open up to Genesis 5, beginning from verse 1.

We’re going to see three things:

We fail to live up to our identity as God’s image.

The Seventh Son walked with God

Walk with Jesus, the perfect image of God

Before we look into God’s word, let’s pray,

“Father, thank you for giving us your word. Please open our hearts, that we might receive your word and live according to it. Don’t let the evil one take your word from us but by your Holy Spirit plant it deep into our hearts. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

We fail to live up to our identity as God’s image

Here’s what’s been happening in Genesis so far.

In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth.

As humans, we were made in God’s image.

However, we disobeyed God and were sentenced to death.

We were kicked out of God’s Garden of Eden.

Adam and Eve, the first man and woman, began their new life outside of Eden, and soon Eve gave birth to Cain and Abel.

However, Cain murdered Abel, and Cain went away from his family as a result.

At the end of Genesis chapter four, we see Adam and Eve having their third son, Seth.

And that’s what we see here in Genesis 5, a new beginning for the human race in the line of Seth.

Look with me at verses 1-2, “This is the document containing the family records of Adam. On the day that God created man, he made him in the likeness of God; 2 he created them male and female. When they were created, he blessed them and called them mankind.”

Did you notice how man (including both male and female) is here described?

God made man (that is, male and female) in the likeness of God.

What does it mean for God to make us in the likeness of God?

Talk with the person next to you.

What does it mean for God to make us in his likeness?

You have 20 seconds.

What does it mean for God to make us in his likeness?

Could it be because we can reason? Is that why we’re like God?

Well, if that’s the case, what do you say to those who have mental challenges, or those who are very young, or those who are very old?

Are they any less of an image of God?

What about justice? Our sense of right and wrong?

Well, that can vary widely from culture to culture, from nation to nation, from judge to judge.

Those with a perverted sense of justice may be wrong, but are they any less human than you? Or what if you were the one in the wrong, does that may you less human?

What makes us humans?

It has to do with the concept of God’s image.

To be an image of something is to reflect its reality; the image always points to something or Someone that it represents.

As the image of God, we were created to reflect who God is.

And central to the way we reflect who God is, is our ability to relate to God.

As human beings, God has made us able to hear his words and respond to his words, like no other creatures in all creation.

As bearers of God’s image, we were made to hear God and speak with God, to communicate with God.

As people made in the likeness of God, we were made to relate to God.

It’s only as we relate to God that we find our true identity.

And what’s our identity? As human beings, we’re at our core, creatures made in the image of God.

It’s a little bit like this.

As an Asian growing up in Australia, when I’m in a conference, or a shopping centre, I naturally look for black hair among the crowd.

That’s because when we were growing up, we didn’t have a lot of people who have black hair around us.

When we were growing up, there were a lot of people from different nationalities around us, which was great.

But almost by default, we naturally look for those with black hair, because we might be able to find someone who understands me, especially in the days when I first came to Australia.

Even now, many first generation immigrant Asians naturally look for black hair.

You want to find someone you can communicate with, someone who you can relate to.

As creatures made in God’s image, we’re hard-wired to want to know who God is.

God has created us to relate to him, to communicate with him.

It’s within our nature to find out who God is.

God has made us that way.

And notice that straight after describing us as being made in the likeness of God, we’re told that God created us to be male and female.

Together, as male and female, we’re the image of God.

And yet we’re still different.

We were designed to reflect God’s image and relate to God in our own unique way.

Some reflect God’s image as men and others reflect God’s image as women. Together, as men and women, we reflect God’s glory.

And together, as men and women, we multiply and have children.

That’s what we see here in Genesis 5.

And so, as men and women, made in God’s image, we were created to be in a relationship with God and through our relationship to one another as men and women, we multiply and have children.

Now that doesn’t mean that everyone will all have relationships with God and that we all communicate with God.

God has created us to relate to him, but together as men and women, as the human race, we have rejected God and turned away from him.

And so, by rejecting God, we have failed to live up to what God has created us to be.

When we rejected God, we faced God’s judgement.

And that’s what we see here in Genesis chapter five.

In this chapter, we see ten generations of mankind, spanning across one thousand years, all facing the same fate.

They live a certain number of years, they father a son, lived another set of years, had other sons and daughters, then died.

And so the pattern is: Live, become a Dad, live, become dad again, died. Next!

We see this pattern here in Genesis five and we see it repeated all around the world, all throughout history and we see it in our lives.

Live, for some of us we become parents, then we die.

It’s a constant reminder that we were doomed to die because we have failed to reflect God’s image.

We fail to reflect God’s image when we would rather destroy vulnerable lives than to save lives.

We fall short of God’s glory when we would rather spent thousands on holidays and give nothing to those in need.

We fail as God’s image bearers when we reject that God has made us in his image, that he made us male and female.

This is what our city has become, this is what our nation has become, one that’s known to reject male and female as bearers of God’s image.

Our city is now in the middle of what’s known as World pride month, where it takes pride in men committing sexual sins with other men, women committing sexual sins with women.

It’s a month where our city takes pride in anything and everything sexual that’s forbidden by God.

The only sexual intimacy that God allows for in the Bible is the sexual intimacy between a husband and a wife.

Anything outside of that, whether it’s boyfriend and girlfriend, consent or without consent, homosexual or heterosexual, is forbidden.

And yet, we’ve become so used to it that we had forgotten how it goes against our very identity as the image of God.

Our sexual sins do not bring glory to God, at all.

They are not beautiful, no matter how you might disguise them.

Instead, they are embarrassing attempts to overthrow God and his word.

That’s our city; it’s a city of sin.

We should be saddened by it because in the end, sin leads to death.

That’s what happens when we stop fulfilling our identity as the image of God; we will face his judgement, we will face death.

That’s what we see here in this record of the first ten generations of the human race.

It’s the pattern of life, become a father, then die.

The Seventh Son walked with God

But there is a break in the pattern.

Look with me at Enoch, the Seventh Son, in verses 21-22, “Enoch was 65 years old when he fathered Methuselah. And after he fathered Methuselah, Enoch walked with God 300 years and fathered other sons and daughters.”

Enoch was known as someone who walked with God for 300 years after he fathered Methuselah.

Three hundred years!

It’s not that the rest of the people in this record didn’t walk with God, but among these ten, Enoch stood out.

He was known as someone who walked with God.

What does it mean to walk with God?

Notice that Enoch wasn’t described as walking after God or walking before God. He wasn’t simply just doing the right thing, even though it certainly included that.

There was something deeper going on.

On my days off, I often walk with Jenny along Botany Bay from Brighton to Ramsgate.

We didn’t walk simply to get from A to B.

We walked to get to know the other person, to talk with the other person.

Similarly, to walk with God is to get to know God.

It is to speak with him in prayer and to listen for him in his Word.

That’s what we’re meant to do as the image of God; that’s our purpose!

That’s our identity!

We were made to walk with God as the image of God.

And it’s a walk that we do by faith.

In the New Testament, in the Letter to the Hebrews, we read this about Enoch, in Hebrews 11:5-6, “By faith Enoch was taken away, and so he did not experience death. He was not to be found because God took him away. For before he was taken away, he was approved as one who pleased God. Now without faith it is impossible to please God, since the one who draws near to him must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him.”

To walk with God is to walk by faith in Him; it’s to trust in him.

You can’t please God if you don’t believe that he exists.

Instead, since you believe that God exists, you live in a way that seeks him and his way.

You want to live in a way that pleases him, knowing that he will reward you if you do.

It’s a good thing to walk with God; it brings much comfort.

That’s the positive side.

The negative side is to not walk in a way that rejects him.

In Jude 14-15, we find another reference to Enoch.

“It was about these that Enoch, in the seventh generation from Adam, prophesied: “Look! The Lord comes with tens of thousands of his holy ones to execute judgment on all and to convict all the ungodly concerning all the ungodly acts that they have done in an ungodly way, and concerning all the harsh things ungodly sinners have said against him.”

Enoch knows that to live in an ungodly life is to walk into God’s judgement.

You can’t claim to walk with God and yet live an ungodly life.

You can’t call yourself a Christian and live an unchristian life.

To walk with God is to leave your ungodliness behind.

To walk with God is to leave your sexual sins behind and walk with God in his holiness.

To walk with God is to leave your hatred behind and to walk with God in his love.

Enoch walked with God for 300 years.

I don’t know how he did it.

300 hours would be a big effort for anyone to walk with God in his love and holiness but to walk 300 years with God in holiness!

After 300 years of walking with Enoch, God took Enoch and he was no more.

Everyone else in the family record was recorded to have died, but not Enoch.

God took Enoch away, so that he didn’t have to face the pain and agony of death.

Was Enoch an impossible ideal?

After all, no one else besides him was described to have walked with God for 300 years.

God didn’t spare anyone else death, not until the time of Elijah thousands of years later.

Why did God put the story of Enoch in the Bible, when no one else in history walked with God for as long as Enoch did?

Walk with Jesus, the perfect image of God

God put the story of Enoch there to give us a glimpse of what he has in store.

For one day, there would be one, a descendant of Enoch, who walked even more closely with God.

There is one whose walk is so close to God that he is indistinguishable from God himself.

That’s because the one walking is the Son of God.

And His name is Jesus.

Jesus is the image of the invisible God.

He is the clearest, most perfect image of God that you’ll see because his relationship with God is perfect.

He is faultless, he is glorious, and He invites us to walk with him.

Only He can lead us to where God is, only he can take us there.

He is the Way, the Truth, and the Life.

Jesus is the Light of the world anyone who follows him will never walk in the darkness but will have the light of life.”

Today, if you want to walk with God, you follow and walk with Jesus.

He’s like the hero that you often see in movies.

In the midst of the chaos and all the bullets flying, he crashes through the wall and he says, “Come with me if you want to live.”

While the heroes in the movies might use guns and throw punches to take you to safety, Jesus gave up his life for you to take you to safety.

Jesus died to provide the way for you to go to God.

Jesus died but he rose again to show you that he knows the way.

Follow him if you want to live.

Now the way that he leads may not be good and easy.

It’s not necessarily going to be happy and healthy.

In fact, Jesus has not promised that you will live a better life than non-believers.

Your life won’t be better according to the world’s standard but why do you want to follow the world’s standard anyway?

In the end it leads to death.

There will always be dissatisfaction.

You will never be truly content, every day is just one day closer to death.

But if you walk with Christ, life may be harder, you life may even be shorter, but there is life at the end.

Every day is one day closer to life, to eternal life.

Enoch walked with God and was taken by God.

Walk with Jesus and we can be sure that Jesus will take us to God.

We’ll be with God not because of how good we are but because Jesus is walking with us and we’re walking with Him.

As we come here today, I know that many of us are facing all sorts of difficulties.

I know that for some of us here today, asking you to follow Jesus is a big ask.

I know that some of you had to overcome extraordinary obstacles to be here today.

I know that some of you come here, even though your husbands or wives, want you to be somewhere else.

I know that some of you come are going through incredible hardships.

I know that some of these hardships are physical, others are mental, and still others are emotional and even spiritual.

I am very thankful to God for your faithfulness and I am praying for you.

I can’t tell you why you are going through them; I can’t give you any simple solutions.

But I can tell you this: As you walk with Jesus, we are walking with you.

And so keep walking with Jesus, don’t give up.

If you are a Christian, you will never walk alone.

Your brothers and sisters in Christ will walk with you.

Jesus will walk with you.

Keep walking with Jesus, don’t give up!

And if you do a brother or sister in Christ struggling, walk with them.

Do not leave them alone in their struggles.

Listen to them, pray with them, read God’s word with them.

Help them to keep walking with Jesus and encourage them to not give up!

How do we encourage one another to walk with Jesus?

Keep encourage one another to read about Jesus in the Bible and to pray toi him.

Ask Jesus, “is this the way you want me to go?”

Actually, go ahead and ask him.

“Is this how you want me to behave? Is this how you want me to live?”

And if reading the Bible you find out that Jesus’ answers is no, ask him,

“How do you want me to behave? How do you want me to live?”

When you realise you couldn’t do it on your own, ask him,

“Help me to live your way. Help me to walk your way.”

And so, to conclude, what does your walk say about you?

Are you walking with Jesus, the One who walks perfectly with God?

Are you fulfilling who you are as the image of God, by following Jesus, the true image of God?

Or are you falling short of who you’re supposed to be?

Are you walking with the world, rather than walking with Jesus?

Friends, God has made you in his likeness.

And so, walk as the likeness of God by walking with Jesus.

Study everything you can about Jesus.

Study his teaching, study his life, study the significance of his life as recorded in the New Testament, study how God has prepared for his coming in the Old Testament.

As you study him, my prayer is that you’ll come to know that glorious Lord, the Son of the Living God.

My prayer is that you won’t just know him as a historical figure in a historical book, as true as that is, but you’ll come to know him as the Living Lord.

The One who is alive today, who is in the Church, in his Church, here today.

That you will trust him and walk with him.

A few years ago a young man by the name of Austin Butler was cast as Elvis for the movie, Elvis.

To prepare himself for the role, he dived into the character of Elvis.

He listened to Elvis’ songs, he studied Elvis, he even thought about how Elvis would brush his teeth!

He trained his voice to be like Elvis so that even after the movie finished, he just kept speaking like Elvis because he was voice was so he used to it.

Even Priscilla Presley, Elvis’ wife, said, “It was more than I ever expected. He became Elvis.”

Friends, Austin studied so much to study for a role in a movie about a man who lived and died.

Shouldn’t we devote our lives to study about the Jesus, the man who died and lives?

Friends, if you’re not a Christian yet, if you don’t walk with Jesus yet, then can I ask you to make this the most urgent priority in your life.

You know the pattern.

You are living and one day you will face death.

Today is the day to turn to Jesus.

He’s the Only Way.

Turn around and follow him; walk with him.

If you want to find out more about how to walk with Jesus, see either me or Range, or Po Ki or Jiawu.

If you already know Jesus, then keep walking with him.

Don’t just know about him; study him, learn from him.

Walk as the likeness of God by walking with Jesus.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How will I live my life differently if I consciously live as the image of God?

How does my walk (the way I live) reveal my identity?

What would Jesus say about my walk with him (according to his own words, the Bible)?

Friends, bearers of Gods’ image, walk in the way that you were created to walk.

Walk as the likeness of God with Jesus.

Forgive us, Lord. Have mercy on us. We do not deserve your salvation and yet, you have been merciful in the Person of Jesus. Father, please forgive us as a nation and as a city for our failure to live as your likeness. By the blood of Jesus, forgive us our sins, that we may walk with Jesus and so walk with you. Let us walk with Jesus, let us follow him and obey his way. Let us follow him and live, for Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
