To marry or not to marry

1 Corinthians 7:25-40

Big Idea: Decide to stay unmarried or to get married by thinking about the present, God’s timeline and Christ’s pleasure.

Look around: “No time for changing ship”

Look beyond: Your time on God’s timeline

Look to Christ: Pleasing Christ and pleasing your spouse

Deciding between the good and the better

To marry, or not to marry, that is the question that many, if not all of us will ask sometime in our lives.

For some of us, we’ve asked, and we have answered that question many years ago.

For some people, we have decided to say, yes, we want to marry.

For others, we might have said no, we won’t marry.

For still others, it might be a not yet; we won’t marry just yet.

In Australia, roughly half of the population is married and the other half either never married, separated, divorced or widowed,

With just over one third who identify themselves as never married.

Last week, we read about what the Bible says to those who are married, how we owe a beautiful debt to our spouse.

This week, we’ll see what the Bible says about those who are deciding whether to marry or not.

How should those who are currently not married think about marriage?

What does a Christian need to think about before saying these important words at the wedding, the words “I will”?

If you have your Bible there, please open up with me to 1 Corinthians 7, beginning from verse 25.

Today, we’ll see four things in the passage:

Look around: “No time for changing ship”

Look beyond: Your time on God’s timeline

Look to Christ: Pleasing Christ and pleasing your spouse

Deciding between the good and the better

Before we begin, let’s pray:

“Father, please help us see you will for us as we hear from your words today. By your Spirit, empower us to do what pleases you. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

Look around: “No time for changing ship”

The Apostle Paul had spoken very briefly, just one short sentence in fact, in verse 8 to those who are unmarried and to widows.

He tells them that it’s good if they remain unmarried, just like him.

Now, in verse 25, he expands on why it’s good to remain unmarried.

Look with me at verses 25-26 (Read out – Enya, please type verses 25-26)

Paul’s here not giving a command but he’s giving a very helpful opinion as someone who’s faithful.

Here he’s speaking to the virgins, the young women and the young men of the Corinthiam Church.

He tells them again to remain unmarried, just as he is.

He expands on that by telling them that in light of the present distress, it’s good to remain unmarried.

In his commentary on this verse, the Bible scholar Leon Morris says, “when high seas are raging, it is no time to for changing ships.”

I don’t know about you, but I’m prone to get seasick.

Anyone else here gets seasick?

Here’s a picture of someone inside the ship on one side of the wave.

Here’s a picture of him when the ship’s on the other side of the waves.

He could barely stand.

Now imagine him trying to change from one ship to another even as the waves were pounding on the ship.

Possible? Maybe. Was it the best time to change ship? Maybe not.

We don’t know what kind of distress the Apostle Paul’s referring to.

Maybe it’s a severe famine.

While it’s certainly possible for couples to get married during a time of distress, Paul’s advising them that it’s good to remain unmarried for now.

Even if we don’t know exactly what distress Paul was referring to, we know that it wouldn’t have been easy to marry in the first century.

When you’re married, you would be expected to have children.

And the infant mortality was high.

Some scholars say one in four infants die at birth, while others say it’s almost one in two.

And of course, sometimes the mother dies as well.

It was a heavy responsibility to marry and start a new family in ancient times.

While the way we approached medicine and childbirth have come a long way since the first century,

Raising a family is still difficult.

Starting a new family unit is still difficult.

While it is wonderful to be married and start a family, it can still bring about a lot of stress.

And so, the Apostle Paul tells the Corinthians to think about the distress that they’re currently facing.

We don’t know what it is, but they are going through an especially difficult time.

And so Paul advises them that in light of the added difficulty, it is good for a man to remain unmarried, just as he is.

If he is already engaged, don’t call off the engagement.

If he’s not engaged, don’t look for a wife.

It’s not a permanent command, but a good advice from a faithful teacher during this difficult time.

The man is free to choose.

Look with me at verse 28 (Read out – Enya, please type verses 28)

They can choose to marry or they can choose not to marry.

They’re not sinning either way.

Paul just wants them to marry with an expectation that they will face trouble in this life.

Last year, many couples would’ve postponed their weddings due to COVID.

I know of at least one couple who planned an overseas wedding who had to postpone it indefinitely.

However, there were others who married in the middle of the COVID lockdown.

I personally know of two couples who wedded with a small group (around 10 people) and attended another couple’s wedding where we had more guests but had to seat 1.5 metres apart.

I’m sure both groups were told about the difficulties and the stress that comes with getting married during COVID.

Neither the couples who married nor the couples who didn’t marry sinned because of their choice.

However, what each couple needed to do was to be informed about the stress and troubles that will come with being married and to be prepared for it,

Especially in a time of distress.

And so, before you think about marriage, think about the situation you’re in.

Is it a good time to change ships?

Is it a good time to go from being unmarried to be married.

For those who are younger among us (and parents and grandparents, uncles and aunties, I hope you’re listening), think carefully before you start a relationship.

Even if the person is the right one, is this a good time to start a relationship?

Are you ready to commit to each other?

Don’t jump ship before you’re ready!

Being in a relationship is good, but there will be times when staying unmarried is even better.

And so firstly, look around.

Second look beyond.

Look beyond: Your time on God’s timeline

Look beyond your own time; look to God’s timeline.

Look with me at verse 29: (Read out – Enya, please type verses 29)

Our time is limited; we only have a short time.

We need to look to God’s timeline.

It’s like this:

You see this part? This represents your time on earth.

You’ve got a few short years here on earth, and then you have eternity somewhere else.

The crazy thing is that for some of you, you only think about this red part.

You study hard, you work hard, you then enjoy what’s left of your life.

You only think about how happy this part of your life is going to be and you don’t care what’s going to happen to you for the rest of your time in eternity.

You think about how comfortable you live for the next few years, or even few decades, and you neglect to think what will happen to you in the next few centuries, the next few millions of years.

It’s insane!

What you do with your life right now will determine what will happen for the next millions of years.

In particular, it’s what you do now in your life with regard to Jesus that will determine what’s going to happen for the rest of your existence.

Seeing where your time fits in God’s timeline should help us know how to use the little time that we have wisely.

We only have a short time; our time is limited.

So, make your time count!

Marriage is good, but live as if you’re not married.

Marriage lasts a lifetime, but it only lasts a lifetime.

Even marriage will pass away.

You might be weeping and crying because you’re not married, but don’t let that define you.

Live as though you’re not weeping, because marriage will pass away.

You might be rejoicing because you’re married.

That’s great, but live as if you’re not rejoicing, because marriage will pass away.

If you have bought many things, anything, live as though you don’t have them, because they, too, will pass away.

If you use the world in any way, and we all do, don’t make full use of it.

Don’t invest everything into it, because the world, and all that’s in it, will pass away.

When Jesus came into our world, God’s kingdom has broken into our world.

Eternity has entered our time.

The future has come into our present.

And so, we now think of our present, in light of the future;

We think of our time, in light of God’s timeline.

This is not about withdrawing from the events and activities of this world;

Rather, it’s about thinking about the importance of the activities of this world in light of heaven.

Is it worth it for you to spend all your time on something that will fade away?

In light of God’s timeline, how urgent are your most urgent projects?

In light of God’s timeline, how important is it for you to have your tiny moment of happiness?

In light of God’s timeline, how will you decide whether to marry or not to marry, as good as marriage is?

As Augustine once said, “We cannot love what is eternal unless we cease to love what is temporal.… Learn to dismiss it before you are dismissed by it.”

It’s important to love people we are in relationships with.

It’s important to love our marriages!

But don’t lose sight of what’s eternal!

Your marriage, as good as it is, is not the final chapter in your story.

Your marriage is not your final happily ever after.

Being in Christ is.

It’s only in Christ that we can truly be happily ever after.

Jesus once told a parable about a man who gave a large banquet and invited many to come.

When everything was ready, he sent his servant to tell those who were invited, “Come, because everything is ready.”

But without exception, they all began to make excuses, “I just bought a field, I must see it.”

Another said, “I just bought five oxen, I’m going to try them out.”

Still another, “I just got married, and I can’t come.”

When the Master found out, he was angry and invited other people instead.

The parable ends with these words, “For I tell you, not one of those people who were invited will enjoy my banquet.”

God has invited us to share in every spiritual blessing in Jesus Christ.

It’s yours to take in Jesus!

And yet, there are those who would give excuses to not coming to celebrate Jesus.

What excuses do you give for putting the temporary things over the eternal things?

What excuses do you give or you’ve heard given for not coming to serve and celebrate Jesus?

Talk to the person next to you.

What excuses do you give or you’ve heard given for not coming to serve and celebrate Jesus?

If you’re here, you can just tell me (it’s faster)

Or if you’re at home, you can send me a message via whatsapp, wechat or Zoom.

What excuses do you give or you’ve heard given for not coming to serve and celebrate Jesus?

You have twenty seconds.

There are so many excuses, aren’t there?

Don’t give into them.

When you’re making decisions, look beyond your time and look towards God’s timeline.

Look around, look beyond.

Look to Christ: Pleasing Christ and pleasing your spouse

Also, look to Christ, think about how you please Christ and how you please your spouse.

Look with me at verses 32-34 (Read out – Enya, please type verses 32-34)

When you’re married, your time and your devotion will be divided.

I still remember a story that a minister once gave about one of his colleagues.

Both this minister and the colleague were given a very long report to read.

The minister was married at that time and could only start reading after spending time with his family and after putting his young children to sleep.

When he met up with his colleague, he found out that his colleague had not only read the report but had prepare a detailed summary of the long report.

Has that been the experience of any other parents here?

Is there any parent who had to divide their time with their kids and preparing for a deadline?

There’s nothing wrong with trying to look after the family.

There’s nothing wrong trying to please your spouse.

In fact, it’s a good thing to do.

However, the fact remains that if you have a spouse, if you have a family, your devotion will be divided.

It’s good to please the spouse but you will need to know that it will affect your devotion to Jesus.

Of course, every one of us who’s called a Christian should want to please Jesus wholeheartedly.

However, the married person must, rightly, also be concerned for their spouse.

Here’s where we see that there’s a great advantage to being unmarried.

They can serve the Lord with an undivided concern; they can serve the Lord without the distractions that married will inevitably have.

Now, there are some rare cases where a married person might spend more time on serving the Lord to the detriment of their family.

It’s not helpful when that that happens.

However, in many cases, and in most cases from my experience, spouses tend to want to please their spouse more than pleasing the Lord.

So often, the decisions that spouses make, are often about what makes their spouse happy rather than what pleases the Lord.

Of course, for married couples, it’s always a balancing act, but often, it leans dangerously close to the spouse, almost every time.

But before we start pointing the finger to the spouse, we need to ask ourselves, “what does my devotion to Jesus look like?

“How am I actually devoting my life to Jesus?”

Again, it’s not just the married couples who need to think about their devotion to Jesus.

Every Christian, whether you’re married or not, need to think about our devotion to Jesus.

But generally speaking, the unmarried person will be in a better position to devote their time to the Lord Jesus without distraction.

A person who is married will need to work harder to work out what devotion to Jesus look like, without neglecting their spouse.

The Apostle Paul is saying it’s better to be not caught in that position; it’s better to remain unmarried.

If you are unmarried at the moment, can I encourage you to think seriously about how you can please the Lord?

You have more opportunities, generally speaking, than someone who’s married.

Don’t waste the time that you have on things that will pass away.

Don’t waste time on pleasing yourself.

Devote your time to please the Lord Jesus.

Serve him wholeheartedly without distraction, whether it’s serving him here at Church or in the privacy of your own home.

If you are married, work extra hard to think how you can please the Lord, even as you’re pleasing your spouse.

Work hard to get the balance right, not neglecting but loving your spouse on the one hand and not neglecting but devoting to the Lord Jesus on the other.

And so, we’re to look around us, look beyond us and look to Christ.

What does that look like in summary?

It’s deciding between the good and the better

Deciding between the good and the better

Look with me at verse 36 (Read out – Enya, please type verses 36)

Here, the Apostle Paul describes a man’s decision to marry his fiancée.

If he’s acting improperly toward a girl that he’s engaged to, and she’s beyond the usual age of marriage, he should marry.

Here, I don’t think the man’s necessarily being sexually improper.

Rather I think it’s a girl that he’s already prepared to marry and had already prepared his family and her family for the marriage.

The girl’s at an age where she should be married.

If that’s the case, he should marry her, especially if that’s the intention he’s been portraying to both the girl and the community around them.

It would be improper for him to lead the girl on, so to speak.

He should marry; he wouldn’t be sinning.

However, if the man has determined in his heart and is under no compulsion to marry, it’s also fine for him not to marry, as Paul had advised.

If he’s decided not to marry at this particular time but to keep her as his fiancée, he’s also doing well.

Here, it’s not a matter of him deciding between the good and the bad,

But deciding on the good and the better.

It’s good for him to marry the fiancée; it’s better for him not to marry her.

The same applied for the widow.

The widow is bound to her husband as long as he lives.

But when he dies, she’s free to marry anyone she wants – but only in the Lord.

She’s free to marry because marriage, as something belong to the world, is finished once one of the spouses pass away.

She’s free to marry, but she’ll be happier if she remains unmarried, as Paul is.

It’s good for her to marry but it’s better for her to remain unmarried.

Often when we make decisions, there will be times when both results are good.

Both results can work and bring glory to Jesus.

In those times, we need to decide which result is better in light of the present situation, in light of God’s timeline and in light of our devotion to Christ.

It’s the abC’s of making decisions.

We need to look around, look beyond, look to Christ.

Far too often, Christians make these life-changing decisions rashly, without looking around, without looking at God’s timeline and without looking to Christ.

As a result, they face many unexpected troubles along the way, which can often test their devotion to Christ.

For example, when we think about moving, we often think about the suburb first, whether it’s a nice place to move in.

Then we think about whether we can go to work around there.

And finally, we think about whether there’s a Church around there that we can attend.

But the Christian, wanting to please Jesus and aware of God’s timeline and their own situation, asks God for where He would want them to serve first.

They would look for a place where they can serve him and a Church where they can minister in.

They would then find an appropriate job nearby.

After that, the would find a suitable house nearby.

That’s how Christians ought to make decisions.

Because we know that Jesus is the Lord, because we know that our time is short in God’s eternal timeline, we think of what honours Christ, what pleases Christ first.

If you’re unmarried and are thinking about who to marry, think about your abC’s.

Look around you: is this a good time for you to get married?

Look beyond your time: what part of this passing world am I holding on far too tightly? What relationships, what things, am I holding on far too closely than I should?

Look at Christ: How will my relationship bring glory to Christ?

If you’re married, also think about your abC’s.

What situation is my spouse and I facing together right now?

How do we need to look beyond what we have and what makes a difference in eternity?

How do we need to commit or recommit our devotion to Christ?

Whether you’re married or unmarried, make your decision according by looking at what’s around you, what’s beyond you and what would please Christ.

Make your life decisions by looking at the abC

Make life decisions by looking around, by looking beyond, and by looking to Christ.

There are many posts, many videos, many blogs, that ask us the question: If you can go back to your younger self, what would you tell yourself?

Some people will say, “Hang in there, you’ll get through.”

“Keep working on that skill; it’ll come in very handy.”

Imagine your future self, from 50 years, no, 500 years, 5000 years in the future coming back to you.

Do you know what your future self say to your present self?

I know exactly what you’ll say.

Do you know what you’ll say?

You’ll say, “Jesus, love Jesus, serve Jesus.”

It doesn’t matter whether someone believes in Jesus now or not.

The Bible tells us that man is destined to die once and after that to face judgement.

And the judge on that judgement day is none other than Jesus.

On that day, we’ll be judged by our relationship to Jesus.

In 5000 years, it won’t matter whether you’ve been rich, or influential or happy all your life.

The only thing that matter is whether you have loved Jesus and whether you have served Jesus.

The things of this world will pass away.

But Jesus is the same yesterday, today and forever.

Today, make your decisions, your life decisions, in light of Jesus.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How can I be more devoted to Jesus today than I was yesterday?

What excuses do I give for not devoting to Jesus?

How does Jesus determine the major life decisions I am / we are making?

Friends, make your life decisions by looking at the abC

Make life decisions by looking around, by looking beyond, and by looking to Christ.

By your Spirit, increase our love for him, intensify our devotion to him, for he is the King of Kings and Lord of lords. Please let him be the reason for all our decisions. We pray this in his name, Amen.”
