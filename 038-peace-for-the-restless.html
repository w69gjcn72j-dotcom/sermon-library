Peace for the Restless

2 Samuel 7:10-16

Big Idea: God himself will build David’s everlasting kingdom in Jesus and His Church

1. We like to celebrate our success

2. We tend to get our definition of success wrong

3. Only God can build a successful kingdom

God builds a successful kingdom in David’s descendant

God will love David’s descendant like his own son

God’s king is Jesus who builds the unstoppable Church

Intro:

Many of us would’ve heard of Apple company.

When I was still a little boy, my Dad brough home our first Home Computer.

It was an Apple Clone. We called it the Apple II Plus but it was actually a clone of the Apple II Plus.

Since then, Apple had made huge impacts all around the world with their technology, whether it’s the use of a mouse, our use of the computer, the way we listen to music or the way we use our phones.

They’ve come a long way!

In an interview, the founder of Apple, Steve Jobs, said that when he hired people, he wanted people who wants to make a little dent in the universe.

He wants to hire people who would make a permanent change to the universe, no matter how small.

He wants people who want to do something significant and worthwhile.

It’s good to know that what we’re doing is worthwhile, isn’t it?

We don’t want to live our lives with little to show.

We want to make something out of our lives.

We want to make our lives count.

We want to make something that’s significant, something that lasts.

But how do I know?

Will what I build in my life last?

In today’s passage, we meet a man call David.

Not me, but King David from the Old Testament.

We’ll pick up the story from the Book of 2 Samuel chapter 7, beginning from verse 10.

Kind David wanted to build something that lasts.

He wanted to build something significant, something that says, David was here.

It seemed like a good idea at first, but God had something even better in mind for David.

We’re going to have three points:

1. We like to celebrate our success

2. We tend to get our definition of success wrong

3. Only God can build a successful kingdom

We like to celebrate our success

We all like to celebrate our success, don’t we?

I know I do.

Earlier this year, I wanted to exercise to keep fit.

I set a little goal for myself: If I can exercise every day for the next two weeks, I’ll give myself a small reward.

And so, I worked hard, I was disciplined and I exercised every day for two weeks.

You know what I got for myself?

I got myself a pair of exercise pants so that I can continue to do more exercises!

By the time we come to King David in today’s passage, he had achieved a lot more!

By all accounts, he was a successful king.

By the time we come to him in today’s passage, he had already won many battles, he had built a secure city and established a fairly peaceful kingdom.

He was living in a nice house and from our point of view, he had it made.

He was in a good place.

And so, with everything set in place, he now wants to build a temple for God.

That’s what many near eastern kings in countries around him would’ve done.

To celebrate their achievements, many ancient kings would build temples to their gods to celebrate what their god has done for them.

And so, David wants to build a temple to the LORD, who gave him victory after victory.

He wants to celebrate what God’s done for him by building a temple.

Of course, he’ll also be remembered as the one who’s built the temple.

In fact, he would be the first one in the history of Israel to do so because from the time the Israelites got out of Egypt until the time of David,

The ark of God had always travelled in tents.

Even as David was living in a nice house, the ark of God, which signified God’s presence, remained in the tent.

And so, David wanted to build a temple for God, something significant, a first, in Israel’s history.

Rather than leaving God in a temporary tent, David wants to build a house for God that lasts.

It’s not just King David who wanted to build a house for God that lasts.

Many people throughout history had built magnificent church buildings that lasts.

One such person was Josep Maria Bocabella, a Spanish bookseller who went to Italy and came back to Spain to build a magnificent Church building.

The Church building that he would begin to build was the Basílica de la Sagrada Família.

Construction first started in 1882 and it hasn’t finished yet.

There had been nine architects working on the Church over the past 138 years.

The most famous architect was the second architect, Antoni Gaudí.

Whenever someone talks about the Church, they know it Gaudí’s Sagrada Família.

It looks magnificent and is listed as UNESCO World Heritage site.

Surely that’s the very definition of success for an architect – to be remembered by your building so many years after your death.

Wouldn’t it be great to build something that lasts?

Wouldn’t it be great if we have something like Gaudi, something by which we will be remembered for years, even centuries to come?

But here’s the problem with Gaudi’s Sagrada Família; it’s Gaudi’s Sagrada Família.

Whenever I searched for Sagrada Família, I see Gaudi’s name and not God’s.

As magnificent as the Church building might be, there’s a problem if it’s Gaudi’s name that comes to mind, even before God.

It’s a magnificent building, but is it really a success?

2. We tend to get our definition of success wrong

And that’s the problem with us; we often get our definition of success wrong.

We might think we’re successful, when in reality we’re not.

It’s like when I would play games on my phone with my children.

I would play a game call ‘Duet’ with my children.

It’s a game where you control a stick so that it doesn’t hit any flying objects.

I would get up to level 9 and thought that I would be pretty good.

When I tell my children about it, they would tell me that they had already finish the whole game, all 49 levels.

That’s a little bit like what King David was doing.

He thought he’s made it; he thought he reached the peak of his success.

He thought that he was in a position to build a magnificent temple for God.

But God said No to David; God said No to David’s definition of success.

Yes, David had been successful on a number of occasions, but he hasn’t reached what God has for him yet.

David hasn’t reached God’s definition of success yet.

As good as he was, King David hasn’t reached his peak yet, according to God.

And more than that, Kind David doesn’t get to decide when and who builds God’s temple.

God, in his own time, will choose who he wants to build his temple.

The initiative is God’s, not David’s.

In fact, God emphasised that it was God himself, not David, who made David great.

It was God who took David from being a shepherd boy to be the ruler of his people.

It was God who destroyed God’s enemies before David.

And it’s God who will make David’s name great, not David.

Look with me at verse 10 and verse 11, and note the tense “I will designate a place for my people Israel and plant them, so that they may live there and not be disturbed again. Evildoers will not continue to oppress them as they have done 11 ever since the day I ordered judges to be over my people Israel. I will give you rest from all your enemies.

What tense is it? It’s future tense.

God will designate a place, God will plant them.

Evildoers will not continue to oppress them

I will give you rest.

God will give these to David; David hasn’t got them yet.

Though David might think that he’s successful, he’s not quite there yet.

When I was a theological student, I went to visit other Churches on mission.

There was one Church I’ve been to that I thought was one of the best Churches I’ve ever been to.

It had hundreds of people, maybe a thousand or more.

They had a big staff team; they often go door-knocking around their neighbourhood.

They had many, many small groups.

Surely that would be a definition of a successful Church!

But the pastor there told us that even though the Church might look big, there are still thousands more in their suburb who didn’t know Jesus.

They could fill the Church many times and there will still be a lot of people who didn’t know Jesus, who would not go to heaven.

And so, no, he doesn’t see his Church as a successful Church.

How about us?

Do we see ourselves as successful?

But more importantly, would God see you, would God see me, as successful?

What does it mean to be successful in God’s eyes?

Only God can build a successful kingdom

To be successful in God’s eyes is to recognise that ultimately, only what God builds is truly successful.

That’s because God is the true master builder.

Just two hours’ drive from Sydney is a place called the Capertee Valley.

It’s near where the Blue Mountains are, between Lithgow and Mudgee.

What’s so special about the Capertee Valley?

It’s the world’s second largest canyon in the world.

It’s even wider than Grand Canyon in the United States!

The valleys there were formed over many thousands, if not millions of years.

It’s something that God has built and it’s truly magnificent.

God is the true master builder.

What God builds will last.

And here God promises to build a house for King David.

Rather than getting David to build him a house, God will build David a house.

God will build a successful kingdom for David’s descendants.

Look with me at verse 11, from the second half,

“The Lord declares to you: The Lord himself will make a house for you. When your time comes and you rest with your ancestors, I will raise up after you your descendant, who will come from your body, and I will establish his kingdom. He is the one who will build a house for my name, and I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever.”

God will build a house for David.

It will be a house that lasts beyond David.

Unlike many families, where the prominence and influence of the family dies with its most famous member,

God promises that David’s house won’t stop with David.

Even the death of David won’t stop his house from flourishing

God will build a house for David that lasts forever.

Here God’s not talking about a physical house, but the family of David, the household of David.

David will have a descendant, a son, and God will establish his kingdom.

God will make David’s kingdom last forever.

It will be a permanent reign.

When you look far into the future, thousands of years, millions of years from now, you will see one of David’s descendants, David’s Son, reigning over a Kingdom that God’s built.

If we keep reading the Book of 2 Samuel, we’ll see that David will have a son.

His name is Solomon and he will build a magnificent temple for God.

God doesn’t talk much about that house, as significant as it might have been.

God’s far more interested talking about the house he will build for David.

It will be a house that lasts forever.

As good as the promise is, there is one problem.

The sons of David themselves.

What happens if they disobey God? Would God still keep his promise if they disobeyed God?

After all, God said that he would’ve made the throne of David’s predecessor, King Saul, permanent but Saul lost his throne because he disobeyed God.

What if the sons of David disobeyed God? Would they lose their throne, just as Saul lost his?

Here’s where the House of David and the House of Saul differs.

God will love the son of David like his own son.

Look with me at verses 14-15, “ I will be his father, and he will be my son. When he does wrong, I will discipline him with a rod of men and blows from mortals. 15 But my faithful love will never leave him as it did when I removed it from Saul, whom I removed from before you.”

God will have a Father-son relationship with the son of David.

God will love the Son of David as if he were his own son.

God will punish him if he does wrong, just like any good and loving father would.

But God will never take his faithful love away.

And for over the next four hundred years or so, the sons of David would put this promise to the test.

All the sons of David, sitting as king in David’s kingdom, would do wrong against God.

Some realised their mistakes and turned back to God.

Others kept sinning despite God warning them time and time again to turn back.

Solomon, who built God’s kingdom, had seriously wronged God and as a result, David’s kingdom was split after him.

Even David himself, the one to whom God gave the promise, even he had seriously wronged God and suffered the consequences of his actions.

God kept the House of David reigning over the Kingdom of Israel, then the Kingdom of Judah for around 400 years.

However, at the end of the 400 years, the Babylonians came and conquered over them.

While the sons of David would continue, they weren’t reigning from a throne.

Has God’s promise to David failed?

What happened to God’s faithful love?

God’s promise to David hasn’t failed.

But rather than establishing an earthly, physical kingdom, God was building something far more significant, far more long-lasting.

All earthly kingdoms, earthly empires, earthly nations, will fade away.

But the Kingdom that God builds for the House of David is one which not fade.

It’s the Kingdom that God builds through King David’s Greater Son, the Lord Jesus Christ.

In Jesus, we see a King who will reign over God’s Kingdom.

In Jesus, we see a King who builds the unstoppable Church!

When Jesus was in Caesarea Philippi, he asked his followers this question, “Who do you say that I am?”

One of his followers, Peter, replied, “You are the Christ, the Son of the Living God.”

Here, Peter sees a clear connection between Jesus and the promise God made to King David all those years ago.

He said that Jesus is the Christ, which means the Anointed One.

He’s God’s King.

If we go through Jesus’ family tree, we’ll see that he’s indeed a Son of David.

Peter also said that Jesus is the Son of the Living God.

Jesus is the king whom God said he would love as his own Son.

We see glimpsed of God’s promise being fulfilled in the sons of David over the history of the nation of Israel.

But it’s in Jesus that we see God’s promised completely fulfilled.

It’s Jesus’ throne that God will establish forever.

It’s Jesus’ kingdom that God will establish forever.

When God said to David, “Your house and kingdom will endure before me forever, and your throne will be established forever’”,

He’s talking about the house, kingdom and throne of Jesus.

What does Jesus’ house look like?

It’s the Church.

Listen to how Jesus replies Peter in Matthew 16:17-18, “Jesus responded, “Blessed are you, Simon son of Jonah, because flesh and blood did not reveal this to you, but my Father in heaven. 18 And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church, and the gates of Hades will not overpower it.”

Jesus will build his Church and even the gate of Hades will not overpower it.

Jesus will build an Unstoppable Church.

Even as oppositions rise against the Church, be they religious, ideological or even political, the Church won’t be stopped.

The Church can’t be stopped because it’s Jesus who builds the Church.

Even the very fires of hell can’t overcome the Church.

The Church is the House that Jesus builds.

The Church, built by Jesus, is what true success looks like because it lasts.

What we build, no matter how significant it is, won’t last.

But what God builds in Jesus, His Kingdom, will last forever.

What does this mean for us?

We need to find rest in the Son’s Kingdom.

At Christmas, we celebrate that the Prince of Peace, Jesus has come.

Jesus is the Prince of Peace because he’s building an everlasting kingdom, a kingdom that’s secure.

We will find rest when we come into his Kingdom.

Even as we gather today for Church, Cyclone Yasa is wreaking havoc on the islands of Fiji.

It’s a Category 5 storm and it threatens the lives of 600,000 people on the island nation.

Yet in the middle of the storm is a little house in the Nagigi village, in Savusavu.

Six families are gathered there singing to God while the storm rages outside.

Take a listen to what they sing.

What a beautiful picture of trust in God, even in the eye of the storm.

They’re not singing because their house is secure.

They’re singing because they their faith is secure, and they trust in God.

Friends, please continue to pray for Fiji as they brace for Cycloe Yasa.

That’s what 2020 is like isn’t it?

It’s a turbulent year where goals are changed, and lives are changed.

Even as we speak, the Christmas plans for thousands of people around our state has changed because of an outbreak, particularly in the North Shore.

For a long time, we think that we’ve been successful as a state, and generally speaking, we’ve been doing well.

But we’re not as successful as we hope to be.

Our plans, our lives, can change all of a sudden.

But even in the midst of all our changes, we know for certain that there’s One Person Who doesn’t change.

He is God.

God’s promise will never fail. The Kingdom that He’s built won’t be moved. The Church that Jesus God the Son builds will never be stopped.

There’s security in God’s everlasting Kingdom.

If you’re not in God’s kingdom yet and you want to find out more about what it means to come to God’s Kingdom, come and see me afterwards.

I would love to talk with you about it.

Also, we’ll be having a Christianity Explored Course starting on 10th January next year.

If you would like to find out more about Christianity, we would love you to join.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Would God see your life as successful?

Do you belong to God’s everlasting kingdom?

What don’t I need to fear because I’m in God’s kingdom?

Friends, this Christmas, find your rest in the Son’s Kingdom.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for the sure hope you give to us in your Son Jesus Christ. Please forgive us when we try to look for our rest and security in our own success and in our own cleverness. Please humble us before you and bring us into the Kingdom of Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.
