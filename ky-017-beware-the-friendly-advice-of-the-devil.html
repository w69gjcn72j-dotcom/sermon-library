Beware the friendly advice of the devil

Luke 4:1–15 · Sunday 18 October 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah · For high-school students · Sermon by Rev David Yung

Big Idea: The devil’s temptations sound like friendly advice: look after yourself, be your own boss, do it your way. Jesus, the Son of God, wouldn’t — he trusted his Father’s word. When you’re tempted, run to Jesus, who has already won.

Aim: by the end, students will

see why the devil’s offers sound like friendly advice — look after yourself, be your own boss, do it your way;

spot where they’re tempted to be their own boss at school, online or with friends, and learn to pause and pray first;

run to Jesus, the Son who won, when they’re tempted — not try to beat it alone.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, paper, about 60–75 minutes.

1. Connect (10 min)

  • What’s the best-sounding bad idea a friend has ever pitched to you — a shortcut on homework, a risky dare, a “trust me, it’ll be fine”? What made it sound so good?
  • You’re at a mate’s house for dinner and the host isn’t at the table yet. Do you start eating? What stops you, and what’s that about?

2. Read: Luke 4:1–15 (CSB)

1 Then Jesus left the Jordan, full of the Holy Spirit, and was led by the Spirit in the wilderness 2 for forty days to be tempted by the devil. He ate nothing during those days, and when they were over, he was hungry. 3 The devil said to him, “If you are the Son of God, tell this stone to become bread.” 4 But Jesus answered him, “It is written: Man must not live on bread alone.”

5 Then he took him up and showed him all the kingdoms of the world in a moment of time. 6 The devil said to him, “I will give you their splendor and all this authority, because it has been given over to me, and I can give it to anyone I want. 7 If you, then, will worship me, all of it will be yours.” 8 Jesus answered him, “It is written: Worship the Lord your God, and serve him only.”

9 Then he took him to Jerusalem, had him stand on the pinnacle of the temple, and said to him, “If you are the Son of God, throw yourself down from here. 10 For it is written: He will give his angels orders concerning you, to protect you, 11 and they will support you with their hands, so that you will not strike your foot against a stone.” 12 Jesus answered him, “It is said: Do not test the Lord your God.”

13 After the devil had finished every temptation, he departed from him for a time. 14 Then Jesus returned in the power of the Spirit to Galilee, and news about him spread throughout the entire vicinity. 15 He was teaching in their synagogues, being praised by everyone.

3. Context

Straight after Jesus is baptised and called “my beloved Son” by his Father, the Holy Spirit leads him into the wilderness for forty days. There the devil makes three friendly-sounding offers: make your own food, rule the world your own way, and force God to rescue you. Each time Jesus answers with God’s word from the book of Deuteronomy. The question underneath: what kind of Son will Jesus be — one who looks after himself, or one who trusts his Father?

4. Discuss

  1. v.1–2 — Jesus is “full of the Holy Spirit” and “led by the Spirit” into the wilderness to be tempted. What surprises you about that? What does it say about temptation not meaning God has left us?
  2. v.3 — The devil says “If you are the Son of God” — he knows who Jesus is, and he sounds helpful. Why is “look after yourself” such friendly advice? Where do you hear it at school or online?
  3. v.3–4 — Jesus could have made bread, so why didn’t he? Think of the little brother grabbing the drumstick before the head of the table sits down. What does “Man must not live on bread alone” mean when you’re stressed, bored or left out?
  4. v.5–7 — What does the devil offer, and what’s the catch? Why does “in a moment of time” matter? How would one little bow have been easier than the cross?
  5. v.8 — “Worship the Lord your God, and serve him only.” What do we “bow” to without noticing — being liked, followers, grades, comfort? How do we excuse it: “everyone does it”, “it’s my life”?
  6. v.9–11 — The devil quotes Psalm 91. How can someone use the Bible to tempt you? Think of a verse used to justify something you already wanted to do.
  7. v.12 — What would it have meant for Jesus to test God by jumping? How is testing God different from trusting him? Where do we say “prove it, God — do it my way”, or do nothing with what he’s already given us?
  8. v.13–15; Hebrews 4:15–16 — Jesus beats every temptation by trusting his Father’s word. How does knowing Jesus was “tempted in every way as we are, yet without sin” change what you do when you’re tempted? What would it look like to run to Jesus this week?

5. Live It Out

KOG 3·2·1·0 — pray for 3, connect with 2, invite 1, 0 left unloved.

  • Pray for 3 — three friends or family members who don’t know Jesus yet. Pray for them by name every day.
  • Connect with 2 — be a real friend this week: a lunch, a walk or a proper chat (not just a reaction in the group chat).
  • Invite 1 — invite one of your three to KOGYouth, to church on Sunday, to the Discover Course or to the Christmas concert on 13 December.
  • 0 left unloved — spot the person who’s on their own at school or at youth, and sit with them.

This week’s challenge: Before one decision this week — a message, a purchase, a plan with friends — “ask the head of the table first”: pause, pray and ask God what he wants before you act. Then notice where you’ve been bowing to likes or approval instead of the Lord.

Memory verse: “Worship the Lord your God, and serve him only.” (Luke 4:8)

6. Pray

  • Thank God for his word, and for Jesus, who trusted his Father when it cost him everything. Say sorry for the times you’ve tried to be your own boss.
  • Ask the Holy Spirit to protect and guide you, and to give you God’s kind of hunger — hunger for what is right.
  • Pray by name for your 3 friends — that they would meet Jesus, who won for us.

7. Leader’s Notes

Framing. Joyfully point to Jesus. Some students may not yet be Christians, or may be Christians only because of their parents; this passage is good news for them — the Son has already won, and the Father’s welcome is given in Jesus, not earned. Never put anyone on the spot, and let students pass on a question if they want to.

Two likely wrong turns

Wrong turn 1 — “try harder to resist.” Students may hear this as a list of ways to beat temptation by willpower. The sermon’s answer is that we can’t overcome the devil by ourselves; we need our great high priest. Keep coming back to Jesus, who won, and invite students to run to him.

Wrong turn 2 — “the devil’s offers are obviously evil.” The offers sound friendly — feed yourself, be in charge, trust God and make big plans. Help students see that good-sounding things become bad when we do them without asking God first. Ask: has the head of the table said so?

Question helps

Q1 — The Spirit leads Jesus into the wilderness, so temptation is not a sign that God has left us. Jesus is a son of Adam like us, yet the Son of God in a way no human being ever was or ever will be.

Q2 — The devil doesn’t doubt who Jesus is: “If you are the Son of God — and of course you are…”. The advice sounds like common sense. Choosing to look after ourselves without trusting God first disrespects God.

Q3 — Jesus could have made a feast, but until God says so he does not move, not even to feed himself — doing so would have ruptured his relationship with the Father. Trusting God’s word is more important than food.

Q4 — The devil’s rule has an expiry date. One bow would have avoided the suffering and death of the cross. The shortcut was easier — but it wasn’t the Father’s way.

Q5 — Deuteronomy 6:13: no one is to be above God. When comfort or approval is our king, we are doing what the devil wanted. Only God is King.

Q6 — The devil knows the Bible and can quote it (Psalm 91:11–12). Scripture twisted to make God do what we want is still temptation.

Q7 — Deuteronomy 6:16: at Massah Israel wanted God to save them on their terms. Jesus would have been saved, but on his own initiative. The issue is waiting for the Father’s leading instead of making our own plans for God.

Q8 — We can’t overcome the devil by ourselves; we need our great high priest. When tempted, run to Jesus: at the throne of grace we find mercy and grace in time of need. If you’re an adopted child of God, you are a child of God — so trust the Son of God and live for God.
