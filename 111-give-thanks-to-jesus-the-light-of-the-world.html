Give Thanks to Jesus, the Light of the World

John 8:12-20 · Thanksgiving 2020
Big IdeaThank God for Jesus, the true light of the world.

Sermon Title: Give thanks to Jesus, the Light of the World

Passage: John 8:12-20

Big Idea: Thank God for Jesus, the true light of the world

Jesus is the Light of the World

Jesus is God’s light

No darkness in the light

The Light of men

Jesus can be trusted

Is Jesus valid?

The Father and the Son testifies

Be thankful for the Light

He leads the way

He triumphs over darkness

He gives the light of life

Intro: Diwali, Jewish Culture, Vivid

Yesterday, many of our subcontinental friends celebrated Diwali.

Diwali is the Hindu festival of lights.

It is a very popular celebration in Hinduism.

It’s a celebration of spiritual victory of light over darkness, good over evil, and often the triumph of knowledge over ignorance.

It’s a festival with live music and food, celebrated by millions of people around the world.

In the Jewish culture, there’s a festival of light also.

It’s called the Feast of the Tabernacles.

In Jesus’ days, in the City of Jerusalem, there would’ve been a great celebration.

There would’ve been a lot of lamps set up in the temple’s courts.

Devout men of God would’ve been dancing with torches in their hands, singing praises to God.

It would’ve been very exciting to see.

Jesus is Light of the world

It’s in the midst of the Feast of the Tabernacles, underneath the lights glowing in Jerusalem,

That Jesus declares, “I am the Light of the World.”

It’s an astonishing thing to say.

Jesus has made some incredible claims about himself throughout the Bible.

He says, “I am the Way, the Truth and the Life”

Another time, he says, “I am the Resurrection and the Life”

Here, he says, “I am the Light of the World”

He’s not saying that there’s mystical battle between light and darkness and that he represents the light.

No, he’s saying, he is the Light.

It’s a very daring thing to say, especially to a Jewish audience.

Why? Because in the Bible, the Lord God is said to be Light.

The Jewish people would’ve sung this song, maybe even during their festival, “The Lord is my light and my salvation.” Psalm 27:1

God’s word is said to be a light for God’s people, a lamp for their path. Psalm 119:105.

Jesus takes this concept of light about God and apply it to himself, by saying, “I am the Light of the World.”

It’s an outrageous thing to say, unless, of course, it’s true.

What does it mean for Jesus to say that He is Light?

First of all, it means that he is God’s Light.

God’s sent Jesus into the world to be his light in the world.

When you see Jesus, you see God’s light.

In Hebrews 1:3 we read, “The Son is the radiance of God’s glory and the exact expression of his nature”

It’s like the Father is like Sun in the centre of our Solar System and Jesus is like the sunbeams that reaches the earth.

When you see the sunrays coming through the clouds, you see the radiance of the Sun.

You can’t separate the sun from its radiance.

In a similar way, when you see Jesus the Son, you see the radiance of God the Father.

You see the Light of the Father’s glory.

You can’t separate the glory of the God the Father from the glory of the God the Son.

And so, in Jesus, you see God’s light.

Second, when you see Jesus, you see men’s light.

That’s how the Bible describes Jesus in John 1:4, “In him was life, and that life was the light of men.”

Jesus is God’s light, leading men and women, boys and girls, back to God.

Why do we need Jesus to lead us back to God?

Because the world that we live in is a dark place.

Even with all the scientific advances, all our wealth, all our attempts to make a better world,

our world remains to be hopelessly divided, miserably dark.

If you needed to be reminded how dark and fallen our world is, just turn on the news, the local ones and the global ones.

You’ll see the second and even third waves of COVID-19 coming back to different countries.

You’ll hear wars and rumours of wars.

You’ll see tragedies upon tragedies.

Why is the world so dark? It’s because from a spiritually point of view, from God’s point of view, we’re blind.

We’re spiritually blind people, walking and living in a dark world.

When I was in Jerusalem a few years ago, I had a chance to walk down Hezekiah’s tunnel.

Hezekiah’s tunnel was dug during the time of King Hezekiah of Judah 2700 years ago.

It’s built under the City of David and provides fresh water for Jerusalem on top of Mount Zion.

The tunnel was built on the side of the mountain and it’s completely dark as you walk through it.

It’s over 550 metres long of darkness.

You cannot see your own hand at all, even if it’s right in front of your face.

To walk down it, you need a headlamp, so that you can see where you’re going.

Jesus says he is the Light of the world, anyone who follows him will never walk in darkness but will have the light of life.

No matter how dark the world is, no matter how hopeless the world is, those who follow Jesus does not need to be afraid.

Jesus is the light that leads us through the deepest darkness.

Those who follow him will never walk in darkness because he is leading the way.

Where there is light, shadows flee.

That’s what we see when we light up a match.

What happens when you light up a match? You see light.

Do you see the light’s shadow? No.

The flame is a light source so it’s not going to cast a shadow.

Jesus is the brightest light, for he is God’s light.

Those who follow him will never walk in darkness.

Jesus can be trusted

But this brings us to an obvious question.

It’s very well to say that Jesus is the light of the world

But can Jesus be trusted?

That’s exactly what the religious leaders were thinking.

Listen to how they respond to Jesus, “You are testifying about yourself. Your testimony is not valid.”

They’re saying, you can’t just say that you’re the Light of the World.

Just because you say you are something doesn’t mean that it’s true.

We all have met someone who say that they are someone special or an expert in something, but it turns out that they’re liars.

How can we trust Jesus’ extraordinary statement about himself?

Jesus replies by saying, in verse 14, “Even if I testify about myself, my testimony is true, because I know where I came from and where I’m going. But you don’t know where I come from or where I’m going.”

Jesus knows his origin and he knows he destination.

Jesus has come from God and Jesus is returning back to God.

The religious leaders of Jesus’ day had no idea who Jesus is; they’re blind to his identity.

Rather than judging Jesus according to his origin and his destination, namely, heaven, they were judging Jesus according to the flesh, according to their own human standards.

They were using human methods to measure the man from heaven, who’s going back to heaven.

That is, they’re using an earthly inferior method to judge Jesus, the very Son of God.

An so Jesus tells them, “You judge by human standards. I judge no one.”

That is, Jesus doesn’t judge by human standards.

It’s a little like this.

In the past few months, many, many people have used their social media a lot more because they’re locked down in their homes.

Many people search through social media to look for ways to prevent getting COVID-19.

Sadly, there are many, many false posts on things like Wechat and Whatsapp that have led many people astray.

There are posts which claimed to be from World Health Organisation or a national health ministry that says eating garlic, or sesame oil and vitamin C can kill the coronavirus.

Other posts say that if you don’t drink enough water, you’ll get coronavirus.

All these are pseudoscience, not based on actual scientific research, that has spread a lot of lies among many people not trained in science.

I don’t know about you, but I would rather hear from someone who had actually done the necessary research,

who’s familiar in the field of epidemiology and virology

than an unknown person with no background on wechat about the latest development in the fight against COVID-19.

Similarly, when men try to judge who Jesus is, they often use inferior, men’s judgement rather than using God’s own superior judgement.

There are people who have done no historical research and they just simply say, Jesus doesn’t exist.

There are others who dismiss Jesus based on what their communities have taught about Jesus, communities who don’t even know who Jesus is.

Others respond to Jesus by according to their feelings.

All these are men’s way of trying to get to know Jesus.

They are substandard, inferior ways of knowing Jesus.

They are like blind men trying to describe the brilliance of the sun, utterly foolish and downright arrogant.

No, the only way by which Jesus’ words can be verified is by God himself.

Only God can testify about God.

Listen to what Jesus says in verse 16, “And if I do judge, my judgment is true, because it is not I alone who judge, but I and the Father who sent me.”

Jesus is verified not according to human standards but by God’s standards.

God’s existence does not depend on what you think of him.

God exists whether you believe him or not.

And so, no, we don’t judge God according to our standards.

Rather, God is judged and verified by God himself.

Jesus, the Son of God himself, testified that He is the Light of the world.

God the Father affirms what Jesus says.

Both God the Father and God the Son affirms Jesus’ testimony.

That’s more convincing than anything that man can come up with!

In this dark, confusing world, it’s a precious thing to know that in Jesus, we have the Light of the world.

It’s a precious thing to know that in a deceitful world like ours, Jesus can be trusted.

He’s the man from heaven, who’s destination is heaven.

Those who walk with him with him to heaven; they’ll never walk in darkness.

What does this mean for us?

We need to be thankful for Jesus, the Light of the World.

There are three things about Jesus that we can thankful for.

Jesus is the Light of the world, who leads the way.

We’re thankful for Jesus because He is the Light of the world and we can trust in him.

He shows us the way to God.

He leads us to God in heaven.

He is the Light of the world but that doesn’t mean that the whole world is going to heaven.

It’s only those who follow him who will never walk in darkness, who will have the light of life.

And so, the question is, are we following him?

Are we following Jesus?

It may be a baby step that he wants you to take.

Maybe Jesus is telling you to repent from a certain sin.

Or maybe, Jesus is asking you to take a big step in faith.

Maybe it’s a change in career, maybe it’s a change in where you live.

Jesus is leading but are you following?

Second, be thankful for Jesus because He is the Light of the world, who triumphs over darkness.

In Jesus, there is true victory over darkness.

In Jesus, we have the victory too.

The victory that we have in Jesus has is not over flesh and blood but over the rulers, the authorities and the power of darkness.

Jesus will triumph over all that’s evil and we’ll share his victory.

Just as darkness flee in the presence of light, all that’s evil and dark will flee in his presence.

Whatever darkness, whatever evil that you’re facing now, know that one day, it will be defeated in the light of Jesus.

Finally, be thankful for Jesus because He is the Light of the world, who gives the light of life.

In Jesus, there’s life; outside of him there is only blindness and darkness.

Don’t take the life that Jesus gives for granted.

Jesus has given his life so that you can have the light of life.

God has raised Jesus from the dead to show that Jesus can be trusted and to show that you can have eternal life in him.

Be thankful for the life that he’s given you in Christ.

At the same time, don’t forget that there are many in your world, in my world, who still live in darkness.

There are many in the world who still don’t know Jesus.

In a little while, we’re going to pray.

But before we do, can I ask you to close your eyes.

At this point, I’ll ask Jenny to translate for us so that our Chinese-speaking brothers and sisters don’t have to follow the subtitles.

Friends, let’s close our eyes for a minute.

Keep them tightly closed; we’ll turn off the lights as well.

Friends, there are many people living in darkness, without the light of Jesus.

There are many people who don’t know Jesus and walking blindly in the darkness.

Do you know of anyone who’s still living in darkness?

Can you see their faces?

Pray for them, that God may shine the light of Christ upon them, that they may be saved.

Perhaps you are walking in darkness still and don’t know Jesus.

If that’s you, can I encourage you to come to the Light of Jesus.

He is the Light of the world, come to him, follow him and have the light of life.

Let’s spend thirty seconds of quietness, either praying for those walking in darkness, or for ourselves, that all of us might know Jesus, the Light.

Let’s pray,

“Lord God, you who said let light shine out of darkness, please shine your light in our hearts to give us the light of the knowledge of your glory, as displayed in the face of Christ. May we always give you thanks for Jesus, the Light of the World, our Leader, our Victor, our Life, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
