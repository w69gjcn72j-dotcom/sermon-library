The Acts of the Holy Spirit

Passage: Acts chapter one, verses one to fourteen

Additional passages: Isaiah chapter 43 verse 10, Luke chapter 24 verses 50-53

Big Idea: The Holy Spirit empowers the apostles (and us) to be prayerful and venturing witnesses of the risen Lord Jesus

The Lord Jesus is not dead but risen

The Kingdom of God is led by the Triune God, not men

Be prayerful and venturous witnesses, not self-determining huddles.

When I was a kid, I was scared of rollercoasters; they’re fast, and they take you to great heights.

And every now and then, they take you upside down on a giant loop.

It’s scary! Why risk it?

However, as I grow older, I decided to give them a go.

It was scary at first but once you get pass the first big drop, it’s ok.

You trust that the harnesses will keep you safe; you’ve seen people have done it before and they seemed to enjoy it.

And so, every now and then, I’ll try out the roller coasters at theme parks.

As my children grow older and more adventurous, I go with them.

I like hearing them scream, for joy.

I got so used to roller coasters now that when I went on this one called Hair Raiser at Ocean Park, I didn’t just do it with the children or for the excitement.

I was enjoying the view from the top; it’s a beautiful view of Hong Kong at the top of the roller coaster ride!

Was the ride completely risk free? No, you could have dropped a phone you forgot you had in your pocket, or someone might vomit on at the top of the loop and you catch their vomit at the bottom.

But you looked at the risks, and you decide that the risks are not big enough for you to avoid it.

That’s what we do with other aspects of our lives.

Nothing in life is risk free, but we look at the situation, decide whether it’s worth taking the risk for.

If the benefits outweigh the risks, then we take these risks on.

Sometimes, in fact many times, people will take great risks, either because the reward is far greater than the risks involved or because the risk is greater if they don’t take the little risks now.

How would you assess Christianity?

Is Christianity safe, risk-free? Or is Christianity risky, even dangerous?

Men and women throughout history have risked their lives for Christianity.

Sometimes they’re rescued from certain death but other times, too many times, they suffer and even die for their beliefs.

But of course, there are those who call themselves Christians who have lived safe and relatively risk-free lives.

Some go to church on Sundays while others only go whenever they can, because they don’t want to risk losing their job.

They want to play it safe; they want to be called a Christian, but they would avoid any risks that come with it.

And so, which is it? Is Christianity safe or not?

To find out, please open up your Bible with me to Acts chapter one beginning from verse one.

Today, we’ll be starting a new sermon series on Acts.

If you would like to take sermon notes, there’s space in your Companion booklet.

In today’s passage, we’ll see three things:

The Lord Jesus is not dead but risen

The Kingdom of God is led by the Triune God, not men

Be prayerful and venturous witnesses, not self-determining huddles.

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Almighty God, as you shine on us by your Word, may we not be blind at midday, seeing nothing of your glory. And may we not willfully seek darkness, to hide from your glory. Instead, wake us up by your word, let us tremble at the majesty and beauty of your word, and by your Spirit, let us be live lives that testify to Jesus, who speaks the truth, and in whose name we pray, Amen.”

The Lord Jesus is not dead but risen

The first thing we see in the book of Acts is that the Lord Jesus is not dead but risen.

Please open up with me to Acts chapter one, beginning from verse one. Let’s read the first two verses together, “I wrote the first narrative, Theophilus, about all that Jesus began to do and teach until the day he was taken up, after he had given instructions through the Holy Spirit to the apostles he had chosen.”

The book of Acts is the sequel, as it were, to the Gospel of Luke.

In the Gospel of Luke, the historian Doctor Luke gives a record of all that Jesus began to do and to teach.

Luke recorded that Jesus was born, grew, taught his disciples (now known as Apostles in the Book of Acts).

Luke recorded that Jesus suffered and died on the cross, but that he also rose again from the dead and ascended with his body back to heaven.

That is, in the gospel of Luke, Luke gave us a record of Jesus’ life from birth until he was taken up to heaven.

Look with me at Luke 24 verses 50 to 53. Let’s read this together: “Then he led them out to the vicinity of Bethany, and lifting up his hands he blessed them. And while he was blessing them, he left them and was carried up into heaven. After worshiping him, they returned to Jerusalem with great joy. And they were continually in the temple praising God. “

The Book of Acts, the second book in Luke’s volume, Luke expands on what happened just before Jesus was carried up into heaven.

Let’s read verse three together, “After he had suffered, he also presented himself alive to them by many convincing proofs, appearing to them over a period of forty days and speaking about the kingdom of God.” It’s not just now, in the modern era, that people wanted proof of Jesus’ resurrection.

People in Jesus’ day wanted proof as well, and Jesus gave it to them.

It reminds me of this creature.

Do any of the children know what it is?

It’s a platypus!

When scientists in England first saw it, they thought somebody was playing a trick on them.

They thought someone had cut up different parts of different animals and then put them all back together in one piece.

I mean, look at it: it has the body of a cat, it has the beak of a duck and the tail of a beaver.

It has four webbed feet like those of ducks (which only have two feet).

It didn’t seem true because they haven’t seen anything like it.

But the evidence convinced them that the platypus is a real animal.

When Jesus came back to life, he gave many convincing proofs that he had rose from the dead.

He ate with his friends; he even cooked breakfast for his friends.

He showed them his feet and his hands, where the nail marks were.

He showed himself to disciples, families, and at least 500 people at one time.

People couldn’t believe it at first but the evidence is there! Jesus had definitely rose from the dead.

But the fact that Jesus rose back from the dead, if understood purely as a fact in and of itself, then we have missed out on its true significance.

The fact that Jesus rose from the dead tells us that the Kingdom of God is near.

God’s ultimate rule is coming; the last days of the world as we know it is coming.

That’s why it’s vital that when we try to understand the significance of the death and resurrection of Jesus,

We must let Jesus do the explaining.

Only Jesus himself can tell us the true meaning of his death and resurrection.

And Jesus explains his death and resurrection through the idea of the Kingdom of God.

This is the heart of the Christian message: Jesus came, Jesus died, Jesus rose again.

The King of God’s Kingdom has risen; the King of God’s kingdom is here.

If you take the theme of the Kingdom of God out of Christianity, then you will only get the shell of Christianity; you’ll get a religion without its power.

For example, in just a little over six months, we’ll be celebrating Christmas.

Christmas is not just celebrated by Christians but by people all over the world, many of which aren’t Christians.

We Christians celebrate the birth of Jesus; why do non-Christians celebrate Christmas?

What for?

And why put up Christmas decorations and Christmas banners that talks about celebration but don’t talk about who we’re celebrating?

For example, during Christmas, instead of seeing the words, “the Birth of Jesus” or“Merry Christmas”, you see the words ‘Season’s Greetings’. What does that even mean?

It’s like saying, “Hello, it’s wintertime”. So what?

When we take the Kingdom of God out of our message of Jesus, it’s like taking the word Christ out of Christmas.

It takes away the heart of the message of Jesus Christ.

When we first came to our Church, we tried to think of new names for our children and youth ministries.

The names that we eventually came up with was based started with the letters K O and G. KOG.

We have KOG Mini, KOG Junior，KOG Primary, KOG Youth, KOGworx.

Why KOG? Well, it’s because we live in Kogarah.

But it’s also because KOG is the abbreviation for the “Kingdom of God”.

We want to always remember that we’re in Kogarah physically, and we’re also spiritually living in the real KOG – the Kingdom of God.

We want the people of Kogarah to come to the KOG, the Kingdom of God.

As a church, we want to be a place where people can come and find out more about KOG.

We do that through our preaching, our songs, our Growth Groups.

We do that explicitly in the School of Christian Living, where we spend three years thinking through how we can read and teach the Bible through thinking the idea of the Kingdom of God.

We produced our own evangelistic brochures, “the 7 stars” that’s based on the Kingdom of God.

Our message is simple: the Risen Lord Jesus Christ is the King of the kingdom of God and we, his followers, are citizens of his kingdom.

The Kingdom of God is led by the Triune God, not men

Now notice that it’s KOG – Kingdom of God, not KOM – Kingdom of Men.

We don’t get to decide where the Kingdom of God goes, God does.

The God that we Christians believe in is the Triune God – One God, Three Persons.

We believe in God the Father, God the Son and God the Holy Spirit.

And we see the Three Persons of the Triune God here as Jesus talks about the direction of the Kingdom of God.

Open to verses 4-5 with me, read it out loud with me and put up your hand when we come across any Persons of the Trinity, in both Chinese and English:

“While he was with them, he commanded them not to leave Jerusalem, but to wait for the Father’s promise. “Which,” he said, “you have heard me speak about; for John baptized with water, but you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit in a few days.”

All three Persons of the Trinity are directly involved with the direction and growth of the Kingdom of God.

God the Father promised to give God the Holy Spirit, to the followers of God the Son, Jesus.

Jesus says this will happen very soon, in a few days.

Listening to Jesus, some of the apostles might have thought that Jesus was referring to a prophecy in Joel chapter 2 verses 29 to 31,

I will even pour out my Spirit on the male and female slaves in those days. I will display wonders in the heavens and on the earth: blood, fire, and columns of smoke. The sun will be turned to darkness and the moon to blood before the great and terrible day of the LORD comes.”

Is this it? When Jesus says that the Holy Spirit will come, does he mean the great and terrible day of the Lord is about to come, in a few days?

Tightly linked to the last day is the restoration of the Kingdom of Israel.

You see, the Jews have been living under foreign oppression for hundreds of years before Jesus came.

The foreign occupier at the time of Jesus were the Romans.

And so, the apostles were probably thinking along these lines.

Jesus said that the Kingdom of God is near.

What’s God’s kingdom? God’s kingdom is God’s people, living under God’s rule and blessings, in God’s land.

And now, with the coming of the Holy Spirit fast approaching, God’s people (that is, the followers of Jesus) will be able to live under God’s rule, by the power of the Holy Spirit.

How about God’s land? Will Israel have their own land and governance now?

And so the apostles asked Jesus, “Lord, are you restoring the Kingdom of Israel at this time?”

Jesus didn’t deny about restoration of the Kingdom of Israel. He didn’t say No!

The Kingdom of Israel will be restored, but not in the way they expected.

For the Kingdom of God is not geo-political but salvific.

That is, the Kingdom of God is about being rescued from the powers of sin and death.

The apostles will play their part in the restoration of the Kingdom of God, and within that Kingdom are Jews who have believed.

In fact, it will begin with the Jews, the first believers of Jesus, and this in time will expand to all the world.

Jesus said that it’s not for the disciples to know when God the Father will restore the kingdom;

God has his own timing; it’s a timing that belongs to God the Father, not us.

In the meantime, Jesus said that the Holy Spirit will come upon them with power.

The Holy Spirit of God will empower them to do the very thing Jesus has asked them to do:

Verse eight, let’s say it together, “But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come on you, and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth.”

That’s what the apostles are called to do.

They are to be witnesses of the risen Jesus, and explaining to people the significance of his death and resurrection.

They say, “Jesus is risen! It’s true! And here’s why it’s important for me, and you.”

It starts with the Jews in Jerusalem but it doesn’t stay there.

It goes to the Jews in Judea and the Samaritans in Samaria as well.

It’s not limited to a certain race; It’s for all people, even for people whom your culture hates and despises.

It’s for all the world.

The apostles were to be gatherings of people, also known as Churches, who testifies to the significance of the death and resurrection of Jesus.

They are to be witnesses to the King of the Kingdom of God.

And they are to do this with the empowerment of the Holy Spirit.

They need the Holy Spirit to do what Jesus had commanded them to do.

They couldn’t do this without the Holy Spirit.

However, with the empowerment of the Holy Spirit, they were to go forth from Jerusalem and into all the world, crossing cultural and political boundaries, venturing into all points of the globe, pointing people to the Lord Jesus.

In time, that very gospel came to us here, at Kogarah.

Today, we can hear the good news of Jesus because an apostle shared the good news with someone almost two thousands years ago.

They then told someone else, and so on, and so on, until it came to us here, at Kogarah.

That’s why you can hear the gospel at our Church this morning.

The gospel has come to Kogarah, in Australia, which, for some people overseas, is almost the end of the world!

Someone along the way obeyed the command of Jesus and brought it here to us.

Though some humans might have planned this along the way, ultimately, we can trace this back to Jesus’ command to his first apostles.

That is, God the Father promised the gift of God the Holy Spirit, God the Son gave the command, and now, God’s people, empowered by the Holy Spirit, goes and tells the good news of Jesus.

Jesus then ascended back into heaven; he was physically lifted towards the sky.

The apostles stared into the sky until Jesus disappeared behind a cloud.

Heavenly beings, then appeared to remind the apostles that now’s not the time for staring into the sky.

Jesus gave them a command; they have a job to do.

Jesus will come back one day, and all of us, everyone on earth, will see him come back from the skies.

In the meantime, Jesus’ followers are to obey his commands.

They are to wait until the Holy Spirit comes, and then go out to be witnesses of Jesus, to the very ends of the earth.

The Gospel of Jesus has come to us, message of the Kingdom of God has come to us, but it mustn’t stop with us.

We mustn’t be the last stop for the gospel.

We need to take the gospel beyond the walls of this Church, even beyond the suburb of Kogarah.

We need to take the gospel beyond the boundaries of our comfort zone, beyond the boundaries of cultural heritage.

Someone crossed their cultural heritage to come to ours, so that we can hear the gospel.

Over the past two weeks, we met two missionaries who left the comfort of their cultural heritage to share the gospel in a different cultural context.

Over the past few weeks, I’ve heard multiple church members asking me how what our church can do to reach people of other cultures.

The great thing is that these people were concerned not about people of their own culture, as much as they love their own culture.

They were keen that people who are not like them will also hear the gospel.

One of the difficulties in multicultural churches is that people of one culture are only concerned for the welfare of the people of their own culture.

They want to make sure that those who are of the same culture as they are get the most benefits, which often leads to bitter divisions.

But when we’re concerned for the welfare and salvation of those who speak a different language, who has a different culture,

We show that we have understood Jesus’ command to be his witnesses in all the world, not just our part of the world.

Since Jesus has commanded us, and since we have God the Holy Spirit with us, be adventurous.

The Holy Spirit of God is with you, what are you afraid of?

Ok, we might be afraid that people won’t like us when we share the good news; we might feel embarrassed.

You know what, it’s not a 21st century problem but a first century problem.

The very first Christians also knew that they won’t be liked when they share the gospel.

They had even more reasons to be afraid, for there were only a few of them, they were worshipping a crucified criminal who rose from the dead, they had no Church property and they could be hunted down, just like Jesus himself was attacked, both verbally and physically.

They had every reason to be afraid and they would’ve been right to be afraid if they had trusted only in themselves.

However, they had the Holy Spirit of God in them, who would empower them.

And if you’re a Christian, you have the same Holy Spirit inside of you.

By the power of the Holy Spirit, by faith, be adventurous.

Don’t be afraid to take the adventure of faith, trusting that the Holy Spirit will be with you as you obey God the Son.

Don’t be afraid to be the witness that God the Son has commanded you to be.

3. Be prayerful and venturous witnesses, not self-determining huddles

After witnessing Jesus’ ascension on the Mount of Olives they returned to Jerusalem and stayed in an Upper Room.

The walk from the Mount of Olives back to Jerusalem is about 1.2km.

Here’s me on the Mount of Olives a few years ago.

It was a Sabbath Day and so there was no public transport, since Jews don’t work on the Sabbath.

Rev David Wong, the minister who covered me earlier this year, another minister and I walked up to the Mount of Olives.

After a while, we took the taxi back to the hotel (the taxi driver wasn’t Jewish) because we were too tired to walk back.

The disciples, who probably were pretty fit, walked back down the mountain.

The Eleven apostles, together with the women, Mary the mother of Jesus and his brothers, were united in prayer.

That’s what God’s people do; that’s the mark of God’s people.

Yes, we can plan and strategize; we can even evangelize.

And there’s a time and place for all these things.

But our priority must be prayer.

It’s a God led, God-inspired, God- empowered mission that God tells us to go on.

And so we need to seek his guidance in prayer before we plan, strategise and even evangelise.

We don’t get to determine by ourselves what the Church’s vision and mission is all about.

We must entrust ourselves to God, seeking his guidance, pleading for his mercy as we come before him in prayer.

And when we do come together in prayer, we’re not gathering in friendship huddles, merely enjoying our time together as we would at a club, or a restaurant.

No, we gather to think about how we can help one another obey God’s command to go out and be witnesses of Jesus in the world.

We ought to gather to talk about the adventures we had in sharing the gospel; we ought to share about our ups and the downs as we seek to tell the gospel.

We ought to gather to talk about how we can help one another share the gospel, whether it’s local or international.

We ought to gather to pray and ask God to grant us wisdom from above, by the Holy Spirit, so that we can boldly go out and be witnesses of Jesus.

Over the past few years, during our Mandarin prayer meetings, Range had made a habit of taking down our prayer points.

When God has answered our prayer points, whether it’s a yes or a no, Range would move them to a page where we keep a record of the prayers answered.

We pray for personal prayer points, church prayer points and missional prayer points.

Here are glimpses of some of them.

We thank God for the opportunity to come together in prayer as a Church, in English and in Chinese.

My hope is that we will be a Church that understands that we must follow Jesus, and that we are to depend on Jesus.

We show our dependence on Jesus, we show our trust in the Spirit’s empowerment, when we come together, united in prayer.

My hope is that the people coming to our prayer nights will double, from five to ten a week this year.

May God the Holy Spirit so move us that we will come together, united in prayer, as we seek his guidance and his empowerment, in order to be witnesses of the risen Jesus.

May we trust that he will be with us as we go on this adventure of faith with him.

Is Christianity safe or risky?

On one hand Christianity is safe because ultimately, we will find our safety in the Lord Jesus;

Jesus will take us safely back to his home.

On the other hand, as Jesus leads us home, we will face danger.

When we trust Jesus and follow his command to be his witnesses, we will face situations that frighten us, we will face people who will hate us.

They hate us because they hated Jesus first.

But it’s a journey worth taking because we won’t be alone.

The Holy Spirit will be with us and He will give us whatever we need to do whatever He asks us to do.

And so, be prayerful, be adventurous.

Be witnesses of Jesus not just here in the church building, but in Kogarah and in all the world.

And so, friends, the Holy Spirit empowers the apostles (and us) to be prayerful and venturing witnesses of the risen Lord Jesus.

Imagine that you’re part of a rescue team and you’re called to rescue people trapped in a lift.

The cables holding the lift is barely holding the lift and most people are out of the lift, except one middle-aged lady.

There’s only one outcome if she stays in the lift; it will drop and she will die.

She’s afraid to come to you, because she doesn’t know if the lift will suddenly fall.

You go a little bit deeper down the lift, putting yourself in even more danger.

You extend your arm and you reassure the lady, “Lady, trust me, I got you.”

She then grabs your arm, the cable breaks and you rescued her just in time.

Friends, if you’re a Christian, you are on the rescue team.

And you’re on a rescue mission to save people from certain judgement from God against sin.

Just like the lady trapped in the lift, there’s only one outcome if they remain as they are.

But the difference is that the people we’re rescuing aren’t even aware of their danger, spending time making the most of their time in this world, even though their lives are hanging by a thread.

They’re only one heartbeat away from the fires of hell.

And Jesus has sent us, to go and be his witness, to tell them that Jesus has come to rescue them.

We don’t tell them to trust us, but to trust Jesus and to grab hold of his hand.

Rescue missions are hard, and they can be risky, but we do it because that’s what Jesus has commanded us to do.

Someone went on a rescue mission sent by Jesus to rescue you.

Now you go and rescue someone else.

Jesus tells you to go, you’re empowered by the Holy Spirit to go; the Church is praying for adventurous and obedient men and women to go.

Will you go?

Here are the three questions for this week:

Why do we feel like we’re taking a risk when we tell people about Jesus?

What’s the risk to the other person if we don’t talk about Jesus?

What would you like the Holy Spirit to empower you with so you can do what he’s telling you to do?

The Holy Spirit empowers the apostles (and us) to be prayerful and venturing witnesses of the risen Lord Jesus.

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you for giving us your one and only Son, our Lord Jesus Christ. Thank you that he’s come to tell us that the Kingdom of God is near. Thank you that we know that Jesus is the King of your kingdom, that he’s lifted to the highest place because he obeyed you, even to the point of death and that you have vindicated him by raising him from the dead. Father, thank you also for sending us your Holy Spirit, to empower us to be witnesses of the Lord Jesus. Father please empower us so that we will walk this adventure of faith in you, knowing that you’re with us by your Spirit. Please unite us in prayer, that we will always depend on you with humble obedience, for we pray in Jesus’ name, Amen.”ww3e3
