Why Aren't We Wearing the Armour of God?

Ephesians 6:10-23 · 2024
Big IdeaFor the love of Christ, be strengthened to stand against the devil's dark forces by putting on the full armour of God, and by praying always in the Spirit.

Sermon Title: Why aren’t we wearing the armour of God?

Passage: Ephesians 6.10_23

Additional passages: Isa 52:7, Eph 1:13, Rev 19:14-15

Big Idea / Take home sentence: For the love of Christ, be strengthened to stand against the devil’s dark forces by putting on the full armour of God, and by praying always in the Spirit.

Wrestling with the devil’s dark forces

Withstanding the devil with the armour of God

Unwavering prayers for all the saints

Undying love for our Lord Jesus Christ

Many years ago there was a country singer by the name of Skeeter Davis who wrote a song called “I’m a lover (not a fighter)”

Here are some of the lyrics, it’s a wife singing to her husband

“I never seemed to please you lately

'Cause all you ever do is complain

I'm sick of this fussin' and fightin'

So baby, let your loving woman explain

I'm a lover not a fighter

I kinda like it that way

If you want a fightin' partner

Go live with Cassius Clay”

Cassius Clay, of course, was the birth name of Mohamad Ali, one of the most famous and powerful boxers of all time.

This country singer’s saying that she doesn’t want her husband to fight with her, she doesn’t want to be in the boxing ring with her husband.

She just wants to love him; she’s a lover not a fighter.

I don’t know about you, but if a wife says that she feels like her husband is fighting with her as if she was Mohamad Ali, I would be very worried!

She says she’s a lover not a fighter, but is what she’s doing really love?

Or is she just enabling him to do what he shouldn’t be doing?

There are people in our society that says Christians should be lovers not fighters.

People don’t mind if Christians talk about how loving and patient God is, but if Christians talks about the righteousness and judgement of God, the world turns around and says, “You’re Christians! You should be lovers not fighters!”

Are Christians lovers or fighters?

That’s what we’ll be looking at in today’s passage.

Please open up with me to Ephesians 6, beginning from verse 10.

We’ll see four things in today’s passage:

Wrestling with the devil’s dark forces

Withstanding the devil with the armour of God

Unwavering prayers for all the saints

Undying love for our Lord Jesus Christ

Let’s pray, “Father, by your Holy Spirit, open our eyes so that we can see the glorious Jesus even more clearly, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

Wrestling with the devil’s dark forces

As we come to the end of Ephesians, Paul tells his readers there’s one last thing he wants them to know.

Ephesians 6:10, “Finally, be strengthened by the Lord and by his vast strength.”

Why do we need to be strengthened by the Lord?

If you have heard and understood what the Letter to the Ephesians, you will realise that living the Christian life is hard, very hard.

It’s hard for wives to submit to their husbands, children to obey their parents, and slaves to obey their masters.

It’s also hard for husbands to sacrifice for their wives, fathers to instruct their children in God’s way and for masters to be responsible for their slaves.

It’s hard to be a Christian because of our own weaknesses.

It’s hard to be a Christian because of the culture we’re living in.

And it’s hard because there are forces that are intentionally making it hard for us.

Verses 11-12, “Put on the full armour of God so that you can stand against the schemes of the devil. 12 For our struggle is not against flesh and blood, but against the rulers, against the authorities, against the cosmic powers of this darkness, against evil, spiritual forces in the heavens.”

Beneath what we can see and hear, beyond our physical world, there’s a spiritual battle happening.

The devil, that is, Satan, and his dark forces are working behind the scenes, in the spiritual realms, to work against God and his people.

The devil’s aim is simple: to bring down God’s people.

His goal is to make you fall.

He wants you to fail as Christians; he wants you to sin.

He’s got a spiritual army behind him - the rulers, against the authorities, against the cosmic powers of this darkness, against evil, spiritual forces in the heavens.

The Bible doesn’t explicitly specify what these forces are, except they are part of the devil’s dark forces.

Their aim is simple: They want us to sin.

When we sin, they win.

That’s how the devil and his forces measure their success, their key performance index, their KPI, as it were.

When we sin, when we doubt, when we disobey, it’s a win for the devil and his team.

Can we fight against it? Well, we can try, but no weapons of ours will work against the devil.

We’re not fighting flesh and blood; our weapons won’t work against the devil.

Of course, we might try to fight against the devil with our wit and by our sheer will power.

We want to say we can stamp out evil with our high-tech surveillance and our military might.

And that’s exactly what the devil wants you to think.

He wants you to think you can overcome evil with physical weapons, he wants you to think you’re fighting with flesh and blood.

But the truth is we shoot the devil and his army with our guns.

No missile powerful enough to stop him;

And we’ve got nothing on the devil’s far superior surveillance on the hearts and minds of billions around the world.

How do you fight against someone who lures the minds, hearts and spirits of the billions of souls around the world?

How do you stop someone who can influence governments, pop culture, market forces, academia, as long as there’s a human around who’s vulnerable to sin?

We can’t do it by ourselves; we need supernatural strength.

This is what Paul wants to see in his readers: Be strengthened by the Lord and by his vast strength.

Jesus is strong, so strong that he took on death and won.

Jesus had won against the devil, he has triumphed over darkness.

He has the power to do it; it is resurrection power.

And God wants to give you this strength, this resurrection power.

How? God strengthens you by giving you his own armour, the armour of God.

He wants you to wear his full armour, every part of it, in order to stand against the devil and his schemes.

It’s like the marvel hero Iron Man.

In the movie, Tony Stark was a human with no special powers.

But he was rich and smart, so he built his own Iron Man armour with a lot of weapons.

Iron Man’s armour made Tony Stark strong.

We’re weak, we’re prone to fail, but God’s armour makes us strong.

What does God’s armour look like?

Does it have weapons and rockets, like Iron Man’s armour?

No, it’s even better.

Look with me at Ephesians 4:24, “and to put on the new self, the one created according to God’s likeness in righteousness and purity of the truth.”

Your new armour is the new you, the one who has left the darkness of the world, the darkness of sin and the darkness of the devil behind to walk in God’s light.

The new you is created according to God’s likeness.

The new you is the one whose character is based on God’s own character.

You walk in the righteousness and the purity of the truth of the gospel.

The new you in Christ is the armour of God.

When we put off the old self and put on the new self, made in God’s likeness, made like Jesus, we put on the armour of God.

It’s the new you, made in God’s likeness, that can stand against the devil and his schemes.

What will that fight look like?

In verse 12, we read that it’s a struggle.

The word can be translated to mean wrestle.

That is, the fight we have with the devil and his dark forces will be like a wrestling match; it’s hand to hand combat.

He’s going to get up close to hurt you; He will hurt those close to your heart to hurt you.

If you are a Christian, if you are obeying Jesus, you should expect to wrestle against the devil and his forces, you should expect to struggle.

You’re not looking for trouble; but trouble is looking for you.

You struggle, you face trouble, you wrestle, because you’re obeying Jesus.

If you don’t obey Jesus, if you give in constantly to the devil’s schemes, then you won’t feel the struggle, just as a dead person doesn’t struggle to live because he’s dead.

But if you obey Jesus, the devil will do whatever it takes to make you fall.

And left to ourselves, we’re weak and we will fail.

We struggle to obey, we struggle to serve Jesus, we struggle even to come to Church.

But though we are weak, the Lord Jesus is strong.

And so be strengthened, be the new you, take up the full armour of God, so that, (verse 13) you may be able to resist in the evil day, and having prepared everything, to take your stand.

Withstanding the devil with the armour of God

What does the armour of God look like? What does the new you, saved by Jesus, made in Christ’s likeness, look like?

It looks like this:

Verse 14, “Stand, therefore, with truth like a belt around your waist”

In ancient times, they wore loose clothing, but loose clothing made fighting very hard.

And so, fighters have belts around their waist to keep the clothes close to the body, especially around the waist, so that they could move faster.

In Isaiah 11:5, the chosen King of God is said to wear the belt of righteousness and faithfulness

It’s part of the King’s armour, his identity.

And now, as people of King Jesus, we are told to wearing his belt.

In the New Testament, we’re told to wear the belt of truth as part of God’s armour.

We’re to be identified with the truth.

The devil is the father of lies but if we’re in Christ, we are the children of God, who speaks the truth.

Truth matters and the most important truth is the truth of the gospel.

The truth of the gospel must define all that we are and all that we do.

We live and breathe the truth of the gospel.

We wear the belt of truth.

Second, we have the breastplate of righteousness.

In Jesus Christ, we’ve been made right by God and we will be people marked by the righteous life that we live.

We’re not perfect, but by God’s grace we’ve been marked as righteous by God.

And so, we live under his righteousness.

We walk in righteousness, we walk according to God’s way.

And as we do, we’re protected from the enemies’ blows.

We don’t fall into the traps of devil by living a hidden life of sin; we don’t make excuses for sins that are unacceptable for God’s people.

We walk with integrity, honesty and humility.

We live lives reflecting God’s righteousness; we imitate our Father, the righteous God, as dearly loved children.

Thirdly, we have feet sandaled with readiness of the gospel of peace.

Good boots are important; they give you protection, and they get you into enemy territories, as you advance your battle line.

Don’t underestimate these shoes, these sandals – they are offensive weapons in God’s armoury.

Look with me at Is 52:7, “How beautiful on the mountains are the feet of the herald, who proclaims peace, who brings news of good things, who proclaims salvation, who says to Zion, “Your God reigns!”

Christians are sent into all the world to tell people about the good news of peace.

We declare that God’s peace has come.

That is, we proclaim that victory is assured, good will triumph over evil, God will win against the devil and his forces.

The Devil, of course, wants to stop you from speaking the gospel.

He doesn’t want people to believe in Jesus; he wants you and the world to think that you’re fighting a losing battle.

But you wear the shoes that take you to the hearts of enemy territories, telling them the good news, the true news,

that there will be an end to evil, that God has done something about it and he has won.

The Day of Victory is coming!

The Devil wants you to wear your slippers and stay comfortable at home.

God wants you to wear battle-ready boots, always ready to tell the good news of Jesus Christ.

We go through the next few more quickly.

Fourthly, the Shield of Faith.

This is a small one, but the one referred to here a big one, big enough to stop arrows of fire from doing major damage.

As part of the armour of God, as the new you, we’re told to carry the shiled of faith.

The devil will shoot fiery arrows of doubt, lies, anger, pride, immoral living directly at us and those around us.

These are arrows to be shot to you to destroy you.

I’ve seen families torn apart, lives destroyed, communities divided because of these fiery arrows from the evil one.

Take on the shield of faith. Trust in God to keep you safe

In Psalm 91, God’s faithfulness is described to be like a protective shield.

His faithfulness will protect us from the arrows of the evil one.

Take your shield of faith and trust in him.

Fifthly, we have the helmet of salvation, to protect your head.

If you lose your head, you lose your life.

In Isaiah 59:17, God’s said to have His helmet of salvation on his head.

It belonged to him but now he’s giving it to you.

He gives us his salvation.

In order to save us he gave us his beloved, one and only Son.

God gave His Son to you to save you.

And so, trust in God’s salvation.

Don’t forget that if you’re in Christ, you’re saved.

And if God’s given us his one and only Son, if he’s saved us, if he’s for us, who can be against us?

Sixth, the Sword of the Spirit, which is the Word of God.

The word of God, the Bible, is the sword of the Spirit.

It can be both defensive and offensive.

By God’s word, Jesus defeated the devil in the wilderness.

On the last and final day, Jesus will destroy the wicked by his word.

When the devil attacks you with his lies, use the sword of the Spirit, use God’s word.

It’s your God-given weapon to fight against the devil.

But remember that it’s a Sword of the Spirit; you need the Holy Spirit’s help to use the Word of God well.

And so, pray before you use God’s word.

Pray before you try to even understand God’s word.

If we take the sword of the Spirit away and we’re open to attack.

And that’s what the devil has been trying to do and sadly in some cases, succeeded.

Churches that don’t teach God’s word, Church members who don’t study God’s word, Church leaders who don’t obey God’s word are doomed to fail, spiritually speaking.

The devil doesn’t want you to depend on God’s word, to depend on the Sword of the Spirit, because that’s when we’re most dangerous to his cause.

Don’t let the devil win; be a Church, be a Christian, that skilfully use God’s word.

That’s the full armour; do you remember it?

Even if you remember it, it’s hard to use all these, let alone be good at all these.

We need one another’s help.

There will be some who are expert swordsmen of God’s word while others are agile proclaimers of the good news.

We need to work together!

3. Unwavering prayers for all the saints

How many weapons are described? Six.

But right at the end, Paul mentions one thing that is vital in our battle against darkness.

He spends more time on this than each of the weapons we just talked about.

The final thing that Paul talks about is prayer.

Let’s read this together, verse 18, “Pray at all times in the Spirit with every prayer and request, and stay alert with all perseverance and intercession for all the saints.

We’re to be praying constantly; we’re to be unwavering in our prayer for the saints.

To use the Iron Man example again, at the heart of Iron Man’s armour is the arc reactor.

It’s the power source for the suit.

Our prayers to God is like our arc reactor.

Unlike Iron Man’s arc reactor, we didn’t make or design prayer.

Instead, prayer is something that God himself made.

He made prayer the channel through which we can come to him.

He wants us to pray to him and in the process give us access to the incredible power and strength he has for us.

He wants us to have his power, his strength to fight the battle against the devil.

We’re to always pray, in both good and bad times, in both the busy and relaxing times.

And we are to pray according to the Spirit’s guidance in light of what the Spirit is teaching according to God’s word.

When we pray, we’re not making a wish.

When we pray, we are to pray in the Spirit, according to the Spirit.

Here, Paul points out that there are two groups of people to pray for.

Firstly, we are to pray for other people, that God will protect them and empower them to fight against the devil and his schemes.

Yes, pray for their physical needs, but don’t forget to pray for their spiritual needs, which is more urgent by far.

And of course, pray for yourself, that you will stand

Also second, pray for yourself, that you will stay alert with all perseverance.

Paul’s here not just saying to make sure you stay awake when you pray,

But to be aware of what’s happening around you.

The world is in utter darkness, Jesus is coming back on a day we don’t expect, and so, what are you praying for?

Are your prayers more like wishes to a genie or the obedient surrender of a servant?

Are your prayers more about your well-being, whether it’s about you passing exams, or getting a promotion?

Or are you asking for God’s glory, are you asking for God to strengthen your faith, even during exam time, even during times of trial?

Are our prayers any different from what a non-Christian would pray for?

I would rather you pray for that our church will be faithful through persecution than to pray that we won’t have persecution.

I would rather you pray that we trust in God in our sorrow than to pray that we won’t have any suffering.

Are our prayers any different from others?

Christians are dependent on God and so we pray, constantly.

We also ask for prayers.

Paul himself asked for boldness to declare God’s word, even though he was in chains.

He could’ve prayed for freedom, but instead he asked for boldness to speak the gospel of truth, as he should.

That’s a prayer request to pray in the Spirit.

Who are you praying for? What are you praying for them?

Pray that our prayers will be spiritual not worldly.

Pray for others and for yourself to fight the spiritual battle against the devil.

4. Undying love for our Lord Jesus Christ

What we’re fighting is not merely an ideal.

We’re fighting for a person.

We’re fighting for the One who loves us with an everlasting love.

Say the final two verses with me, “Peace to the brothers and sisters, and love with faith, from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. 24 Grace be with all who have undying love for our Lord Jesus Christ.”

We fight because we love our Lord Jesus Christ with an undying love.

He’s the reason we fight.

Jesus fought and overcame for us.

He wrestled with darkness and triumphed.

He loved us and he fought for us.

We respond and fight for him because we love him.

He loves us with an everlasting love, we respond with an undying love.

It’s not a sentimental, sappy love song sort of undying love.

It’s a love that never dies because it’s based on a love that has died bur rose again.

Jesus died and rose from the grave to give us eternal life.

And so, the one who loves Jesus will love him forever because his love gives us the life to live forever, in order to love him forever.

It’s for this love that we fight.

A famous interviewer, Drew Barrymore, once said to Keanu Reeves that she’s a lover not a fighter.

To which Keanu Reeves, “No, no, no. Because you’re a lover you gotta be a fighter. Because if you don’t fight for your love, what kind of love do you have.”

Brothers and sisters, we are fighters because we’re lovers.

We will wear the full armour of God, we will wrestle, we will stand against the devil and his forces, because of our undying love for Jesus Christ.

We have an undying love for Jesus Christ because he first loved us with an everlasting love.

And so, we get ready.

We suit up; we wear the armour of God.

We put down our old self and put on the new self that God has made us to be.

Friends, For the love of Christ, be strengthened to stand against the devil’s dark forces by putting on the full armour of God, and by praying always in the Spirit.

We love Jesus and so we will fight against the devil for Jesus

We do it by the strength of God; we do it as we wear the full armour or God

We pray in the Spirit at all times, for ourselves, for the saints, out of our love for Christ.

When you wake up in the morning, suit up, look at yourself in the mirror. Have you suit up? Do you have the armour of God on?

Remember that today, even as you take your first step, the devil is trying to make you fall.

He will try to make you doubt God’s word, he will make it hard for you to obey God.

Recognise that struggle is real.

Sometimes you’ll be aware of it; other times, we don’t because we haven’t been staying alert.

It’s a spiritual battle, it comes in obvious and subtle forms.

And so, wear the full armour of God always.

Be ready when the devil strikes.

Pray for one another.

The devil doesn’t want you to pray; he wants you to be practical and do things without praying.

But to live the Christian life without praying is as futile as flying a kite on a windless day instead of asking God to give us the gentle breeze.

We don’t control the wind, but we can ask the one who does.

And so, pray!

Here are the three questions for this week:

Are we wrestling with the devil’s dark forces? Why / why not?

Are you wearing the full armour of God? If so, where are the clinks in your armour?

Who of God’s people have you been you praying for? When?

For the love of Christ, be strengthened to stand against the devil’s dark forces by putting on the full armour of God, and by praying always in the Spirit.

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you that in Jesus Christ, you love us with an everlasting love. Thank you that for giving us the hope of an eternal life in Jesus. We thank you for the hope of the resurrection we have beyond the grave. In the meantime, keep us safe. Don’t make us fall into the devil’s traps. Please forgive us for our foolish arrogance, our numbness to all things spiritual, our desire to run away from any struggles. Please strengthen us as we put on your amour, putting on truth, righteousness, faith, salvation, spreading the good news as we yield the sword of the Spirit, that is your word. And help us to pray for one another, upholding one another in prayer, that together we will persevere until that great and final day. Let us fight, because we love Jesus with an undying love, and in whose name we pray, Amen.”
