How to eat the Bread of Life

Passage: John 6.35_58

Big Idea: Believers are the Father’s gift to the Son, who themselves receive the Son’s gift of his flesh and blood.

Believers are the Father’s gift to the Son

The Son’s gift to the world

Depend on the Son’s death-defeating life

Over the past few weeks, we’ve seen Jesus performed two incredible miracles.

Jesus miraculously fed at least 5000 men, as well many women and children, using only five loaves of bread and two fish.

Jesus miraculously walked across the Sea of Galilee in the middle of a storm.

Thousands of people have seen the incredible things that Jesus could do!

As Jesus was teaching them what the miracle of the feeding of the 5000 meant, He said, “I am the Bread of Life”!

They’ve seen the miracle, now believe what the miracle points to.

It points to Jesus as the Bread of Life!

That was the point of the miracle; it points to Jesus as the Bread of Life.

However, Jesus gave a damning assessment of their faith.

Look with me at verse 36, “But as I told you, you’ve seen me, and yet you do not believe.”

They’ve seen Jesus, they’ve seen the miracles, but they still didn’t believe.

Why wouldn’t they believe, even after seeing Jesus’ miracles for themselves?

It’s a problem that is still prevalent today.

Today, there are many people who have heard about Jesus.

They’ve learnt it in Sunday Schools, they’ve heard it in Churches, they might even have studied it in universities.

They have Christian friends, Christian families, and even Christian spouses.

They’ve heard the teachings of Jesus and might even agree with some of them.

And yet, they don’t believe.

Do you know someone like that? Are you someone like that?

You’ve heard the gospel; you’ve seen the gospel but you still don’t believe the gospel.

Why?

Sadly, there are people in Churches who might have been here for many years, who might have even served in Churches for many years, but who still don’t believe the gospel.

Jesus himself has said that there will be those who call him “Lord, Lord” on the last day but Jesus will say to them, ‘I never knew you. Depart from me, you lawbreakers.”

These are people who thought they knew Jesus, but they didn’t.

They’ve seen the gospel but haven’t believed the gospel.

How do we know whether we genuinely believed the gospel?

How do I know that when Jesus says, “You have seen me and yet do not believe”, he’s not talking to me?

That’s what we’re going to see in today’s passappge.

If you have your Bible with you, please open up to John 6, beginning from verse 35.

Today, we’re going to see three things:

Believers are the Father’s gift to the Son

The Son’s gift to the world

Depend on the Son’s death-defeating life

Before we do, let’s pray,

“Father, thank you for your word. By your Spirit, please help us to see what it means to genuinely believe the Gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

Believers are the Father’s gift to the Son

To understand what it means to genuinely believe, we mustn’t start with ourselves.

Instead, we must start with God the Father.

Look with me at verse 37, “Everyone the Father gives me will come to me, and the one who comes to me I will never cast out.”

First and foremost, believers are gifts from God the Father to God the Son.

Everyone the Father gives…

Our faith in Jesus doesn’t begin with us but from God the Father.

Those whom God the Father gives to Jesus will go to Jesus.

Or to put it another way, we can only go to Jesus, believe in Jesus, IF God the Father has given us to Jesus.

It all starts with God the Father.

Last week, we held a funeral here at St Paul’s.

It was for a former member of the Church.

His family used to come to St Paul’s from the 1950’s to the 1970’s.

He was at one stage a constable at Kogarah Police station.

Sadly, he passed away quite suddenly about three weeks ago.

I had a meeting with the family to talk about the funeral two weeks ago.

Now it just so happened that the meeting took place on a Thursday.

It was the same day when we have the Mobile Community Pantry and so the Church was open to the public on Thursday afternoon.

That afternoon, a gentleman who hadn’t been at Church for at least over ten years came to visit.

He was from the Central Coast, at least over 90 minutes away from us.

He dropped by to see the Church and I spoke to him briefly.

He said he came many years ago to the Church and so I asked whether he knew about the man who died.

When I told him about it, he said he knew the man.

In fact he knew the man very well.

I found out afterwards that this man who came to Church was at one time the Senior Constable who helped to train the man who had just died.

He was greatly saddened by the news and in fact came back two weeks afterwards to the funeral.

What was the chance of that happening? Almost zero!

However, God in his providence brought the man to the Church at exactly the right time.

It was all God’s arrangement and timing.

God is the one who plans all things and ordains all things.

And in his providence he prepares all those who he’s chosen to come to Jesus.

No once comes to Jesus by coincidence or purely as a result of their own free will.

It all starts from God the Father.

Indeed, God even planned for and ordained Jesus’ very own presence on earth.

Look with me at verse 38, “For I have come down from heaven, not to do my own will, but the will of him who sent me.”

Jesus didn’t come to earth to do his own will but to do the will of the Father, who sent him.

Jesus came to do God’s will.

Last week, we read that God’s job for us, his task for us, is to believe in Jesus.

Here, we read that God’s task for Jesus, his will for Jesus, is this, as recorded in verses 39-40:

“This is the will of him who sent me: that I should lose none of those he has given me but should raise them up on the last day. 40 For this is the will of my Father: that everyone who sees the Son and believes in him will have eternal life, and I will raise him up on the last day.”

That’s God’s will for Jesus: to lose none whom God’s gave to Jesus, to ensure that everyone who believes in Jesus have eternal life, and to raise him up on the last day.

God’s job for us and God’s will for Jesus are tightly related.

We’re to believe Jesus and Jesus is to not lose any who believes in him.

That’s the comfort that believers in Jesus have.

It doesn’t depend ultimately on us to be saved.

Rather it ultimately depends on God who gave us to Jesus

and Jesus who by his power and obedience will see to it that

we’re not lost but that we’ll have eternal life and be raised on the last day.

And Jesus, being perfect in power and obedience, will do God’s will perfectly.

Jesus will keep those who believe in him to the end.

Our journey of faith begins with God the Father and will continue with God the Son.

Our eternity is safe in the Father’s gracious providence and the Son’s perfect obedience.

When our kids were young, and before COVID, we took them back to Hong Kong and China several times.

While they would pack their toys and some books, generally speaking they didn’t need to worry too much about the journey.

We would book the flights, the hotels, the homes that we’re staying at, the visas and so on.

They didn’t need to worry because they knew that they were relying on us the parents to take them safely to their destination and back.

That’s the kind of comfort that believers in Jesus have.

We are safe in the Father’s plan and in the Son’s carrying out of the plan.

Look with me at verse 45, “It is written in the Prophets: And they will all be taught by God. Everyone who has listened to and learned from the Father comes to me”

The Father is the One who draws believers to the Son, who teaches the people about Jesus, God the Son, so that they can believe in him.

We can come to Jesus only because the Father has taught us about the Son.

And it’s the Son himself who ultimately reveals to us who the Father is.

Verse 46, “not that anyone has seen the Father except the one who is from God. He has seen the Father.”

We don’t learn from the Father from hearing voices or from our own research.

No, we can only come to learn from the Father through the Son.

And so, only the Father can draw us to the Son and only the Son can show us the Father.

It’s like a closed loop.

Only the Father can show us the Son and only the Son can show us the Father.

How do we get into this loop?

The Son’s gift to the world

We need the Son’s gift to the world.

Jesus comes back the phrase the Bread of Life to describe who He is.

He is the Bread of Life!

The ancestors of the Jews ate manna, a bread from heaven that God’s given them when they were in the wilderness.

As good as manna was, those who ate it eventually died.

But not so the person who eats the bread of heaven.

Those who eat the bread of heaven will not die.

Look with me at verse 51, “I am the living bread that came down from heaven. If anyone eats of this bread he will live forever. The bread that I will give for the life of the world is my flesh.”

When Jesus says that He’s the Bread of Life, the living bread that comes down from heaven,

He’s not saying that he’s made of dough.

He’s not a Gingerbread man that you eat from.

No, he says that the bread that he gives for the life of the world is his flesh.

Eew! His flesh? Did he just say his flesh?

Did we hear it right?

Even the Jews were confused by what Jesus said.

They argued among themselves and said, “How can this man give us his flesh to eat?”

It was a very confronting image, not only for us, but also for the Jews who first heard Jesus.

What does he mean by ‘his flesh’?

Rather than toning it down, Jesus dials it up to 11! (11 out of 10)

Look at verse 53, “So Jesus said to them, “Truly I tell you, unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood, you do not have life in yourselves.”

In case Jesus didn’t make it clear, Jesus said that unless you eat his flesh and drink his blood, you won’t have life in yourselves.

It’s very confronting language, isn’t it?

We almost instinctively try to turn away from it.

It’s like saying to a vegan, “Bring home the bacon”!

In English, to bring home the bacon means to bring the good things or to bring success back home

It’s offensive to a vegan because a vegan is someone who doesn’t eat any meat or food derived from animals.

They don’t like bacon! It’s offensive to tell them to bring home the bacon.

When Jesus says that unless we eat his flesh and drink his blood, we won’t have life in ourselves, we might feel uncomfortable, even offended.

For the Jews, it was especially offensive because in the Old Testament, God himself had explicitly taught his people not to eat blood (Leviticus 7:12, 14).

However, Jesus is saying that those who would have eternal life must eat the flesh and to drink the blood of the Son of Man!

This is the Son of Man that Old Testament points to as the One ordained by God to rule every nation.

Here, Jesus is referring to himself as the Son of Man, that those who wants to have eternal life must eat his flesh and drink his blood.

How? What does it mean to eat the flesh of Jesus and to drink his blood?

To understand how, we need to compare what Jesus says here in verse 54 and in verse 40.

In verse 54, Jesus says, “The one who eats my flesh and drinks my blood has eternal life, and I will raise him up on the last day,”

In verse 40, Jesus says, “For this is the will of my Father: that everyone who sees the Son and believes in him will have eternal life, and I will raise him up on the last day.”

There’s a very strong parallel between the two verses.

Both verses talk about how someone has eternal life and the fact that Jesus will raise him up on the last day.

How do they get there?

They see the Son and believe or they eat the flesh of Jesus and to drink his blood.

To believe in the Son and to eat the flesh and to drink the blood of Jesus refer to the same thing.

To believe in the Son is to eat his flesh and to drink his blood.

This is not literal, of course.

Jesus’ flesh is not magical and eating the physical flesh and blood of Jesus doesn’t give you special magical powers.

Unlike many fictional stories, drinking the physical blood of Jesus or touching items which had contact with the blood of Jesus won’t give you eternal life.

So this doesn’t refer to eating the physical flesh and drinking the physical blood of Jesus.

Nor does this refer to the Lord’s Supper either.

The Lord’s supper is not magical either.

It doesn’t become the flesh and blood of Jesus at the Eucharist or the Holy Communion.

You don’t get eternal life by taking it.

We mustn’t treat the Lord’s Supper as magical.

What does it mean then?

To eat the flesh of Jesus and to drink his blood means that we accept the death and resurrection of Jesus Christ by faith.

Jesus Christ took on flesh to become one of us.

He became a mortal human being in order to die for the life of the world.

He, the immortal one, took on weak flesh to die.

In the Bible, blood is often associated with violent death or death of an animal slaughtered for sacrifice.

When Jesus speaks of his flesh and his blood, he’s referring to his sacrificial death on the cross.

He died a violent death on the cross.

He offered us his flesh for the life of the world.

All of us have been born a sinner, disobeying God in thought, word and deed, deserving God’s judgement of sin against us.

For our sins God’s sentenced us to death.

In order to save us from the sentence of death, Jesus died for us.

That’s the cost for our salvation.

That’s the cost of our eternal life.

The cost of our eternal life with God is the flesh and blood of the Jesus.

Those who believe that Jesus had died for their sins are those who have received the benefits of Jesus’ death.

It’s in the sense they have believed and received the benefits of Jesus’ death that they are said to eat the flesh and they have drunk the blood of Jesus.

They believed what Jesus has done for them and they received the benefits of his death for them.

This isn’t a superficial affirmation.

It’s not just understanding it on the surface.

It’s a belief that goes to the very being of who you are.

When you eat food, it become part of who you are.

The food becomes your protein.

The energy stored in the carbohydrates is transformed in your body to become fuel for you to live by.

When you believe in Jesus, he goes even deeper than any food can go.

Jesus doesn’t just become part of you; he becomes your very identity.

He powers your very being; he is the reason you live.

He’s the reason why you get out of bed in the morning.

Belief in Jesus goes to the very heart, the very soul, the very being of who you are.

That’s why the Apostle can say in Galatians 2:20, “I have been crucified with Christ, and I no longer live, but Christ lives in me. The life I now live in the body, I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me.”

Paul’s life is all about Jesus.

It’s Jesus living in Paul.

It’s all about Jesus.

“The one who eats my flesh and drinks my blood has eternal life, and I will raise him up on the last day”

It’s confronting but it’s also comforting.

To believe in Jesus is to eat his flesh and to drink his blood.

It’s to believe that he’s the Son of man who took the sentence of death on behalf of those who believe in him, that they might be saved.

What does believing in Jesus look like?

How does someone who eats the flesh of Jesus and drink his blood live?

They live as someone who depend on the Son’s death-defeating life.

Depend on the Son’s death-defeating life

Look with me at verses 55-56, “because my flesh is true food and my blood is true drink. 56 The one who eats my flesh and drinks my blood remains in me, and I in him. 57 Just as the living Father sent me and I live because of the Father, so the one who feeds on me will live because of me.”

Those who believe in Jesus believe in what his death has achieved as well as the hope that his resurrection brings.

The death of Jesus didn’t stop him; death in general could not stop Jesus because His life is a death-defeating life.

He has defeated death when he rose from the dead.

Those who trust in him will have eternal life because Jesus is life.

When you eat normal food, it’ll last you until your body uses it up.

You’ll need to get other food in order to continue living.

You will die if you stop eating.

However, Jesus’ flesh is real food and Jesus’ blood is real drink.

You don’t need to go to another place for spiritual nutrition.

You always come back to Jesus; you depend on him to live from day to day.

Jesus is what all of our food ultimately point to because only Jesus can give real, lasting sustenance.

Only Jesus can give us eternal life, sustaining us forever.

We can be sure that we’ll have eternal life in Jesus because just as Jesus live because the Father lives, we, who feed on Jesus, will live, because of Jesus lives.

As we depend on Jesus, as we trust in Jesus, we are drawn into the relationship between God the Father and God the Son.

The relationship is a closed circle, but we are drawn into it by the Father’s will and by feeding on Jesus, by believing in his death and resurrection.

That’s how we come into having an eternal life with God the Father and God the Son.

We come, depending on the death-defeating life of Jesus.

This is Jesus’ gift to the world: his flesh, his blood, his life-giving death, his death-defeating life.

It’s on Jesus that we depend on, every day, every hour.

One of my favourite hymns is “I need Thee every hour” written by Annie Hawks in 1872.

Annie Hawks was working at home, doing her regular household tasks.

Here’s what happened in her own words, “Suddenly, I became so filled with the sense of nearness to the Master that, wondering how one could live without Him, either in joy or pain, these words were ushered into my mind, the thought at once taking full possession of me -- 'I Need Thee Every Hour. . . .”

Here is a verse from the hymn:

I need thee every hour, most gracious Lord; no tender voice like thine can peace afford. I need thee, O I need thee; every hour I need thee; O bless me now, my Saviour, I come to thee.

Christians need Jesus every hour.

We depend on him always.

That’s what genuine belief in him looks like.

It’s a constant look on Jesus; it’s a constant dependence on Jesus.

He’s our only hope in this life and the next.

He’s the only one who can raise us up on the last day.

It’s all about Jesus.

And so, how do you know that you not only see the gospel but that you are a genuine believer of Jesus?

You trust that it’s God the Father himself who’s given you to God the Son and that you have received the Son’s gift of his flesh and blood.

Only God can draw us to Jesus; we can’t do it on our own.

By faith, we accept what he’s done for us on the cross.

We believe that the cost of giving us eternal life is his flesh and blood.

We believe that we depend on him day by day, hour by hour to continue living.

We live with Jesus being both the purpose and the goal of our lives.

We live because He lives.

What does this mean for us?

Don’t just look at Jesus! Eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood.

The Church is like a lifeboat with Jesus as the captain.

He will take us back to his home in his lifeboat.

Don’t swim next to the lifeboat; you’ll drown before you get there!

Come onto the lifeboat!

See Jesus and believe.

What are you waiting for? Believe and receive him now!

If you haven’t fully believed in Jesus, if you have only been seeing from the outside, don’t delay.

Believe and accept him today.

By faith, eat his flesh and drink his blood.

Let him be your purpose and your goal in life.

By faith, live because He lives.

Don’t just look at Jesus! Eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Do you only see Jesus like the crowd, or do you see and believe Jesus?

Have you by faith eaten the flesh of Jesus and drunk his blood? Is he the reason for your very being?

How is he asking you to depend on him at this very hour?

Friends, don’t just look at Jesus! Eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood.

For unless we have Jesus, we have no life, we have no chance of rising on the last day.

Let him be the reason for your very being.

“Father, we thank you for bringing us to Jesus this morning. We know that it’s no coincidence that we’re here. We’re here according to your providence and good pleasure. Please draw our hearts to Jesus. Please draw those who haven’t eaten the flesh of Jesus or to drink the blood of Jesus to do so by faith. Please help them to genuinely, unreservedly, believe in Jesus. For unless we have Jesus, we have no life, we have no chance of rising on the last day. Father, have mercy on us, for we pray in the name of Jesus, Amen.”
