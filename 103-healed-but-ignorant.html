Healed but faithless

John 5:1-15

Big Idea: Even after witnessing Jesus’ love and power a faithless fool will keep on sinning

The love and power of Jesus

The healed yet ignorant witness

Worse than being disabled for 38 years

Have you ever had a life-changing moment in your life, a moment that redefines who you are?

One such moment happened to a young Bill Gates back in 1975.

Bill Gates was a student at Harvard University when his friend Paul Allen showed him the cover of the Popular Electronics magazine.

This made Bill Gates realise that computers are the future.

And so, Bill Gates decided to quit College and to start Microsoft.

Today, more than 7 out of every ten computers around the world use Microsoft.

I’m sure that many people have seen the exact same magazine cover as Bill Gates, but it wasn’t a life-changing moment for them.

However, for Bill Gates, that was his “A-ha” moment, his life-changing moment.

Most of us don’t have life-changing moments like Bill Gates.

For all of us who are here this morning, we come to Church on an ordinary Sunday, to an ordinary Church.

Like most people who have also seen the magazine cover, we don’t see anything extraordinary or life-changing at Church.

Yes, there’s teachings about Jesus and the Bible, maybe some singing and praying,

But nothing extraordinary, nothing life changing.

Should we really expect life-changing moments when we come to Church on Sunday mornings?

Does my life need to change after I come to a Sunday service?

Just like the people who saw the same magazine cover and let their opportunities slip by,

Many who come to Church and hear about Jesus also let their opportunities slip by.

In today’s passage, we meet a man who does exactly that.

He experienced something extraordinary and yet completely misses its significance.

If you have your Bible there, please open up to John 5, beginning from verse 1.

We’ll see three things:

The love and power of Jesus

The healed yet ignorant witness

Worse than being disabled for 38 years

Before we look into God’s word, let’s pray,

“Father, by your Spirit, help us see Jesus more clearly as we hear your word and meditate them upon our hearts. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

The love and power of Jesus

Our passage begins with Jesus going to Jerusalem during the time of a Jewish festival.

There was pool called Bethesda.

A few years ago, Jenny and I went to Bethlehem and saw the remains of the pool.

Here’s our picture of it.

It’s located near the Church of St Anne’s in the north-east quarter of the Old Jerusalem City.

Look with me at verse 3, “Within these lay a large number of the disabled—blind, lame, and paralyzed.﻿”

The reason why there were many sick people there was that a lot of people believed that when the water in the pool was stirred,

The first one to get in the pool would be healed.

One of the many people at the pool was a man who had been disabled for thirty-eight years.

We don’t know exactly what the disability was, but we do know that he couldn’t move and had to lie on a mat.

Out of the many people that Jesus could’ve gone to, he approached this man and asked whether he wanted to get better.

We don’t know why Jesus picked this man in particular.

There were many more at the pool but Jesus was concerned with this man in particular.

Jesus went out of his way to show compassion to this man.

Why? Was it because he believed in Jesus?

No, he didn’t even know who Jesus was.

In fact, he had more faith in the healing powers of the water than Jesus.

The Bible records nothing special about this man.

His response to Jesus sounded more like grumbling than a response of thanks to Jesus’ concern.

He said, “Sir, I have no one to put me into the pool when the water is stirred up, but while I’m coming, someone goes down ahead of me.”

He was complaining that no one helps him and that the other disabled people were too quick!

There was nothing particularly worthy about this man but Jesus chose him, out of his compassion for the man.

Jesus who took the initiative to speak with him.

It was out of his compassion, out of his love, that Jesus approached this disabled man.

Jesus said to him, “Get up, pick up your mat and walk.”

And just like that, Jesus healed this man by his powerful word.

Jesus spoke and the man was healed.

Prior to this, the muscles in the man’s leg wouldn’t obey the man’s command.

But these muscles obeyed Jesus and became strong as soon as Jesus uttered the words.

The healing was instant; the man’s recovery was total.

This man had just witnessed the love and power of Jesus.

He had witnessed Jesus’ love from the compassion he’s shown to the man.

There were many people there, but Jesus chose this specifically, even though he didn’t deserve it.

He had witnessed Jesus’ power when he was healed by the powerful word of Jesus.

After living with disability for thirty-eight years, the was finally healed!

It has to be one of the most significant moments in his life, almost certainly the most significant one in the past thirty-eight years.

Being healed after thirty-eight years? That’s what I call a life-changing experience.

What an incredible experience it would’ve been to witness the love and power of Jesus first-hand!

In a way, that’s what we do here at Church, every Sunday.

While we might not witness the same kind of healing as the man in the passage, people who believe in Jesus are also witnesses of his love and power.

Believers in Jesus have seen the love and power of Jesus in God’s word and in their own lives.

Indeed, the whole life of a Christian is one of living witness to the power and love of Jesus.

That’s why our motto has the words, See the Gospel, Hear the Gospel.

When people come to Church, they are witnesses of the Gospel of Jesus Christ.

They see the Lord Jesus reigning and ruling lovingly over his people, like a Shepherd looking after his sheep.

Last week, we’ve seen and heard Michael’s sharing at his baptism.

When asked what changed since becoming a Christian, Michael responded, "I learnt to be more humble. I realise even if I don't know everything, what the Bible teaches is enough"

What a wonderful testimony of a life changed by the good news of Jesus!

That’s what we should be expecting to see when we come to Church.

We come expecting to see the power and love of Jesus in the lives of the Church members and in God’s word.

The healed yet ignorant witness

However, though the man was healed, he was yet still an ignorant witness.

How do we know?

The passage continues by telling us that the man was healed on the Sabbath.

One of the Ten commandments that God gave to his people as that they were not to work on the Sabbath.

Today, if you go to Jerusalem, where many people are Jews, you’ll find that many people still obey the Sabbath rule and don’t work on the Sabbath.

On Friday afternoon, they would close shops early to prepare for Friday evening, the beginning of their Sabbath.

Public Transport doesn’t operate on the Sabbath; Jewish people don’t work on the Sabbath.

They were very serious about keeping the Sabbath Holy for God.

While God did command the Jews not to work on the Sabbath, the Jews have added a lot more prohibitions on God’s command.

They developed 39 classes of work to ensure that absolutely no work was done on the day.

In our passage, the man who was healed picked up his mat and walked.

According to the Jews, picking up the mat was work.

Though the man hadn’t picked up his mat and walked for at least 38 years, it was considered work.

What he did, picking up the mat, was controversial because it was done on the Sabbath Day.

After being accused of breaking the Sabbath, the man blamed it on the person who told him to pick up his mat and walk.

When interrogated further about the identity of his healer, the man didn’t know who it was because Jesus has slipped away into the crowd.

The man who was disable for 38 years was healed but all they could focus on was that the man carried the mat that he was lying on,

and so broke one of the 39 rules they’ve drawn up about the Sabbath.

Haven’t they got their priorities messed up?

Haven’t they missed the big picture?

Jesus healed the man by his powerful word.

They should’ve been focussing on the walking part, which was far more significant, that the mat-carrying part.

They had missed out on the most important part.

They have witnessed the love and power of Jesus in this man’s healing.

This man had experienced first-hand the power and love of Jesus.

And yet, they had completely missed out on its significance and focussed on religious, man-made laws instead.

We can be like that too, can’t we?

We’re here to worship Jesus; we’re here to witness his love and power.

But there will be those who would rather focus on the less significant aspects of the Church service rather than the love and power of Jesus.

Like the Jews in today’s story, they focus on the peripheral and miss out on the central reason for us to come together.

They focus on the insignificant and completely miss out on seeing the love and power of Jesus.

Jesus himself had elsewhere said that some of the religious leaders are blind guides, they strain out a gnat and gulp down a camel!

Gnats are tiny insects that are attracted to fruits, water and the human body.

Jesus says that the religious leaders of his days were careful to observe the law by obeying the tiniest details, making sure they don’t disobey them by mistake.

It makes them look like they’re serious about obeying God.

However, they miss out on what God’s law ultimately points to, namely Jesus as the fulfilment of the law.

And so, Jesus says that all their straining to get rid of gnats were useless because they missed out on seeing the significance of Jesus.

They might as well have gulp down a camel!

In the same way, some of us might be good, even experts, in picking out the less significant parts of church service.

That’s not necessarily wrong to want to improve on church service.

However, if the focus is only on these and not on Jesus, then we’re messed up, just like the Jews in Jesus’ days.

If we are legalistic and focus only on the insignificant aspects of Church service, and in the process miss out on the love and power of Jesus,

Then we are no better than the Jews and the man who was healed in today’s passage.

What’s fascinating was that Jesus probably knew that the man would be questioned by the Jews.

Jesus didn’t have to tell the man to pick up the mat; he could’ve just told the man to leave the mat there to avoid confrontation with the Jews.

By the same token, Jesus didn’t have to heal the man on the Sabbath at all; he could’ve just leave it until the next day to avoid upsetting the Jews.

But he told the man to pick up his mat, to walk, on the Sabbath.

He wanted the Jews to show the Jews how they’ve been blind to the power and love that God’s shown in Jesus.

That’s what we’ll be looking at over the next few weeks as we look at the rest of John chapter 5.

But for now, our attention is back on this man who was healed.

Worse than being disabled for 38 years

Look with me at verse 14, “After this, Jesus found him in the temple and said to him, “See, you are well. Do not sin anymore, so that something worse doesn’t happen to you.”

Jesus healed this man for a purpose.

Jesus healed him so that this man won’t sin anymore.

Jesus is not telling the man to be sinless, for that would be impossible.

It looks like Jesus is telling the man to not sin a specific sin.

We don’t know what specific sin Jesus was referring to, but Jesus knew what it was, and the man knew what it was.

Now it’s important to know that not all sin leads to sickness or disability.

However, here Jesus seems to suggest that what happened to the man

is due to a specific sin of some sort and that if he continues to sin,

something worse would happen,

just as something bad has already happened to this man because of this man’s sin.

Let’s say, this man, hypothetically speaking, was a thief.

While he was escaping with what he’s stolen, he fell down the cliff and as a result, was unable to walk again, hypothetically speaking.

If that’s the case, his disability would be a direct result of his sin.

I’m not saying that that’s what happened to the man.

However, whatever sin it was, Jesus told him to stop sinning, lest something worse might happen to him.

What’s worse that being unable to move for thirty-eight years?

It’s the judgement at the end of time, reserved for all those who refuse to obey God in Jesus.

Will this man sin that unknown, specific sin again?

Again, we’re not told, but the chance of him obeying Jesus seems unlikely.

We’re told that this man reported to the Jews that it was Jesus who had made him well.

This man would’ve known that revealing to the Jews that it was Jesus healed him on the Sabbath Day, who told him to “work”, would land Jesus in trouble.

Even so, he went out of his way to report to the Jews that it was Jesus.

How ironic!

Jesus went out of his way to show his love and power to this man.

But this man went out of his way to land Jesus in trouble.

This man was ungrateful!

Even worse, this man was faithless!

He wasn’t healed because he had faith in Jesus; even after he was healed, he didn’t have faith in Jesus.

Being made well by the love and power of Jesus did not lead this man to believe in Jesus.

Experiencing a miracle of Jesus did not lead this man to salvation!

He was a faithless fool who most likely will suffer a fate worse than being disabled for thirty-eight years.

His response to Jesus showed that he had no faith in Jesus.

Again, this can happen to us as well.

Just because we’ve experienced the low and power of Jesus at Church doesn’t automatically make us Christians.

Witnessing the love and power of Jesus ought to strengthen our trust in Jesus,

But merely witnessing the love and power of Jesus doesn’t automatically mean that you’re now a believer in Jesus.

By merely participating in Church life doesn’t automatically mean that you’re saved.

You can be so near to God and yet still remain so far off.

You can come to Church every Sunday but still remain a non-believer.

If you are to be saved, if you want to have eternal life, you must believe Jesus.

You must repent and you must stop sinning.

Stop sinning and turn back to God.

To keep living a life of sin, after experiencing the love and power of Jesus, is to be like this man,

A thankless, faithless fool.

Stop sinning and turn back to God.

To keep sinning after knowing who Jesus is, is worse than being a disabled person for 38 years.

In fact, it’s better, far better, to be a disabled person and know and trust in Jesus than to be an able-bodied person who keeps on sinning.

One of the wonderful ministries that we have here at Church is our SRE or Bible ministry to St George School for children with special needs.

Jenny would go to teach every Friday during school term to teach children who are disabled.

These children would come to Jenny’s lesson in wheelchairs because they have difficulties moving.

But when Jenny shares the Bible with them, when Jenny sings Christian songs to them, they light up.

They smile and they respond with great joy.

What a wonderful blessing and privilege it is to share the gospel with these children.

Now, while we pray that they will be healed, the likelihood of them being healed will be quite small.

But with them we wait for the second coming of Jesus.

When Jesus comes back, they will have a new body, new legs to jump higher than any athletes can today.

On that day, they’ll jump to the glory of Jesus.

They’ll have new and transformed minds, able to praise God in ways that they can’t imagine.

And we’ll be praising God with them.

That’s the hope that we share; it’s the hope that we have in Jesus.

Until that day, we keep trusting Jesus, we keep our eyes on Jesus.

We keep marveling at the love and power of Jesus and we keep witnessing the power and love of Jesus.

We’ll also stop sinning and repent.

It might be a sinful attitude or a sinful action or sinful speech.

We’ll stop sinning and turn back to him.

That’s why you’re here today, whether you’re here live in person or watching online.

God brought you here to witness the power of and love of Jesus.

You have see the power and love of Jesus.

Don’t be the faithless fool who keeps sinning even after he witnesses the power of and love of God.

Stop sinning and turn back to God.

What does this mean for us?

Observe Jesus and change your life.

There’s a video on the internet now of this little boy who had some serious (very good) dance moves.

Someone recognised the moves from the movie Spiderman 3 where Spiderman was doing exactly the same moves.

The little boy watched Spiderman very carefully and copied his dance moves!

Be like the little boy but rather than watching spiderman, observe Jesus.

Follow Jesus’ moves.

Watched how Jesus loves others and ask God how you can love like Jesus.

Watched how Jesus trusts in God and ask God how you can trust him like Jesus.

Every time you come to Church, observe how other people follow Jesus.

Observe the Christian who trusts in God as she makes a difficult decision in her life for Christ’s glory.

Observe the Christian child who listens God’s word attentively and trusts Jesus joyfully.

Observe and follow Jesus’ moves.

Should we really expect life-changing moments when we come to Church on Sunday mornings?

Does my life need to change after I come to a Sunday service?

Absolutely! Come expecting to witness the power and love of Jesus.

Come, expecting your life to be changed.

Observe Jesus and change your life.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Have I come expecting to see Jesus’ power and love this morning?

Have I been a witness of Jesus’ power and love this morning?

What sin is Jesus telling me to repent from this morning?

Witness the power and love of Jesus.

Observe Jesus and change your life.

By your Spirit, please turn us to Jesus so that we might be saved. By your Spirit, please help us to stop sinning. Please help us to be witnesses of Jesus so that others will come to know and believe him. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
