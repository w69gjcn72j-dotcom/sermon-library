Vowing before the House of God

Nehemiah 9:38-10:39

Big Idea: Genuine confession leads to serious and thorough dedication to the House of God, namely Jesus.

Not another “Imagine” rendition

Damned if the House of God is neglected

Jesus, the true House of God

Not another “Imagine” Rendition

Can you believe it? It’s been now two years since the World Health Organisation declared COVID-19 a pandemic.

And what a crazy two years it’s been!

Many people around the world had contracted COVID since then.

This week, my own family contracted COVID as well and so we’re now in isolation.

That’s why I’m speaking to you via video today.

It’s strange living in isolation.

You must cancel appointments and lessons.

You must think about getting supplies for the house and all the while, trying to get better!

That’s why I’m so thankful for the brothers and sisters who have brought us food, so much food, over the past few days.

Thank you to all those leaders who have stepped up to make this Church service possible while we’re away.

Thank you for your love for us shown in action!

During the COVID, many people, including many celebrities, wanted to show their love and support for others in isolation.

One famous person who tried to make people feel better was Gal Gadot, the actress who plays the role of Wonder Woman.

Gal Gadot and her friends recorded different sections to the song, “Imagine” almost two years ago, at the start of the Pandemic.

There were many stars who helped; stars who were comedians, stars who were actors, stars who were singers, all chipped in to help.

But rather than feeling better, people felt worse after hearing it.

How can you feel bad after hearing so many stars sing, “Imagine”.

Well, firstly, they didn’t all sing in the same key, and so it was physically hard to listen to.

Second, the lyrics didn’t give people any real sense of hope.

“Imagine no possessions, I wonder if you can”

People were literally losing their possessions in the Pandemic; they had no job and no way to earn a living.

Thirdly, the song was sung for the benefit of the stars who sang rather than the general public.

Ricky Gervais describes it like this, “You can see in their eye — ‘I could cry at the beauty of my personality, I’m just so beautiful for doing this’ and everyone sees that — we get it.”

They didn’t do much but they want to be at least seen as doing something.

Even Gal Gadot herself admitted in 2022, “It wasn’t the right timing, and it wasn’t the right thing. It was in poor taste.”

It might had been a nice thought and could’ve sounded nice, but it wasn’t substantial.

Another group of people who often sing songs are Christians like us.

Christians have been singing since the time of Jesus.

We sing whenever we can.

One of the things Christians miss the most during pandemic is singing and when we could sing together again, we did it immediately.

We would sing to Christ our King.

We sing about God’s love for us and we come before him in worship.

And rightly so! We should keep singing praises about God!

But does our worship stop there?

Do we mean what we sing when we sing about that God is our King and that we’ve come to bow in worship before Him?

Or are we like Gal Gadot and her friends, singing wonderful songs, which may sound nice, but not substantial?

Are my confessions about Jesus nice but not genuine?

Friends, we don’t want to be like another rendition of ‘imagine’ when we sing in our Churches

We want to have genuine and substantial confession of Jesus.

That’s what we’ll see in today’s passage.

In today’s passage, we’ll see two things:

Damned if the House of God is neglected

Jesus, the True House of God

Open your Bible with me to Nehemiah , beginning from 9:38-10:1

“In view of all this, we are making a binding agreement in writing on a sealed document containing the names of our leaders, Levites, and priests.

Those whose seals were on the document were the governor Nehemiah son of Hacaliah, and Zedekiah”

Over the past few weeks, we’ve seen the Israelites in Nehemiah’s time gathered to hear from God’s word.

Last week we saw them gathered to confess their sins before God.

Now, in view of the confession of their sins, the people and their leaders signed a binding agreement.

They’ve put their names to the document, signed their names on the contract, as it were.

But they went one step further.

Look with me at verses 28-29, “The rest of the people—the priests, Levites, gatekeepers, singers, and temple servants, along with their wives, sons, and daughters, everyone who is able to understand and who has separated themselves from the surrounding peoples to obey the law of God— 29 join with their noble brothers and commit themselves with a sworn oath to follow the law of God given through God’s servant Moses and to obey carefully all the commands, ordinances, and statutes of the Lord our Lord.”

The people

Commit themselves with a sworn oath to follow the law of God.

Literally, they entered into a curse and in an oath to obey the law of God.

They themselves has said that if they don’t obey God’s law, they would be cursed!

It’s a little like this.

I only found out about this: Domino has a special delivery guarantee.

If you pay a miserly $3.00 more, Domino promises to deliver the pizza to you in 20 minutes, or it’s free.

It’s a promise they make to their customers and they’re ready to pay the consequence if they don’t keep the promise.

In Nehemiah the people are making a promise before God.

If they don’t obey God’s commands, then they are ready to be cursed.

In the Old Testament, both saints and sinners would make a vow along the following line: “May God deal with me, be it ever so severely, if I don’t do this or that”

In today’s language, it’ll be something like saying, “I’ll be damned (that is, condemned) if I don’t do this or that”

It’s very strong language for a very serious vow.

It’s a very serious commitment to keep a vow before God.

What have they vowed before God?

They separate it into two groups.

First group has four vows.

Vow 1: They will not give their daughters in marriage to surrounding people nor take their daughters for their sons.

Vow 2: They will not buy from the surrounding people on the Sabbath Day.

Vow 3: They will leave the land uncultivated in the seventh year.

Vow 4: They will cancel every debt in the seventh year.

That is, they vow to be holy in their identity as a people of God.

They vow to be holy in their use of time by dedicating the Sabbath as a holy day to the Lord.

They vow to be holy in their use of the land, recognising that their land is not theirs, but ultimately the Lord’s.

Finally, they vow to be holy in their possessions, not keeping someone indebted to them forever but to cancel their debt in due time.

Then from verses 32 to 39, they imposed a set of commands on themselves, all of which focusses on how they contribute to the service of the House of God.

Firstly, they were to pay a fee for the upkeep of the house of God.

The fee would cover the daily offertories, the special offerings on special days, sin offerings and for the work of the house of God

Second, they would cast lots for the priests, the Levites and the people to donate wood to the house of God so that they can be used as fuel for the offering.

Thirdly, they were to give the firstfruits of the land, the firstfruits of every tree, the firstborn of their sons, the firstborn of livestock, the firstborn of herds, the firstborn of flocks to the priests who serve at God’s house.

Though the firstborn of their sons belong to God, the Law asks the parents to pay a price to ‘redeem’ their firstborn son.

Also, they were to give bread, fruits, new wine and fresh oil to the priests at the house of God and one tenth of the land’s produce to the Levites, who also work at the house of God.

The focus of the commands they vowed to keep was on making sure the house of God was kept in order and that the priests and Levites who worked there were well looked after.

They even promised to organise the priests and Levites to collect what they had vowed to give, to make sure that they actually follow through on what they’ve promised.

It’s a very thorough promise, with a very sharp focus on the house of God.

Why did they have such a sharp focus on the upkeep of the temple?

Why didn’t they talk about other things that God commanded?

Why not talk about being generous to the poor, or not being greedy, or setting up a justice system?

Why so much focus on the House of God?

It’s because the House of God is where God meets with his people.

God has designated one place, the Temple, to be the meeting place between God and people.

It’s in the House of God that God teaches his people His ways.

It’s in the House of God where his people come to confess their sins and where God forgives.

The House of God, the Temple, is not just one building among many in the city of Jerusalem.

The Temple is the centre of the city and their lives flow from it.

The Temple is the heart of the city of God.

The Temple is the heart of their lives.

And so, they vowed to not neglect the House of God.

They call on God to curse them if they ever, ever neglect the House of God.

I have a friend called Charles who went to Bible college with me.

Charles speaks Indonesian and was invited to speak at a Christian conference in Indonesia.

However, he got very sick there and had to go to the hospital.

But rather than leaving the conference with no speaker, Charles kept his promise and he went to speak at the conference.

He was still very sick and had to use an intravenous drip.

But he taught the Bible to the conference, with a drip by his side.

Charles was very sick but it didn’t stop him.

He made a promise and he did his utmost to keep it!

How serious are we in keeping our promises to God?

What would stop us from obeying God’s command?

The Israelites were very serious about keeping God’s commandments and even made a vow and a curse to show how determined they were.

That’s very noble, but if you know the history of God’s people,

Both the history before Nehemiah’s time and the history after Nehemiah’s time,

You won’t have a lot of confidence in their vow.

They’ve failed before and they’ll fail again.

They will be cursed because they will neglect the house of God.

It’s not until we come to Jesus that there is any hope for God’s people.

For Jesus is the true House of God

Jesus, the true House of God

God’s people no longer have to worship at a physical temple like the God’s people did in Nehemiah’s days.

The temple in Jerusalem served as a meeting place between God and men and it did serve its purpose in its time.

But its greater service is to point to Jesus as the Ultimate Meeting Place between God and Man.

Jesus is the true and ultimate House of God.

It’s in Jesus now that we meet with God.

And so, rather than dedicating ourselves to the service of the temple, as God’s people did in the time of Nehemiah,

We now dedicate ourselves to Jesus.

Jesus is the One we turn to in order to worship God.

Jesus is the centre of our worship of God.

Jesus is the centre of our lives.

Just as the lives of the God’s people in Nehemiah’s time flowed from the temple.

Our lives as God’s people now flow from Jesus.

Just as God taught and forgave people in the temple, God now teaches and forgives people in Jesus.

Our worship of God starts with Jesus, our worship of God focusses on Jesus and our worship of God is completed in Jesus.

Just as the Temple used to be the heart of God’s city, Jesus is the Heart of God’s Kingdom.

In Matthew 18:20 Jesus says, “For where two or three are gathered together in my name, I am there among them.”

Where two or three are gathered in Jesus’ name, Jesus is there, the house of God is there.

When we gather as Church, we become the house of God as we gather in Jesus’ name.

When we read God’s word, people meet with Jesus as they hear, receive, and believe God’s word.

And when we physically gather together, we become the physical place, the physical house, that people of the world come to meet with Jesus.

Together as the Church of God, we are the House of God through whom people can come to meet Jesus.

And so, members of our Church dedicate their time and resources to keep the Church going, not because it’s a nice historical building, even though it is.

We dedicate our time and resources to keep the going not because it benefits the community, even though it does.

No, we dedicate our time and resources to keep the Church going because we are dedicating ourselves to Jesus.

We want to see members of our Church gather to worship God the Father in Jesus God the Son.

We want to see members of our community come to meet with Jesus.

And so, we, too, like the Jews in Nehemiah’s time, will dedicate ourselves to the worship of God.

We, too, will not neglect the House of God.

For we have come to dedicate ourselves to Jesus, the true House of God.

Last week, the Multicultural Bible Ministry, or MBM, the Anglican Church at Rooty Hill, farewelled their Pastor Ray Galea and his wife Sandy.

Ray and Sandy started their Church in a small lounge room in Blacktown back in 1991 which grew to be a large purpose-built church in Rooty Hill.

They have dedicated 31 years of their lives to this ministry and has blessed many, many people around them and many more far beyond.

Our Church has used material from Sandy for our children ministry.

Many people at their stage in life will take it easy and ease their way toward retirement.

However, rather than slowing down, the Ray and Sandy will leave the church they began, with people they love, and head to the United Arab Emirates – where Ray will become lead pastor of independent evangelical church Fellowship Dubai.

They’re about to move to a new country and start a new role in a new church!

They have dedicated much of their lives to serving in the House of God in Rooty Hill and now they will continue a new chapter serving the House of God in Dubai.

That’s because they are committed to the Ultimate House of God, they are dedicated to Jesus and will not neglect Him, wherever he might send them.

If you have confessed that Jesus is the King of Kings and the Lord of lords, and He is, then commit your life to him.

By your actions, show that your confession about Jesus is genuine.

By your dedication, show that you mean what you sing.

By your life, show that you’ve been saved by Jesus and that you’ve been born again.

By all means, Tell the Gospel and by your life, show the Gospel, so that people can see, hear and by God’s grace believe the Gospel through you

And so, don’t be another Christian rendition of ‘Imagine’; don’t just sing the words of Christian songs or confess Christian things because it’s a nice thing to do.

Show that your confession and your singing is genuine by seriously and thoroughly dedicating your life to the House of God.

Seriously and thoroughly dedicate your life to Jesus Christ.

What does this mean for us?

We’ll need to ask ourselves a question: What is your vow to your Saviour?

What is my vow to my Saviour?

When doctors begin their professional lives as doctors, they would declare the Hippocratic oath.

In it he promises to do no harm or injustice to his patients.

Neither will he administer a poison to anybody when asked to do so.

It’s in light of that oath that doctors treat patients for their good.

As Christians, there are many instances where we make pledges before God.

In baptism we pledge to have a clean conscience before God.

In marriage, we pledge to take our spouse to have and to hold, for better or for worse in the presence of God.

Even in our training of our children leaders, the leaders are required to make a Safe Ministry pledge, where they pledge to never abuse children, youth, or other vulnerable people.

What is your vow to your Saviour?

What is your pledge?

Friends, here are the three questions for this week:

Is my confession of God genuine, or is it merely lip service?

How am I dedicating my life to Jesus?

What will I vow before my Saviour?

In the words of the hymn I vow to you my Saviour…

I vow to you, my Saviour, all earthly things above, entire and whole and perfect, the service of my love; the love that asks no questions, the love that stands the test, that lays upon his altar the dearest and the best; the love that never falters, the love that knows the price, the love that stands indebted before your sacrifice.

What is your vow to your Saviour?
