To wrong or to be wronged?

Passage: 1 Corinthians 6:1-11

Big Idea: Don’t fight for your rights with a brother in an unrighteous court; rather be wronged, knowing that you’ve been made right by God.

Unrighteous to fight family in an unrighteous court

Righteous to be wronged

In Jesus and by the Spirit, God’s rights those who were unrighteous

Intro: Who likes Yum cha?

Recently, I’ve seen a video of two families who had to share a table because they were running out of room.

I don’t know about you, but it can be a bit awkward to share a table with strangers at Yum cha.

For this two families, things got so bad that they started to throw cups and punches at each other.

They were literally fighting for space at Yum Cha!

No matter what culture you’re from, we’re taught to fight for what’s ours.

We don’t need to be taught to fight for what’s ours!

Just look at what happens when you try to take a toy away from a young baby.

What happens? He or she starts to cry and even throw a tantrum because you’re taking away what’s theirs.

And it’s not just adults! I’ve seen some adults get upset and throw tantrums when someone take their things away. Have you?

Of course, it’s not other people.

We get upset when we feel that something unfair has been done to us.

And we’ll fight to get back what’s rightfully ours.

It’s a natural reaction, an instinct.

But what happens if the person who has wronged you is a fellow Christian?

How do I respond I’m wronged by a fellow Christian?

Do we fight to get back what rightfully belongs to us?

Over the past few weeks, we’ve seen that the Corinthian Church is a messy Church.

There are divisions in the Church, there are people looking down on preachers, there is outright sexual immorality in the Church!

In today’s passage, we add one more: lawsuits among its members.

Should Christians sue another Christian in court?

In today’s passage, we’ll see three things:

Unrighteous to fight family in an unrighteous court

Righteous to be wronged

In Jesus and by the Spirit, God’s rights those who were unrighteous

But before we look into it, let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for your word. Father, by your Spirit, please cleanse our minds, still our hearts, ready our resolve, so that we might walk according to your word and glorify you in all that we say and do. We pray this in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, Amen.”

1. Unrighteous to fight family in an unrighteous court

Firstly, it’s unrighteous to fight family in an unrighteous court.

Look with me at 1 Corinthians 6:1

“If any of you has a dispute against another, how dare you take it to court before the unrighteous, and not before the saints?”

Now notice what Paul’s not saying.

Paul’s not saying that it’s wrong to have disputes between believers.

There will be times when believers don’t agree with one another.

But what’s unacceptable is if the disputes get to a point when the believers take one another to court over the issue.

Why is it unacceptable?

It’s because the believers are saints, people who have been made holy because they now believe in Jesus.

Rather than trusting that believers, as saints, can sort out the issue among themselves, the believers in Corinth resort to going to the unrighteous court to sort things out.

Now, it would be a mistake to think that the court in Corinth was just an ancient version of what we have today in modern Australia.

While the justice system we have in Australia is not perfect, it is far better, far more robust that the justice system the Corinthians had back in Ancient Corinth.

Being a Roman justice system, the Corinthian court would have had a lot of ‘improper influences’ that made justice impossible to administer.

It was a justice system heavily influence by wealth and status.

It was very expensive to go to court and it would’ve been impossible for most people to fight a charge in court.

And so, it was the rich and powerful who dominated the outcome of the court.

It was very likely that one or both parties that had this dispute in the Corinthian Church were wealthy themselves.

It would’ve been standard practice to use their wealth to bribe judges and witnesses.

Young lawyers would be hired to slander the opponent.

It would’ve no doubt caused divisions in the Church.

Paul says that it’s unacceptable that Christian believers, people saved by Jesus, should resort to the court of the unrighteous, people who don’t know Jesus, to settle their differences.

It’s a little like this:

Imagine a student was sent to afternoon detention for being rude to his teacher.

He’s there all alone by himself that afternoon in the detention room.

And then the Principal comes in and asks the student, “We have a problem on our hands.

“The Deputy Principal thinks we should do things this way, but I think we should do things another way.

What should we do? Who’s right and who’s wrong?”

Of course, this is not going to happen!

No Principal in their right mind is going to ask a student in detention about what’s right and what’s wrong.

It’s the student in detention who needs to know what’s right and what’s wrong, not the Principal.

It’s the student in detention who needs to know the Principal’s judgement, not the other way around.

This is Paul’s argument here.

The Christian, saved by Jesus, is in a better place to make a judgement call about a dispute between Christians, then the unrighteous judge outside of the Church.

Paul makes his points stronger by saying that the saints, that is, the Christians, will judge the world and the angels.

When Jesus comes back to judge the world and the cosmos, Christians will judge the world and the cosmos with him.

On that day, it will be Jesus’, and Jesus’ judgement alone that counts.

And Christians, that is, those one who believe and who have been living according to Jesus’ teachings, can confirm that what Jesus’ judgement is true.

It’ll be by Jesus’ teaching that the whole world and the cosmos will be judged.

If that’s where we’re heading, then why do we go to unrighteous courts to solve our disputes.

Paul says that it’s shameful for Christians to take other Christians to unrighteous courts.

It’s shameful for the Church and it brings shame to God.

God’s people are God’s family.

Is there really no one wise enough in God’s family to settle disputes among God’s family members?

Verse 6, “Instead, brother goes to court against brother, and that before unbelievers!”

When brother goes against brother in court, it brings shame to God’s family, it brings shame to God!

It’s like a family that I read about.

A family was staying with the wife’s mother at a holiday cottage near Forster, in mid north Coast, in New South Wales.

As the grandmother was carrying baby Molly, she slipped on the stairs and the baby was severely hurt.

It was an accident, and she has been holding on to the baby during the fall but she had let the baby go when she fell on the ground.

As a result, the parents sued the grandmother for hundreds of thousands of dollars.

Imagine that, suing your own mother for thousands of dollars of damages when she tried her best to save your baby.

Of course, it might have been possible that the grandmother was at fault for carrying the baby.

But really, suing your own mother for falling down the stairs?

You can’t tell me that the family is exactly a picture of bliss.

You can’t tell me that that’s the kind of family that I should want to belong to.

That’s what happens when Christians take each other to court.

It shows that we’re not much better than the world; it shows that we’re just as unrighteous as the world.

Now, this doesn’t mean that we should hide criminal cases in the Church.

If someone has broken the law, whether it’s someone who had been abusive or stole money or committed violent offences, they should be reported to the police.

This passage is not talking about hiding someone from the law.

Rather, the passage is about Christians going to the court for civil reasons, like property issues or defamation or money issues.

This passage is telling us not to take our brother or sister to court over civil issues about our properties, our reputation or our finances, even if we think we have reasons to.

Why not?

Because there are times when it’s more righteous to be wronged.

Righteous to be wronged

Look with me at verse 7, “As it is, to have legal disputes against one another is already a defeat for you. Why not rather be wronged? Why not rather be cheated?”

No matter how the result turns out, when a Christian takes his brother to the court, it’s going to be a lose / lose situation.

Rather than fighting for your rights, there are times when it’s better to be wronged.

It’s a shocking thing to hear in ancient Corinth and in modern Sydney.

If a Christian brother wrongs you, you would rather be wronged than to take him to court.

It would be better to lose, to miss out, to be wronged than to do wrong by bringing your Christian brother or sister to court.

That’s the Christian way.

I saw a little like this.

Many years ago, our kids remembered that we have some cheese balls left.

And so, we’ve divided theme equally among them.

We’ve put some in each of their bowls.

We had to be very careful that they all had the same amount, because if someone had more, we would have a major dispute.

As it happened, Caitlyn finished hers early and held out her bowl, asking for more.

OK, potential for major conflicts and disputes; if Caitlyn gets some, then Zoe would want more and Ezekiel would more and the vicious cycle continues.

Before we decided on what to do, Ezekiel puts one in Caitlyn’s mouth and smiles.

And when Zoe asked for more, Ezekiel happily gave one to her.

Ezekiel was willing to lose out; he was willing to not let his desire for fairness get in the way he relates to his sisters

Now this doesn’t happen all the time; I wish it did!

Can you think of a story in your life where you or someone has given up what’s rightfully theirs for the sake of someone else?

If you have, text me via Whatsapp, Wechat or Zoom.

Love to hear from you!

But that’s what Christians, to an extent, are called to do.

We’re called to be willing to give up our rights for the other, even when, and especially when, they don’t deserve it.

That’s the biblical standard in the Bible: The righteous would rather suffer, they would rather be wronged, than to wrong others.

The righteous would rather sacrifice their rights for the others than to fight for their rights.

We see this supremely in the Lord Jesus Christ.

As the Son of God, as the Creator, Jesus has the right to be respected and honoured by everyone.

As the Son of God, Jesus had the right to be honoured as much as God himself is honoured.

But what does he do?

Jesus, existing in the form of God, did not consider equality with God as something to be exploited.

Instead he emptied himself by assuming the form of a servant, taking on the likeness of humanity. (Philippians 2:6-7)

Jesus gave up his rights, all his rights, and was willing to suffer injustice for God his Father and for God’s Kingdom.

Paul himself follows the example of Christ.

Paul is the Apostle of Christ, he’s the founder of the Corinthian Church.

And yet, how does he live? In the best room in Corinth? No!

Come with me to two chapters before this, in 4:11-13, “Up to the present hour we are both hungry and thirsty; we are poorly clothed, roughly treated, homeless; 12 we labor, working with our own hands. When we are reviled, we bless; when we are persecuted, we endure it; 13 when we are slandered, we respond graciously. Even now, we are like the scum of the earth, like everyone’s garbage.”

Everyone who calls Jesus Lord, like the Apostle Paul, like many of us here, will follow in Jesus’ footsteps.

We, too, are called to let go of our rights, let go of what’s rightfully ours, for the sake of Jesus Christ.

It’s not always easy to give up our rights for others in the Church, is it?

I’ve seen cases of dispute where members of a Church had threatened to leave and did leave, because the service time has changed by a matter of minutes.

In some Churches, not ours, high ranking bishops would take Bible-believing Christians to the court in order to kick them out of Church.

It’s horrible and downright shameful when members of a Church would rather wrong Christians than be wronged for the sake of Christ.

And yet, I’ve also seen genuine Christian love where some faithful Christians would give up their land, give up their Church property, so that other Christians can use it to grow.

I’ve seen faithful Christians who would rather live with music that’s not to their taste so that they can encourage younger Christians.

I’ve even seen faithful English-speaking Christians who would attend Chinese-speaking services in order to encourage their Chinese brothers and sisters, to let them know that they’re not alone.

They could easily have said that it’s their right to have English services and that the Chinese should assimilate to the Australian culture.

But these English-speaking brothers and sisters gave up their rights to support and to encourage their Chinese brothers and sisters.

Brothers and sisters, we are called to follow the footsteps of Jesus.

Rather than fighting for our rights, let’s be ready to give up our rights; rather than wronging others, let’s be ready to rather be wronged.

Why?

It’s because that’s the righteous way; that’s Jesus’ way.

We can choose Jesus’ way because in Jesus and by the Spirit, God rights those of us who were at one stage unrighteous.

In Jesus and by the Spirit, God rights those who are unrighteous

Look with me at verse 9-10, “Don’t you know that the unrighteous will not inherit God’s kingdom? Do not be deceived: No sexually immoral people, idolaters, adulterers, or males who have sex with males, no thieves, greedy people, drunkards, verbally abusive people, or swindlers will inherit God’s kingdom.”

Over the past year, signs like this have become a familiar sight.

Here’s a sign that asks whether you have fever, cough, sore throat or any other symptoms that might be COVID-related.

If you do, then you need to self-isolate.

Or if you’ve been in any hot-spots, then you. Need to be self isolate and get tested.

Well these two verses are like the COVID signs, except that rather than stopping you from entering though the door of a clinic, it stops you from entering the gates of heaven.

Rather than symptoms of COVID, it asks you to look for symptoms of something far more deadly.

It asks you to look for symptoms of unrighteousness.

Heaven is where God lives, it’s the most holy place.

Nothing unclean, nothing sinful belongs there; nothing impure, nothing unrighteous enters there.

If we are honest with ourselves, we’ll see that none of us, even the best of us, have the right to enter.

The Bible lists ten groups of sinners here, four of them are people who indulge in sexual sins.

They tend to get the most attention.

But there are other sinners here mentioned here as well, some more subtle than others.

Thieves, drunks, verbally abusive people, swindlers, people who worship idols or false gods and greedy people.

None of these can enter heaven.

Some of us might say that we haven’t been drunk or swindled other people’s money.

But how about being verbally abusive in the heat of the moment, or just plain greed?

This includes those who want to sue other brothers and sisters in an unrighteous court.

In many cases, the reason why we have civil cases is someone has been greedy, whether it’s the person suing or the person being sued.

We’re all sinners; none of us are worthy to enter God’s kingdom.

But God’s provided a way!

Look with me at verse 11, “11 And some of you used to be like this.”

The people of the Corinthian Church used to be like this.

They used to be sinners, they used to be sexually immoral, they used to be greedy, they used to be homosexuals, they used to worship idols.

But something’s changed within them! Something’s not the same!

Look with me at the rest of the verse:

“But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God.”

They were washed; they were sanctified and they were justified.

Their sins were washed away because of Jesus died for them and had made them clean in God’s eyes.

They were sanctified, they’ve been made holy.

They are set apart for God and from now on are to be known as saints, the holy ones.

They were justified, they were declared righteous by God even though they had sinned and had been unrighteous.

God had declared them righteous because Jesus had died in their place.

As Christians who have put our trust in Jesus, this is who we are:

We’re washed; we’re sanctified and we’re justified.

As a people washed, sanctified and justified, we can enter into God’s kingdom, we can inherit God’s kingdom.

And it’s not something that we can do in and of ourselves.

We’re washed, sanctified, and justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God.

It’s by the Lord Jesus and by the Spirit’s work on us and in us that we’re washed, sanctified and justified.

It’s by the work of the Lord Jesus and the Holy Spirit that we can enter into God’s kingdom.

And that’s who we are.

If you believe in Jesus, that’s who you are.

And if that’s who you are, don’t go back to what you were.

Don’t go back to practice the unrighteous acts anymore.

You were unrighteous, but you’re not any longer.

You’re now made righteous by Jesus and the Holy Spirit and so do what’s righteous.

You see, who you are determines what you must do.

You are a person washed by the blood of Jesus, sanctified and justified, do what’s expected of someone who’s washed, sanctified and justified.

You are washed, sanctified and justified, therefore there will be things that you must do.

You are washed, sanctified and justified, therefore there will be things that you mustn’t do.

Who you are will determine what you do or do not.

In five days’ time, the Archbishop Elect, the very Reverend Kanishka Raffel will be consecrated as the 13th Diocesan Bishop of Sydney.

After he was elected, Kanishka read these words to the people who elected him:

“Someone wrote to the four nominees earlier this week and reminded us that there is no higher dignity than to be called a child of God - born not of natural descent, nor of human decision or a husband’s will - but born of God through faith in Jesus. No greater dignity than to be called a Child of God.”

Friends, brothers and sisters, to be a Child of God is to be washed, sanctified and justified.

There is no higher dignity than to be called a child of God.

If you’ve accepted Jesus as your Lord, then that’s your calling; you’re called a child of God.

Act like a child of God.

Act as one who have been washed, sanctified and justified.

Choose righteousness, not unrighteousness.

And so, don’t fight for your rights with a brother or sister in an unrighteous court;

Rather be wronged, knowing that you’ve been made right by God.

Live as a cleansed, sanctified justified Child of God.

What does this mean for us?

Our focus is not our rights, but that we’re made right in Christ.

Our rights may be important, but they are not as important as being made right in Christ.

Many of us here would’ve heard of Bill Gates, the founder of Windows.

Bill Gates is also one of the richest men in the world.

How rich?

Someone said that he makes $US380 per second.

That means that if Bill Gates dropped $100, it’s not worth his time to pick it up.

Our rights might be important, but if it stops us from living the righteous life that Jesus calls us to live, then even if we did lose them, they’re not worth picking up.

What are some things, some rights, in your life that you treasure that might actually not be worth fighting for?

Is it the bragging right for being a leader at Church?

Maybe it’s a right for more me-time instead of serving others?

Or maybe it’s simply claiming have a right to be better looked after than other people at Church?

Friends, don’t let your entitlement to these rights lead you to wrong others.

Don’t let your entitlement to these rights bring down the dignity of the Church, the glory of God and your love for others.

You are washed, sanctified, justified.

What unrighteous acts must you say no to?

What righteous acts must you do?

Remember, our focus is not our rights but that we’re made right in Christ.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Am I in dispute with fellow brothers or sisters that’s causing shame to the Church?

Are there things in my life that I would rather lose out for Jesus’ sake?

Finish the sentence: I am a washed, sanctified, and justified Christian, therefore I will….

Friends, let’s not focus on our rights; rather let us focus on the fact that we’re made right in Christ.

Let’s pray,

“Father, thank you for the Lord Jesus Christ. Thank you that in him, we’re washed, we’re sanctified, we’re justified. Father, please forgive us for the times, the many times, when we would rather wrong others than to be wronged, and so dishonoured you, dishonoured the Church and dishonoured what Jesus has done for us. Please by your Spirit, transform us so that we would rather suffer for Jesus and for his kingdom. We pray this in his name, Amen.”
