The greatest gift

Passage: Romans 3:21-26

Big Idea: The righteousness of God is shown through Christ’s propitiation, through whose faithfulness the faithful are justified and redeemed

The righteousness of God is revealed through Jesus’ faithfulness

The righteousness of God justifies and redeems the inglorious

The righteousness of God satisfies the righteous God and justifies the faithful in Jesus

Thank you, everyone, who helped to celebrate Jenny’s birthday last week.

It was a very pleasant surprise!

Our family had also planned a surprise for Jenny.

In fact, it’s a surprise that Caitlyn’s planned for one year.

For a long time, Jenny mentioned that she used to play an instrument called the Melodica.

Has anyone played one before?

I think many children from Hong Kong learnt it during primary school.

For Jenny’s birthday last year, Caitlyn wanted to get one for Jenny but it was too late.

And so, a few months ago, Caitlyn asked whether we can get a Melodica for Jenny.

They’re not that common in Australia but we did manage to find one.

As Jenny was opening the present, we were thinking, would she love it? Or will she hate it?

What do you think?

Here’s a photo of Jenny playing it.

I think she loved it, is that right?

Look at Caitlyn’s face in the photo; she was happy that her Mum was happy with the present.

We think she got Jenny’s tick of approval for the present!

It’s a nice feeling to be approved, isn’t it?

It’s a joy to see satisfaction and joy in someone else, especially your parents, when you do something for them.

In today’s passage, we’re going to see what it takes to get God’s tick of approval.

Is God happy with us? Do we bring joy to God?

Some people think that God automatically must be pleased with us.

They see God as a doting father who would approve anything that their children do.

Others see God as a harsh tyrant, whose standard is so high that nothing we do would ever please him.

Nothing we do could satisfy him.

Is God the doting Father or the harsh tyrant?

What does it take to get God’s tick of approval?

That’s what we’ll be looking at over the next few weeks.

As Christmas approaches, we’re going to see from the Bible that it’s God’s gift to us that gives him the greatest joy.

In today’s passage, we’ll see three things:

The righteousness of God is revealed through Jesus’ faithfulness

The righteousness of God justifies and redeems the inglorious

But before we look into it, let’s pray.

“Father, please open our eyes now as we hear from your word. Please fill our hearts with your Spirit, that we might rejoice by faith as we hear and obey your word. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

The righteousness of God is revealed through Jesus’ faithfulness

Please open up with me to Romans 3, beginning from verse 21.

“But now, apart from the law, the righteousness of God has been revealed, attested by the Law and the Prophets.”

Laws, whether it’s laws in Australia, or laws in other lands, tell us whether something is right or wrong.

I know that’s a very general statement and I know that law can be a lot more complicated than that.

But in principle, that’s what laws do, they tell us what’s right and wrong.

Some laws are the same across countries and across centuries.

Other laws are bound very much to a particular location.

For example, the World Cup is now being played in Qatar.

It’s a country that has very strict laws about alcohol.

And so, when football fans arrived at the stadiums in Qatar, they were disappointed that they couldn’t buy any alcohol at all.

Some people agreed with the strict laws against alcohol; others strongly disagreed.

In the Bible, God has given his people laws to live by.

The laws show us what is right and wrong, according to God’s standards.

And God’s standard is far higher, far more demanding, than the strictest law in Qatar.

For the laws of Qatar speaks against our actions; things that we’ve done or haven’t done.

And God’s law speaks against our heart, what we love or don’t love, what we believe or don’t believe.

In God’s laws, there are things that are right things to love and believe as well as wrong things to love and believe.

One way we use to describe God’s standard is the “righteousness of God”.

It’s what God sees as right.

Who does God sees as right? No one!

Look with me at Romans 3:10-12, “as it is written: There is no one righteous, not even one. 11 There is no one who understands; there is no one who seeks God. 12 All have turned away; all alike have become worthless. There is no one who does what is good, not even one.

Not one person is righteous in God’s eyes, not even one.

None of our attempts, even the very best of us, comes even close to satisfying God.

According to the righteousness of God, according to God’s standards, we, as a human race, had all failed.

It’s a sad picture of the human race.

To use the words of some of our young people, we’re all losers!

However, all’s not lost, not yet.

Look with me at verse 21, “But now, apart from the law, the righteousness of God has been revealed, attested by the Law and the Prophets. 22 The righteousness of God is through faith in Jesus Christ to all who believe, since there is no distinction.”

There is a way for God to be satisfied; there is a way for us to experience and live the righteousness of God.

And it’s apart from the law.

We don’t achieve righteousness of God by trying to live according to the law.

What is the new way?

The righteousness of God is now available through faith in Jesus Christ.

Another way to translate this is, “The righteousness of God is through the faithfulness of Jesus Christ (22 那就是：神的義藉著對耶穌基督的信仰)

What is this faithfulness of Christ?

It’s Jesus’ trust in God; it’s Jesus’ belief in God.

Now, obviously, Jesus believes in the existence of God and so Jesus’ faith in God is not simply believing that he exists.

No, it’s far deeper than that.

Jesus believes that God is righteous, and that God’s way is always, always right.

And so, as Jesus trusts God and puts his faith in God, the righteousness of God is shown.

Who is this righteousness of God shown to?

Those who believe, those who have put their trust in Jesus.

This is something that many people get wrong.

Too often, our focus is on our faith, on how much trust we have.

However, our focus shouldn’t be on our faith.

Our focus should be on the faithfulness of Jesus.

Of course, it’s not one or the other; if we are to believe in God, we must believe in Jesus, we must have faith in Jesus.

But far too often, our eyes are on us rather than on Jesus.

We think that we have blessings from God because we’ve been faithful to God.

However, the focus of our faith must be on Jesus and his faithfulness.

We can see God’s righteousness and be blessed by God only because of Jesus’ faithfulness.

Yes, keep working on your faith, keep asking God to grow in your faith.

But always remember that your faithfulness to God is secondary.

Jesus’ faithfulness to God is the primary; it’s his faithfulness to God that’s of the utmost importance.

Every now and then, I would get to hear testimonies of Christians in outreach meetings and evangelistic events.

Sometimes, I would hear about how a person has had a dramatic change in their lives, usually for the better.

Once I remembered a sister gave a testimony that started at around 8:00pm lasted well past midnight.

She was basically telling the story of her life!

While there is a place to talk about your growth as a Christian, we must always give glory to Christ in our testimonies.

We need to talk about his faithfulness more than we talk about our own.

Our faithfulness is secondary; it’s his faithfulness that must come first.

And so, God’s righteousness, his righteous standard, comes through the faithfulness of Jesus to those who have faith in Jesus.

If you have faith in Jesus, you will see God’s righteousness.

If you don’t have faith in Jesus, you won’t see God’s righteousness.

If you don’t have faith in Jesus, you’ll question God’s righteousness.

The righteousness of God justifies and redeems the inglorious

One common question that they ask is this is based on the last part of verse 22.

“Since there is no distinction.”

Their question is this: Is there really no distinction?

Does God’s righteous standard really apply to everyone?

What about those who have never heard? What about those who were born before Jesus’ time?

Will they be judged differently?

Do they still need to believe in Jesus to see God’s righteousness?

Look with me at verses 23-24.

If you haven’t memorised this, memorise them.

“For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God; they are justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.”

Does the Bible mention any exceptions?

Is there someone out there, deep in the long lost tribe or somewhere in my ancestry who are exempted from believing in Jesus?

Does the Bible say that there’s any distinctions?

No. Zero. Nada.

The Bible says that all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.

Everyone is in that category, without distinction, whether the person was born before Jesus’ times or after Jesus’ time,

Whether the person had heard the gospel or not.

That’s because in the beginning, we, the human race, were made to reflect God’s glory.

But because we have sinned, because we chose to turn our backs on God, we became sinners, and we’ve fallen short of God’s glory.

Rather than reflecting God’s glory, we’ve become inglorious.

Rather than walking as children of light, we live as creatures of the dark.

This applies to all of us!

All have fallen short!

Everyone in this church have fallen short, and everyone outside of the Church have fallen short.

There are no loopholes; there are no alternative way to go to see God’s righteousness.

If you want to see God’s righteousness, if you want to experience God’s righteousness, Jesus is the only way.

Why must it be Jesus? What has Jesus done for those who believe him to turn them from being creatures of darkness to children of light?

Firstly, through Jesus, God has justified freely by his grace.

Imagine that you’re in a court and that you’re in the stand on trial for all the sins you’ve committed in your life.

You know that you’re guilty.

As God the Judge approaches his desk to give his verdict, you stand with shame and guilt, knowing that he’s about to sentence you.

Instead of sentencing you, God says, “That person is free of all charges.

That person is no longer guilty, according to my perfect, righteous standard, because my Son, my one and only Son, has paid the price that they deserved.

Since the price has been paid, he is now justified.

He is righteous by my standard.”

That’s what it means to be justified.

In English, the word “justified” and “righteousness” looks and sound very different.

However, in Greek and in Chinese, the words are very similar.

In Greek, it’s δικαιοσυνη and δικαιουμενοι (righteousness and justified)

In Chinese it’s 義 and 稱義.

Our Greek and Chinese speakers can see the similarity straight away.

But our English speakers need to work a little harder to see the similarity.

For something similar, the English word for justify will need to be something like, “righteousfy”

In Jesus, we’ve been righteousfied; we’re made right in God’s eyes.

In order to justify us, in order to righteousfy us, God paid a great price.

That’s what the word, “redemption” means.

To redeem means to pay something in order to bring something or someone back.

God has paid the greatest price to declare us right in his eyes.

Our justification came freely for us, but it came at a great price for God.

There’s no distinction, for only one way to be justified, to be declared right before God.

It’s only through belief in the Lord Jesus and the redemption he brought that anyone can come before God justified, declared right in God’s eyes.

That’s why we must keep telling people about Jesus.

That’s why we must keep inviting people to come to Church.

That’s why we have outreaches in KOGworx at the Children Centre,

That’s why we go to St Patrick’s Green to have Communion services.

That’s why we teach the Bible in three local schools, and support Nick Hadges to teach in two high schools.

That’s why we have digital classes.

We do all these things because we believe there is no distinction, that all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God;

That all are justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.

Jesus is the only way to be justified by God’s grace.

That’s why we support missionaries.

That’s why Christians have been sending missionaries to countries all over the world, to long lost tribes, to people groups, who have never heard about Jesus,

Because there is no other way to God.

If they don’t hear about Jesus, they will remain in the darkness.

If we don’t tell them, how will they know?

In light of what’s at stake, how can we not go?

Penn Jillette is a very famous magician, probably one of the most famous magicians in the world at the moment.

He and his show partner Teller have been doing magic shows as Penn and Teller for many years.

One night, a man came up to Penn and said he enjoyed the show.

And he gave Penn a New Testament with Psalm, a little like this book that I’m holding now.

Now Penn was not a Christian but an atheist.

I think he still probably is an atheist.

However, he really appreciated what this man did.

Listen to what Penn said,

“I’ve always said I don’t respect people who don’t proselytize (that is, sharing the gospel with them)

I don’t respect that at all.

If you believe there is a heaven and hell, and people could be going to hell or not getting eternal life or whatever, and you think it’s not really worth telling them this because it would make it socially awkward.

“How much do you have to hate somebody to not proselytize? How much do you have to hate someone to believe everlasting life is possible and not tell them that?”

You know what, he’s right!

If we do believe in heaven and hell, if we do believe that we have fallen short of God’s glory and that all those around us have fallen short of God’s glory and if Jesus is the only way, then we must tell our friends and families about Jesus.

Don’t give up.

I know it’s hard; There are people that I’ve invited many times but who have constantly turned me down.

There are people who said they would come but never show up.

It can feel awkward; it can feel embarrassing.

It can be very hard.

But I’m glad someone cared me enough and told me about Jesus.

I’m glad that someone has cared for you enough to tell you about Jesus.

Because of that person’s persistence, because of that person’s faithfulness, you’re redeemed, you’re justified, you’re experiencing God’s righteousness.

May God move our hearts so that we can see his clearly.

May the light of his righteousness help us see how the ingloriously dark our world really is.

May he open our eyes to see how glorious Jesus is and how much we need him.

For the greatest need of man requires the greatest gift of God.

And so, is God satisfied with you?

If you have put your faith in Jesus, if you believe in him, if you depend on him with your life, your whole life, then yes, God is satisfied with you.

For in Jesus, he’s justified you; he’s righteousfied you.

However, if you have not believe in Jesus, if you’re still living according to your own righteousness, then no, God is not satisfied with you.

No matter how hard you try, you will never satisfy God.

And if you don’t satisfy God, you will never be truly satisfied.

For it’s only when God is satisfied with us, that we find true, everlasting satisfaction.

And so, friends, by faith, live in light of Christ’s faithfulness.

Jesus is faithful to God.

Jesus firmly believes that God is righteous and all his ways are right.

It’s through Jesus’ faithfulness in God that we experience God’s righteousness.

How should we respond?

We should respond with joy at being justified, made right, righeoufied by God.

We should be grateful at being redeemed, paid at the great price of God the Son.

We should be bold as we seek to bring God’s righteousness, God’s greatest gift to those who haven’t received it yet.

The missionary Charles Studd was a missionary who followed Hudson Taylor to China.

When he was a young man, he started sharing the gospel with others.

Listen to his joy as he recounted how he brought his first soul to the Lord.

“I cannot tell you what joy it gave me to bring the first soul to the Lord Jesus Christ. I have tasted almost all the pleasures that this world can give ... but those pleasures were as nothing compared to the joy that the saving of that one soul gave me.”

There is no distinction; there is only one way by which God is satisfied.

Salvation is found in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven given to mankind by which we must be saved.

Which soul will you bring to Jesus?

Over which soul will heaven rejoice with you as you bring that soul to Jesus?

Jesus is faithful to God, and so, friends, by faith, let’s live in light of Christ’s faithfulness.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How can we learn to focus more on Jesus’ faithfulness instead of our own?

Who in my life needs to hear about the justification that God brings in Jesus?

Who can I work with to bring the gospel to people who have never heard about Jesus?

This Christmas, tell your loved ones about the greatest gift that God has given us.

Tell them about the gift that we need the most, above all things.

Tell them about Jesus.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for giving us the greatest gift of all. Thank you that because of the faithfulness of Jesus, those of us who believe him are redeemed and justified. We thank you that because of Jesus, by faith, we see your righteousness. Please don’t let us lose the urgency of your gospel. There is no other way to you but through Jesus. There is no other hope we have besides Jesus. Who in heaven do we have, except for you? Please fix our eyes on Jesus and by faith, let us live in light of his faithfulness. For Jesus’ sake we pray, Amen.”
