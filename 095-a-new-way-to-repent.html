A New Way to Repent

Luke 5:27-39 · Preached at St Paul's Kogarah on 8 November 2026
Big IdeaFellow sick sinners, Jesus calls us to a new repentance centred on him, to leave everything, follow him, feast with him and fast in his absence.

Leave everything and follow the Doctor (vv.27–32)

Feast with the Bridegroom, fast in his absence (vv.33–35)

New repentance, new wineskins (vv.36–39)

You might have heard of this before; if you know the answer shout it out.

A man went to a florist to buy flowers for his wife.

It’s not Valentine’s Day and so the lady at the counter asked the man, “Oh, is it for your wife’s birthday?”

“No,” the man replied.

The man waiting behind him then said, “What did you do this time?”

It’s not Valentine’s Day, it’s not his wife’s birthday.

The man must have done something wrong and is now getting flowers to say he’s sorry.

You can imagine the guy saying, “Darling, it’s hard for me to say I’m sorry. You know, Sorry seems to be the hardest word.”

It’s all good and well to say that he’s sorry, but if his life hasn’t changed, then we would say that his sorry was not genuine.

It would be like he’s saying, “Sorry, not sorry” (a common saying today).

How about us?

What do we really mean when we say sorry to God?

What happens when we repent?

Is saying ‘sorry’ to God the end of repentance, or only the beginning?

In today’s passage, we’re going to see what happens when someone is truly sorry and they truly repent.

Please open your Bible to Luke chapter 5 beginning from verse 27.

We’ll see three things:

Leave everything and follow the Doctor (vv.27–32)

Feast with the Bridegroom, fast in his absence (vv.33–35)

New repentance, new wineskins (vv.36–39)

Before we go further, let’s pray, “Father, thank you for sending Jesus to save sinners like us. Please help us to listen to your word, not only to understand but to be convicted, so that we may live the new life of repentance that you’ve given us your Spirit to live. For we pray in Jesus’ name, Amen.”

Leave everything and follow the Doctor (vv.27–32)

We don’t often hear the word ‘sorry’ much nowadays.

We don’t want to sound weak or to admit that we’ve made a mistake.

However, even when we do hear it, it’s often for something trivial like ‘sorry, I’m late’ or ‘sorry that I bumped into you by accident’.

Other times we say sorry when we get caught doing something wrong.

Other times, the offended party might come to us and force us to say sorry, against our will.

As human beings, it’s hard for us to squeeze the word ‘sorry’ out of us.

However, when Jesus comes to a person, and that person recognises his or her need to say sorry to God, something beautiful happens.

The repentance is not forced out of the person.

Rather the repentance blossoms out of the person’s life.

Look with me at Luke 5:27, “After this, Jesus went out and saw a tax collector named Levi sitting at the tax office, and he said to him, ‘Follow me.’”

Levi was a tax collector, and his job as a tax collector was one of the most hated jobs during Jesus’ time.

Why?

It was because he was a Jew, and he collected taxes for their oppressors, the Romans.

He would’ve been seen as a traitor to his own people.

More than that, he probably would’ve charged a higher commission than what the Romans asked for and so he was taking advantage of his own people as well.

Levi and all his tax collector friends were not the most popular people in town.

That’s why it would’ve seemed strange to Levi and everyone around him when Jesus looked at Levi, while Levi was still working at his tax office, and said to him, “Follow me”.

Unlike Peter and the other fishermen, who made an honest living out of fishing, Levi chose a very questionable career, one that a lot of people would find unethical, even sinful.

But rather than being shunned by Jesus, he was invited by Jesus to follow him.

Out of all the people that Jesus could’ve chosen, he chose Levi, the tax collector.

Levi didn’t hesitate but got up, left everything behind, and followed Jesus.

The word repentance means recognising that we’ve been wrong, recognising that we’ve been sinning against God and turning our minds and our hearts back to God.

There are times when Jesus would command people to repent of their sins, but here, there was almost an instant recognition from Levi that he would rather follow Jesus than to stay in his tax booth.

It would be like Jesus walking into a bank, telling the bank manager to follow him, and the bank manager standing up and leaving.

We don’t have a record of what Levi said, but his action spoke a thousand words.

He changed his mind about his life, and he followed Jesus.

That’s the first thing we see about what genuine repentance looks like.

It’s not merely going through what a person did or didn’t do, as important as that is.

It’s about how a person responds when Jesus calls them.

Will they stay seated or will they stand up to follow him?

Genuine godly repentance is dependent on your relationship with Jesus.

It’s not transactional but personal.

If you are genuinely sorry to God, you will follow Jesus.

If you’re not, you’ll stay seated.

Where was Jesus going?

Back to Levi’s house!

Levi would leave everything to follow Jesus, he would lose all his income as a tax collector.

But before he did, he invited all his tax collectors and other friends to a banquet he held for Jesus.

It was a grand banquet; there would’ve been a lot of food and a lot of joy.

Can you imagine all the tax collectors coming together to hear what had happened to Levi?

All these tax collectors and their friends who were shunned by the society were invited as guests to Levi’s party for Jesus!

Instead of being shunned, they were welcomed!

However, the Pharisees and their scribes, who were usually the guests of honour at parties like these, were not the guests of honour this time.

The shunners themselves were shunned.

And so, they came and complained to the disciples.

Look with me at verse 30, “But the Pharisees and their scribes were complaining to his disciples, ‘Why do you eat and drink with tax collectors and sinners?’”

When the Israelites were in the wilderness, they complained and grumbled against Moses about not having water to drink or food to eat.

And now, the Pharisees and their scribes complained about food and drinks as well, only this time, they were complaining that the disciples (and by implication their teacher) ought not be eating and drinking with these tax collectors and sinners.

Jesus then told them a profound truth in verse 31, “Jesus replied to them, ‘It is not those who are healthy who need a doctor, but those who are sick.’”

I just love how Doctor Luke, the person who wrote this account, kept picking up the Doctor analogy that Jesus applied to himself.

Jesus is the great Doctor and he’s come for those who are sick.

And so, who’s sick?

All of us.

Every man and woman, boy and girl, who had ever lived are sick.

We might not be physically sick now, but all of us will face our own sickness and death one day.

Our physical sicknesses are only symptoms of our spiritual sickness.

We are spiritually sick and dead in God’s eyes.

The Bible calls this sin and we sin against God every day.

We can say a million sorrys to God but it won’t be enough to save us from our spiritual sickness.

We need to be healed, and only Jesus can heal us.

The irony is that though all of us are sick, only those who recognise themselves as sick can come before Jesus for healing.

Those who refuse to see themselves as sick, those who see themselves as healthy, won’t seek Jesus the Doctor for healing.

Verse 32, “I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.”

Those who think of themselves as righteous won’t say sorry to Jesus and so won’t have forgiveness.

Jesus has come to call sinners like you and me, to say sorry to him.

Jesus has come to heal spiritually sick people, not to make us well so that we can sin again, but to repent and turn back to God.

Jesus has called sinners like us to repent, will we get up and follow him?

Or will we stay seated?

Or worse, will we be like the Pharisees and their scribes, complaining that others are enjoying the blessings from Jesus, and grumbling that others got more grace than they deserved?

I remember that when I was in high school, one of my friends said this about me, “If Jesus comes into the classroom and tells David to go, he would”.

In his mind, it seemed like I would be such a fool to leave everything to follow Jesus.

But in my mind, it makes perfect sense, not just spiritually but logically.

If Jesus, the Son of God, walks in through the door right now, and asks me to follow him, of course it makes perfect sense to do so.

He’s God, I’m not.

Of course I would follow.

And that’s what repentance looks like.

It’s not just about saying sorry, as important as that is as a first step, but it’s about leaving your life, it’s about leaving your everything behind, and following Jesus.

It’s not about chasing an ideal, a way of life.

It’s about following a Person, it’s about following Jesus.

Levi left his tax booth but opened his home.

While we open our Church every Sunday, we’ll be making an extra effort to invite our friends and families to come to our Christmas concert on 13 Dec.

By God’s grace, we want to see 120 people coming and joining us to worship Jesus.

It’ll be like our banquet.

Let’s pray and continue to invite people to Jesus.

Let’s pray that by faith, we’ll invite tax collectors and others to come.

We’re slowly making our way to the 75 households.

After that, there are still many more to invite in our 10-minute radius.

When they do come, welcome them as Jesus would have you.

And stop pretending to be healthy.

Repentance starts by admitting you are sick and hearing Jesus' 'Follow me'.

As you do, make your home, make our church, a place of spiritual banquet for sinners who know of their need to be healed by Jesus.

Feast with the Bridegroom, fast in his absence (vv.33–35)

The discussion between the Pharisees and Jesus is like a badminton game.

The Pharisees and their scribes fired the first shot by complaining that Jesus’ disciples were eating with tax collectors and sinners.

Jesus returned serve by saying that Jesus has come for people like them, people who recognised that they’re sinners, not the so-called ‘righteous’ people who don’t recognise their sins.

The Pharisees and the scribes then shot back and said, in verse 33, “John’s disciples fast often and say prayers, and those of the Pharisees do the same, but yours eat and drink.”

It’s as if the Pharisees and their scribes were saying, “Repentance? What are you talking about Jesus? Repentance means praying and fasting. That’s what John’s disciples do; that’s what our disciples do. But look at yours. Yours eat and drink! You call that repentance?”

You can see where the Pharisees and their scribes are coming from, can’t you?

Usually, when you’re sorry about what you’ve done, you’re sad.

Throughout the centuries, when God’s people wanted to express they’re sorry for their sins, they fast and they pray.

In the Bible, God commanded only one day of fasting.

Fasting is to commit a time of not eating food, usually from dawn to dusk.

The one day that God commanded his people to fast was the Day of Atonement, when the whole nation of Israel don’t eat but remember how they’ve sinned against God.

It’s a national Sorry Day.

Besides that day, all other fasting is voluntary.

People may fast when there’s a tragedy or when they prepare in prayer for an impending disaster.

However, the Pharisees decided that it wasn’t enough to fast one day a year.

What does real repentance, real fasting, look like for the Pharisees?

Not one day of fasting per year but 104 days of fasting a year.

They fast twice a week, Mondays and Thursdays.

God’s got to be pleased with that, right?

It’s much higher than what God asked for and it’s certainly more ‘spiritual’ than the disciples’ partying with tax collectors and sinners.

In their minds, they thought of their disciples as superior to Jesus’ disciples, when it comes to repentance.

However, Jesus basically asks them to take a step back and think about what time it is.

What time is it?

It’s time to celebrate, because the Bridegroom is here.

Look with me at verse 34, “Jesus said to them, ‘You can’t make the wedding guests fast while the groom is with them, can you?’”

A few weeks ago, I went to the wedding of a friend and his wife.

It was a lovely reception with a lot of food.

Here are some photos of the food and the people.

Now imagine that I sit at the table, and the waiter comes with the plate, and I say, “No thanks, I’m not in the mood for eating tonight.”

That would be weird.

And also disrespectful to the couple.

They spent a lot to make sure that I can share their happiness on their wedding day.

To not eat, to let the food go to waste, was not a very respectful thing to do. (And as an aside, children and adults, when you go to a friend’s place, especially a friend from a different culture, be respectful and eat what they prepared for you.

It’s respect and etiquette).

Just as you don’t fast at a wedding feast when the Bride and the Bridegroom are present, you don’t fast when Jesus is present.

For a long time, God’s people recognised that they have sinned against God.

They would come before God in prayer and offer God’s sacrifices for their sins.

But all these sacrifices to God for their sins won’t be enough to change their hearts.

In their heart of hearts, they are still sinners.

To be truly forgiven of their sins, they need a change of heart, a heart surgery, as it were.

Not a physical heart surgery but a spiritual heart surgery.

That’s what God promised them in Ezekiel 36:26, “I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you; I will remove your heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh.”

In the fullness of time, God sent Jesus, the Doctor, the Heart Surgeon Supreme, to remove our heart of stone to give us a heart of flesh.

What’s the time?

It’s time for God to fulfil his promise.

It’s time to celebrate because Jesus is here!

That’s why the disciples shouldn’t be fasting.

They’re celebrating because Jesus is here.

A time will come, however, for them to grieve.

A time will come for them to be sad and for them to fast.

When will that be?

Verse 35, “But the time will come when the groom will be taken away from them—then they will fast in those days.”

A time will come when Jesus will be taken away from the disciples.

That’s when Jesus would be taken away to be crucified on the cross.

They would feel Jesus’ absence on that day, and it would be appropriate for them to fast then.

What does this mean for us who are Christians in the 21st century?

How do we express our repentance?

Do we fast or do we feast?

We should do both.

We should feast because God has forgiven us in Jesus Christ and adopted us into his family when we believe Jesus and what he’s done for us.

Jesus himself has promised that he will be with us always, to the very end of the age.

He is with us, even now, in our Church.

Knowing Jesus and following Him gives us joy, even in the midst of our suffering.

Even so, we do suffer for Jesus’ sake and there will be times when we should fast and ask him for help.

While he is with us by His Spirit, we still long to see him, face to face.

We long for the day when he comes back to take us home with him.

There will be times when it is appropriate to weep and fast in times of distress, in times of his physical absence.

But we can take heart knowing that He will never forsake us nor abandon us.

We will never be alone, if we belong to Christ.

Once again, Jesus emphasises that repentance is not only about how you express your sorrow for your sin.

It’s about how you express yourself in light of the presence and the absence of Jesus.

Repentance is celebrating the joy that only his forgiveness can bring.

Repentance is yearning in anguish, even weeping tears of sorrow, because Jesus hasn’t come back yet.

It’s both the joy anticipated for Jesus’ presence and the yearning experienced because of his absence that makes us cry out, “Come, Lord Jesus, come.”

That’s why the Bible tells us in Hebrews 10:25 to not “neglect to gather together, as some are in the habit of doing, but encouraging each other, and all the more as you see the day approaching.”

There will be times when we’ll be tempted to feel so despairing of our world that we lose our hope in Christ.

That’s when we need our brothers and sisters to remind us of the joy that we have in Christ and that we ought to be celebrating what he’s given us.

There will also be times when we are tempted to only want our happiness above all else and forget that we’re in a fallen and broken world.

Or worse, we become so engrossed in our own happiness, even the happiness we find in Christ, that we neglect those who are going through a difficult time around us.

Who wants to share their deepest struggles with you when all you want to talk about is having fun?

My brothers and sisters, let’s show true repentance, not only in our prayers, but in how we feast and how we fast.

Be there for the brother who needs to be reminded to fast.

Be there for the sister who needs to be reminded to feast.

New repentance, new wineskins (vv.36–39)

Before the Pharisees and their scribes could respond, Jesus gives them a parable to think about.

Look with me at verse 36, “He also told them a parable: ‘No one tears a patch from a new garment and puts it on an old garment. Otherwise, not only will he tear the new, but also the piece from the new garment will not match the old.’”

Has anyone bought a new cotton shirt that shrank after the first wash?

I remember getting a new T-shirt a long time ago.

It was a good fit when I got it, but after the first or second wash, it shrank by a lot.

Apparently, untreated cotton can shrink up to 20% after the first wash, unless you wash it properly, in cold water.

Jesus says that if you take a new cloth, untreated, unwashed, tear out one piece, and put it on an old garment, you won’t save the old garment.

You’ll only ruin both the old and the new.

They will not work together.

What is he talking about?

Jesus has been speaking to the Pharisees and their scribes about two ways of living as repentant people before God, the old way and the new way.

The old way of living was to shun and complain about those who were sinners, and to show you’re sorry for your sins by prayer and fasting.

However, Jesus is saying that there’s now a new way to live as repentant people of God.

Jesus is not saying that the sin of the sinners doesn’t matter anymore.

Jesus says that he’s come to call the sinners to repentance.

Nor is Jesus saying that you shouldn’t fast or pray to express your sorrow for your sin.

There are appropriate times for prayer and fasting.

However, the new way of repentance that Jesus speaks of is one that focuses on him.

In the new way of repentance, you recognise your need for Doctor Jesus.

You acknowledge that the presence of Jesus is a cause for celebration and the absence of Jesus ought to lead us to a time of deep yearning for him.

A genuine sorry, a genuine repentance, hinges on how we relate to Jesus.

This new way is the new cloth.

The new way of repentance centred around Jesus is not just a patch that you sew on the old clothes.

No, it’s a completely new way of living, that’s incompatible with the old way.

You can’t patch Jesus to be part of your old life.

True repentance means Jesus is all of your life.

Just as the Bible teaches us in Romans 13:14, “But put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh to gratify its desires.”

You put on Christ Jesus and you don’t leave any room for the old life to take part in your life any more.

It’s Levi’s leaving everything behind to follow Jesus.

He didn’t take a few days of leave to organise Jesus’ feast.

He left his job and organised Jesus’ feast.

It’s all or nothing.

Jesus then uses the illustration of new wine in old wineskins.

Look with me at verses 37-38, “And no one puts new wine into old wineskins. Otherwise, the new wine will burst the skins, it will spill, and the skins will be ruined. No, new wine is put into fresh wineskins.”

To make wine, you need to add yeast to the sugar from the grapes.

When they come together, the fermentation process happens, producing alcohol and carbon dioxide.

Old wine which had already finished its fermentation can stay happily in old wineskins since there’s no more carbon dioxide to be released from the fermentation process.

However, what happens if you put new wine, which hasn’t finished its fermentation process yet, in old wineskins?

The carbon dioxide released from the new wine will apply pressure to the old wineskin.

The old wineskin will crack under pressure and the wineskin would be ruined.

No, you put new wine in new wineskins.

That’s what’s been happening since the beginning of the sermon.

We applied yeast to a sugary solution in a plastic bag.

And look, the bag is expanding.

The bag is flexible and so it can change its shape to adjust the change in volume inside.

What would happen if it’s in an old, inflexible bag?

It would probably make a big mess.

With Jesus comes a new way of repentance that’s incompatible with the old.

There’s life and creativity in the new way of repentance that the old can’t contain.

That’s certainly true of the Pharisees and their scribes, but it can also be true for us.

Jesus cannot be just one part of your life.

You can’t serve Jesus for one hour on Sundays from your week.

You can’t serve Jesus under your own discretion; that would make you lord.

However, if he’s your Lord, it’s Jesus who decides how much of your life is his and his answer is: all of it.

Your old way of life can’t handle the new life of Jesus.

Your life will burst if you try to contain Jesus to only certain parts or to certain days.

No, the new life of repentance that Jesus gives is a new way of living because it’s a new life that he brings.

A while ago, in my old Church, they didn’t have a youth group on Friday night.

They tried for a long time, but only three youths came.

They had little hope that there would ever be a thriving youth group at that Church.

However, over time, in God’s kindness and the labour of many people, the youth in the Church grew.

They still don’t have a youth group on Friday night, but they had a youth congregation in the morning.

Over time, those youth grew to the other morning services and also in the evening service.

There are now so many youths there that they have two afternoon services, filled mostly with young people.

They didn’t do the same things as every other Church, they did not restrict themselves to one particular way of doing youth ministry, but they followed God’s guidance, even when things weren’t clear.

I don’t know what God has planned for our Church’s future; I’m still praying.

But God’s been kind and has provided many, many opportunities around us.

Please pray in your home groups, on prayer nights and at home to ask for his direction and provision for our youths and young adults.

My prayer is that the youths, the young adults, the adults and those of us who are older, will not be satisfied with our old way of repentance but to yearn for, to long for, Jesus’ new way of repentance.

You don’t treat Jesus’ life as a patch in your life.

You can’t, not really.

You don't add Jesus to an unchanged life.

Repent and let him take all of your life, that you may have life that is truly life.

If you are a Christian, ask yourself, has there been any change, or am I still living the old way?

He has called you to repentance.

Let him transform your life.

Let his life in you, his new wine, stretch you as you live for him.

Is saying ‘sorry’ to God the end of repentance, or only the beginning?

It’s only the beginning.

It’s the beginning of living a new life that he gives to all those who have repented and now follow him.

We have seen that repentance starts when the Doctor calls the sick, and Levi leaves everything to follow him.

We have seen that it means feasting with the Bridegroom while he is here and fasting while he is away.

And we have seen that this new repentance needs new wineskins: Jesus cannot be patched onto the old.

Repentance isn’t transactional, it’s personal.

The heart of repentance is not a Jesus patch, but the Person of Jesus.

Jesus has come to call the sick to repent.

Have you heeded his call?

My fellow sick sinners, Jesus calls us to a new repentance centred on him, to leave everything, follow him, feast with him and fast in his absence.

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you for sending Jesus, our great Doctor to save sick and wicked sinners like us. By your Holy Spirit, please change our hearts so that they will be transformed from a heart of stone to a heart of flesh. We need Jesus; help us to recognise who he is, leave everything we have, and follow him. Let us rejoice and praise you for the forgiveness you give to us through him. Please make us yearn for his return. And please stretch, mould, and reshape us by your Holy Spirit, that we may bring your life to all those around us. For we pray in Jesus’ name, Amen.”
