Home Groups (adults)

When Grace Breaks into the Impossible

Luke 1:26–45 · Sunday 4 October 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

For adult home groups · from Sunday’s sermon, preached by Jerry Zhu

Big Idea: When grace breaks into the impossible, God uses those the world considers “unqualified”: his grace comes first, faith rests on his word, and faith is strengthened in fellowship.

Aim: By the end of tonight, each member should be able to (1) explain why God’s choice of Nazareth and Mary shows the “logic of the gospel” — grace comes before our response; (2) describe, from Mary’s “How can this be?” and “I am the Lord’s servant”, what faith in God’s word looks like when we cannot control the future; (3) leave with one “impossibility” to entrust to God, and one person to be an “Elizabeth” to this week.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • Think of a time you felt “unqualified” — a new job, a first baby, being asked to lead or speak. What helped you, or what did you wish someone had said?
  • When big, unsettling news arrives — a diagnosis, a restructure at work, a phone call about family — who is the first person you ring? Why them?

Read

26 In the sixth month, the angel Gabriel was sent by God to a town in Galilee called Nazareth,

27 to a virgin engaged to a man named Joseph, of the house of David. The virgin’s name was Mary.

28 And the angel came to her and said, “Greetings, favored woman! The Lord is with you.”

29 But she was deeply troubled by this statement, wondering what kind of greeting this could be.

30 Then the angel told her, “Do not be afraid, Mary, for you have found favor with God.

31 Now listen: You will conceive and give birth to a son, and you will name him Jesus.

32 He will be great and will be called the Son of the Most High, and the Lord God will give him the throne of his father David.

33 He will reign over the house of Jacob forever, and his kingdom will have no end.”

34 Mary asked the angel, “How can this be, since I have not had sexual relations with a man?”

35 The angel replied to her, “The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you. Therefore, the holy one to be born will be called the Son of God.

36 And consider your relative Elizabeth — even she has conceived a son in her old age, and this is the sixth month for her who was called childless.

37 For nothing will be impossible with God.”

38 “See, I am the Lord’s servant,” said Mary. “May it happen to me as you have said.” Then the angel left her.

39 In those days Mary set out and hurried to a town in the hill country of Judah

40 where she entered Zechariah’s house and greeted Elizabeth.

41 When Elizabeth heard Mary’s greeting, the baby leaped inside her, and Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Spirit.

42 Then she exclaimed with a loud cry, “Blessed are you among women, and your child will be blessed!

43 How could this happen to me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?

44 For you see, when the sound of your greeting reached my ears, the baby leaped for joy inside me.

45 Blessed is she who has believed that the Lord would fulfill what he has spoken to her!”

Context

Luke sets two angel visits side by side. Six months earlier, Gabriel announced John the Baptist’s birth to the priest Zechariah in the temple in Jerusalem (Luke 1:5–25). Now he is sent to Nazareth — an obscure Galilean village never mentioned in the Old Testament — to Mary, a young woman engaged to Joseph. Engagement was legally binding, and a pregnancy before marriage could bring disgrace, or worse (Deuteronomy 22:23–24).

Discuss

  1. Verses 26–27: Luke tells us the time, the place and the person. What does each detail tell us? Why might it matter that God sent Gabriel to Nazareth rather than to the temple in Jerusalem (compare John 1:46)?
  2. Verses 28–30: What are the angel’s first words to Mary? The sermon noted they were not “You must obey” but “Greetings, favored woman!” Why does it matter that grace comes before our response — for Mary, and for us (1 John 4:19)?
  3. Verses 31–33: List everything the angel says about Jesus. How does this fulfil God’s promises in 2 Samuel 7:12–16 and Isaiah 9:6–7? What does the name “Jesus” — “Yahweh is salvation” — tell us about his mission?
  4. Verse 34 (compare 1:18): Mary and Zechariah both ask “how?” The sermon said Zechariah’s question came from “I do not believe”, Mary’s from “I do not understand”. What is the difference? How can we bring honest questions to God without sliding into unbelief?
  5. Verses 35–37: How does the angel answer Mary? What does “nothing will be impossible with God” mean — and what does it not mean? Notice that Mary receives a promise, not a full explanation of her future.
  6. Verse 38: What did Mary risk by saying yes — with Joseph, her family, her village? The sermon called her response “a commitment made after counting the cost”. Where is God asking you to trust his word when you can’t control the outcome — at work, in your family, with your health?
  7. Verses 39–45: Why did Mary hurry to Elizabeth? What could Elizabeth give Mary that no one else could? According to verse 45, what was Mary’s real blessing?
  8. The sermon said small groups, fellowships and spiritual friends are “not optional extras” but necessities for a living faith. In what ways is this group an “Elizabeth” to its members? What would help us be more honest about our weak spots, and quicker to strengthen each other’s faith?

Live It Out

KOG 3·2·1·0 — pray for 3, connect with 2, invite 1, 0 left unloved.

  • Pray for 3 — three people who think they are too ordinary, too far gone or “not the religious type” for God. Pray for them by name each day.
  • Connect with 2 — be an “Elizabeth” to two people facing something hard: a call, a visit, a meal. Listen before you advise.
  • Invite 1 — invite one of your three to church on Sunday, or to this home group.
  • 0 left unloved — at morning tea, look for the person standing on their own, especially someone new.

This week’s challenge: name one “impossibility” you have been carrying alone. Tell one person from this group, and pray about it together before Sunday.

Memory verse: “For nothing will be impossible with God.” (Luke 1:37, CSB)

Pray

  • Thank God that his grace comes first — that in Christ we are already favoured, before we have done anything.
  • Bring the “impossibilities” named tonight to God, and ask for Mary’s trust: “I am the Lord’s servant. May it happen to me as you have said.”
  • Pray by name for your three — that God’s kindness would lead them to repentance, and for an opportunity to point one of them to Jesus.

Leader’s Notes

Joyfully point to Jesus. Assume guests and not-yet-Christians are present: this passage is good news for anyone who feels ordinary or overlooked — God looks for willing vessels in “Nazareth”. Some members may be carrying private “impossibilities” (the sermon named a broken marriage, an addiction, a person they cannot forgive). Treat Question 6 and the challenge as invitations, never demands, and never corner anyone.

Two likely wrong turns. First, making Mary the hero: the sermon stressed she was chosen by grace, not merit, and Elizabeth’s “blessed are you” is praise for God’s grace, not worship of Mary. Second, turning verse 37 into “God will give me whatever I want”: Mary received a promise, not a guarantee of comfort — her yes led to risk and nine months of waiting.

Question helps

Q1 — “The sixth month” ties Mary’s story to Elizabeth’s: God’s work is seamless — John first, then Jesus. Nazareth had no temple, prophets or priests, was never mentioned in the Old Testament, and was looked down on (John 1:46). Mary was young, poor and without status. God chose the most impossible place and person — the logic of the gospel (1 Corinthians 1:27–28).

Q2 — “Favored” means she has already received grace, and it remains in effect. She was not chosen for being more devout or clever. Obedience is a response to grace, not a way to earn it: identity comes before performance.

Q3 — Great in God’s eyes; Son of the Most High; given David’s throne; reigning forever; a kingdom without end — not a political restoration of Israel but an everlasting kingdom. His name is his mission: he came to save.

Q4 — Zechariah’s doubt led to silence; Mary’s question led to deeper revelation. God deals with each of us personally. Honest questions are welcome; the issue is whether we ask in order to trust, or refuse to trust until we understand.

Q5 — The Spirit who brought life at creation (Genesis 1:2) and the power of the Most High: God himself will act. The holy child is the Son of God — fully God and fully human. Verse 37 is not a blank cheque for our wishes; it is the certainty that God can do all he has promised.

Q6 — Divorce by Joseph, stoning under the law (Deuteronomy 22:23–24), public shame. Her yes was not naive optimism. It points forward to Jesus in Gethsemane: “Not my will, but yours, be done” (Luke 22:42).

Q7 — Elizabeth was the one person who had also experienced an “impossible” pregnancy, and the angel had pointed Mary to her (v.36). Filled with the Spirit, she calls the unborn Jesus “my Lord”. Mary is blessed because she believed God’s word (v.45) — a blessing for every believer.

Q8 — The sermon contrasted an AI chat window late at night with a real “Elizabeth” who can hug, cry and rejoice with us. God often does his greatest work through small, devoted groups — two believers encouraging each other.

Our church vision

This passage is a picture of what a home group is for: two people saved by grace, sharing what God has done, strengthening each other’s faith and praising him together. That sits at the heart of our “from faith to faith” vision — growing towards 75 households serving densely at St Paul’s (we are at 50), on the way to 500 households within ten minutes of us by 2035. Home groups are the engine, which is why three new mid-week fellowships is a live 90-day goal: ask whether your group should multiply.
