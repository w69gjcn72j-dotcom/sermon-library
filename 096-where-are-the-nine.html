Where Are the Nine?

where are the nine?

Passage: Luke 17.11_19

Big Idea: Thankful worship of Jesus is a sign of saving faith

The blessed but thankless ones

One faithful foreigner was thankful and saved

Faith is seen through thankful worship

Many years ago, a Christian man by the name of William Stidger was talking to with other Christians bout being thankful.

And so, William thought hard about it and decided to write a letter to a teacher who taught him English, not just the language but English literature and poetry.

To his big surprise, the teacher wrote back.

This is what the teacher wrote: “My Dear Willy: I can’t tell you how much your note meant to me. I am in my eighties, living alone in a small room, cooking my own meals, lonely, and like the last leaf of autumn lingering behind.  You’ll be interested to know that I taught in school for more than fifty years, and yours is the first note of appreciation I ever received. It came on a blue, cold morning, and it cheered me as nothing has done in years.”

Aww, it's beautiful, isn’t it?

The English teacher must have taught hundreds, if not thousands of students over fifty years, helped many of them fall in love with literature and yet she only had one letter of thanks.

But that one letter of thanks made a profound difference to that English teacher that lonely morning.

When Willy found out, he found himself weeping over that note.

Thankfulness is rare in our world, but when you see it, when you experience it, it’s a beautiful thing.

But thankfulness is more than just a thing of beauty.

It’s a sign of something far deeper, far more profound.

In fact, we can put it another way: if we don’t have thankfulness, if we are thankless, then we are in grave danger.

Why is being thankless a danger?

That’s what we’re going to see in today’s passage.

And so, if you have your Bible, please open up to Luke 17, beginning from verse 11.

We’re going to see three things:

The blessed but thankless ones

One faithful foreigner was thankful and saved

Faith is seen through thankful worship

Before we do, let’s pray,

“Father, thank you for showing us the love you have for us in Jesus Christ. By your Spirit, open our ears, that we may hear what you have to say to each one of us today. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

The blessed but thankless ones

Jesus was on his way to Jerusalem when he was passing between Samaria and Galilee.

Look with me at what happens next in verse 12, “As he entered a village, ten men with leprosy met him. They stood at a distance 13 and raised their voices, saying, “Jesus, Master, have mercy on us!”

Leprosy is a horrible skin disease.

The common practice from ancient times was that if you had leprosy, you had to stay away from the rest of the people.

Somehow, these ten lepers had found each other and so they have each other for company.

And so, when they saw Jesus, they stood at a distance and called to Jesus in a loud voice, “Jesus, Master, have mercy on us!”

They begged Jesus for mercy.

When Jesus saw them, he told them to go to the priests and show themselves to them.

Usually, if you’re a leper, you would only go to the priests if you were healed and the priests were there to confirm that you’ve been healed.

But Jesus told them to go even though they still had leprosy.

As they were walking to the priests, all ten were miraculously healed.

Jesus has had mercy on them; he healed them all!

They could go back to their families! They could go back to their lives!

However, nine out of ten kept going and didn’t come back to thank Jesus.

Why do you think that is?

Talk with the person next to you.

Why didn’t the nine come back? You have thirty seconds.

Maybe some had to finish something very important in his life and didn’t have time to go back to Jesus.

Maybe some just thought that Jesus was doing what he was supposed to do, why should they thank Him?

Maybe some just simply didn’t feel like thanking Jesus.

Nine of them received mercy but they were thankless. They got what they wanted and then they move on from Jesus.

Now before we point the finger at them, we need to look at ourselves.

When we pray, we often ask God for things.

We pray God to help us pass an exam, we pray that God will give us good health, we pray for healing, we pray for a good family, we even pray for our Church to grow.

These are all great things to pray for.

But what happens when we God answered our prayer? What happened after God gave us what we asked for?

Like the nine, we often just move on.

Are we better than the ungrateful nine?

We often ask Jesus for things, but we seldom thank Jesus.

We might give the same excuses that we talked about earlier.

We got the blessings, but we’re too busy to return and thank God for them.

God gave us what we have prayed for, but we’ve got better more important things to do than to thank him.

We justify our thanklessness.

Though God has blessed us with so much, we don’t thank him for his blessings.

I’ve spoken with a minister who looked after many immigrants who come to Australia.

He would welcome them to his church and provide support for them.

He would write letters for them to help them get visas to stay in Australia.

But what happens after they got settled in Australia, what happens after they got their visa? They leave.

Like the nine lepers, they got the blessings and then they leave.

It’s a common experience for many Christian Churches.

It’s sad and it can hurt because these acts of love were not appreciated.

These people who asked for and received acts of love and mercy were unthankful; they were thankless.

Jesus himself experienced it firsthand.

The nine lepers were blessed but thankless.

One faithful foreigner was thankful and saved

But there was one out of the ten who came back.

He was a foreigner, a Samaritan.

Look with me at verse 15-16, “ But one of them, seeing that he was healed, returned and, with a loud voice, gave glory to God. 16 He fell facedown at his feet, thanking him. And he was a Samaritan.

This reminds me of what happened at the Oscars (Academy Awards) back in 1999.

On that night, the Italian Director Roberto Benigni received the oscar for his film, Life is Beautiful.

It’s a great film.

When the film was announced, Roberto was so excited, he got up on top of his chair and he cheered!

He couldn’t contain his excitement!

When the foreign Samaritan was healed, he couldn’t contain his excitement either.

He returned and he gave glory to God.

He fell facedown at Jesus’ feet.

He didn’t need to keep a social distance anymore because he was healed!

He came thanking Jesus!

He thanked Jesus by bowing facedown at his feet.

It’s an act of deep reverence usually meant for king or at the temple.

But here, he bowed before Jesus.

That’s often how worship is described in the Bible; it’s falling down before God in deep reverence and thankfulness.

What the Samaritan did was a beautiful act of thankfulness.

Look at Jesus’ response in verse 17-18, “Were not ten cleansed? Where are the nine? Didn’t any return to give glory to God except this foreigner?”

The word translated ‘Foreigner’ here is a strong word.

This is the only time it appears in the New Testament.

In Jesus’ time, it also appeared on the wall separating the Court of the Gentiles from the Jewish Sanctuary.

It read, ““No foreigner is permitted inside the partition and wall around the temple. Whoever is caught will have himself to blame for his ensuing death.”

It was a word that divided people.

In Jesus’ time, it was a derogatory term of a non-Jewish person, not fit to enter God’s temple.

Perhaps you can think of a derogatory term that people have used of other cultures.

Maybe people have used a derogatory term on you.

Or maybe you have used it on other people.

Do you have a derogatory term in mind?

Now, use that word in Jesus’ sentence.

“Didn’t any return to give glory to God except for this *insert your word*?”

It would’ve been shocking for people to hear.

It would’ve been offensive for the people to hear that this person, this foreigner, this *insert your word* returned and gave glory to God.

He wasn’t a person from respectable tribe; he wasn’t one of the Jewish people.

He was a foreigner.

But he did the right thing! He returned and gave glory to God.

And more than that, this person was saved!

Jesus said that his faith had saved him.

The other nine were cleansed; they were blessed.

But this foreigner was even more blessed; he was saved.

Now notice that there was no explicit mention of his faith prior to this.

The passage didn’t say that he had faith in God, or that he has faith in Jesus.

However, Jesus saw his faith, which was expressed through thankfulness.

3. Faith is seen through thankful worship

This foreigner returns because those with true faith returns to God in thankful worship.

Those without faith won’t return to God; they won’t be thankful to God.

They might thank themselves; they might say that they had good luck; they might thank their stars and they might even thank people.

But those who have true faith thank God because they know that God’s hand is behind all of these.

They know that God is in control.

And so they thank God for what he has done.

They return to God in thankful worship.

A while ago, I saw a video of a Church couple who were very thankful for their ministry.

They thanked their church, they thanked other Christians, they even thanked politicians.

But one obvious omission was God.

In their speech, God was not mentioned at all.

God was not thanked even as they serve at Chruch.

In contrast, the foreigner was thankful for what God’s done.

He believed that out of his mercy God healed him through Jesus.

And so, he glorified God and thanked Jesus.

And so, thankfulness to God and our faith in God goes hand in hand.

Thankfulness to God overflows from our faith in God.

Our worship of God is shown through our thankfulness to God.

But where were the other nine?

The nine were blessed but not saved.

They know of the goodness of God.

They have experienced God’s miraculous healing for themselves.

But there was no thankful worship, there was no returning to Jesus, there was no glorifying of God because there was no faith.

They were in great danger, far greater than living with the horrible disease of leprosy.

They’ve been thoroughly blessed by God and yet, they still refused to thank him for what he’s done.

Jesus said that the foreigner’s faith has saved him.

But the same could not be said of the other nine.

Their thanklessness is a sign that shows that they’re not saved.

They are blessed but they not saved.

They’re happy to receive the blessings from God but utterly foolish to be thankless and so miss out on the greatest blessing of all – the blessing of salvation.

The same question can be asked of us, can’t it?

Hasn’t God been good to us as a nation, to us as a community?

Why do so few of us return to thank him?

Hasn’t God been good to us and our family?

Why do so few come before him in thankful worship?

Jesus’ question remains as relevant today as it was back then:

Weren’t there ten cleansed? Where are the nine?

May we cry out to God for mercy, but not for the mercy to be healed, nor for the mercy to take our problems away.

May we cry out to God for mercy, not for a happy life, not even for a blessed family.

May God have mercy on us and save us, that we might be truly saved, and return to him with thankful worship.

May he save us from the danger of being blessed but not saved.

That’s the question we need to think about this week:

Have I been blessed but not saved? How do I know?

Those who have been truly saved will come before Jesus with thankful worship.

Thankful worship of Jesus is a sign of saving faith.

We will return to Jesus with a thankful heart; We will come bowing down before him with thankful worship.

And so, brothers and sisters, fall facedown at Jesus’ feet and thank him.

By default, we’re a thankless people.

What does being thankless look like?

A thankless person would come before God and think that he’s giving so much to God.

In fact, a thankless person will think that giving him too much.

They foolishly think that God should be thanking them for merely turning up, rather than being thankful that we can come in worship.

That’s what thanklessness look like.

That’s what the nine people who didn’t return to Jesus look like.

In contrast, those who are truly saved will come with a thankful worship.

A thankful person will worship God with everything they have.

Though they may not physically bow before God, they will come before God with the utmost respect.

In their hearts, they’ll fall facedown before God.

A thankful person will come to worship with joy, even when they have little time, even when their lives are filled with difficulties and pain.

They will come in thankful worship because of what God has done for them in Jesus.

They will constantly look over their life in faith and thank God for what He’s done in their lives.

In a little while, we’re going to do that as a Church as we thank God together for what he has done.

Even for those who are truly saved, thankfulness won’t necessarily come easily.

It’s something that we learn more of as we grow in Christ.

It won’t be easy, but we’ll learn to do give thanks to God together.

We come together to help one another come before Jesus in thankful worship.

So brothers and sisters, fall facedown at Jesus’ feet and thank him.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Have I been blessed but thankless?

Have I thanked God for the prayers that he’s answered recently?

How can I mutually encourage my brother or sister this week to come before God in Thankful Worship?

“Were not ten cleansed? Where are the nine?”

Friends, don’t be the thankless nine, join the foreigner and be a thankful one.

Fall facedown at Jesus’ feet and thank him.

Let’s pray,

“Father, thank you for the blessings you have given us in the Lord Jesus. Please forgive our stubborn, sinful thanklessness. By your Spirit, move us to Jesus so that we might believe in him and be saved. Make our hearts thankful, no matter what situation we have found ourselves in. Open our eyes to see your faithful, loving kindness so that we may come before you, run to you, return to you in thankful worship. Jesus, Master, have mercy on us! Amen.”
