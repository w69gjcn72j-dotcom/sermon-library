In March this year, a young Australian by the name of Natalie Gatz was in San Francisco.

She was 27 weeks’ pregnant with twin boys at the time.

However, when the pandemic struck, and Australians were called to come home, the Doctors told Natalie that it was too dangerous for her to travel.

And so, Natalie stayed and gave birth to the twin boys.

And she’s also told that she’ll possibly be stuck in San Francisco until March 2021.

Natalie finds it very difficult to stay in a strange land, with minimal support for her babies.

She said, “There's no light at the end of the tunnel. I don't know when we'll be able to come home.”

She’s homesick and she wants to bring her family home as soon as possible.

And she’s only one example.

There are thousands all around the world, stuck in a foreign land, not able to go home because of the travel restrictions.

They long for their journey home.

Throughout the Bible, Christians are often described as people who are on our journey home.

For Christians, our home is in heaven, where Jesus is.

Like Natalie, we don’t know when we’ll actually come home.

But unlike Natalie, we know the way home and we’re on our journey home.

What does this journey involve?

How do we travel on this journey?

In today’s passage, we’ll see how the Apostle Paul describes his journey home. We’ll see that we are to run to Christ,

we run by forgetting and pursuing,

we run by maturing and imitating the mature and

we run home to the risen Christ

Before we look into the Bible, let’s pray, “Father, open our eyes that we might see what it means for us to make our journey home to you. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”

Paul runs to Christ

Look with me at verse 12, “Not that I have already reached the goal or am already perfect, but I make every effort to take hold of it because I also have been taken hold of by Christ Jesus.”

Just a few sentences earlier, Paul said that his goal is to “know him (that is Jesus Christ) and the power of his resurrection and the fellowship of his sufferings, being conformed to his death,”

That’s Paul’s goal in life, to know Christ.

But Paul hasn’t reached his goal yet; he’s not there yet.

He’s still on his way running towards the goal of knowing Jesus.

To be sure, he knows Jesus, but he hasn’t reached the goal of knowing Jesus perfectly yet.

Paul’s like an eagle in full flight, flying through the air, on his way back to his nest.

He’s not there yet, but on his way.

As we’ve seen throughout Paul’s life, following Jesus is not easy.

Paul’s in prison while he’s writing this letter.

What keeps him running towards Christ? What keeps him going even when things are tough?

Paul keeps going, keeps striving for the goal of knowing Christ because Christ has taken a hold of him.

Jesus has come for Paul; Jesus has suffered for Paul; Jesus has died for Paul.

More than that, Jesus has risen to show that Jesus is who he’s said he is, that is, that Jesus is indeed the Son of God.

Jesus has come to take hold of Paul, to save him.

And now Paul runs towards Jesus, to take hold of Jesus.

Jesus knows Paul fully and loves Paul fully.

Paul now wants to know Jesus fully and love Jesus fully.

He’s not there yet but he’s well on his way.

How does he do it?

Paul runs to Jesus by forgetting and pursuing.

Running by forgetting and pursuing

Look with me at verse 13, “Brothers and sisters, I do not consider myself to have taken hold of it. But one thing I do: Forgetting what is behind and reaching forward to what is ahead,”

Paul doesn’t know Jesus fully yet, but he knows one thing: forgetting what is behind and reaching, straining forward to what is ahead.

Paul’s here not telling us to forget the bad things.

For example, for many people, 2020 has been a hard year.

Many of us may be looking forward to leaving 2020 behind as we prepare for the new year (only 12 weeks away!)

Paul’s saying, yes, leave years like 2020 behind, and 2019, 2000, 1990.

In fact, leave all the years behind, whether they are good or bad, whether they are shameful of glorious.

Leave the past behind.

I know many older Christians, who look back to their glory days.

They look back to a time when the Churches are filled with people.

They look back to a time when they have hundreds of children attending Sunday School.

They look back with satisfaction at their glory days.

We’re to learn from these days but we’re not to live in them.

They’ve been and gone.

Now’s the time to keep going; now’s the time to keep running.

As good as the past might have been, don’t let it be your spiritual peak.

Don’t look back to your youth or to your glory days and say that that’s as good as your spiritual life is going to get.

Keep running!

You haven’t reached your absolute peak yet!

Physically, some of us might have reached our peak.

I know that I can’t run as fast as I could.

In fact, I know that I get tired a lot more easily now than when I was younger.

Physically I might have reached my peak.

But not spiritually.

Spiritually, as long as we’re on this side of heaven, as long as we’re on this side of death, we won’t be reaching our spiritual peak.

It doesn’t matter whether you’re a young Christian, or a Christian for over 50 years, we can still grow spiritually.

We can still come to know Jesus more fully.

We can still love Jesus more truly.

Look with me at verse 14, “I pursue as my goal the prize promised by God’s heavenly call in Christ Jesus.”

Paul pursues his goal.

He doesn’t follow Jesus leisurely, relaxingly, lazily only on one day of the week; he pursues the goal of knowing Jesus.

He’s like a cheetah, going at top speed, running towards his goal.

That’s God’s calling for us, he calls us from heaven in Christ Jesus to run towards the goal of knowing Jesus.

God calls us to pursue the goal of knowing Jesus.

And so let’s keep running towards heaven, towards God’s home, towards Jesus.

As well as forgetting what is behind and pursuing Jesus, we run to Jesus by maturing and imitating the mature.

Running by maturing and imitating the mature

Look with me at verse 15, “Therefore, let all of us who are mature think this way. And if you think differently about anything, God will reveal this also to you.”

Those who are spiritually mature ought to think like Paul, that is, that they still need to grow in Christ.

The spiritually mature will want to keep growing in Christ.

Those who are spiritually immature tend to think the opposite; they tend to think that they know everything there is to know about Christ.

Over time, they grow lazy with reading the Bible, half-hearted in serving Jesus and eventually grow cold as a Christian.

A mature Christian, on the other hand, recognises his own weakness and his own ignorance of how great Jesus is.

And so, he will have a desire to grow in his knowledge of Jesus.

Rather than being lazy, he will be diligent in reading the Bible, serve Jesus wholeheartedly and grow hot as a Christian.

That’s how the mature Christian would think.

Paul says that if you don’t get that, may God reveal this to you.

But even if you’re growing very slowly spiritually, Paul says, “we should live up to whatever truth we have attained.”

In other words, don’t be discouraged if you are not growing as quickly as you hope.

Be faithful with what you already know.

Live up to whatever you’ve already attained.

Don’t lose whatever you have as a Christian up to this point.

Don’t lost the growth that you’ve already got.

To use the running illustration again, if you feel you can’t run any faster, at least keep your pace.

Don’t stop running, don’t go backwards.

At the very least, keep running at your pace.

If you’ve been coming to Church, at the very least keep coming to Church.

If you’ve been praying at least once a day, don’t be discouraged if you don’t pray more.

At the very least keep praying at least once a day.

The hope is that one day soon, you can start picking up your pace again, and to keep growing as a Christian.

Every Christian ought to have a desire for growth; every Christian ought to grow.

Take a look back in your life over the past year. Have you grown as a Christian?

If you haven’t, then something is wrong.

For those of us who have been Christian for twenty years or more, take a look back.

How have we grown in the past twenty years?

There are people who became a Christians twenty years ago and have twenty years of Christian experience.

There are others who have become a Christian twenty years ago and have had the experience of being a young Christian twenty times.

They might have been a Christian for twenty years but they have not grown at all.

What about us? How have we grown as Christians?

The Bible teacher Don Carson puts it like this:

As we grow…

“Our sins become less and less excusable.

Holiness becomes more and more attractive.

The glories of the world to come make all the glories of this world fade into full greys by way of comparison.”

Is that true of us? Has our views of our sin, our holiness, our world changed as we grow?

As well as reviewing our own personal growth, Paul tells us to look at good, Christian examples.

Look with me at verse 17, “Join in imitating me, brothers and sisters, and pay careful attention to those who live according to the example you have in us.”

Paul tells the Philippians to imitate him and observe others who have followed his footsteps.

For us here in our Church, we’re to imitate the examples of others who are more mature in their faith.

To grow as a Christian takes more than just head knowledge.

An essential part of Christian growth is to observe and to imitate the life of those who are more mature in the faith than we are.

The Christian way of life is often more caught than taught.

When we see examples of faithful Christians living out their lives, we will often catch the way they live and follow their examples.

This is often more effective than for the same person teaching the same thing to the less mature Christian.

For example, new Christians don’t often pray in public because it’s not something that they’ve done before.

But they will often listen to how Christians pray in public.

And when the opportunity for them to pray, they often use the very same words that they’ve heard in other people’s prayer.

That’s how many of us learn to pray.

We learn by listening.

As Christians share our lives together, those who are younger in their faith ought to observe the more mature Christians to see how they approach different situations in life.

They’ll see the how a mature Christian husband loves his wife and how a mature Christian wife responds to her husband.

They’ll see how a mature Christian man makes decision about his time and money.

They’ll see how a mature Christian woman makes a decision to trust God despite her difficulties.

Many of these lessons are powerfully learnt when we share our lives in the community of Christians.

That’s why it’s so important for us to keep engaging with one another here at Church on Sundays as well as in our pastoral networks throughout the week.

We want the younger Christians to observe more mature Christians so that they can grow by imitating godly examples of Christians.

Having godly examples to imitate can help us grow in Christ.

But having bad examples to imitate can be detrimental for our growth in Christ.

Verse 18, “or I have often told you, and now say again with tears, that many live as enemies of the cross of Christ. 19 Their end is destruction; their god is their stomach; their glory is in their shame; and they are focused on earthly things.”

There are people who live as enemies of the cross.

These enemies of the cross that Paul refers to are not necessarily explicitly anti-Christians, like some atheists.

In the context of the chapter, where he’s talked about bad teachers who taught that the Philippians must become a Jew to be saved and good teachers should be imitated,

It looks like Paul is talking once again about bad teachers.

Paul’s very concerned that the Philippians might follow the steps of bad teachers.

Why are these teachers bad?

It’s because they want to teach about Jesus without following Jesus themselves.

They want to teach young Christians about the Christian faith but they do so without teaching the heart of the Christian faith.

At the very heart of the Christian faith is the cross of Jesus and his resurrection.

Paul’s goal is to know Jesus and the power of his resurrection and the fellowship of his suffering.

Being a Christian will mean sharing in the suffering of Jesus.

Jesus himself has said, “"If anyone wants to follow after me, let him deny himself, take up his cross, and follow me.”

But these bad teachers don’t see the cross of Jesus at the heart of Christianity.

They diminish the importance of the cross of Christ and so they’ve become enemies of the cross of Christ.

Instead of the cross, what do these bad teachers have as priority?

Verse 19, “Their end is destruction; their god is their stomach; their glory is in their shame; and they are focused on earthly things.”

They want to serve their stomach, their wants, above all other things.

What they think is glorious is actually shameful in God’s eyes.

Their eyes are set on earthly things, things that will one day be destroyed.

And so, their end is destruction.

But that’s not the case for true Christians.

For true Christians, our home is heaven and we’re to run home to the Risen Christ.

Run home to the risen Christ

Look with me at verses 20-21, “20 Our citizenship is in heaven, and we eagerly wait for a Saviour from there, the Lord Jesus Christ. 21 He will transform the body of our humble condition into the likeness of his glorious body, by the power that enables him to subject everything to himself.”

We long of Jesus to return from heaven.

That’s where our citizenship is.

If you’re a Christian you belong to heaven, you are citizens in heaven, your home is in heaven.

Heaven is where you long for.

If you’re a Christian, you’ll be homesick for heaven.

You’ll long for the day when Jesus, whom you’re running after, comes back and takes you home.

On that day, Jesus will give every Christian a new body, like his own glorious body.

This body of ours, which has a used-by date, will be given a new body which had no used-by date.

That’s what followers of Jesus can look forward to.

That’s what followers of Jesus are running after: we run towards Jesus as he comes towards us from heaven.

What does this running look like here at Church?

Firstly, run to beat your own personal records

Think about how you can grow personally as a Christian at home.

You might be reading your Bible just once a week.

When was the last time you’ve improved on that record?

How can we beat our record of reading the Bible once a week?

Reading the Bible is vital to a Christian’s growth.

Follow a Bible reading plan (we have some at the back), Read the Bible with the help of devotionals (we have these at the back too), use a Bible reading app.

Or if you’re old-school like me, write in a Bible journal.

I love using a Bible journal because it helps me slow down and think more deeply about God’s word.

Choose a method, whatever it is, and stick to a goal.

Once you’ve reached it, aim to beat that goal the next time.

That’s how we grow.

Second, run with a cross-focussed coach

I’m really thankful that we have faithful teachers at our Church point us to Jesus.

Looking back in my life, I’m thankful for all the faithful, cross-focussed teachers I have in my life.

And they’re more than teachers, they’re more like coaches.

They didn’t just teach me head knowledge but they also shared their lives with me, showing me how to live as a Christian by their example.

How about you? Who’s your coach?

A good place to start are the Bible studies.

At the moment, we have three Bible studies across the Church.

If you are not going to one yet, make it your goal to join them!

If you can’t come physically, join by Zoom.

Make it your priority!

And when you go, don’t just go there for the head knowledge, as important as it is.

Learn from the lives of your leaders.

A lot of what we learn about the Christian life is caught rather than taught.

Join the activities in your pastoral network; share your lives whenever you can.

Observe how the more mature Christians are trusting Jesus with their decisions.

Imitate them.

Finally, run with heaven in your heart.

Someone has once said, “Of this blessed man let this praise be given;

Heaven was in him before he was in heaven.”

If you’re a Christian, you’ll have heaven in your heart.

That is heaven is what you long for; heaven is where you’re running to.

In a world of chaos and hardships, remember that as a follower of Jesus, you belong to heaven, where Jesus is.

If all that you have is in this world, then seeing the world as it is today, it can be distressing, even depressing.

But if you’re a Christian, you can rejoice, even though you’re looking at the same distressing, depressing world.

You can rejoice because you know that this world is not ultimately your home.

Your home is in heaven.

As you journey through life, as difficult as it is, it is the journey home.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What are your goals for your spiritual growth?

Who can coach you for further spiritual growth?

Do you run with heaven in your heart?

Friends, no matter where we are on our spiritual journey, let us all run with joy, as we run home to Christ,

Let’s pray,

“Father, thank you for the hope that we have in Christ. We thank you that Jesus has come, that he suffered for us, that he’s died for us, that he’s risen for us. We thank you that he’s coming again. We thank you that he’s taken a hold of us. Father, please help us run to take a hold of him. Let us forget what’s behind and run towards Jesus. Please provide good Christian examples who can show us what it means to run faithfully, in both words and deeds. And please, by your Spirit, help us to run faithfully till see you face to face in heaven. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”
