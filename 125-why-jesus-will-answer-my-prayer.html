Why Jesus will answer my prayer

Passage: John 14:1-18

Big idea: Since Jesus (the Way) has gone to the Father’s House, ask Jesus for the Spirit of Truth, through whom we do even greater works for the Father’s glory.

Jesus is the Way

Jesus has gone away (and why that’s good)

Jesus’ Gift is with His disciples always

My late godfather was a wonderful music teacher.

He was funny but he was also very strict.

My godmother once told a story about my godfather greeting one of his young students in the morning.

“Good morning, little friend! (that’s how we greet children in Hong Kong)”.

The little friend ignored him.

“Good morning, little friend!” Again, the little friend ignored him.

Then my godfather, went up to him, got down to his level, and looked straight into his eyes, “I said, good morning, little friend”

“Good morning Lee Sir! (that’s how we greet teachers in Hong Kong)”

From then on, the little friend greets my godfather every morning without prompting!

Let’s try that at morning tea! Greet as many people as you can!

Children, greet as many adults as you can. There’s a prize for the one who greets the most adults!

When we greet somebody, it’s good to be greeted back, isn’t it?

That’s how communication works.

We say something to one person, the other person responds.

We ask a question, the other person answers.

In today’s passage, Jesus says something extraordinary about our communication with him.

Did you hear it? Look with me at verse 13:

Jesus says, “If you ask me anything in my name, I will do it.”

That is, if we ask Jesus anything in prayer, prayed in his name, He will respond by doing it.

What! Anything?

That’s a massive promise.

Will Jesus really be able to do anything that I ask for? How sure can we be about Jesus answering our prayer?

Can we be that sure?

Open your Bible with me to John 14, beginning from verse 1.

We’ll see why Jesus we can be sure that he will do what we ask for because of three things:

Jesus is the Way

Jesus has gone away (and why that’s good)

Jesus’ Gift is with His disciples always

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Father, please fill our hearts with your Holy Spirit, that we might know Jesus and see him even more clearly, in Jesus’ name, amen.”

Jesus is the Way

The passage is set on the night of Jesus’ betrayal.

They were having a meal together and Jesus told them that one of them would betray him.

In time, we would find out that it was Judas Iscariot.

He also said that Peter would deny him not once, but three times.

And Jesus told them that he was about to go, and where he was going, they couldn’t follow.

Jesus was about to leave them.

They were sad and confused; their time with Jesus is about to come to an end.

Over the past two weeks, Zoe was in Kogarah primary as a learner teacher.

She got on very well with the students!

On the last day, a little boy was crying by himself in the corner.

Why? Because he didn’t want Zoe to go.

He was sad to see Zoe go, even though she was only there for two weeks!

Jesus’ disciples didn’t want to see Jesus go.

But Jesus comforted them by saying to them, in verse 1, “Don’t let your heart be troubled. Believe in God; believe also in me.”

What were they to believe about God and Jesus?

They were to believe what Jesus was about to do for them, starting from verse 2, “In my Father’s house are many rooms. If it were not so, would I have told you that I am going to prepare a place for you? 3 If I go away and prepare a place for you, I will come again and take you to myself, so that where I am you may be also.”

Jesus was about to go to the cross, where he will die.

However, in three days, Jesus would rise from the dead, then he would ascend back into heaven to prepare a place for them.

He will come back one day to take those who belong to him back to heaven with him.

Yes, their physical time together was about to come to an end, but it’s only temporary.

When Jesus returns, he will take those who belong to him to be with him forever.

Thomas didn’t know what Jesus was talking about and so he asked, “Lord, we don’t know where you’re going. How can we know the way?”

How does someone like Thomas who doesn’t know the way go to where Jesus will be?

Didn’t Jesus himself say earlier that his disciples wouldn’t be able to come?

How would Thomas find his way to Jesus?

Listen to Jesus’ response in verse 6, “I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me.”

Jesus had said five other I am’s before this in the Gospel of John.

Let’s see if you remember them. Jesus says, I am:

1)The Bread of Life 2) The light of the world 3) the Door, 4) the Good Shepherd, 5) The Resurrection and the Life.

And now Jesus says that He’s the Way, the Truth and the Life.

Jesus is the Way.

Jesus wasn’t just paving the way ahead and we simply follow his lead.

Jesus is the Way, the only One with access to heaven.

He is the Only Way.

He is the Way because he’s also the Life and the Truth.

Jesus is the Truth; Jesus truly reveals who God is.

Jesus is the Life; Jesus is the Resurrection and the Life.

He is the Life that never fades away; His is the Life that never dies.

When you know Jesus the Life and you believe in Him, Jesus the Truth, then you will walk in Jesus, the Way.

It is through Jesus the Way that you come to know God.

How do you know whether you truly know God?

It will depend on whether you know Jesus.

If you don’t know Jesus, then any concept you have of God cannot be true;

Your definition of God is one that you simply imagined.

Some people imagine who their god must be like and define him according to their own knowledge and imagination.

Others imagine what other people’s gods are like and then dismiss them according to their own knowledge and imagination.

However, you will only know God truly, if you know Jesus.

Because Jesus is the Way.

When I was small, I learnt a song that helped me remember this verse for the past forty years.

It’s still being sung in many places around the world today.

I am the Way, the Truth and the Life.

You might know it as well.

If you know it, then you know the second part of the song: without the way, there is no going, without the truth, there is no knowing, without the life, there is no living.

I found out this week that this song was taken from a paragraph from the meditation of Thomas à Kempis.

Here’s the full paragraph of what à Kempis thought about this verse:

Follow thou me. I am the way and the truth and the life. Without the way there is no going; without the truth there is no knowing; without the life there is no living. I am the way which thou must follow; the truth which thou must believe; the life for which thou must hope. I am the inviolable way; the infallible truth, the never-ending life. I am the straightest way; the sovereign truth; life true, life blessed, life uncreated.

For Jesus to be the Way is to identify our way of life completely with Jesus.

He is the Way, He is our Identity, He is our Life, He is our Truth.

He is our Purpose, our reason for living.

Don’t let the world tell you who God is.

Don’t let your imagination or your experience tell you who God is.

Let Jesus tell you who God is.

If you know Jesus, you will also know God.

And so believe God, believe also in Jesus.

2. Jesus has gone away (and why that’s good)

But there’s a problem: The Way is going away!

And so, another disciple, Philip, asked Jesus, “Lord show us the Father, and that’s enough for us!”

Before Jesus goes away, Philip asked Jesus to give them a glimpse of God.

If Philip and the disciples saw God the Father, then it would be enough.

According to Philip, seeing God would be the ultimate experience; nothing would top it.

But Philip just didn’t get it, did he?

Jesus had just said, if they knew Jesus, they knew the Father.

If they had seen Jesus, they had seen the Father.

If they had heard Jesus, they had heard the Father.

Jesus lives in the Father and the Father lives in the Son.

“Show us the Father”? Philip, you’ve already seen the Father, because you have seen the Son.

If Philip needed more proof, just look at the works Jesus did.

Jesus turned water into wine, healed the sick and raised the dead.

Jesus tells Philip, “Believe because of the works themselves”

Jesus’ work shows Philip that he is from God.

But Jesus then tells Philip and the disciples something extraordinary, in verse 12, “Truly I tell you, the one who believes in me will also do the works that I do. And he will do even greater works than these, because I am going to the Father.”

Jesus says that the one who believes in Him will do even greater works than the ones Jesus did!

What! How can it be?

Do we have believers of Jesus here? Has anyone turned water into wine lately?

Two Mondays ago, the Monday men’s group had a dinner and I thought I’ll bring something along.

Since I didn’t want to bring alcohol to the dinner, I bought some non-alcoholic wine.

I gave all the men a sample and almost every man’s look was like this:

It started with a curious look, and then a disappointed look that tells you that what they drank was nothing like wine.

I couldn’t even convince what looked like wine, into something that kind of resembled wine.

It proves that I can’t do miracles; I can’t turn water, or even grape juice, into wine!

What does Jesus mean when He says that those who believe in him will do even greater works than these?

These greater works can include spectacular miracles, but it means something much more.

Those who believe in Jesus can do these greater works, not because they’re greater than Jesus.

Can you see the reason in verse 12?

“Truly I tell you, the one who believes in me will also do the works that I do. And he will do even greater works than these, because I am going to the Father”

Believers of Jesus can do these greater works because Jesus has gone to the Father.

The death and resurrection of Jesus is utterly unique to Jesus.

No one can die for the sins of the world and rise from the dead like Jesus.

Jesus’ death and resurrection is his greatest work, and nothing we can do will be greater than what Jesus has done.

However, when we do the works of the Lord Jesus, when we serve, when we tell the gospel,

and they are converted, something great has happened!

Those who believe Jesus have believe the truth about God in Jesus, they are receiving the eternal life found only in Jesus, and they are walking in the Way of Jesus, the only Way that leads to God.

That’s the greater work.

These works are greater than the works of Jesus in his earthly ministry before the cross

because they glorify Jesus and God the Father in a way that wasn’t possible before the death and resurrection of Jesus.

Yes, people had a better time at he wedding because they had more wine and their stomachs were filled when they ate the fish and the bread,

But they didn’t believe that Jesus died for their sins.

They couldn’t believe because Jesus hasn’t died at the cross yet!

They saw and believed that the miracles of Jesus happened, but they didn’t understand the greatest work of Jesus wasn’t any of these.

The greatest work of Jesus was his death on the cross and his resurrection from the grave.

When ordinary people hear the gospel and come to believe in Jesus after the death and resurrection of Jesus, and after his ascension into heaven

They will receive something far greater than a miraculous wine or a supernatural meal.

They will have a restored relationship with God and they will have eternal life, which is better and greater by far.

And so, the works of believers will be greater because they point to the Father’s glory and the Son’s glory on the last day.

So often, we get our definition of greatness all messed up.

On one extreme, we think that something is great when it’s big and spectacular.

We think something is great when it looks professional and done by young and beautiful people.

That’s how the world defines greatness.

But true greatness lies in whether something glorifies God.

What brings glory to God?

Conversions glorify God, discipleship glorifies God.

When people come to know Jesus, when they are willing to give sacrificially, choosing the cross rather than the world, that glorifies God.

These are the greater works that believers of Jesus would seek to do.

On the other extreme, we avoid growing big.

We make Christianity small, private and unprofessional.

We think that small churches, even house churches, are the only genuine form of Church.

The problem with that is that you don’t necessarily glorify God just by being small.

When you’re small, you have far less opportunities to reach out to others, to love other people, to tell them about Jesus.

The danger of small churches only wanting to staying small is that we want to be comfortable rather than a Church that does the greater things that Jesus said his followers would do.

What should we do then?

Seek greater things, ask Jesus for greater things, do the greater things, not for yourself, but for God’s glory!

How will you know you’re doing it for God’s glory?

When you do it for God’s glory, people won’t talk about you, about how good you are.

Instead, they will talk about how good Jesus is.

Be a Church that lives for God’s glory!

3. Jesus’ Gift is with His disciples always

Where do we start?

We start by asking Jesus

Verse 14, “If you ask me anything in my name, I will do it.”

Jesus’ greatest work was finished on the cross.

Jesus Died and rose from the dead; He’s now exalted and in heaven.

But he continues to do his work through us.

Did Jesus say, “If you ask me anything in my name, I will grant it?”

No, he says, “If you ask me anything in my name, I will do it”

We do the greater works, but only because Jesus is the One who does it through us.

That’s why it’s good that Jesus has gone away.

It’s good that Jesus has gone away because as the exalted Christ he can do things that wasn’t possible while he was in his earthly ministry before the cross.

And so, believers can pray to him, and he will do what we ask for.

What do we as believers ask for?

As his disciples, we will ask to keep his commands.

We ask Jesus to make us into a people who follow his commands because we love him.

In answer to our prayer, Jesus himself will ask God to give us what we need the most.

In fact, Not what we need the most, but who we need the most.

This Person will enable us to love Jesus; He’s also the Person by whom we do the greater things.

Who is he? Look with me at verses 16-17,

“And I will ask the Father, and he will give you another Counselor to be with you forever. 17 He is the Spirit of truth. The world is unable to receive him because it doesn’t see him or know him. But you do know him, because he remains with you and will be in you.”

The Holy Spirit is who we need the most as we seek to love and follow Jesus.

It’s the Holy Spirit who will make the greater works happen in and around us.

When people come to know and believe Jesus, it’s not because we’re great or clever.

It’s only by the Holy Spirit that anyone, us included, can come to know, love and follow Jesus.

Last week, I heard the story about a recently divorced man who was walking on a treadmill in the gym when he realized that that was what his life was like.

He was doing many things but not going anywhere.

And so, he called his Christian friend and told him that he wanted to read the Bible with him.

The Christian friend didn’t do anything special.

It was all the Spirit’s work; it’s the Holy Spirit who works in us and around us to do the greater works that Jesus has said we would do.

The Holy Spirit is Jesus’ answer to our prayers.

The Holy Spirit is the Spirit of Truth.

He is Jesus’ Spirit and He is the Father’s Spirit.

The Holy Spirit is the One who will be with us forever.

By His Spirit, Jesus does the greater things through us.

By His Spirit, Jesus leads us.

The Holy Spirit is another Counsellor, after Jesus, that the Father gives us to tell us where to go, and how we are to proceed.

The world doesn’t know the Holy Spirit, but if you know Jesus then you will know the Holy Spirit as well.

We know the Father because of Jesus.

We know the Holy Spirit because Jesus has asked the Father to give the Holy Spirit to us.

It’s by the Holy Spirit, that we do the greater things which brings glory to the Father and the Son.

One of the greatest periods of conversions in Australia happened in 1959.

Thousands of people were coming to believe in Jesus, many of whom only knew about Jesus at Church but didn’t know Jesus personally until 1959, at Billy Graham’s Crusade.

However, it wasn’t just an event that happened in 1959.

People had been praying for Australia for ten years before that.

At the Holy Spirit’s timing, tens of thousands of people believed in Jesus.

By the Holy Spirit, believers in that time have done greater things which brought much glory to Jesus.

If you are a believer in Jesus, the Holy Spirit is the Gift that Jesus has asked God the Father to give you.

Ask the Holy Spirit for strength and courage to follow Jesus.

Ask the Holy Spirit for the fire to love Jesus and his people.

The Holy Spirit is the Gift that will be with Jesus’ followers always.

How sure are you about God answering your prayer?

If you don’t believe in Jesus, if you don’t believe he is the only Way to God, then you won’t be sure when you pray.

You can’t be sure because you don’t believe Jesus and you don’t believe God.

Pray to God for the faith to trust in Him, to believe in Him.

This is the first and most important prayer for you right now.

Come and see me or Hien or one of the leaders to find out more about how to pray to Jesus.

If you believe that Jesus is the Way, the truth and the Life,

if you love him and keep his commands, then go ahead, ask Jesus anything in his name, and he will give it to you.

As someone who loves Jesus, you will ask him for what glorifies him and the Father.

And Jesus will respond by giving you the Holy Spirit.

The Holy Spirit is the first gift He gives you as a believer in Jesus.

It’s by the Holy Spirit that we can come to know the Truth about Jesus, that we can receive the eternal life that’s only found in Jesus, that we can walk in the Way of Jesus.

It’s by Him that you will do the greater things that you are asking Jesus for.

Ask for greater things for God’s glory.

Jesus himself will do what you ask for, for Jesus longs to see God glorified.

You can be sure of it!

And so, “Go ahead, ask Jesus! He will do it by His Spirit”

Today is Pentecost Sunday, when we remember the Holy Spirit coming on the Church fifty days after the Easter.

And the first thing the Church did once they received the Holy Spirit?

They preached Jesus!

Ask for the greater things, not for yourselves, but for Jesus’ glory.

What great things will you ask Jesus for?

For a long time, we’ve been asking Jesus to grow our Church.

We want to see one hundred families coming together serving him.

Is it too big of a ask of Jesus? Absolutely not!

I spoke with a friend on Friday who challenged me to think much bigger.

Not just one hundred, not just two hundred, but more, more than what this building can hold.

But rather than just being big, what me and my friend pray for was for the conversion of people through our ministries here.

We’ve had some people among us who came to know Jesus over the past few years.

Wouldn’t it be great to have more?

Let’s ask God to save more people from sin through what we do here.

Would three this year be too much to ask for? How about five people?

How about ten?

Friends, let’s ask Jesus for the greater things!

What greater things do you want Jesus to do through you?

Believe him, he can and will do it, when it’s asked in his name, according to his Way!

Go ahead, ask Jesus! He will do it by His Spirit

Here are the three questions for this week:

Do I believe that Jesus is the Way, the Truth and the Life?

Do I pray for the great things that reflect on what I believe about Jesus?

Do I depend on the Holy Spirit for whatever it takes to love and follow Jesus?

Go ahead, ask Jesus! He will do it by His Spirit

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you for giving us your Son, our Lord Jesus Christ. He is the Way, the Truth and the Life, by Whom we come into your presence. Father, thank you for showing us who you are through Jesus. Father, thank you for giving us your Holy Spirit. Thank you that He is our Counsellor and that He’s always with us. We pray that you will work great works through our little Church, that those dead in sin may be made alive in Christ, that those timid in nature will rise up to the challenge, that those proud in heart will humble themselves before you. We pray that you, along with the Son and the Holy Spirit, will be glorified through the great works that you will do through us. For we pray in the mighty name of Jesus, amen.”
