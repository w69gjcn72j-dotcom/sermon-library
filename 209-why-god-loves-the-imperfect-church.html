Why God loves the imperfect Church (and you should too)

Ephesians 4:1-16

Be worthy of your God-given calling by striving for unity in love, using the diversity of Christ’s gifts to build up His Church and maturing to Christ’s fullness by speaking the truth in love.

Four goals: discipleship centre, 100 families serving at Church, Masterplan, intergenerational ministry,

Be worthy by striving for unity in love

Be worthy by using the diversity of Christ’s gifts

Be worthy by speaking the truth in love

Good morning, my name is David Yung, the minister here at St Paul’s Kogarah.

It’s good to see you all!

As some of you know, my family and I will be going away for long service leave soon, in seven days!

One of the things I like to do while on leave is to visit other churches.

We don’t go every Sunday, but we do go every now and then, to visit and to encourage other brothers and sisters in Christ.

Sometimes it’ll be a massive Church like this one here in Seoul.

Other times, they’ll be smaller ones, like this one in Oxford.

There are always very impressive things to learn from the big churches, but I’m always very encouraged by the small and faithful churches.

While I visit other churches during the holidays, other people visit other Churches on a regular basis.

They visit Churches to find which is the best one for them.

They might be looking for good friendships, or good music, or even good sermons.

They might have an evaluation form in their mind and give the Church a score.

In their hearts, they’re looking for a perfect Church, or a church that’s as perfect as possible, to meet their needs.

However, as we’ll see in today’s passage, these people miss out on seeing the importance of an imperfect Church.

In fact, we’ll see why going to an imperfect Church is vital, if you want to live a life worthy of your calling.

That is, if God has called you to be a Christian, you must go to an imperfect Church, not just occasionally but regularly, which in most cases should be weekly.

Why is going to an imperfect Church is so vital to someone who wants to live a life worthy of the calling of a Christian?

Please open your Bible to Ephesians 4, beginning from verse 1.

We’ll see three things in today’s passage:

Be worthy by striving for unity in love

Be worthy by using the diversity of Christ’s gifts

Be worthy by speaking the truth in love

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Father, thank you for giving us your word, the Bible. Please open the eyes of our hearts that we might see the importance of the imperfect church in all the lives of all who call themselves Christians. For Jesus’ sake, Amen”

Be worthy by striving for unity in love

For the first three chapters in Ephesians, the Apostle Paul has been talking about how good the gospel of Jesus is.

He tells us that in Jesus, God has chosen those who would be his, even before the foundations of the world.

That God gives us his Holy Spirit as his guarantee, and that in Jesus, we have the hope of glory.

In Jesus we have the hope that after our physical death, we will one day be raised and be with him forever.

We were not worthy of what God has done for us.

We were dead in our sins, following the ways of the world, living in outright rebellion against God.

And yet, though we were dead in our sins, God has rescued us in Christ.

It is by God’s grace that we have been saved, through faith.

To demonstrate that this has really happened in us, Paul points to the Church.

The Church is the place where former enemies, the Jews and the Gentiles, the non-Jews, come together.

Paul tells us to see the wisdom of God in the Church.

He says that God points to the Church as his jewel, his most precious diamond.

If you want to see what God’s wisdom looks like, look at the Church!

It’s not because we’re wise ourselves, but our very existence shows that God is immeasurably wise.

It’s beyond our comprehension! And so Paul prays that we’ll understand this wisdom from God, as shown in Christ’s love that surpasses knowledge.

Paul prays that we’ll get it, that we’ll understand it.

What will it look it if we do get understand God’s love for us?

Look with me at 4:1, “Therefore I, the prisoner in the Lord, urge you to walk worthy of the calling you have received,”

We will live a life worthy of the calling we’ve received, if we have understood Christ’s love for us.

We’re not worthy of what God’s given us, but now that we’ve received it, we’re called to live a life worthy of the calling we’ve received.

It’s a little like this.

I only found out about this recently, that famous actresses often don’t own the jewelleries they wear at ceremonies

The Jewellers let them borrow these to show off the range to potential customers.

And so, these actresses must be very careful with these because these jewelleries would be too expensive even for some of them to buy!

Just like these actresses, we’re given a beautiful jewel that we’re not ready to show.

It’s the beautiful jewel of being called sons and daughters of God, saved by God’s one and only Son.

We’re not worthy to belong to God’s family, but now that we are, we must live a life worthy of the calling we’ve received.

And it starts with striving for unity in love.

Look with me at verses 2-3, “with all humility and gentleness, with patience, bearing with one another in love, making every effort to keep the unity of the Spirit through the bond of peace.”

The first thing about living a life worthy of the calling we’ve received is that we can’t do it alone.

The word “you” in 4:1 is plural (this is made clear in Chinese).

To live the high calling of being a child of God, we must unite with other people who are also in Christ, other children of God.

These people are not like you, and not always nice, like you (if you happen to think that you’re perfectly nice all the time).

No, it takes great effort to relate well to these imperfect Christians.

It will take humility, willing to admit that you can be wrong or at fault.

It takes gentleness, having the ability to exercise control over your strength rather than being harsh and thoughtless with your words.

That’s what a worthy group of Christians look like, they will be humble and gentle, like Jesus their Lord.

They will be making every effort to keep the unity of the Spirit.

It will not be easy, but it will take every effort.

They will take the initiative to make unity happen.

They know that unity just doesn’t happen naturally, not even with Christians.

They will be intentional about making unity happen, they will have a sense of urgency to see unity happen.

Why? Because God is One.

The God of the Christians is the triune God: One God in Three Person, living in perfect unity.

Look with me at verse 4, “There is one body and one Spirit  — just as you were called to one hope at your calling —

There is one body, that is the Church.

There is one Spirit, that is, the Holy Spirit

There is one hope, the hope of glory

Verse 5 - one Lord, one faith, one baptism,

There is one Lord – the Lord Jesus Christ

There is one faith – since there is only one Lord, he’s the only one we can fully trust; he is the only One we Christians put our faith in.

There is one baptism – As Christians, we’re baptised publicly to affirm what it is that we believe.

Verse 6, one God and Father of all, who is above all and through all and in all.

There is only one God and Father; he is above all, through all and in all.

There’s no place where he doesn’t rule.

We strive for unity, we work hard at it, because it reflects the reality of who God is.

When we strive for unity, we show that there is unity in the Trinity.

The Father has sent the Son so that we’ll believe the gospel; the Father sent the Son so that we might united with him, and that we might be united with one another.

If you have united with God, you will strive to unite with one another, even when it’s hard to do.

If you’re not united with God, you won’t care much for unity and it will show for you won’t care much for your brother either.

Listen to how the Apostle John explains it in 1 John 4:20, “If anyone says, “I love God,” and yet hates his brother or sister, he is a liar. For the person who does not love his brother or sister whom he has seen cannot love God whom he has not seen.

We’re called to live a life worthy of the calling.

This will mean striving to live in unity, striving to live in love for one another.

It’s sad but true, that many, many Chinese Churches have split over the years.

The history of the Chinese speaking churches in Sydney shows that new churches form often from splits or strong disagreements.

It might be a split between two groups of Chinese speakers due to differences in worldviews or even politics.

Many times, it’s differences between languages.

They often have the same background, same culture, same blood, for some of them come from the same family.

Even though they should be together as a church, they end up splitting.

Though language and culture can be a barrier, usually it comes down to power.

Usually, it’s one side not willing to let go of the little power think we might have in the Church.

Usually, it involves looking down on the other side, belittling them.

Of course it’s not just the Chinese; it happens in other groups as well.

Tensions in Churches have been there since the New Testament times!

But it doesn’t have to be that way.

Last week, I met with someone from Power to Change mission team where the transition of power happened fairly peacefully.

The Church leaders were traditionally Chinese speaking, but they decided to hand the position of the Senior Minister to a younger, English speaking pastor.

That’s great to hear!

They worked hard and they were striving for unity.

That’s not to say that every Church that had split weren’t trying; rather our history teaches us that striving for unity can be very difficult.

And yet, when unity in the Spirit happens in Churches, when we act in humility and gentleness towards one another,

It’s evidence that we’re living lives worthy of the calling we’ve received.

2. Be worthy by using the diversity of Christ’s gifts

But how do we keep this unity?

Look with me at verse 7, “Now grace was given to each one of us according to the measure of Christ’s gift.”

Being united doesn’t mean we must all be the same, speaking the same language.

Being united means that we recognised our diversity in Christ.

More than that, we need to recognised the diversity of gifts that God’s given to each member of his Church.

Each Christian has a measure of Christ’s gift.

What is this gift? Verse 8, “For it says: When he ascended on high, he took the captives captive; he gave gifts to people.”

When Jesus died and rose from the grave, he spent 40 days with his disciples and then ascended back into the highest heavens, far above all the heavens.

And now he’s at the highest place, he fills all things everywhere with his rule.

On his journey to the highest places, he took the captives captive.

Who are these captives? They were once enemies of Jesus but have now been saved by Jesus.

Now that they’re saved, Jesus gives them back as gifts to the people.

They are once captives of sin, but Jesus has saved them and now they’re captivated by Jesus.

Some, like Paul, are willing to be captured physically in prison for the sake of Jesus, so that he can keep telling people about the good news of Jesus.

These captives, including prisoners like Paul, are gifts from Christ to the Church.

Sometimes, when we talk about gifts in people, it’s tempting just to talk about the gift but not the person.

For example, we might say that the person has a gift for playing music at Church, but we don’t talk about the person at all.

We might not even know the name of the person; we only know him as the piano player.

But God didn’t give merely skills or talents as gifts to the Church.

He gave persons as gifts to the Church.

Each person, captivated by the love of Christ, is Christ’s gift to the Church.

That’s how we must see all those who serve at Church.

They are not merely people with gifts; they are the gifts from Christ himself.

And if we are captivated by Christ, we are gifts from God to the Church.

There are five gifts that Paul focuses one, in verses 11-12, “And he himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, some pastors and teachers, to equip the saints for the work of ministry, to build up the body of Christ,”

These people are gifts that focus on the word ministries.

The apostle and the prophets teach the foundational truths about God, as recorded in the Bible.

The evangelist go out to proclaim the gospel to those who haven’t believed in Jesus yet.

The pastors are teachers who will command, encourage and look after the people of the Church, like a shepherd would his sheep.

The teachers are those who will teach God’s truth, not merely as intellectual truths but will demonstrate the way they live by their example.

The aim of what they do is to equip the saints for works of ministry, to serve one another as they serve the Lord Jesus.

Those of us who are in the Word ministry equip the saints, equip God’s people, by teaching them what God’s word says and to show them how to apply this in their service of God.

As the saints are equipped with the Word of God, we will work in ministry together, building up the body of Christ together.

That’s why in our church, there’s a focus on the word ministry.

As Christians, we have to make sure that we understand God’s word rightly.

That’s why we have Growth Groups throughout the week to help us understand God’s word.

There are currently five growth groups that meet regularly, along with the KOG Youth and KOG children ministries.

In Growth Groups, we meet to think more deeply about what God’s word means in our lives.

That’s why we have the School of Christian Living, where we train potential Growth Group leaders to make sure they understand the Bible correctly.

It takes time to train and equip the saints, but it’s worth it.

That’s why many of us have attended the Discovery Course led by the Giftedness Team over the past few weeks.

We do it because Christ has given us all different gifts, and we want to be sure that we’re using God’s gift in us for God’s glory.

You use God’s gift by being in a Church and actively seeking to see how you can serve the saints with it.

That’s what love looks like.

You can’t obey Jesus’ command to serve one another if you’re not at Church.

You can’t obey Jesus’ command to love one another if you’re not at Church.

If you want to live a life worthy of the calling you received, you will use the gifts that God’s given to the Church, especially one that’s imperfect.

3. Be worthy by speaking the truth in love

How long will we be doing this for?

Verse 13, “until we all reach unity in the faith and in the knowledge of God’s Son, growing into maturity with a stature measured by Christ’s fullness.”

We’re not there yet! Paul knows that Churches are imperfect.

But he also knows that the Church has been called to reflect the glory of God.

We’re not perfect yet, but God has promised that we will be one day.

In the meantime, let’s keep working on becoming worthy of what we’ve received until we’re mature, until we grow into the fullness of Christ.

That’s the standard for our growth.

The standard of our growth is not how big our Church becomes, or how good our songs are, or even how good the sermon is,

but how filled we are to the fullness of Christ.

When we do reach the fullness of Christ, when we do come to the unity in the faith, we wouldn’t be moved by strange teachings of men.

In my conversations with other ministers and missionary partners, we’ve seen people who have taught all kinds of strange things about Christianity.

They have mishandled God’s word and taught not just wrong things but harmful things.

And they’re around our universities, on the streets, some meeting in public places, many teaching false doctrines on social media.

How can we tell the truth from the lies?

It takes maturity as a Christian church to recognise truth from error.

You don’t just suddenly become mature; It takes time.

More than that, it takes intentional effort to strive for unity.

It takes conscious applications of Christ’s gift, being actively involved in the local, imperfect Church.

And it takes speaking the truth in love.

Verse 15, “But speaking the truth in love, let us grow in every way into him who is the head  — Christ.”

It’s not just speaking honestly but speaking the truth about God in contrast to the cunning lies of men.

We grow as we speak words of truth about Jesus to others in the Church, in the context of God’s love.

That’s how we mature; that’s how we grow in Christ.

Christ gives us the resources we need to grow in him.

Look with me at verse 16, “From him the whole body, fitted and knit together by every supporting ligament, promotes the growth of the body for building itself up in love by the proper working of each individual part.”

As each of us do our part in works of service, as we speak words of truth in love, we grow in unity in Christ.

I thank God for what he’s done for us in the past year.

Looking back over the past year, God has taken us a long way in terms of training, in terms of numbers and in terms of better facilities.

However, this is not the end goal; it’s just us slowly beginning to grow.

My prayer is that we will keep growing.

Over the past week, we’ve been preparing the hall for a potential second generation in the hall.

We already asked for an extra classroom for next year’s KOG ministries.

And our toilets are ready!

What can stop us growing as a Church?

If we don’t speak the word of truth in love to one another, we won’t grow.

If we don’t preach and listen to the truth on Sundays, we won’t grow.

If we don’t teach and learn in Growth Groups, we won’t grow.

If we don’t apply the gifts God’s given us, if we only keep God’s gifts, meant for the Church, for ourselves, we won’t grow.

If we don’t actively strive for unity, if we let our cultures, our pride, our interests divide us, we won’t grow.

And if we don’t grow, we are not worthy of what God has called us to be.

I’m not talking necessarily about numbers, but who we are as the people of God.

We must grow firstly into him, which means growing in our unity, using the diverse gifts Christ’s given us and speaking the truth in love.

If we strive for these things, by God’s grace, with much prayer, we will grow, as we have already seen over this year.

And so, why is going to an imperfect Church is vital if you want to live a life worthy of your calling?

In an imperfect Church, we learn to be patient, we learn to be loving and we learn to be gentle, just as God has treated us in Christ and will continue to do so.

There is no perfect Church – it’s an illusion.

There’s a saying that says if you have found the perfect church, don’t go there because you will make it imperfect.

Instead, go to a church that recognises their own imperfections, their own immaturity and have a desire to grow.

That’s the church we want to be.

We want to be a church that is worthy of the calling we have received from God

And so, Be worthy, strive for unity, use your Christ-given diversity, and speak the truth lovingly

Some of us have been on cruises before.

God willing, I’ll go on one very soon.

While the cruise will be a relaxing time for a lot of its passengers, the crew of the cruise have to work hard to make it happen.

Friends, while the Church is not a cruise, it is a rescue boat.

We need to work together to save those who are already on the rescue boat as well as those who are outside of the rescue boat.

We need to strive for unity as team, each one of us need to use our Christ-given gifts for the Church, as Christ intended.

And we need to speak the truth about God in the love of God.

As 2023 draws to a close, start thinking now how you can serve other saints in the context of the church.

Be worthy, strive for unity, use your Christ-given diversity, and speak the truth lovingly.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Why is striving for unity so hard, even for Christians in Churches?

What gifts has Christ given through me to the Church? How do I know I’m right?

Do I look forward to serving in an imperfect Church, as part of my calling in Christ?

Friends, if you’ve been called by Christ, if you’re a Christian, as a Church be worthy, strive for unity, use your Christ-given diversity, and speak the truth lovingly.

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you for saving us, people who were once dead but now made alive in Christ. We are not worthy of your grace. By your Spirit, make us worthy of your calling by striving for unity, serving your people with what God has given us and speaking to one another in the truth of Jesus, in the love of Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
