Our survival is grounded in God’s pleasure

Live by the pleasing aroma of Christ

“As long as you’re happy, that’s all that matters.”

Have you heard of that saying before?

Maybe it’s from a mother who doesn’t agree with her daughter’s decision, but what can she do?

In the end, if her daughter’s happy, that’s all that matters.

Or it may be from a husband who doesn’t agree with his wife’s decision, but you know what they say, “Happy Wife, Happy Life”

That is, as long as she’s happy, that’s all that matters.

You can try to tell them that they’re wrong by explaining the facts, or by even by pointing them to the Bible.

But so often, what wins the day is not the truth, nor the facts, but their happiness.

If it makes them happy, that’s all that matters.

The missiologist David Williams made this very interesting observation, “In a

pain–pleasure worldview, you make decisions based on what feels good to you and what makes you happy. Your identity is as a pleasure seeker and a pain avoider.”

Happiness really matters to people; it’s how they identify themselves.

It’s how they make important decisions; it’s how even how they make life and death decisions.

If they’re happy, that’s all that matters.

Today, I want to go one step further.

Happiness is not just important; it’s vital to our survival.

In fact, without happiness, without pleasure, we will die.

We, as a human race, cannot survive without pleasure.

Why is pleasure so vital to our survival?

This is what we’ll see in today’s passage.

Open up with me to Genesis 8, beginning from verse 1.

We’re going to see three things:

The life of faith is an adventure

Our survival is grounded in God’s pleasure

Live by the pleasing aroma of Christ

Before we start, let’s pray,

“Father, please open our eyes to your truth and by your Spirit, transform us to be more like your Son Jesus Christ, for his sake we pray, Amen.”

The life of faith is an adventure

Look with me at Genesis 8 beginning from verse 1,

“God remembered Noah, as well as all the wildlife and all the livestock that were with him in the ark. God caused a wind to pass over the earth, and the water began to subside.”

At the end of the last chapter, we read that the water has surged on earth 150 days, that is, for around five months.

But now, God remembered Noah.

That doesn’t mean that God had forgotten Noah in his ark.

In the Bible, when God remembers someone, it’s usually in the context of that person being in trouble and God rescuing that person.

And so, God remembers Noah and he starts saving Noah.

God caused a wind to blow over the earth and the water began to subside.

The sources from deep within the earth closed their opening and the floodgates of heavens closed.

The waters receded significantly by the end of the 150th day.

To help us get a better idea of the timeline, I’m going to use Noah’s birthday to mark the dates, just as the Bible has been doing.

And so, in 7:6, Noah was 600 years old when water covered the earth.

We will label this date 1/1/600 NB (Noah’s Birth)

What’s the date when Noah’s ark rested on the mountains of Ararat? 17/7/600NB

What happened on 1/10/600NB? Verse 6, The tops of the mountains were visible.

Forty days after that, on 10/11/600NB, Noah sent a raven out and then he sent a dove out.

Why did he send them out? Because he didn’t know what was happening outside his ark.

He sent the birds to find out what was happening in the outside world.

It wasn’t that long ago when sailors were using birds to find out whether there was land near by.

Noah was using these birds to find out what it was like out there and specifically for the dove, whether there was anything near by.

It’s a little like this:

Noah sent a raven to fly around until the water had dried up.

The raven is a smart bird.

It’s also a very tough bird and can fly in rough weather conditions.

So it’s possible that Noah sent the raven to find out what the weather pattern was like and whether the environment was ready for them to go out.

Noah then sent a dove to see whether the waters had gone down.

The dove, of course, was a gentler bird.

It needed to find a safe place to land.

If it could find a safe place to land, then other animals could probably go down as well.

However, it couldn’t find any resting place and so returned to land on Noah’s hand.

Seven days later, Noah sent it on a second attempt and it brought a leaf back.

Seven days later, on the third attempt, the dove went and didn’t come back.

And so, what date was it when the water on the earth was dried up? 1/1/601NB.

What a great birthday present for Noah!

It wasn’t until 27/2/601NB that the earth was dry.

I love this account of the Bible not only because it’s beautiful and full of hope.

It’s also so practical!

This wasn’t a fairy-tale where things just magically turned out right.

Noah didn’t know what was happening; he didn’t have all the answers.

He obeyed God by building the ark and got all his family and the animals to go and stay in there during the flood.

But what was he supposed to do now?

He obediently waited; he was literally counting the days in the ark.

He didn’t know when his journey in the ark was going to end.

And so, he had to explore; he had to send the raven and the dove to find out what was going on.

The life of faith is often like that.

We often don’t know exactly what our next step should be.

God doesn’t tell us a detailed plan of what to expect from day to day.

Often, we just don’t know what’s going to happen; we just live from day to day.

We trust God and we wait for God, patiently and obediently.

In the meantime, we do what we can to find out what’s happening.

We explore different options using the gifts and skills God’s given us.

That’s the beauty of the life of faith; it’s an adventure.

A life of faith is not boring; it can’t be boring!

You can’t predict it.

You trust in God, but that doesn’t mean you know exactly what’s going to happen next.

But that’s what faith is.

Faith is listening to God and obeying him as you trust him one day at a time, one step at a time.

Someone with faith will ask, “Where will God take me today? What’s the next step that he wants me to take?”

God is always the same, he doesn’t change.

But that doesn’t mean he’s predictable.

When someone walks with God, they go on an adventure of faith with God.

We can’t see everything that’s ahead; but He can.

And so, in all the ups and downs of life, we trust that he will lead us through.

Marnus Labuschagne is an Australian cricketer and is currently ranked No.1 in the world for his batting.

He loves cricket and has been playing representative cricket since he was 10.

And Marnus is also a Christian and his mother is a Christian too.

This is what he says about the sport, “Sport is a fickle game and injuries play a big part.

In the big scheme of things, what you’re worth, what you put your value in, isn’t out there on the pitch; it’s internal and in Christ.”

I’m sure there’s a lot of ups and downs in Marnus’ life as a cricketer and as a Christian.

But he recognises the most important thing is not whether he’s world No.1 or not.

At the end of the day, it’s whether he is walking with Christ or not.

To help him remember Christ, Marnus’ mother would write Bible verses everywhere around the house.

Sometimes she’ll even write a Bible verse in his cricket shoes to remind him.

That’s a great idea!

I’d love to see our fathers and mothers writing Bible verses all around the house for their children to remind them to keep walking with Jesus!

The life of faith is not boring; it’s an adventure of learning to trust in God through the ups and downs of life.

You don’t know exactly what to expect, but you know that following God is always the right thing to do.

But how do you know?

How do you know which way God wants you to go?

Our survival is grounded in God’s pleasure

To find out which way God wants us to go, we need to know what makes God happy, we need to know what gives God pleasure.

In fact, our very survival is grounded in what gives God pleasure.

That’s what we see in the second part of today’s passage.

When the earth was dry, God commanded Noah, his family and all of the creatures to come out.

All the birds, the livestock, all those that crawl on the earth, including caterpillars, snails and ants, came out of the ark.

God commanded them to spread and multiply all over the earth.

It’s a new beginning for our world.

One of the first things that Noah did in the new world is to offer a sacrifice to God.

Look with me at verse 20, “Then Noah built an altar to the LORD. He took some of every kind of clean animal and every kind of clean bird and offered burnt offerings on the altar.”

This is why Noah needed extra clean animals and extra clean birds.

He would sacrifice some of them to the LORD as soon as he got out of the ark.

It wouldn’t have looked impressive; given the circumstances, there would’ve been only a few animals and birds that he could use.

The result? Look with me at verse 21:

21 When the LORD smelled the pleasing aroma, he said to himself, “I will never again curse the ground because of human beings, even though the inclination of the human heart is evil from youth onward. And I will never again strike down every living thing as I have done.”

For such a small sacrifice, it brought about a seriously impressive response from God.

In fact, there’s a sense that this little sacrifice is the highlight, the climax of Noah’s adventure, of Noah’s story.

God smelled the aroma of the sacrifice, and he was pleased.

Can you smell that?

Does it smell good?

It’s biscuits and we’re going to have it for morning tea later on.

It’s a pleasing aroma.

God was so pleased with the aroma of the sacrifice that he said he would not curse the ground again and strike down every living thing as he had done.

Is that what the Bible says?

Look again, “I will never again curse the ground because of human beings, even though the inclination of the human heart is evil from youth onward. And I will never again strike down every living thing as I have done.”

What’s so striking about God’s response to Noah’s sacrifice isn’t the fact that he promised to never curse the ground again, as great and as wonderful that it.

What’s striking is the fact that the human beings haven’t changed!

The flood hasn’t changed the human heart.

All the floods that destroyed most of the planet, including most of the humans and the animals, didn’t wash away the evil of the human heart.

God says the inclination of the hearts of the remaining eight human beings is evil from youth onwards.

The human heart didn’t improve even after being rescued by God in the flood.

It’s still evil.

And so, here’s our problem.

We haven’t changed, our heart is still inclined to do evil and if God’s reason for destroying the world with the flood was because of our hearts, then aren’t we back at where we started?

Aren’t we still as guilty and damnable as we were before the flood?

And so, we haven’t changed, we’re just as bad as we were before the flood.

What changed?

It would appear from the passage that the change came from God himself.

That’s not to say that God’s character changed but his attitude towards us and our sin has changed.

The human heart is just as evil as it was before the flood, we’re just as damnable as we were before the flood.

But his attitude towards us, his posture towards us, has changed.

What led to this change? It was Noah’s sacrifice.

That little sacrifice was what made the difference in our relationship to God.

That little sacrifice was the key.

Though the animals that were sacrificed were just normal, clean animals, they represented the obedience and righteousness of Noah.

These animals were brought to the ark, survived through the flood, walked safely out to dry ground because Noah obeyed God.

And so these sacrifices were offered with thanks to God and as a pledge for humans to continue to follow and worship God.

And God was pleased with that sacrifice.

But more importantly, the sacrifice that Noah made was one that pointed to a sacrifice in the future.

There will come a time when the offer of one sacrifice will change our relationship with God forever.

Noah’s sacrifice led God to promise he’ll never curse the ground and strike down every living thing as he had done, that is, through the flood.

This sacrifice that would come one day will stop the very curse of death,

Because this sacrifice will deal with the root of our sins, this sacrifice will deal with the evil that’s in our hearts.

This sacrifice was made some two thousand years ago in the death of Jesus.

Jesus has come into the world to save us from our hearts.

He came and sacrificed himself on the cross to give us a new heart, a heart that beats for God, a heart that longs for God.

When God smelled Noah’s sacrifice, it reminded him that one day, he will see the sacrifice of His Own Son.

It reminded him that one day, the precious sacrifice of Jesus would lead to the salvation of anyone and everyone who turns to him.

It would be a salvation that would save far more people than Noah ever could.

And so, God’s pleasure to Noah’s sacrifice is a foretaste of his pleasure in the ultimate sacrifice of Jesus on the cross.

That’s why the symbol for Christianity had been the cross since the very beginning.

John Stott, a Christian theologian and writer once said, “They (the Christians) wished to commemorate as central to their understanding of Jesus neither his birth or his youth, neither his teaching nor his service, neither his resurrection nor his reign, nor his gift of the Spirit, but his death, his crucifixion.”

The sacrificial death of Jesus is central to Christianity.

The cross of Jesus is central to our relationship with God.

For without the cross of Jesus, our hearts will remain evil.

We can try all we want, but we will never be good enough to please God.

Just as we would be helpless in a flood, left to ourselves, we would be helplessly drowning in our own sin.

And even after the flood, evil remained in the human heart.

But what the flood could not wash away, the blood of Jesus can.

By his death on the cross, Jesus has satisfied God’s justice.

God was pleased with Jesus’ sacrifice.

And so, for anyone who believes in Jesus, their sin can be washed away by the blood of Jesus.

And if our sins are washed away, we’ll be set free from the curse of death, we’re promised to have eternal life.

Live by the pleasing aroma of Christ

And so, as Christians, we live because God is pleased with Jesus Christ.

We live because of the pleasing aroma of Christ.

Our very survival depends on the fact that God is pleased with the aroma of Christ.

And so, as Christians, we live by, we live according to the pleasing aroma of Christ.

We live by Christ and according to what he has done for us on the cross.

We live in the shadow of the cross, we find our delight and our pleasure in the cross of Jesus.

If God himself is pleased with the sacrifice of Jesus, we too, should find our pleasure and our joy in the sacrifice of Jesus.

As God’s people, we find pleasure in what pleases him.

As we make decisions in our lives, we want to choose what pleases him.

And what pleases God, what brings pleasure to God?

The cross of Christ.

And so, as we make our decisions, we must make them in the light of the cross of Christ.

We live according to the pleasing aroma of Christ, we live according to the way of the cross.

One day, after Church, I came out of the church building and a child suddenly said to me in a very serious voice, “I can smell sausage sizzle.”

I looked around but couldn’t see any.

Before I could ask where, another child said, “Yes, a sausage sizzle, with BBQ sauce.”

They knew the aroma of a sausage sizzle and the smell of BBQ sauce.

And they followed their nose to the sausage sizzle, which gave them a lot of pleasure.

Sometimes we might not know what our next step in our faith adventure should be.

We might not know which way we ought to go.

When that happens, follow your nose, your spiritual nose.

Where is the aroma of Christ taking you?

Which step will reflect the cross of Jesus more?

You might need to choose between keeping something for yourself or giving something up precious for the sake of Jesus.

Will you follow the aroma of Christ and give up something sacrificially, just as Christ gave himself sacrificially at the cross?

You might need to make a decision between doing something that’s difficult but right, or easy but wrong.

Will you follow the aroma of Christ and obey God, even as Jesus himself became obedient to death, even death on a cross?

Paul tells us in 2 Cor 2:15 that “For to God we are the fragrance of Christ among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing.”

If we are followers of Christ, then we will be carrying the fragrance of Christ wherever we might be.

We carry the fragrance of Christ as people who have been saved by him, who follow and obey his teachings, who point people to him.

Our fragrance doesn’t draw people to us, rather our fragrance ought to draw people to Christ.

For it is Christ’s fragrance that we wear, as it were, as we live the life that he gave us.

For those who hate Christ, they will be repulsed by us.

We’ll smell like death to them.

But for those who see the beauty and the life of Christ, our life will bring them joy, for it’s the aroma of life.

When they see what we do, when they see Christ’s teaching lived out in our lives, when they hear us speaking the words of Christ, they’ll want to find out more.

When they accept what Christ has done for them, and how he has died on the cross and ross from the grave for them, they will find comfort and joy because they’ve been set free from the curse of death.

We will rejoice with them that they’ve been saved by Christ.

But most importantly, God is now pleased with them for their sins have been washed away by the perfect and sufficient sacrifice of Jesus Christ.

And so, to conclude,

Our survival does depend on pleasure.

But it’s not our pleasure, but God’s.

Our survival depends on God’s pleasure.

The aroma of Noah pleased God for it anticipates the aroma of Christ’s sacrifice.

As followers of Jesus, we live a life grounded in the sacrifice of Jesus Christ, we live in the light of the cross.

And as we do, we spread the fragrance of Christ wherever we go.

As you walk by faith, be led by the pleasing aroma of Christ.

Don’t make your decision according to what makes you happy.

Make decisions that follows the aroma of Christ.

If your daughter or wife, or son or husband, or anyone else is making decisions based only on what makes them happy,

persuade them, instruct them to be led by the pleasing aroma of Christ.

Encourage them to make their decisions based on what Jesus has taught and what He has done.

Point them to the way of the cross.

For that’s where our life, our survival lies.

Our survival lies in not what makes us happy, but what gives God pleasure.

And nothing gives more pleasure to God than the salvation that Jesus has given us by his sacrifice on the cross.

And so, in you venture in faith, as you walk by faith, be led by the pleasing aroma of Christ.

When in doubt, follow your spiritual nose!

Here are the three questions for this week:

What would change in your week’s timetable now if you see your walk with God as an adventure in faith?

God is pleased with the aroma of Christ. Am I pleased with what pleases God?

Would people around me know that I carry the fragrance of Christ? Will they know that I follow Christ by my words and deeds?

Friends, as you walk by faith, be led by the pleasing aroma of Christ.

When in doubt, follow your spiritual nose!

Let’s pray,

“Father, thank you for remembering your people. Thank you that you remembered Noah and you saved him. Thank you for remembering us and saving us in Jesus Christ. Forgive us when we live in doubt rather than following you in faith. Please strengthen our faith and walk on an adventure of faith with you. Let us be led by the aroma of Christ. Let us follow his way of the cross in our day to day decisions. And as we do, may people notice the fragrance of Christ in us, and in turn follow Christ himself, who lives and reigns with you and the Holy spirit, one God, forever and ever. Amen.”
