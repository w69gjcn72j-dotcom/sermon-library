Young Adults (19–30)

The Lord of Rest, Prayer and Mercy

Luke 6:1–19 · Sunday 15 November 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah · For young adults (19–30) · Sermon by Rev David Yung

Big Idea: Jesus is the Lord of Rest, the Lord of Prayer and the Lord of Mercy: true rest is not “I stop working” but “Christ has already finished the work for me”; prayer comes before action; and God often uses people who seem “impossible”.

Aim: by the end of the study, group members will

see that Jesus is Lord of the Sabbath, and that rest is a gift of grace received in him, not something we earn by getting everything done;

learn from Jesus’ night of prayer to bring decisions about study, work and relationships to the Father before deciding;

be encouraged that Jesus chooses people who feel “not qualified”, and stretch out their own withered places to him.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

1. Connect (10 min)

  • Always online, always responding, always on call: the work Slack, the uni group chat, the notifications at midnight. On a scale of 1–10, how rested are you right now? What keeps your mind “on” even when you lie down?
  • You’ve probably made a big call recently: a course, a job offer, a move out of home, a relationship. Did you decide by spreadsheet, by asking friends, by scrolling other people’s lives, or by prayer?

2. Read: Luke 6:1–19 (CSB)

1 On a Sabbath, he passed through the grainfields. His disciples were picking heads of grain, rubbing them in their hands, and eating them.

2 But some of the Pharisees said, “Why are you doing what is not lawful on the Sabbath?”

3 Jesus answered them, “Haven’t you read what David did when he and those who were with him were hungry—

4 how he entered the house of God, and took and ate the bread of the Presence, which is not lawful for any but the priests to eat? He even gave some to those who were with him.”

5 Then he told them, “The Son of Man is Lord of the Sabbath.”

6 On another Sabbath he entered the synagogue and was teaching. A man was there whose right hand was shriveled.

7 The scribes and Pharisees were watching him closely, to see if he would heal on the Sabbath, so that they could find a charge against him.

8 But he knew their thoughts and told the man with the shriveled hand, “Get up and stand here.” So he got up and stood there.

9 Then Jesus said to them, “I ask you: Is it lawful to do good on the Sabbath or to do evil, to save life or to destroy it?”

10 After looking around at them all, he told him, “Stretch out your hand.” He did, and his hand was restored.

11 They, however, were filled with rage and started discussing with one another what they might do to Jesus.

12 During those days he went out to the mountain to pray and spent all night in prayer to God.

13 When daylight came, he summoned his disciples, and he chose twelve of them, whom he also named apostles:

14 Simon, whom he also named Peter, and Andrew his brother; and James, John, Philip, and Bartholomew;

15 Matthew and Thomas; James the son of Alphaeus, and Simon called the Zealot;

16 Judas the son of James, and Judas Iscariot, who became a traitor.

17 After coming down with them, he stood on a level place with a large crowd of his disciples and a great number of people from all Judea and Jerusalem and from the seacoast of Tyre and Sidon.

18 They came to hear him and to be healed of their diseases; and those tormented by unclean spirits were made well.

19 The whole crowd was trying to touch him, because power was coming out from him and healing them all.

3. Context

Luke 6:1–19 follows a run of clashes between Jesus and the Pharisees (5:17–39). God gave the Sabbath as a gift of rest (Genesis 2:3; Exodus 20:8–11), but the oral law (later written down in the Mishnah) listed 39 kinds of work forbidden on the Sabbath, including “reaping” and “threshing”. The Law of Moses itself allowed a hungry person to pluck heads of grain by hand (Deuteronomy 23:25). After two Sabbath disputes, Jesus prays all night, chooses the Twelve, and comes down to a level place where crowds from as far as Tyre and Sidon press in to touch him.

4. Discuss

  1. (vv.1–2) What were the disciples doing, and what did the Pharisees accuse them of? Moses never forbade it (Deuteronomy 23:25). Where do Christians our age turn their own preferences into rules, and then call others out by them?
  2. (vv.3–5) How does Jesus answer, first with David and then with v.5? What is he claiming about himself when he calls himself “Lord of the Sabbath”?
  3. (vv.1–5, application) The sermon said rest is not “doing nothing” but “finding rest in God”, even in the middle of work. What would “having Christ in the work” look like in your study, shifts or commute?
  4. (vv.6–9) Why were the scribes and Pharisees watching Jesus so closely? Why does Jesus put the man in the middle of the room, and what does their silence after v.9 reveal?
  5. (vv.10–11) How is the man healed? What does it mean that rest is “letting God do the work”, not “I stop working”? Where do you feel “withered”, a hurdle you just can’t get over yourself?
  6. (vv.11–12) The Pharisees are filled with rage. What does Jesus do instead of hitting back or rallying his followers? What do you usually do first when facing a big decision or conflict?
  7. (vv.13–16) Look at the Twelve: fishermen, a tax collector, a Zealot, and Judas. Why might a tax collector and a Zealot have struggled to be in the same group chat? What does their being in one team say to us in a world of call-outs and curated feeds?
  8. (vv.17–19) Jesus comes down to a level place, and power comes out from him to heal. What does that downward movement tell you about Jesus? What would it look like this week to come near him, not because you deserve it but because he is willing?

  • Thank Jesus, the Lord of the Sabbath, that rest is his gift, not our achievement. Confess our “rest anxiety”, and the ways we judge others by our own rules.
  • Ask the Father for wisdom for the decisions ahead (study, work, where to live, who to marry), and for unity across our differences. Pray for more helpers for KOGKids, KOGYouth and KOGMini, and for accompanists for the Chinese Parallel service.

7. Leader’s Notes

Framing. Joyfully point to Jesus. Guests and not-yet-Christians may be present; never corner anyone. Many young adults live with constant “rest anxiety” and feel “not qualified”. The good news is that rest is a gift of grace, and Jesus makes his instruments out of the willing.

Question helps

Q1 — The disciples picked and rubbed heads of grain. The oral law counted picking as “reaping” and rubbing as “threshing”, two of its 39 forbidden categories of work. But Moses never forbade it; Deuteronomy 23:25 allows it. The Pharisees replaced God’s law with human tradition, then used that tradition to condemn others. Examples: worship style, “quiet time” habits, what counts as a “real” Christian online.

Q2 — David, God’s chosen king, ate the bread of the Presence because of real need, and God did not condemn him: “If David could, so can my disciples, because I am greater than David.” Then v.5: only God, who instituted the Sabbath (Genesis 2:3; Exodus 20:8–11), can define it. Jesus is in effect saying, “I am the God who instituted the Sabbath,” and “I am the one the Sabbath points to.”

Q3 — True rest is “finding rest in God”, not “doing nothing”. The Christian programmer in the sermon couldn’t rest on Sundays because of work chats and code he could always optimise; his rest was knowing “who holds tomorrow” and not relying on his own work to be justified.

Q4 — They wanted a charge against him (v.7). Jesus knew their thoughts, took the initiative and put the man in front of everyone. Their silence exposed that they cared about keeping the rules, not doing good and showing mercy. If the Sabbath is for people to be set free, healed and rested, refusing to heal is what truly breaks it.

Q5 — No medicine, no massage, no surgery: one word, and the hand was restored. The man could not heal himself, but when he obeyed and stretched out his hand, God’s power came. So rest is not “I stop working” but “I let Christ work in me”. Mark 3:5 says Jesus’ look held anger and grief at their hard hearts.

Q6 — No counter-attack, no rallying the crowd: he spent all night in prayer before choosing the Twelve. In the sermon, a young man torn between two job offers needed not more pros-and-cons lists but unhurried prayer, phone off, asking, “Lord, which way do you want me to go?” Prayer turned his eyes from “what I want” to “what God wants”.

Q7 — Fishermen with no rabbinic training, a tax collector seen as a traitor, a Zealot from a violent resistance movement, and Judas who betrayed him. God makes instruments out of the willing, “so that no one may boast”. If Jesus could take in both Matthew and Simon, can I take in my brother? A church that cannot live out unity across political divides loses her witness.

Q8 — The gospel always moves downward: heaven to earth, throne to manger, glory to cross. Luke’s “level place” foreshadows Christ standing where we stand to save us. Power flowed from him “like water from a spring, like blood from a wound”: grace, not performance. The crowd were not worthy; God had mercy on their need.

Our vision. “From faith to faith”: the Lord of Mercy chose ordinary, unlikely people to carry the gospel, and he still does.
