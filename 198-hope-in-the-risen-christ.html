Hope in The Risen Christ

Passage: 1 Corinthians 15.20-28

Friends, it’s good to be with you on our online service this week.

My family and I had a wonderful break; thank you to those who have helped to look after the Church while we’ve been (virtually) away.

It feels like it’s been a long time.

And so, let’s have a quick revision.

Christ is Risen!

Last month, we started on a sermon series on the seven doctrines, or seven sets of teachings, to help us get a better grasp of the Bible.

These seven doctrines are like seven stars to help us see the bigger picture of the story of the Bible.

Do you remember them?

I hope you have your pen and paper ready.

If you do, let’s have a go at drawing the four pictures that we have so far.

The first one was creation.

In the doctrine of creation, we see that God is the Creator and we’re the creatures.

The second one was on the Fall.

In the doctrine of the Fall, we see that God is the Holy One and that we’re the sinners.

The third is the doctrine of Judgement.

In the doctrine of Judgement, we see that God is the Judge and that we’re the Judged.

The fourth is the doctrine of salvation.

In the doctrine of Salvation, we see that God is the Saviour and that we’re the saved.

Today, we’re going to look at the doctrine of resurrection.

In the doctrine of resurrection, we see that Christ is risen and those in Christ will be raised in him.

This doctrine of resurrection is central to Christianity.

Chapter 15 of 1 Corinthians in particular focuses on the doctrine of Resurrection.

If you have your Bible there, open up with me to 1 Corinthians 15.

Here, the Apostle Paul wanted to pass on to the Corinthian Christians the most important things about the Christian faith.

Paul wanted the early Christians to know that Christ has died and risen, according to the Scriptures.

And that there were witnesses of the risen Christ, including Cephas (that is Peter), the Twelve Apostles, 500 brothers and sisters and even Paul himself.

Jesus has definitely been risen!

Does it really matter that he’s risen?

Paul says with an emphatic yes! It really matters that he’s risen.

In fact, he says that if Christ has not been risen, then your faith is worthless.

If Jesus wasn’t risen, then you have wasted your time believing in Jesus.

Why is resurrection so central to Christianity?

We’re going to see at least two reasons in today’s passage.

We’re going to see that Christ is the Firstfruits and the Christ is the Great Restorer.

2. Christ is the Firstfruits

Of those who have fallen asleep

Look with me at verse 20, “But as it is, Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep.”

The firstfruits is the first portion of the crop that the Ancient Israelite farmer offers to God in thanksgiving to God.

It’s trusting that God will provide the rest in due time.

In the resurrection of Jesus, we see him as the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep.

He is the first one of many more to come who will rise from the dead.

Jesus’ resurrection was a guarantee.

Just as Jesus was risen from the dead, those who are in him will also rise from the dead.

Notice how the death of Christians are described; they’re asleep.

As Christians, our deaths are like sleep and we have the hope of waking up from our sleep to see Jesus face to face.

We have this hope of waking up from the sleep of death because of our trust in Jesus.

That’s the hope that all Christians have.

Who reverses what Adam’s done

While this hope is deeply personal, it’s not just a private and individual affair.

The resurrection of Jesus from death has implication for all of humanity just as the death of Adam has implication for all of humanity.

Look with me at verses 21-22. “21 For since death came through a man, the resurrection of the dead also comes through a man. 22 For just as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive.”

Adam sinned against God and was sentenced to death.

Through one man came death.

But because Jesus obeyed God perfectly, even to death, God raised him from the dead.

Through one man, came the resurrection of the dead.

In Christ, in the new Adam, all will be made alive.

The action of one man had implication for us all.

It’s a little bit like this.

You might have heard of this saying before, “we’re all in this together.”

Have you heard of it?

While we might agree with this generally, in this season of pandemic, we see how true this phrase is, for better or for worse.

In the pandemic season, we see how the action of one person can quickly have an effect on many others.

A person who might have a slight fever might decide to travel to work.

His action might have affected the lives of thousands, even millions in another state.

For every story about the spread of a virus there will be at least one other who have decided not to go to travel and decided not to go to work.

His action might also have affected thousands and potentially even save the lives of many others.

One decision from one person, have an impact on many more.

That’s what we see in the person of Adam and the person of Jesus.

Adam the first man, made a decision that had an effect on everyone who’s ever born.

He decided to sin against God.

Adam’s action brought death to all of us.

And like him, our decisions to sin against God bring death to us and death to others.

But Jesus Christ also made his decision.

He decided to obey God and he did it perfectly, even to death on a cross.

His decision brought resurrection.

Jesus’ action brought us the hope of our own resurrection.

Jesus brought a new start to humanity!

For those who trust in him, we’ll be made alive in him.

That’s the hope of all those who put their trust in Jesus; we will rise to be like him.

We will rise to be like Jesus our firstfruits.

3. Christ is the Great Restorer

Who must reign

As important as it is for the risen Jesus to bring a new start to humanity, there’s more!

Look with me at verses 23-24, “23 But each in his own order: Christ, the firstfruits; afterward, at his coming, those who belong to Christ. 24 Then comes the end, when he hands over the kingdom to God the Father, when he abolishes all rule and all authority and power.”

Here’s the order of what will happen as we approach the end.

Are you ready?

Firstly, Christ the firstfruits, he would rise from the dead.

Praise God, he’s done that already!

Secondly, at the coming of Jesus, those who belong to Christ will rise again.

Praise God, that that’s what every man and woman who puts their trust in Jesus can look forward to.

Thirdly, the end will come!

The world, as we know it, will come to an end.

When the end does come, Jesus will hand over the kingdom to God the Father.

When that happens, God’s order will be restored.

At the beginning, God’s order was God, man and woman, creation.

But we’ve rejected God’s order and so sinned against God, the Holy One.

And it’s not just individual human beings who have rejected God’s rule.

The Bible speaks of rule and authority and power that oppose God’s rule as well.

These can refer to political powers, like nations, countries, kings, emperors, commercial companies and other religions.

These are groups of people who want to rule in God’s place.

These are groups who outright oppose God and oppress God’s people.

One of the most dreadful political party to have risen to power over the past century are the NAZI’s.

NAZI is actually an acronym, which stands for “Nationalsozialistische Deutsche Arbeiterpartei”, the National-Socialist German Worker’s Party.

As part of their climb to power, they infiltrated into the Church.

They first appeared to be very friendly to the Church, even calling themselves Christians.

But over time, they’ve changed the words of the Bible to suit their own agendas.

They’ve changed the Lord’s Supper; they’ve even put NAZI banners over the Church.

After they’ve taken over the Church, they committed horrible crimes, crimes that were against the Bible’s teaching but by then, the Church was no longer in a position to question the powerful NAZI party.

The rule, authority and power here in 1 Corinthians can also refer to spiritual realities.

These are agents of Satan who actively move people to disobey God.

Of course, these two groups are not mutually exclusive; it’s very possible for those who are inclined to oppose God to be moved by agents of the evil one.

Whatever the case may be, at the end, when Jesus comes back, all rule, all authority and power will be overthrown.

We’re thankful that the NAZI’s were overthrown.

But they’re not the last group to oppose God.

Oppositions against God still stands today.

One day, they too, will be overthrown.

The greatest opposition, the last enemy, is death itself.

Jesus has come to destroy death and he succeeded when he rose from the grave.

As Christians, we share Christ’s victory and we look forward to the day when we, too, by the power that raised Jesus from the dead, will also be raised from the dead.

Who restores order in God’s creation

When that day comes, when Jesus comes again, when we rise from the dead, it’s the day when we’ll see that God has put everything under his feet.

On that day, there will be no more oppositions.

All enemies, including death, will be finally and completely conquered.

On that day, Jesus will restore order in God’s creation.

Look with me at verse 27-28, “For God has put everything under his feet. Now when it says “everything” is put under him, it is obvious that he who puts everything under him is the exception. 28 When everything is subject to Christ, then the Son himself will also be subject to the one who subjected everything to him, so that God may be all in all.”

God the Father has put everything under Jesus.

And so, on that final day, where will God the Father be?

On that day, Jesus will hand the kingdom over to God.

On that day, Jesus, God the Son, will subject himself to God the Father as well.

That doesn’t mean that Jesus became lesser than God the Father.

Jesus, God the Son, being in very substance God, shares God’s glory.

Jesus, in his very essence, is not less than God.

But as the Son and as the perfect Representative of Man, he submits himself to God.

When Jesus as Man’s Representative has finished conquering every enemy, he presents a reordered creation, the creation ordered according to God’s will, back to God.

The end result will be that God will be all in all.

So that at the end, through the risen Christ, God will have perfect rule over all.

What does this mean for us?

Three things.

In Christ,

We hope

Firstly, In Christ, we have hope.

In Christ, we have a risen life; in Christ we’re made alive.

When we trust in Jesus, we’re made alive spiritually.

When we sleep in death, we have hope that we’ll be made alive with a new body.

That’s what Christians hope in.

Our hope is not in getting a better house, or a better car or even a healthier body.

It’s good to get these but they’ll all fade away.

Our hope is in the risen Christ and that because of him, we’ll have a risen life.

It’s a hope that brings us joy; it’s a hope that brings us peace.

Rest in that joy.

We trust

Second, we trust in Christ.

Those who hold power in this world might seem intimidating.

The prevalent ideologies of this world might be very persuasive.

But don’t rest in them; don’t put your hope in them.

Know that only Christ’s rule is sure.

We bow our knees only to Jesus Christ and not to anyone else.

That doesn’t mean that we don’t submit to the government.

God has put governments and our leaders there to help our society run smoothly and peacefully.

When they do what’s right according to Christ, we praise God for them.

However, when they oppose Christ and oppress people, especially God’s people, we must speak up.

We submit

Finally, we will submit.

One of the most incredible pictures that the Bible paints about Jesus is that he submits himself to God.

Jesus has everything; God has put everything under his feet.

Even though he’s equal to God in power and glory, Jesus submits himself to God.

That’s what humility looks like.

And humility is not foreign to God.

In fact, it is in the very heart of God to be humble, because we see this in the heart of Jesus.

If Jesus Christ, the one who’s before all things, to whom all things are subjected, doesn’t seek his own glory

but willing to humble himself before God,

how much more should we humble ourselves before God.

So Friends, let’s humble ourselves before God in Jesus Christ, and let’s put our hope on that final day, when Jesus will hand the kingdom to God,

So that God may be all in all.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What is your hope, in life and death?

How certain are you about your relationship with Jesus?

In what area of your life do you have a special need to submit to God?

Friends, Christ is risen, he is risen indeed!

Set your hope firmly on that, come what may.

Let’s pray,

“Father, thank you for giving us a new hope in the risen Lord Jesus Christ. We thank you that because of him, we have certainty in rising to new life. Father, as go through this pandemic, there are many whose hopes are fading. As their hope in the things of this world fades, please help them and help us to turn our eyes towards Jesus. He is the risen Christ, whose glory with you and the Holy Spirit will never dim or fade. May we find our hope and our rest in him always. We pray this in the name of Jesus, Amen.”
