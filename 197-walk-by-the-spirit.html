Hi friends, it’s good to have you with us at Church today!

Over the past few weeks, we’ve been looking at the seven doctrines to help us get a better grasp of the Bible.

They are like seven stars to help us see the bigger picture of the story of the Bible.

The first one was creation.

God is the Creator and we’re the creatures.

The second one was on the Fall.

God is the Holy One and that we’re the sinners.

The third is the doctrine of Judgement.

God is the Judge and that we’re the Judged.

The fourth is the doctrine of salvation.

God is the Saviour and that we’re the saved.

The fifth is the doctrine of resurrection.

Christ is risen and we are those who are risen in Christ

Today, we’re going to look at the doctrine of the Spirit.

In the doctrine of the Spirit, we see that God’s sent His Spirit and that His Spirit lives in us.

What does it mean to have the Spirit live in us?

It means war. What do I mean by that?

The war between the Spirit and the Flesh

The Bible tells us that there’s a war going on between the Spirit and the Flesh.

Look with me at Galatians 5:16, “16 I say, then, walk by the Spirit and you will certainly not carry out the desire of the flesh.”

What’s the Flesh?

The flesh refers to who we are as a person pre-Jesus.

It’s who we are by nature.

Originally, as created beings in God’s image, our nature, our flesh, was good.

Our desires, our actions, our words, were good.

However, when we’ve sinned against the Holy One, our nature has become corrupted with sin.

Rather than following and obeying God as King, our flesh desires to be our own king.

What our flesh wants contrast with what the Spirit wants.

The Spirit is God’s Holy Spirit.

When someone believes in Jesus, God sends His own Holy Spirit to live inside them.

They have a new spiritual birth.

They’re born of the Spirit.

They have a new heart, with new desires.

When the Bible says that the flesh is at war with the spirit, it’s not saying that physical is bad and spiritual is good.

No! When the Bible says that the flesh is at war with the spirit, it’s saying that the old life we have before Jesus is at war with the new life that we have in Jesus.

The part of us that belongs to our life Pre-Jesus is at war with the part of us that’s following Jesus.

There’s no overlap; they’re mutually exclusive.

It’s what we want vs what God wants; it’s what our heart desires vs what the Spirit desires.

That’s what happens inside every person who call themselves a Christian.

There’s a constant battle for you inside of you.

Your flesh wants you to do one thing and God’s Spirit in you wants you to do another.

And so, you don’t do what you want.

One part of you will win and the other part of you will lose.

Is our flesh really that bad? Are we by nature completely opposed to the Spirit?

The Bible’s answer is a resounding ‘Yes’!

Left to our own nature, we oppose God’s Spirit.

That’s why God gave us the law.

God gave us the law to show us what’s right and wrong.

But no matter how hard we try, by our nature, we’re law breakers.

Let me give you an example.

Imagine that for one night, there’ll be no police on our streets, no law in our courts.

And everyone is allowed to do whatever their heart desires in the streets of Sydney, or whatever town you’re in.

For one night, you can get away with murder, robbery, speeding and general lawlessness.

Would you let your children go out that night?

Absolutely not, because if everyone in our community, including us, live out every desire of our flesh, there will be madness and chaos, violence and disorder.

However, look with me at verse 18, “But if you are led by the Spirit, you are not under the law.”

That doesn’t mean that the person led by the Spirit can do whatever they want regardless of the law.

Rather, there’s such a change inside of them that the law doesn’t apply to them in the same way.

There’s such a transformation in them that they are no longer led by a fallen, rebellious nature.

They’re now led by the Spirit, God’s Holy Spirit.

Works of the flesh and the Fruit of the Spirit

How do you know whether you are led by the Spirit or by the flesh?

You do that by observing your life.

Works of the Flesh

Beginning from verse 19, “But now the works of the flesh are obvious: sexual immorality, moral impurity, promiscuity.

Here is a list of bad things that people do.

Many religions have a similar list.

Many people, whether religious or not, will generally agree that these things are wrong.

Many will say that people are generally good and sometimes they behave badly.

These bad behaviours like weeds that we need to get rid of.

If we get rid of them, then the person will generally be a good person.

Rather than saying that these are just weeds to get rid of, the Bible says that these actions spring from the very nature of who we are.

These are the works of the flesh.

It’s essentially saying that we are the problem; we are the weed.

In this list, there are sixteen things listed.

We’re just going to look at them briefly and focus on one in particular.

The first three are closely related and it has to do with sexual purity, moral impurity and sensuality.

The next two have to deal with our corrupted worship.

They are idolatry and sorcery.

And then there are eight things listed that has to do with the way we relate to one another.

hatred, strife, jealousy, outbursts of anger, selfish ambitions, dissensions,

factions, envy – they all describe relationships that have fractured.

The one I want us to focus on is selfish ambition.

It’s trying to get ahead by putting other people down.

It’s being so competitive in the rat race that you climb over other people in order to get to the top.

Over the last few weeks, our family has been listening to the musical ‘Hamilton’ over and over again.

It’s a great musical with many great tunes.

One of the tunes was “The Room where it happens”

It’s a song by Aaron Burr, a man who was envious of Alexander Hamilton’s climb to the top.

He wanted to be an important person, to get to the inner circle, to get in the room where it happens.

Burr’s selfish ambition to get into the inner circle was his drive for the musical.

In the end, it leads him to a duel with Hamilton.

By then, he realises that his selfish ambition which springs from his flesh only leads to more sorrow and suffering.

What does the Bible say is the ultimate destination of the desire of our flesh?

Verse 21, “I am warning you about these things—as I warned you before—that those who practice such things will not inherit the kingdom of God.”

Everyone who continues to follow the desires of his heart will be banned from the Kingdom of Heaven.

As I was preparing the sermon, I saw on the news that the Queensland border is now shut to the people of Greater Sydney.

I’m not allowed to travel through to Queensland anymore; I’m banned from entering Queensland.

While that can be disappointing, it’s nothing comparing with being banned from heaven.

But that’s where everyone who lives by the desires of his or her flesh will be heading.

Whether it’s your family, your friends, or even you, if you haven’t believed in Jesus yet.

If you follow the desires of your flesh, you will be banned from God’s Kingdom forever.

What’s the alternative?

The alternative is to be filled with the Spirit and to bear the fruit of the Spirit.

Fruit of the Spirit

Just as it’s natural to expect an apple from an apple tree, it’s natural to expect someone who was born in the Spirit to bear the fruit of the Spirit.

You will expect someone who has been born again to show sings of the new birth, signs of the new life!

In this passage, there are nine signs that tell us someone has the Holy Spirit.

We’ll go through them briefly.

Again, I’ll just focus on one.

The first one is love.

Love is the one that governs all the others.

Someone who has the Spirit of God will love God and love others.

They’ll also have joy and peace that accompanies that love, no matter what situation they may be facing.

Those who have the Spirit of God will love God and others, express joy and peace, even in the midst of difficulties.

Next, we see a list of qualities that’s other-person centred: patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness.

It’s how they engage with other people.

In particular, I want us to focus on kindness.

To be kind is to be generous and considerate.

To be kind is to think about what’s best for the other person.

It’s such a wonderful quality; it’s so refreshing to have someone being kind to you in a cruel world.

Yet that’s the quality that Christians ought to have because it’s the quality that Jesus their Lord has in abundance.

Just a few verses prior to today’s passage, Christians are told to serve one another in love.

We’re to be kind to one another.

Over the past few weeks, I have often seen your kindness to one another when we catch up with one another via Zoom.

It’s so good to see your concern for one another even though you’re physically apart.

It’s great to see how a number of you step up in a time of crisis and kindly help others who are in need.

As I go through the list, I’m astonished at how desperate our pandemic infested world needs to see the fruit of the Spirit.

Our world needs Love for God and love for neighbour in a time of hate.

Our world needs Joy in a time of deep sorrow.

Our world needs Peace in a time of turmoil.

Our world needs Patience in a time of desperation.

Our world needs Kindness in a time of selfishness

Our world needs Goodness in a time of evil

Our world needs Faithfulness in a time of faithlessness

Our world needs gentleness in a time of a harsh and trying time.

Our world needs self-control in a world driven by selfish desires.

This is not a list of moral things that we need to tick off.

Rather this is a list of prayer points for us.

We need to ask God to so fill us by his Holy Spirit so that we will display his fruit of the Spirit.

We need to ask God to mould us, transform us and shape us by his Spirit, so that we’ll be like Jesus His Son.

There is no law against people who do these things.

Why can a Christian say no to the desires of the flesh and yes to the leading of the Holy Spirit?

3.We’ve crucified the flesh in Christ

It’s because we’ve crucified the flesh in Christ.

Look with me at verse 24, “24 Now those who belong to Christ Jesus have crucified the flesh with its passions and desires.”

If you belong to Christ Jesus then your flesh, along with its passions and desires, died with Jesus on the cross.

On the cross, Jesus carried your sin of sexual immorality, your sin of selfish ambition, your sin of idolatry.

If you belong to Jesus, He took those sins and died on your behalf for you for those sins.

Those desires belong on the cross and died with Jesus.

Don’t let them deceive you and make you think that you’re still under their power, that you’re powerless to resist them.

If you belong to Jesus, then He’s given you His Spirit.

You’re under His Spirit’s lead now; you’re not under the flesh’s lead anymore.

That doesn’t mean that you won’t have struggles against the flesh.

As long as we’re on this side of heaven, we’ll still struggle against the flesh inside of us.

But we don’t do it alone; we struggle and overcome by the power of the Holy Spirit.

Verse 25, “ If we live by the Spirit, let us also keep in step with the Spirit.”

The Spirit who lives in us can transform us.

Let the Holy Spirit does His work in you.

Ask God to transform you so that you can love him more, by the power of the Holy Spirit.

Ask God to change you so that you can be patient with others, by the power of the Holy Spirit.

Live by the Spirit.

Let his fruit in you be a blessing to you and to those around you.

What does it mean then for us to live Spirit-filled lives?

Living Spirit filled lives

Spirit-filled life walks by the Spirit

Firstly, a Spirit-filled life walks by the Spirit

Walk in step with the Spirit.

The Holy Spirit tells us how to walk with him through his Word, the Bible.

Read the Bible, day by day.

Listen to him in the words of the Bible as he teaches you how to walk with him

Spirit-filled life observes the war within and the war without

Second, a Spirit filled life observes the war within you and the war without you, outside of you.

Be honest and take a good look at your life and compare it with the list of the works of the flesh and the list of the fruit of the Spirit.

What does your life show?

What areas of your life show works of the flesh?

What areas of your life show the fruit of the Spirit?

Ask a Christian friend you can trust to be honest with you about the works and fruit in your life.

What do they see in you?

Do they see works of the flesh or the fruit of the Spirit?

Spirit-filled life is a faithful and prayerful life

And finally, bring what you see in your life before God in prayer.

These two lists are not meant to be a list of do’s and don’ts.

It’s not a list for us to make us proud of what we can achieve and to shame others who struggle with the flesh.

Rather it’s a list for us to bring before God in prayer.

It’s a list of prayer points to ask God for his forgiveness when we see the influence of the flesh in our lives.

It’s a list of prayer points to ask God to transform us so that we’ll see more of the fruit of His Spirit in our lives.

The world, now more than ever, needs to see your fruit of the Spirit.

Ask God to transform you, so that wherever you might be, along with other Christians in Christ, we may show the Fruit of the Spirit.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Look at the list of the works of the flesh. What do I need to ask God for forgiveness for?

Look at the list of the fruit of the Spirit. In what area of my life do I especially need God’s power to transform?

How can I walk along in the Spirit this week?

Let’s pray,

‘Father, thank you that on the cross, Jesus has died for my sin. Thank you that on the cross, He has triumphed over the power of sin and has crucified our flesh, with is passions and desires. Father forgive us when we’re tempted to give in to the flesh. Forgive us for cheapening your grace for us. Thank you that you have given us your Holy Spirit, Please transform us by your Spirit so that we may be more like Jesus, in all that we think, say and do. We pray in the name of Jesus, Amen.’
