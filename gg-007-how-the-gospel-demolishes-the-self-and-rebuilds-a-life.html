Growth Groups (adults)

How the Gospel Demolishes the Self and Rebuilds a Life

Galatians 2:15–21 · Sunday 23 August 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

For adult home groups · preached by Jerry Zhu

Big Idea: Every one of us is quietly building a tower of self to prove we are worth something. At the cross God posts a demolition order on it — and then, astonishingly, Christ himself moves in and becomes our life.

Aim: By the end of tonight we want each person to (1) name one thing they have been adding to Christ as the ground of their standing with God, (2) be able to say Galatians 2:20 in the first person and mean it, and (3) leave with three names to pray for and one person to invite before Father’s Day on 6 September.

You will need: Bibles (CSB), pens, and about 60–75 minutes.

1. Connect (10 min)

  • Tell us about a renovation you have done — at home or at work. Was there anything you kept patching up that really should have been knocked down and rebuilt?
  • At morning tea, when you meet someone new, what is the first thing you tell them about yourself — your job, your kids, your suburb, how long you have been at church? What does that reveal about where we quietly get our sense of worth?

2. Read — Galatians 2:15–21 (CSB)

15 We are Jews by birth and not “Gentile sinners,”

16 and yet because we know that a person is not justified by the works of the law but by faith in Jesus Christ, even we ourselves have believed in Christ Jesus. This was so that we might be justified by faith in Christ and not by the works of the law, because by the works of the law no human being will be justified.

17 But if we ourselves are also found to be “sinners” while seeking to be justified by Christ, is Christ then a promoter of sin? Absolutely not!

18 If I rebuild those things that I tore down, I show myself to be a lawbreaker.

19 For through the law I died to the law, so that I might live for God.

20 I have been crucified with Christ, and I no longer live, but Christ lives in me. The life I now live in the body, I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me.

21 I do not set aside the grace of God, for if righteousness comes through the law, then Christ died for nothing.

3. Context

Paul is writing to churches in Galatia that were being unsettled by teachers insisting that believing in Jesus was not quite enough — you also had to be circumcised and keep the law of Moses.

Just before our passage, Paul had to confront Peter publicly at Antioch. Peter had been happily eating with Gentile believers until some Jewish visitors arrived, and then he quietly pulled back (2:11–14). It looked like table manners; it was actually the gospel at stake.

So Galatians 2:15–21 is the heart of Paul’s argument: if right standing with God comes through law-keeping, then Christ died for nothing. This is not a fine point of theology — it is life or death for the good news.

4. Discuss

  1. In verse 16 Paul says the same thing three times over. What exactly is he ruling out, and what is he putting in its place?
  2. Verse 19 says “through the law I died to the law.” What job is the law doing here — and what is it unable to do? (Romans 3:20 may help.)
  3. Paul says he would be a lawbreaker if he rebuilt what he tore down (v.18). What was the building he had demolished?
  4. Verse 20 says “I no longer live, but Christ lives in me.” What does Paul not mean by that? What does he mean?
  5. Why does the last line of verse 20 — “who loved me and gave himself for me” — matter so much? What difference does the word “me” make?
  6. Adults are experts at renaming our sins: “I’m just being honest,” “it was righteous anger,” “I’m only sharing it as a prayer point.” Which of those sounds most like you, and what is it protecting?
  7. Where do you find yourself adding something to Christ — years of service, a decent marriage, a good reputation at church, never missing a Sunday? Be specific.
  8. If verse 20 were true of you on Monday morning at work, or on Tuesday afternoon with your children, what would actually look different?

5. Live It Out

KOG 3·2·1·0 — this week:

  • Pray for 3. Write down three people in your street, workplace or extended family who do not yet know Jesus. Bring the same three names back next week.
  • Connect with 2. Do something ordinary and unhurried with two of them — a coffee, a lift, a hand with something heavy.
  • Invite 1. Invite one person to something. Father’s Day, Sunday 6 September, is a natural door — particularly for dads and grandfathers.
  • 0 left unloved. Notice who in this group, or on the edge of church, has gone quiet. Ring them this week.

Weekly challenge: Each morning this week, before you pick up your phone, say Galatians 2:20 out loud in the first person. Then finish the sentence: “So today I do not have to prove...” and name the thing.

Memory verse: “I have been crucified with Christ, and I no longer live, but Christ lives in me.” (Galatians 2:20)

6. Pray

  • Thank God that our standing is settled — not “God is making me good enough” but “God has declared my case closed.”
  • Pray by name for the three people each person wrote down. Pray for one specific opportunity this week.
  • Ask God to show each of us the tower we are still quietly building, and to give us the freedom of letting it come down.

7. Leader’s Notes

Before you start

Your job tonight is to point people joyfully to Jesus, not to catch anyone out. Guests and not-yet-Christians may well be in the room. Never corner anyone — offer your own answer first on the personal questions (6, 7, 8), and let silence do some work.

Brief answers

  • Q1. Paul rules out “works of the law” — the whole Mosaic system: circumcision, food laws, Sabbath, sacrifices. In its place: faith in Jesus Christ. “Justified” is a courtroom word: God declares the verdict and gives a standing. It is not God slowly making me nice.
  • Q2. The law acts as the executioner, not the surgeon. It exposes sin (Rom 3:20) but cannot remove it — a mirror shows the dirt, it does not wash it off. Death to the law is not the goal; “so that I might live for God” is.
  • Q3. The whole system of being justified by law-keeping. To rebuild it would be to say the cross was not quite enough — an insult to Christ’s death (v.21).
  • Q4. Not “I have been erased” or “I have no personality.” The verb “crucified with” is perfect tense — already done, permanently in force. The self-centred, self-justifying “I” no longer holds the throne. Our sinful nature has not vanished; we still put sin to death daily by the Spirit, but from a settled identity rather than for one.
  • Q5. Christ does not love humanity in the abstract. He loved particular people — you. That personal “me” is the engine of the whole verse and the answer to self-loathing.
  • Q6–8. Personal. Look for the pattern: our worth quietly resting on performance. Gently name grace rather than piling on more effort.

Where this is heading

Our vision is a church growing from faith to faith — 50 of our 75 households now serving densely, on the way to 500 in ten years. Households like that are not built by trying harder; they are built by people who know they are already accepted and therefore have something to give away.
