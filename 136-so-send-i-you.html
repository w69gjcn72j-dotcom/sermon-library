So Send I You

John 20:19-31 · 2020

Big idea: Jesus gives peace to fearful followers and sends us out as Spirit-filled forgiveness givers.

Jesus’ followers hide in fear

Jesus’ presence and peace sparks great joy

Jesus’ followers commissioned

What’s one thing that you’re afraid of?

For many people, it’s cockroaches.

Even though they’re a lot smaller than human beings, many people scream when they see one.

They don’t want to be near one.

One such person was a programmer by the name of Andrew Stein.

Andrew Stein a computer programmer who grew up in New Orleans but now lives in New York, also recalls once being trapped by a roach.

Andrew was in his newly renovated department when he saw a cockroach clinging to his bath towel.

But rather than killing it, he spent the next two hours camped out in the hallway.

When his roommate came home at around 2am, he decided he needed to do something about it.

He paid his roommate $10 to kill the cockroach rather than face it himself.

He said that was one of the most embarrassing moments in his life.

When we face fear, we usually have a flight or fight moment.

For Andrew, his fear drove him to flight.

In today’s passage, we see a group of frightened disciples, who also chose flight over fight.

It was Easter Sunday evening, and they were afraid, they were scared.

How could the first followers of Christ be so afraid of the first part of the Easter message?

They were afraid because of the first part of the Easter message; they were afraid because of the death of Jesus on the cross.

Their fear drove them to flight, to hide in the shadows, very much like cockroaches.

But as we’ll see, though they were the first, these followers of Christ would not be the last to be afraid of the Easter message.

Who could possibly be afraid of the Easter message?

If you have your Bible, please open up with me to John 20 beginning from verse 19.

We’ll see three things:

Jesus’ followers commissioned

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Father, please take all distractions away from us, that we might hear your word about Jesus, the Only One who can calm our darkest fears. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

Jesus’ followers hide in fear

Please open up your Bible with me to John 20:19, ‘When it was evening on that first day of the week, the disciples were gathered together with the doors locked because they feared the Jews. Jesus came, stood among them, and said to them, “Peace be with you.”’

On the night of the first night of Easter, the disciples locked themselves in; they didn’t want to leave the house.

Their Master and Teacher, the Lord Jesus, was crucified on the cross and it had been three days since his death.

They knew that it was the Jews who had plotted against Jesus.

The Jews succeeded to killing Jesus; and now they’ve come for his followers.

And so, they locked their door, and they hid behind it.

We’ve seen this in wars throughout history, don’t we?

A powerful army of one nation overpowered a weaker nation and soldiers were sent to hunt down any survivors of the weaker nation.

If the army, the professional men and women, could not overcome the enemies, what chance did the civilians have?

Similarly, if the loving, perfect and powerful Jesus was led was sentenced to death because of the Jews, what chance did the disciples have?

Now that Jesus was gone, who could protect them from the Jews?

The first followers of Jesus were frightened by those who opposed Jesus and many followers of Jesus since have also been frightened by those who also oppose Jesus.

There are many countries in the world where it’s illegal to be a Christian.

There are countries in the world where Christians are actively hunted.

More broadly, there are many places, including both Eastern and Western countries, where conversion to Christianity is prohibited by the law.

Even where they’re not illegal, those who oppose Jesus will openly ridicule people who call themselves Christians.

I recently watched am episode of a very well-made Science fiction TV show where people keep asking about their belief in God.

A scientist would ask other people and even other scientist, “Do you believe in God?”

And the response will almost always be negative.

It’ll be along the lines of, “Seriously, believing in God? Are you kidding me?” or “Even though all the scientific evidence I’ve seen say that there are a lot of problems with my theories, I would believe in science and I don’t believe in God.”

Instead of investigating the evidence and look at the plausibility of believing in God against the plausibility of not believing in God,

And letting the evidence speak in favour of God,

They still refuse to believe in God and ridicule anyone who does.

As a result, many Christians shut the door on discussion about what they believe.

They don’t tell anyone that they’re Christians or stand up for what they believe in.

Instead, they hide behind the door, living in fear, lest they get ridiculed, sued, or even killed for being a Christian.

However, Jesus doesn’t want them to live in fear.

And so, he comes in among the disciples and brings them peace and joy.

Jesus’ presence and peace sparks great joy

‘Jesus came, stood among them, and said to them, “Peace be with you.”’

Though their doors were locked, Jesus suddenly stood among them.

It must have given the disciples quite a fright!

Can you imagine someone suddenly appear next to you in your locked room?

They probably would’ve asked themselves, “Is this a man? Or a ghost?”

And to reassure them, Jesus says to them, “Peace be with you”.

It’s a common phrase that would’ve been used by the Jews in Jesus’ time.

In fact, it’s still used by many Jews today.

שָׁלוֹם עֲלֵיכֶם - Shalom Aleikhem

Peace be with you.

What does peace mean?

Someone might say that peace is the absence of war.

If we don’t have war, then we have peace.

But strictly speaking, the absence of war is not peace, but a truce.

Just because you’re not fighting doesn’t mean that you have peace.

Two months ago, my family and I went to the Demilitarised Zone (DMZ).

It’s an area 250km long and 2km wide and it marks the separation between North Korea and South Korea.

Even though the Korean war ended over 70 years ago, officially it’s at a stalemate.

The North Koreans can’t go to South Korea and the South Koreans can’t go to the north.

It’s considered one of the most dangerous places on Earth.

There’s currently no fighting, but there certainly is no peace.

The peace that Jesus talks about is not a truce.

Instead, Jesus is talking about a perfect, harmonious life.

It’s a life where we’re right with God, where we’re right with people, living under God’s righteous rule.

It’s a life that one day will be realised in the new heaven and the new earth, but it’s a life that those who follow Jesus can get a glimpse of.

Those of us who follow Jesus have the hope of this new life as we follow what Jesus teaches, as written in the Bible.

This is the life that Jesus wants for his disciples; this is the hope that Jesus wants for his disciples.

This is the news, the good news, that the disciples needed to hear.

In order to bring them peace of mind, Jesus showed them his hands and his side.

Why his hands? Because there would’ve been nail marks where they nailed him to the cross.

Why the side? Because to make sure Jesus was really dead, they pierced his side with a spear and blood and spear.

Jesus was showing them that he was the One whom they saw dying on the cross just three days ago.

He was the One who died and was placed in the tomb.

He was the One who was raised from the dead, never to die again.

And he had the scars to prove it.

When Jesus rose from the dead, it wasn’t a spirit flowing away from his body, like you see in cartoons.

No, his body was raised from the dead and he has a new body, a body that will never die again.

And incredibly, Jesus rose with a body that had the scars from the cross.

Our God, our Lord Jesus Christ, is known by his scars!

A poet by the name of Temple wrote a poem called Jesus of the Scars.

Here’s an excerpt from it:

“The other gods were strong; but Thou wast weak;

They rode, but Thou didst stumble to a throne;

But to our wounds only God’s wounds can speak,

And not a god has wounds, but Thou alone.”

One day, when Jesus comes back, his followers will recognise him.

Yes, he will be full of power and glory, and angels upon angels will come with him.

But how will his followers know that it’s really Jesus? They will know him by his scars, for Revelation 5, Jesus is described to be like a slaughtered lamb, standing in the midst of the throne of God.

And thousands upon thousands of angels will all sing in one voice, “Worthy is he Lamb who was slaughtered to receive power and riches and wisdom and strength and honour and glory and blessing!”

Jesus Christ, the slaughtered Lamb, the one with the scars is the one who rose from the dead, who conquered death.

It’s because Jesus has risen that we have hope, hope not in ourselves but hope that he will take us to him, no matter what happens.

In this life, we will have troubles, we will have sickness, we will have fears.

But the risen Lord Jesus will be there, always.

The Risen Lord Jesus is the answer to all our fears.

When the disciples saw the evidence of the risen Lord, their fear turned to joy.

When we feel overwhelmed by the troubles of this life, the heartache this life brings, the worries that each day brings, turn to Jesus.

He can turn our fear into joy, even in the very midst of our fears.

He doesn’t say he’ll take what causes the fear away; he doesn’t promise that you will never be afraid any more.

However, he promises that no matter what happens, he will be there.

And because he’s there, we will have peace, we will rejoice.

One of the most encouraging message I have received recently was from Range and Jenny about a sister called Vera.

Not our Vera, another Vera.

We’ve prayed for Vera here at Church and in our prayer groups.

Vera has been unwell at St George Hospital and I was called on a Sunday to go and visit her.

I didn’t know, but Vera had COVID at that time and so the hospital staff told me not to go in.

However, when Vera felt better, she called the Church, which was diverted to Range at that time.

Range was able to pray for her and share Bible passage with her in English.

Jenny also had a chance to visit her last week.

I can see that in her messages, she was initially afraid because she had a number of illnesses.

However over time, her faith strengthened.

In one of her messages she said, “God be my strength made perfect in my weaknesses.”

Oh, she recognised her weaknesses, she felt weak but God is her strength in her weaknesses.

She has joy, even in the midst of her fears.

Friends, that’s what those who follow Jesus have.

We have joy even in the midst of our fears, even in the midst of our weaknesses, because we follow the risen Jesus.

For we know that he will be with us, all the way until we reach our home in heaven.

Because Jesus is with us, we have hope, an endless hope.

Because we have this endless hope, we also have joy, we also have peace.

Jesus’ followers commissioned

But this peace, this joy, is not selfish.

The peace the Christians enjoy are not to be enjoyed alone.

We see this in 20:21, “Jesus said to them again, “Peace be with you. As the Father has sent me, I also send you.”

Jesus’ disciples have received peace from the God, and now Jesus sends them out so that other people too, will have this peace.

As the Father has sent me, I also send you.

How does the Father send Jesus?

Look with me at John 3:16-17, “ 16 For God loved the world in this way: He gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who believes in him will not perish but have eternal life. 17 For God did not send his Son into the world to condemn the world, but to save the world through him.”

Jesus took on flesh, Jesus obeyed God perfectly, suffered willingly, died willingly, in order to save the world.

Christians, as sons and daughters of God, also obey God the Father and God the Son, and will bring the good news of Jesus to all the world, so that the world may be saved.

When I was learning to be a student minister, I observed my mentors, and I learn how to preach and teach from them.

Even now, Jenny says that I preach and sound like my first mentor!

How does Jesus send us out? Like how God the Father sent him out.

Now that doesn’t mean we are to come down from heaven like Jesus, born in a manger like Jesus and heal all kinds of Jesus, like Jesus.

There are actions and events that are unique to Jesus that only Jesus can do as part of him being sent by God the Father.

However, just as Jesus obeyed God the Father and followed the Father’s will in all that he does as part of his mission,

as part of what the Father has sent the Son to do.

The followers of Jesus are called to obey Jesus, God the Son and follow his teachings, as part of our mission,

as part of what the Son has sent us to do.

We don’t start a new mission, we don’t replace Jesus’ mission.

Rather, we continue Jesus’ mission, which was originated from what God the Father had sent Jesus to do.

Jesus, who obeyed God perfectly, has brought us peace with God through his death on the cross and his resurrection from the dead.

We, though we obey Jesus imperfectly, also bring this message of peace to others as we proclaim the good news of the death and resurrection of Jesus Christ.

You see, this sending out from both God the Father and God the Son is more than just telling people, it’s more than just a transfer of facts.

At the heart of this sending out from God the Father and God the Son is obedience.

At the heart of this sending out is God the Son obeying God the Father, and our obedience to God the Son.

We’re more than just proclaimers of the good news, we are to be the message of the good news.

That is, we are to live out in obedience what the good news of Jesus looks like.

As someone once said, the medium is the message.

Before someone can hear the good news of Jesus, they will see whether you live out the good news of Jesus, they will see whether you obey the Lord you say you follow.

If you believe the gospel, you will obey Jesus and your audience, your friends and your families will see it.

If you don’t believe the gospel, you won’t obey Jesus and your audience, though they hear you talk about it, they won’t believe it.

People see the gospel in us before they hear the gospel from us.

How can we obey Jesus, when his standards are so high, when his standards are perfect?

To be a Christian is a high calling; as many true Christians will tell you, being a not a calling for the faint-hearted.

We don’t try to obey Jesus by our own power.

Rather, we obey Jesus by the Spirit that Jesus has given.

Look with me at verse 22, “22 After saying this, he breathed on them and said, “Receive the Holy Spirit.”

What Jesus did here is like a living parable.

He visibly breathed, showing them that one day, they will receive the Holy Spirit.

On that day, they will be filled with the Holy Spirit of God

On that day, they won’t be locking themselves at home.

On that day, they will fearlessly proclaim the gospel.

On that day, they will live out a life of obedience to Jesus.

The Gospel that Christians proclaim aren’t just thoughts of a scholar or some general principles about right living.

No, ultimately, the gospel is about whether someone obeys Jesus.

If a person believes the gospel and obeys Jesus, they will be forgiven and can look forward to having eternal life with Jesus.

If a person rejects the gospel and disobey Jesus, they won’t be forgiven and will remain destined for eternal destruction.

Look with me at verse 23:

“23 If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven them; if you retain the sins of any, they are retained.”

Sin is our innate desire to do what we want instead of what God wants.

Sin is really our desire to have even more authority over our lives, over the lives of others, than even God himself.

And so, ultimately, every sin, whether it’s a sinful thought or sinful word or a sinful deed, is a sin against God himself.

Forgiveness of sins, then, is something that only God can ultimately do since he’s the One that’s been ultimately been sinned against.

We can forgive people, on a smaller scale, but true forgiveness can only from God himself.

However, Jesus here gives the authority to forgive to his disciples:

If the disciples forgive you your sins, your sins against God are forgiven, you will be right with God.

If the disciples don’t forgive you your sins, your sins are retained, you will remain on the wrong side of God.

If the disciples forgive you, you will have eternal bliss because your sins are forgiven.

If the disciples don’t forgive you, you will face eternal suffering because your sins are retained.

That’s a lot of authority that’s been given to the disciples.

How did the disciples exercise this authority? How do they determine whose sins will be forgiven and whose sins will be retained?

By their proclamation of the good news of Jesus, by their sending out by Jesus, God the Son.

We can respond to the gospel by either receiving or rejecting the Gospel.

There is no middle ground.

It is God himself who does the forgiving and we confirm God’s forgiveness according to what he promised in the gospel.

If we receive Jesus then God the Father will receive us, as he would his Son

That’s the Good News of Jesus,

However, if we reject him, then he will reject us; we will remain God’s enemies.

And so, friends, please trust in Jesus, and your sins against God is forgiven.

Your sin is what stops your from entering heaven.

Until your sins are forgiven, until you put your trust in Jesus, you will never enter into heaven.

This is the authority that the first disciples of Jesus and all the disciples of Jesus who follow their teaching have:

If we tell our world the gospel, and they believe in Jesus, they’re forgiven.

But if we don’t tell them the gospel, they won’t be forgiven.

There’s nothing more important than to know that you’re forgiven by God.

Would you tell this gospel of forgiveness to your friends? Your enemies?

Amber Guyger is a former Dallas police officer who was found guilty of murdering Botham Jean.

Guyger was white, her victim Botham Jean was afro-American.

During the trial, Botham’s brother Brandt gave a statement in which he said he forgave Amber Guyger.

Instead, he told her to look to Christ.

Brandt told her that loved her and asked if the Judge whether he could give her a hug.

Here’s a brief excerpt of what happened:

“I forgive you. I know that if you go to God and ask him, he will forgive you.

Again, I’m speaking for myself and not even for my family, but I love you just like anyone else.

And I’m not gonna say I hope you rot and die, just like my brother did.

But I personally want the best for you.

And I wasn’t gonna say this in front of my family or anyone but I don’t even want you to go to jail.

I want the best for you because that’s exactly what Botham (my brother) would want you to do.

And the best would be to give your life to Christ.

I’m not gonna say anything else but I think giving your life to Christ would be the best thing that Botham wants.

Again I love you as a person…..

I don’t know if it’s possible, but can I give her a hug please? Please?”

Friends, there’s great power in forgiveness.

Brandt wanted that forgiveness for Amber; he wants to see her in heaven, even though she killed his brother.

He wants her to be forgiven, not just by him, but by God.

The judge might pronounce her guilty, and rightly so.

But he can pronounce her forgiven.

Guilty, but forgiven, by God and by him.

So, who’s afraid of the Easter message?

Christians might be afraid because of how the world might respond.

However, since Jesus has risen from the dead, we don’t need to be fear.

Even in the midst of our fears, we will have joy.

Why does the world react so angrily to the Easter message?

I think it’s because the world is afraid.

The world is afraid of what it will mean for them if the Easter message was true.

They are afraid of how they might be exposed if they do trust in Jesus.

Their fear is shown in anger and in ridicule.

And if the gospel that we Christians speak of is true, and it is, then the world should be afraid.

For forgiveness comes only through the gospel of Jesus Christ.

If Jesus is the only way of forgiveness, and He is, then rejecting him is rejecting heaven.

To reject Jesus is to reject God.

To reject Jesus is for sins to be retained and to face the eternal punishment from God.

There is every reason for those who don’t believe in Jesus to be afraid.

For in Jesus there is Endless Hope but without Jesus, there is only a Hopeless End.

What do you have? A hopeless end, or an endless hope?

Friends, this Easter, remember that:

Jesus gives peace to fearful followers and sends us out as Spirit-filled forgiveness givers.

After the trial of Amber Guyger, the judge Tammy Kemp, gave Guyger a Bible and said, “‘This is the one I use every day. This is your job for the next month. It says right here. John 3:16—and this is where you start—“For God so loved the world…”’”

Don’t be afraid to share the good news of forgiveness that’s found in Jesus Christ.

Over the past few months, we have talked with the people around Kogarah and many are willing to have a chat about their beliefs and hear what we have to say about Jesus.

We have outreach programme like KOGWorx in the Children centre, Open Days throughout the year, where we send out our church members to share the good news of Jesus.

There will be more events coming up this year.

Keep an eye out for them in the coming months!

If you’re not a Christian yet, then don’t delay.

Do business with God today.

The Easter message is a good news message because it shows us how we can have endless hope instead of a hopeless end.

If you want to be forgiven by God, if you want to trust in Jesus and become a follower of Jesus, then you can do that now by praying to him.

I’m going to pray a short prayer.

I’ll say it slowly, one sentence at a time.

If you agree with it, then say it in your heart with me.

Let’s pray,

“Our heavenly Father,

Thank you for sending your Son into the world to save someone like me.

Thank you, Lord Jesus, that you were obedient to Jesus, died on the cross to save someone like me.

Please forgive me for trying to run my own life, trying to do things my way instead of yours.

Please forgive me for my foolishness and my sinfulness.

I praise and thank you for rising to life, giving me an eternal hope that will neither spoil nor fade.

Holy Spirit, please help me to obey Jesus and give me the faith to believe that in Jesus, I no longer have a hopeless end but an endless hope.

Amen.”

Friends, if you have prayed that prayer, your sins are forgiven.

Please don’t go until you speak to someone about it today.

We’re about to have the Lord’s Supper, where we remember what Jesus has done for us.

Before we have the Lord’s Supper, here are three questions for us to ponder on both now and later on throughout the week:

What makes you afraid?

Why does the resurrection of Jesus give us joy?

Where does Jesus want you to go today?

On this Easter morning, let’s remember that Jesus gives peace to his fearful followers and he sends us out as Spirit-filled forgiveness givers.
