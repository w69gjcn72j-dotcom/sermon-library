Mary’s treasure – Hope for the fearful

Passage: Luke 2:8-20

Big Idea: Don’t fear but receive Christ the Lord with great joy

A weary world in fear

Good news for a weary world

Christ the Lord is born

Glory to God, Peace on earth to men he favours

Joyfully receiving the good news

Be Amazed like the hearers

Investigate like the shepherds

Treasure the news like Mary

Glorify God with the angels and fellow men

Over the past few weeks, our Church has welcomed two new babies.

One’s Becky‘s baby, Luca and the other is Hazel, En Bo and Enya’s baby girl.

In the coming weeks, the two new mothers (and fathers) will learn more about their new baby.

They will learn about the new baby‘s sleeping pattern

Maybe not in the first few weeks because there’s probably none.

But they will learn.

They‘ll learn about the baby’s eating pattern.

They’ll soon find out whether the baby is good at feeding.

But over time, they‘ll learn about their crying pattern.

They‘ll learn that some cries are cries for tiredness, others are cries for hunger.

Over time, they‘ll be able to tell which is which.

But they will also hear cries that they’re not familiar with.

Sometimes the baby will cry out of fear for something the parents quite can‘t understand.

And the parents will do the best they can to calm those fears.

As the children grow up, the parents will continue to do the best they can to calm any fears that the children will face.

That’s what parents do.

However, where do parents go when they have fears?

Who do the parents turn to?

In the middle of the Christmas story, we hear this kind command from an angel, “Do not fear”.

In the Bible, God‘s often been described as the Heavenly Father.

God knows what makes us afraid.

What does God do to calm our fears?

What’s God‘s answer to our fear?

Before we look into God’s word, let‘s pray,

“Father, on this Christmas morning, please help us see your answer to our fear. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”

A weary world in fear

In many ways, this year has been a dreadful year for many people around the world.

Around this time last year, many people had already had a difficult 2020 and were looking for a new year with a new hope.

And the year started well, the COVID numbers were slowly decreasing all around the world.

Things were looking like it‘s going back to normal.

And then, in June, COVID delta struck and the lockdown process came back.

Homeschooling resumed, people worked from home again.

Things were looking better about a month ago as we slowly lift the restriction process.

Then COVID Omicron came and you can feel the dread level in our community starting to rise again.

You can hear the collective sigh from all around the world, ’Not this again!‘

Here’s a French cartoon that describes the mood around the world.

It shows a man arriving in a Doctor‘s office for a COVID-19 vaccine with this caption:

”I am here for the fifth shot because of the third wave. Or vice versa.“

This captures the mood of many people from all around the world

People are exhausted.

Plans are changed; Christmas gatherings rescheduled; Holidays cancelled.

Chen Jun is a tech company worker in Shenzhen.

He had to take multiple covid test during the outbreak there back in June.

Jun says, “I’m starting to think we’ll never see an end to the pandemic.”

Sound familiar?

For many of us, we are fearful of this sense of endlessness.

We have no idea when it’s going to end and it’s this endlessness that we’re fearful about.

We are afraid that it’s not going to end anytime soon.

Different people have responded differently to the crisis.

Some have responded by denying it.

“It’s not that bad; it’s just like the flu!”

Others have responded by trying to fix the problem themselves.

That’s what many researchers, health professionals and government officials are doing all over the world.

I am very thankful for them and I wish them every success.

However, it looks like we’re still in the middle of the pandemic and haven’t found the way out just yet.

In the meantime, many in the community are still living in fear and frustration.

And so, still some others simply given up hope and just try to isolate themselves in the hope that they’ll ride the COVID wave through.

They isolate themselves from the society for fear that they will catch COVID.

It’s horrible to live in fear of COVID but dig a little deeper and we’ll see that it’s really a fear of something even more common than COVID.

Our fear of COVID lies in our fundamental fear of death.

We fear death and all that the pain and misery that comes before it.

But…the Good news is that God knows our fear of death and he knows how to deal with it.

This is what we see in the good news that he tells the shepherds at the first Christmas.

It’s Good News for a weary world.

Good news for a weary world

Look with me at Luke 2:8, “In the same region, shepherds were staying out in the fields and keeping watch at night over their flock.”

Shepherds are people who looked after sheep.

In the New Testament times, people used to look down on shepherds.

Because they spent most of their time away from people to look after sheep, many people didn’t trust them.

In fact, they were disqualified from being legal witnesses.

That’s why what happened next was so incredible, in verse 9,

“Then an angel of the Lord stood before them, and the glory of the Lord shone around them, and they were terrified.”

Out of all the people that the angel of the Lord could’ve appeared to, he appeared to the shepherds.

If the Bible was fake and made up by people, they would not have chosen to use the shepherds as witnesses of the angel.

They would’ve used priests, kings, the important people.

But God chose to reveal his good news to the world through these lowly shepherds.

When these shepherds saw the angel of the Lord, they were terrified.

Literally, the Bible said that they feared a great fear.

And it’s understandable, when you’ve been looking after the sheep on a dark night and suddenly someone shining with the brilliant glory of the angel appeared,

You’ll fear a great fear as well.

The angel of the Lord saw their fear and spoke to them in words of comfort, “Don’t be afraid”.

Why shouldn’t they be afraid?

“For look, I proclaim to you good news of great joy that will be for all the people.”

The answer to the shepherd’s great fear is the good news of great joy.

Many years ago, a young mother was sitting outside a surgery with her husband.

She had an unexplained growth and her Doctor had told her to get tested.

Fearing the worst, she and her husband went to see to see the Doctor for the result.

She had a procedure done many years ago which meant that she wouldn’t be able to have more children, so they had already ruled pregnancy out.

When the Doctor saw them, he said to them, “It’s not cancer, it’s a baby!”

For some reason, the procedure that she had didn’t stop her from having a baby.

The fear that they had had turned into good news of great joy!

And here in the Bible, we have an even greater news.

The Child that was born this day was no ordinary child.

Listen to how the angel describes this child in verse 11, “Today in the city of David a Saviour was born for you, who is the Messiah, the Lord.”

This child will be the Saviour, the Christ, the Lord.

Firstly, he’s the Saviour.

The word Saviour means that he’s the rescuer.

In the past, when God’s people were in trouble, God would send his saviours, his rescuers, to save his people.

This Baby that was born that night will be the Saviour.

God’s people are facing danger and he’s come to save them.

At this point, the angel doesn’t tell us explicitly what danger he’s come to save them from.

That will become more clearer later.

But the angel does want us to remember: This Baby is the Saviour.

This Baby will also be the Messiah.

The word Messiah means the anointed One.

In the Old Testament, when God wanted to choose a King for his people, he would send someone to anoint him.

And so, throughout the Bible the King of Israel has often been described as the Anointed One.

This Baby will be the Anointed One, chosen by God to be the King of his people.

This Baby will also be the Lord.

This Baby will be the Master, the One-in-charge.

Again, in the Old Testament, the word translated Lord here is often used of God.

God is the Lord, the Master.

While it’s possible for an ordinary human to be also called Lord in the Bible, most of the time the term refers to God.

Seeing that it’s an angel of the Lord announcing this message, there’s a very close association between this baby, who is called Lord, and the One who’s sent the angel, the Lord!

This Baby who is born will be the Saviour, the Christ, the Lord.

While today we see these three terms, Saviour, Christ and Lord as religious terms,

The people in the first century would’ve seen them as political terms.

The Roman ruler at that time of Jesus’ birth, Caesar Augustus, was widely celebrated as the Saviour of the world.

Augustus, of course, would see himself as the King, the Ruler and of course, the Lord.

Saviour, Christ, and Lord were highly political terms.

Indeed, even if some political leaders don’t use the exact term today, they also see themselves in these terms.

They describe themselves as saving the country from ruin and corruption, from external forces and even from COVID.

Though they might not be kings, they may still be celebrated as the supreme ruler and the one in charge.

They are the answers to the country’s problems; some even claim to the be the answer to the world’s problems.

In one way or another, some political leaders see themselves as Saviour, Christ and Lord, just as Caesar Augustus did.

But the angel says that the Baby born this night, in the City of David, is the true Saviour, Christ and Lord.

He’s the One God’s chosen to be the Saviour, Christ and Lord.

And on this night, the shepherds will find him tightly wrapped in cloth and lying in a manger.

To echo the significance of this announcement, a multitude of the heavenly host, an army of heavenly beings, praised God in the presence of the shepherds saying,

“Glory to God in the highest heaven, and peace on earth to people he favours!”

Joy filled the heavens at the announcement of this Baby’s birth.

God gets the glory in the highest heaven because his Baby is born.

And on earth, there is peace.

This is Shalom, the peace that the Bible speaks of when there is perfect harmony between God and his people and his creation.

This is the greeting we heard last week when our Jewish friends came to visit us.

This is Shalom, a peace that drives out fear.

We might have many things we’re fearful of, but at the root of it is the fear of death and all the pain and suffering that comes with it.

There is death, pain and suffering in our world because of our rebellion against God.

Our rebellion against God has robbed us of the peace that God has intended for us from the beginning.

But with the coming of this baby, our peace, our Shalom with God will be restored.

However, note that this peace is not for everyone.

This peace that God promises is not a blanket well-wishing peace for the whole world.

This peace is for people he favours.

Not everyone gets God’s peace.

The peace that God offers is a gift from God on those whom he favours.

His peace is a gift that he gives to those according to his good pleasure, to those according to his will.

Over the past few weeks, my children have been getting us presents and they’ve placed them under the Christmas tree.

There are gifts there with my name on it.

Out of their free will, using their pocket money, they prepared gifts for me and put my name on it.

It’s a gift they’ve prepared for me.

I can’t wait to open it!

God’s peace is God’s gift to those whom he chooses.

It’s for us, personally, with our name on it, as it were.

God’s gift of peace is not experienced by everyone, but only those whom he favours.

We can wish for peace, we can try to achieve peace, we can even try to buy peace, but it’ll always be out of reach for only God can give true peace.

Peace is something that God alone can give, and he wants to give us his peace.

And the way God’s given us his peace is through the Lord Jesus.

True peace lies with him; He is the gift of peace that God gives to the world.

If you want peace, true peace, you need to know Jesus, the Saviour, the Christ, the Lord.

As the old saying goes, “Know Jesus, know peace; no Jesus, no peace.”

It’s through Jesus that God will bring peace to us for Jesus is God’s answer to our fear of death.

It’s a peace that we can’t buy or achieve on our own.

It’s a peace that we can only receive by trusting in the Lord Jesus.

If you believe in Jesus, then this peace is yours, then you know that you have God’s favour.

If you don’t believe in Jesus, then this peace remains out of reach.

However, the fact that you’re here today, in person or online, means that you are already in a more favoured place than those who aren’t.

The fact that you are here today, hearing this message, brings you closer to God’s favour than those who aren’t here and haven’t believed in Jesus yet.

Don’t waste this opportunity to receive God’s gift.

Make the most of this opportunity today to receive God’s gift.

Joyfully receiving the good news

Like the first hearers of the good news, joyfully receive the good news!

After the shepherds heard the good news from the angel of the Lord, they investigated.

They went to look for the Baby lying in the manger.

They investigated not because they doubted the angel, but they want to see the Good News for themselves.

Good news is worth investigating and celebrating.

Be like the shepherds, receive the good news and investigate it.

Second, receive the good news by glorifying God with angels and fellow men.

The mighty heavenly host responded to the announcement of the Birth of Jesus with joy and praises.

The lowly shepherds responded to the good news of the Birth of Jesus with joy and praises.

Be like the angels and the shepherds, glorify God with joy and the loudest of praises!

And be like Mary, who treasured up all these things in her heart and meditated on them.

Mary responded on what God has promised about the child by reflecting on them.

Be like Mary, keep reflecting, keep thinking about what God’s has promised about Jesus.

As Baby Jesus grew, Mary would’ve done all shoe could to look after him and to help him grow.

Like a good mother, Mary would’ve learnt his cries, Mary would’ve learnt what made Jesus afraid.

Mary would’ve calmed Jesus, perhaps with a song and maybe even a prayer.

In time, this Baby would grow and become the Saviour that God’s promised him to be.

He would deal with death itself, the fear that plagues us all.

With one loud cry, he would give up his spirit, to bring God’s peace to us.

Three days later, he would rise from the dead to show us that he has triumphed over death.

Jesus is God’s answer to our greatest fear.

Indeed, Jesus is God’s answer to all our fears.

Jesus is the Saviour; he is the Christ; he is the Lord.

Jesus is God’s answer to a weary world.

How should we respond?

In the words of the song we just sang, O Come let us adore Him, Christ the Lord.

Like many in our community, you might be going through a time of fear and dread.

You might be fearful of what 2022 and the future hold.

Hear the words of the angel to the shepherds, ““Don’t be afraid, for look, I proclaim to you good news of great joy that will be for all the people: 11 Today in the city of David a Saviour was born for you, who is the Messiah, the Lord.”

Come to him like the shepherds; adore him like Mary; Praise and invite people to come to him like the angels and the shepherds.

Don’t miss out on God’s favour; receive the good news of Jesus today.

O Come let us adore Him, Christ the Lord.

Let us pray,

“Father, we thank you for Jesus, the Saviour, Christ, the Lord. We thank you that you’ve sent him to give us peace. Father, if there are those listening who still haven’t received the good news of Jesus yet, please be merciful and show your favour to them, that they may know the peace that calms all our fears. Please show mercy to us, and calm our fears, as we come and adore Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

Soli Deo Gloria · St Paul's Anglican Kogarah
