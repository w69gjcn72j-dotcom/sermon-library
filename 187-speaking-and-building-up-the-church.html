One of the most exclusive pieces of music in the 18th Century was Allegri’s famous “Miserere mei Deus”.

It was exclusive because it was performed only in Vatican in the Holy Week before Easter.

And it was forbidden to be copied.

However, in the spring of 1770, a fourteen years old boy by the name of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart was in Vatican with his father.

He knew it was forbidden to copy the music but he listened and memorised the 12 minutes long music after listening to it once.

He went home and wrote down every note, every choral part he could remember.

He went back to listen to it again on Friday and completed it with some minor adjustments.

Mozart was gifted and use his gift to share Allegri’s Miserere with the world.

Wouldn’t it be incredible to have a gift like Mozart’s?

If you are as gifted as Mozart, what musical piece would you to memorise?

Over the past three weeks, we’ve been looking at the gifts that God gives at Church.

God’s given gifts like faith, wisdom, gifts of healing and even the gifts of miracles to the Church.

Last week, we’ve learnt that vital to all these gifts is love.

We’ve seen that it’s love, God’s love, that gives all these gifts their meaning.

Paul wants us to pursue love, to make sure that we have love.

As well as pursuing love, there’s one gift that he wants us to go after above all other gifts.

What’s the one gift that Paul wants us to long for?

What’s one gift that all of us should want for? Even pray for?

Building yourself or building the Church

Building yourself or the Church

If there’s one gift that Paul wants us to long for, it’s the gift of prophesy.

Look with me at 1 Corinthians 14:1-3

“Pursue love and desire spiritual gifts, and especially that you may prophesy. 2 For the person who speaks in a tongue is not speaking to people but to God, since no one understands him; he speaks mysteries in the Spirit. 3 On the other hand, the person who prophesies speaks to people for their strengthening, encouragement, and consolation.”

Paul wants his readers to long for the gift of prophesy.

Throughout today’s passage, Paul contrasts the gift of prophesy and the gift of speaking in tongues.

What are these two gifts?

Firstly, tongues (方言).

While the word translated tongue can refer to languages, the way Paul presents it here seem to suggest a special language that somebody uses when speaking with God.

This doesn’t happen to every Christian, but apparently, it must have happened to some Corinthian Christians.

There would be times, probably as they met in Church or as they prayed to God, when they would speak a language that was only understood by God.

Without interpretation, no one understood them.

In fact, they couldn’t understand the words themselves.

That’s the gift of tongues, a gift that the Corinthians appeared to champion, even to boast about.

Then there’s the gift of prophecy.

A few weeks ago, I said that the gift of prophecy here refers to a conviction (strong belief) about God’s word and the future consequence of our obedience or disobedience to him.

It’s a response to special needs and issues that the listeners are facing.

It’s a word about how to respond to our situation in light of God’s word.

In the Old Testament, we have prophets who have often brought God’s message to both God’s people and even God’s enemies, telling them to repent or else face the judgements of God.

They are the spokespeople of God who spoke with God’s authority when they say. “Thus says the Lord.”

Here, in 1 Corinthians 14, those who have the gift of prophesy doesn’t have the same authority as the Old Testament prophets.

They also speak the truth of course, but they speak it in light of what God has already revealed in His word, and they will speak with reference to who Jesus is and what he’s done.

While it can have a future element, the gift of prophecy primarily has to do with understanding and applying God’s word in a believer’s life.

Now, an interesting thing happens in the Chinese translation.

Rather than using the word that’s usually translated as prophecy (預言), it’s here translated as 先 知 講 道, or simply 講 道.

In Chinese, we usually restrict 講 道 to what I do here on the Pulpit on Sunday mornings, namely preaching.

While the gift of prophecy 先知傳道can and does include preaching, its application is far wider than that.

Verse 3 tells us that the one who prophesies 先知傳道speaks to the people for their

strengthening, encouragement, and consolation.

The one who prophesises 先知傳道use God’s word to strengthen the weak, they use God’s word to encourage those who are scared and they use God’s word to console those who have loss.

While I certainly hope my sermons do that, I’m sure there are many other opportunities around the Church where this happens as well.

I hope that many brothers and sisters around the Church would join me as I use God’s word to strengthen, to encourage and to console.

It’s as those who use God’s word to strengthen, encourage and to console others at Church that they build up the Church.

Look with me at verse 4, “4 The person who speaks in a tongue builds himself up, but the one who prophesies builds up the church.”

Throughout this passage, Paul asks the question: do you come to Church to build yourself or to build the Church?

It’s like the difference between a back-up band and a band like the Beatles.

The back-up band is there mainly for the star performer, usually the singer.

The focus is on the star-performer.

However, a band like the Beatles is a band that travels around together, and they write songs together.

It’s not about one particular person but they’re there to support one another, to back up one another.

The Church is more like a band like the Beatles than a back-up band.

We come to Church to support one another and to back up one another.

If there’s any focus, it shouldn’t be on us but on Jesus Christ.

We come together to sing Jesus’ praise.

We don’t come to bring glory to ourselves but to bring glory to Jesus and to help one another bring glory to Jesus.

Church is not all about us; Church is about how to build up one another.

The gift of prophesy 先知傳道builds up the members of the Church.

The one who speaks in tongues doesn’t build up others; he builds up himself as he speaks in tongues.

It might be helpful to speak in tongues as you pray to God in private but in a public setting, it’s far, far better to prophesy.

When we prophesy in public meetings, we bring honour to God and we help others focus on God too, as we speak his word.

As we speak God’s word, we strengthen, we encourage and we console.

And as people speak God’s word to us, we are strengthened, we are encouraged and we are consoled.

That’s how the Church is built up; this is what someone who has the love of God long to see.

Earlier this week, Kochie from the morning show interviewed our Archbishop, Kanishka Raffel and asked why can’t those who are unvaccinated simply watch the church from home?

They’re getting their teachings from the comfort of their own home; what’s the problem?

The problem is that Church is not meant to be enjoyed from the comfort of our own home.

It’s to be a gathering of God’s people where they encourage, strengthen and console one another with God’s word.

It’s like saying going to see a Doctor from the comfort of your own home is a good thing.

In a way, it is a good thing, as it prevents the spread of diseases.

But it’s not the best thing by far as it’s far better for the person to be seen by the Doctor face to face so that she can diagnose you properly.

In the same way, while it’s good to be able to see one another on screen on online Church, it’s far better to see and to encourage a person face to face.

I know that for medical reasons, some of our members might stay longer at home for now.

But for most of us, meeting face to face is what we want because we want to encourage one another, face to face.

And we want to do that by using God’s word.

That’s why we put so much emphasis on the Word ministries. We emphasise on God’s word and the explanation of it in sermons as we come to Church.

We emphasise Growth Groups, where we study God’s word in small groups.

We have KOG youth and KOG junior.

We do all these word ministries because we want to exercise the gift of prophecy whenever we have the chance.

Currently we have 57% of people attending a word ministry besides coming to Church.

Our hope earlier this year is 60%; we’re almost there!

If you are in a group, please make it there whenever you can.

It’ll be a huge encouragement to the leaders.

They’ll be encouraged by you by simply having you there and I’m sure that they will encourage you too.

Long for the gift of prophecy because it’s a gift that build up the Church.

Limits of the gifts of tongues

Paul then focuses on the limits of the gifts of tongues.

Look with me at verse 6, “ So now, brothers and sisters, if I come to you speaking in tongues, how will I benefit you unless I speak to you with a revelation or knowledge or prophecy or teaching?”

Paul says that if he were to speak in tongues, but not teach them anything from God’s word, how would that benefit the Corinthians?

Paul would not have been helpful at all!

He then uses three illustrations to demonstrate how unhelpful he would be, if he only spoke to the Corinthian in tongues.

They are there from verses 7-12. Can you see them?

If you can, message to me via Whatsapp, Wechat or Zoom.

To save time, if you have Zoom, put your hand up and Po Ki will unmute you and you can share your answer with us.

What are the three examples Paul uses to tell us how unhelpful he would be if he had only spoken to the Corinthians in tongues?

You have twenty seconds.

That’s right!

Paul would’ve been like flute or a harp that doesn’t play distinct notes.

He would’ve been like an unclear bugle that doesn’t give a clear signal in battle.

He would’ve been like a foreigner to the Corinthians.

They wouldn’t be able to understand each other.

While some of us might not have played the flute, the harp or the bugle, almost all of us would’ve experienced what it’s like to be a foreigner in a strange land.

For some of us who recently came to Australia, we might still feel like a foreigner.

But that’s what it would be like if Paul had spoken to the Corinthians in tongues.

So what should the Corinthians long for instead?

Look with me at verse 12, “So also you—since you are zealous for spiritual gifts, seek to excel in building up the church.”

If you’re longing to be gifted at Church, long for gifts that excel in building up the Church.

The idea is not just to be better in gifts that build up the Church but to have plenty of gifts in the Church that are for building up the Church.

In our context, it’s the gift of prophecy, the gift to speak God’s word to God’s people.

To make his case for how much better the gift of prophecy over the gift of tongues even stronger,

Paul lists seven limitations on the gift of tongues.

Limit One: If you are going to speak in tongue, no one else understands you.

And so, if you have the gift of tongue and want to use it in public, you should pray for the gift of interpretation as well.

The gift of interpretation is to interpret what you’ve said in tongues.

If you don’t have the gift of interpretation, you shouldn’t speak in tongues in public.

Limit Two: When you pray in tongue, your spirit prays but your mind is unfruitful.

Since you don’t know what you are saying in tongues, your mind switches off.

Rather than just speaking in tongues as you pray in the spirit, make sure your mind also prays.

If you do sing in tongues in the spirit, make sure you sing with your mind as well.

The Bible commentator Thisleton says something very helpful about this verse:

“If only the mind is active, everything remains at a theoretical level;

if only the heart is active, the door lies open to self-deception and credulity.

If both are open to the Holy Spirit, the result can build up the community and bear the fruit (v. 14a) of love for the other.”

So if you pray in tongue, make sure you pray in both spirit and mind.

Limit 3: When you pray in tongue, people can’t agree with you.

When someone in a group finishes a prayer, the Christians around them usually say, Amen.

The word Amen, means, “And So be it.” Or “certainly”.

And so when Christians say “Amen” at the end of prayer, they’re saying, “and so be it”

If someone prays in tongue, the people around them can’t agree because they don’t know what the person has been praying.

Look with me at verse 17, “For you may very well be giving thanks, but the other person is not being built up.”

And yet, that’s the attitude of some people when they come to Church.

They think that when they come to Church, it’s a private matter between them and God; if they give thanks to God then that’s enough.

But Paul says that’s not enough because it’s not love.

The love of Christ demands us to love one another.

We’re here mutually encourage one another and to help one another grow.

And so, when we come together and we don’t build each other up, we fail as a Church.

Limit 4: The effectiveness of speaking in tongues is limited.

Paul is proficient in speaking in tongues, but even for someone as proficient as Paul, he would rather speak five words with his understanding than 10000 words in tongues.

Because speaking five words in understanding would benefit more people spiritually than speaking 10000 words in tongues.

Limit 5: Insisting in speaking in tongues is childish, even if the person thinks he’s a mature Christian.

Limit 6: Speaking in tongues has traditionally been act of judgment from God.

In the Old Testament, God had sent his message to his people through the prophets.

When the people rejected God’s message, he’s sent people of strange tongues to Israel.

Look with me at verse 21, which is a quote from Isaiah 28:11-12,

“It is written in the law,

I will speak to this people

by people of other tongues

and by the lips of foreigners,

and even then, they will not listen to me,

says the Lord.”

The people of other tongues were the invaders who came to attack Israel.

Surely by then the Israelites would know that God was punishing them.

But the Israelites were so stubborn that they would not recognise God’s judgements on them.

And so, verse 22, “Speaking in tongues, then, is intended as a sign, not for believers but for unbelievers, while prophecy is not for unbelievers but for believers.”

Speaking in tongues is a sign of judgement for unbelievers; it shows that they are under judgement from God.

Hearing God’s message in a language that you don’t understand won’t bring you back to God.

Prophecy, on the other hand, is a sign for believers; it tells the people how they can turn back to God in languages that they understand.

There’s a chance that someone might turn back to God if they understand God’s message.

Limit 7: The inquirer will find a church that only speaks in tongues repulsive.

Can you imagine the whole church gathering together, all speaking in tongues?

It would be utterly chaotic and even a bit frightening.

They would look like a group of crazy people!

Quite a few years ago, I visited a Church that happened to speak in tongues.

I didn’t know that they would speak in tongues but I know that theoretically it could happen.

Things were fairly normal for most of the service until about one and a half hours in.

The preacher invited everyone to speak in tongues.

And everyone started to speak in tongues.

My friends and I were extremely uncomfortable with it as we didn’t speak in tongues.

There was no interpretation.

They were definitely not doing what Paul taught, which was to have an interpreter when someone does speak in tongues.

We felt excluded as foreigners, just as Paul had said.

The experience did not draw us closer to God at all.

The gift of tongue has a lot of limitations.

While it may not be a big issue here in our Church, the principle that Paul teaches remain.

We ought to long for gifts that build up the Church.

When we come to Church, our focus is not on what we can gain but on what we can give to others.

When we do use our gifts, our focus is not on getting attention but on how we can encourage and to build one another up.

This brings us back to the gift of prophecy and how it can result in worship.

Worship resulting from the gift of prophecy

Look with me at verses 24-25,

“But if all are prophesying and some unbeliever or outsider comes in, he is convicted by all and is called to account by all. The secrets of his heart will be revealed, and as a result he will fall facedown and worship God, proclaiming, “God is really among you.”

Worship resulting from the gift of prophecy.

That’s what we want to see in our Church, right?

Rather than speaking in tongues, if everyone prophesied, if everyone spoke God’s word to God’s people,

Then the unbeliever will be convicted by all, he would know that he’s a sinner who needs to repent.

He would know to know that he needs to confess his sins.

And he would bow down in worship before God.

When we prophesy, when we speak God’s word to God’s people, we strengthen one another, we encourage one another, we console one another.

And we also bring people to salvation in Jesus Christ.

When we prophesy, we build God’s Church and we bring glory to God through the conversion of unbelievers who come to Church.

Over the years, I’ve seen testimonies of people who have come to Christ because they have heard the words of love between God’s people.

More than that, they themselves have heard the words of love from God’s people.

When they hear the gospel spoken and obeyed among God’s people, they’re convicted of their sins and they come before God.

That’s what we do here, every Sunday.

When we come to Church on Sundays, every week, we exercise our gifts of prophecy as we speak the words of God to strengthen, encourage and to console one another.

We exercise our gifts of prophecy when we, by God’s Spirit, and in Christ, speak God’s word to one another, to God’s glory.

That’s one powerful way for us to lead people to Jesus.

We lead people to Jesus when we speak God’s word to one another, By God’s Spirit, in God’s Son, to God’s glory.

We do this on Sundays when we come together at Church.

When we come together for service, our whole service order is set up so that people can see and understand the gospel.

We praise God, we confess our sins before God, we affirm together that we’re forgiven in Jesus’ name, we hear from God’s word the bible, we say what we believe together in the creed, we pray.

Every element of the service is to help us see why we all need Jesus.

And to a smaller extent we also do that when we meet in Growth Groups.

As we engage in all these ministries, we do it not for our own sake but foe each others’ sake and for the sake of everyone who comes to Church, online for now and in person later.

We engage in all these ministries so that people may come, be convicted of their sins, their need for Christ.

We engage in the gifts of prophecy so that people will bow in worship before God.

When someone comes to Church, will they know that God is among us?

That will depend on whether we are exercising our gifts of prophecy.

That will depend on whether we’re speaking God’s word to one another and to the new person.

And so friends, what’s the one gift that we should long for?

It’s the gift of prophecy.

For the gift of prophecy, speaking God’s word to God’s people, builds up and is superior to building yourself up by speaking in tongues.

What does this mean for us?

Speak God’s word to build God’s house.

For those of us who are parents, no matter which culture we’re from, we often teach our kids to say “Please” if they want something.

We teach our kids to say “please”, not only because it’s manners but because we want to build their respect for other people.

At Church, we teach one another to speak God’s word.

We teach one another to say, “Please God” when we pray, because that’s what the Bible teaches.

We teach one another to say, “How can I serve?” because that’s what God’s word teaches us about love.

We teach one another to say, “Fear not, trust Jesus” because that’s what God’s word teaches us about our Lord Jesus Christ.

At Church, we build up one another as we teach God’s word to one another.

That’s why we put so much of our emphasis on God’s word because it’s as we speak to one another in God’s word that we build God’s house.
