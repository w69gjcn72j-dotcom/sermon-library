Young Adults (19–30)

Beware the friendly advice of the devil

Luke 4:1–15 · Sunday 18 October 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

For young adults (19–30) · Sermon by Rev David Yung

Big Idea: Reject the friendly devil’s temptation to be your own man, and rely on Jesus, who triumphed as the Son of God by trusting his Father’s word.

Aim: by the end of the study, group members will

see why the devil’s offers sound like friendly advice — look after yourself, rule yourself, do it your way;

name where they are tempted to be their “own man or woman” — in study, work, the share house, the group chat or the feed;

run to Jesus, the Son who won, instead of trying to make it out of the wilderness alone.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

1. Connect (10 min)

  • What is the best “friendly advice” you have ever been given that turned out to be terrible — a mate’s plan, a too-good-to-be-true deal, a confident influencer?
  • When you’re hungry and flatmates have left the good food, what stops you? Where do you jump the gun at uni, work or home?

2. Read: Luke 4:1–15 (CSB)

1 Then Jesus left the Jordan, full of the Holy Spirit, and was led by the Spirit in the wilderness 2 for forty days to be tempted by the devil. He ate nothing during those days, and when they were over, he was hungry. 3 The devil said to him, “If you are the Son of God, tell this stone to become bread.” 4 But Jesus answered him, “It is written: Man must not live on bread alone.”

5 Then he took him up and showed him all the kingdoms of the world in a moment of time. 6 The devil said to him, “I will give you their splendor and all this authority, because it has been given over to me, and I can give it to anyone I want. 7 If you, then, will worship me, all of it will be yours.” 8 Jesus answered him, “It is written: Worship the Lord your God, and serve him only.”

9 Then he took him to Jerusalem, had him stand on the pinnacle of the temple, and said to him, “If you are the Son of God, throw yourself down from here. 10 For it is written: He will give his angels orders concerning you, to protect you, 11 and they will support you with their hands, so that you will not strike your foot against a stone.” 12 Jesus answered him, “It is said: Do not test the Lord your God.”

13 After the devil had finished every temptation, he departed from him for a time. 14 Then Jesus returned in the power of the Spirit to Galilee, and news about him spread throughout the entire vicinity. 15 He was teaching in their synagogues, being praised by everyone.

3. Context

Luke 4 follows straight on from Jesus’ baptism, where the Father called him “my beloved Son”, and from Luke’s genealogy, which ends “son of Adam, son of God”. Led by the Spirit into the wilderness for forty days, Jesus faces three offers from the devil, and each time he answers from Deuteronomy, the book that recalls how God’s people lived — and failed — in the wilderness. The question underneath all three: what kind of Son will Jesus be — one who lives for himself, or one who trusts his Father’s word?

4. Discuss

  1. (vv.1–2) Jesus is “full of the Holy Spirit” and “led by the Spirit” into the wilderness to be tempted. What surprises you about that? In what way is Jesus the Son of God differently from us (compare Luke 3:22, 38)?
  2. (v.3) The devil says “If you are the Son of God” — he knows who Jesus is, and sounds helpful. Why is “be your own provider” such friendly advice? Where do you hear it — about money, career, comfort, being independent?
  3. (vv.3–4) Jesus could have fed himself, so why didn’t he? What’s the picture of the little brother and the head of the table? What does “Man must not live on bread alone” (Deuteronomy 8:3) mean when you’re stressed, broke or lonely?
  4. (vv.5–7) What does the devil offer, and what’s the catch? Why does “in a moment of time” matter? How would one bow have been easier than the cross?
  5. (v.8) “Worship the Lord your God, and serve him only.” What do we bow to without noticing — approval, being liked, comfort, our own plans? How do we rename it: “I’m just being real”, “it’s my life”, “I deserve a break”?
  6. (vv.9–11) The devil quotes Psalm 91. How can the Bible be twisted to tempt us? Think of verses taken out of context to justify a decision we’ve already made.
  7. (v.12) What would it have meant for Jesus to “test” God by jumping? How is testing God different from trusting him? Where do we demand that God act on our terms — or do nothing with what he’s already given us?
  8. (vv.13–15; Hebrews 4:15–16) The Son triumphs by trusting his Father’s word. How does knowing Jesus was “tempted in every way as we are, yet without sin” change what you do when you’re tempted? What would it look like to run to Jesus this week?

5. Live It Out

KOG 3·2·1·0 — pray for 3, connect with 2, invite 1, 0 left unloved.

  • Pray for 3 — three friends, flatmates, colleagues or family who don’t yet know Jesus. Pray by name daily.
  • Connect with 2 — coffee, a meal or a walk with two of them this week — a real catch-up, not just a reaction in the group chat.
  • Invite 1 — invite one of your three to the Welcome to the Family course, the Discover Course, the Christmas concert on 13 December or the next young adults gathering.
  • 0 left unloved — in church and in the group, look out for the person who is new, alone or drifting, and sit with them.

This week’s challenge: Before one decision this week — a purchase, a commitment, a message you want to send — “ask the head of the table first”: pause, pray and ask God whether it is his will before you act. Then notice where you’ve been bowing to likes, approval or comfort instead of the Lord.

Memory verse: “Worship the Lord your God, and serve him only.” (Luke 4:8)

6. Pray

  • Thank God for his word, and for Jesus, who trusted his Father when it cost him everything. Confess where you have tried to be your own provider, your own king, or the orchestrator of your own salvation.
  • Ask the Holy Spirit to protect and guide you, and to give you God’s kind of hunger — hunger for righteousness.
  • Pray by name for your 3 — that they would meet Jesus, who triumphed for us.

7. Leader’s Notes

Framing. Joyfully point to Jesus. Some in the room may be new, unsure, or tired of trying to hold life together on their own. The good news is not “try harder to resist the devil” but that the Son has already triumphed for us. Guests may be present; never corner anyone, and let people pass on a question.

Q1 — The Spirit leads Jesus into the wilderness, so temptation is not a sign that God has left us. Jesus is a son of Adam like us, yet the Son of God in a way no human being ever was or ever will be.

Q2 — The devil doesn’t doubt who Jesus is: “If you are the Son of God — and of course you are…”. The offer sounds like common sense and independence. Choosing to be our own provider without trusting God first disrespects God.

Q3 — Jesus could have made a feast, but until God says so he does not move, not even to feed himself — doing so would have ruptured his relationship with the Father. God sometimes allows us to be hungry and vulnerable; trusting his word matters more than food.

Q4 — The devil’s rule has an expiry date. One bow would have avoided the suffering, hunger, pain and death of the cross. The shortcut was a lot easier — but it was not the Father’s way.

Q5 — Deuteronomy 6:13: no one is to be above God. When comfort or approval is our king, or we think no one, not even God, can tell us what to do with our time and resources, we are doing what the devil wanted.

Q6 — The devil knows the Bible and can quote it (Psalm 91:11–12). Scripture twisted to demand that God serve our plan is still temptation. Ask: has the head of the table said so?

Q7 — Deuteronomy 6:16: at Massah Israel wanted God to save them on their terms. Jesus would have been saved, but on his own initiative. The issue is whether we wait for the Father’s leading instead of orchestrating our own way of doing his will.

Q8 — We can’t overcome the devil by ourselves; we need our great high priest. When tempted, run to Jesus: at the throne of grace we find mercy and grace in time of need. Jesus triumphed because he is not his own man — he is the Son of God, and if we are adopted children of God, that is our identity too.
