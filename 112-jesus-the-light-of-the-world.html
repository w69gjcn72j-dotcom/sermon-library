It was a dark and foggy night.

Our family was in Katoomba in the Blue Mountains for a conference, but Ezekiel had some training at work in Hurstville.

And so, Ezekiel and I drove back for an afternoon.

On our way back to Katoomba, a thick fog descended on the Blue Mountains.

It was so thick that we couldn’t see a more than a few metres from the front.

Here’s a picture of it.

We could hardly see what’s in front of us.

I was driving a lot slower than usual, just in case.

We were glad that lights of the car were working, otherwise, we would be in greater danger, to both us and others.

Though it took us a little longer than usual, we arrived safely at Katoomba and could spend the rest of our time there with the family.

Turning the lights on at night and driving slowly in the dark is a simple and straightforward thing to do.

Most people will do it almost by instinct.

However, many people don’t apply the same approach in other, more important areas of their lives.

Most people in the world are in the dark with regards to their relationship with God.

They don’t know where they stand with their relationship with God.

Others are simply in the dark about God; they have no idea who or what God is.

If you ask someone where their lives are heading, they might tell you about the course they want to get into or the place where they want to move in.

But where, ultimately, are they heading?

Where will they ultimately end up?

It might be a question that has never occurred to them.

Do they know where their live is heading? Do I know where my life is heading?

Or am I just living in the dark?

That’s the question that we’re going to explore today.

If you have your Bible there, please open up to John 8 beginning from verse 11.

We’re going to see three things:

“Father, by your Spirit, open our eyes so that we can see your truth as we hear from your words. Help us to marvel at the words of Jesus today. Help us to follow Jesus today. We pray this in His name, Amen.”

Jesus is light of the world

Jesus was at the Festival of the Tabernacles, one of the great Jewish festivals of the year.

It was a great time of celebration!

One of the Rabbis (Jewish teachers) of old said, “He who has not seen the joy of the place of water-drawing has never in his life seen joy”

One of the highlights of the festival was the lighting ceremony.

It’s the lighting of four huge lamps in the temple’s court of women.

The Jewish people will celebrate the under the lights of these lamps.

It’s in the middle of the festival that Jesus told them, in verse 12, “I am the light of the world. Anyone who follows me will never walk in the darkness but will have the light of life.”

This is now the second “I am” statement in the Gospel of John.

Can anyone tell me the first one? “I am the Bread of Life”

The second one is “I am the Light of the world”.

There are some pieces of paper in front of you in the pews.

You might like to take some out and draw these two things to help you remember:

Jesus says, “I am the Bread of Life”; “I am the Light of the World”

When you come into Church, you’ll see a bright sign outside.

It’s one of the brightest Church signs I’ve seen around Sydney.

It’s so bright that we must make sure we turn it off at night otherwise our neighbours can’t get to sleep!

Jesus says, “I am the Light of the World”.

Jesus shines far brighter than our Church sign ever will!

What does it mean for Jesus to be the Light of the world?

Throughout the Old Testament, we’ve seen many references of God being the light.

When God led the Israelites out of Egypt and they were in the middle of the wilderness, God led them with a pillar of cloud by day and a pillar for fire by night.

The pillar of fire provided light for the Israelites and led them in the darkness.

In Psalm 27:1, the Psalmist declares, “The Lord is my light and my salvation— whom should I fear? The Lord is the stronghold of my life—whom should I dread?”

In the New Testament, Jesus is described as the light.

In John 1:4, “In him was life,[a]and that life was the light of men.”

Jesus is the Light of the world that shines in the darkness of our world.

And he’s not just a light simple to be observed and marvelled at.

Look at the second part of the verse, “Anyone who follows me will never walk in the darkness but will have the light of life.”

There’s a very practical implication to knowing that Jesus is the Light of the world.

Those who follow him will never walk in darkness.

We know where we’re going if we follow Jesus; we’ll always be in the light.

We’re to be like the Israelites of old, but instead of following the pillar of fire, we’re following Jesus.

Jesus, the Light of the World, is on the move.

But are we following him?

Last week, a group of us have met with an older sister in Christ.

Her name is Elizabeth.

Elizabeth and her husband Dudley went to Africa to serve God and his people there.

But it wasn’t an easy assignment.

Back then, southern Africa was a dangerous place to be.

More than once, Dudley’s life was threatened, once with a knife and another time with a gun.

He’s an easy target. He was a white foreign minister in Africa.

Even so, they kept serving the Lord Jesus because that’s where the Lord Jesus had led them to be.

Yes, it was dangerous place and the darkness surrounding them might seem threatening.

But they were never walking in darkness.

They were walking with Jesus and so they were walking in the light and never in darkness.

And so, in God’s providence, it was lovely to see Elizabeth praying for a missionary couple that we support last week.

This couple is about to a new place and might face darkness.

But they will never walk in darkness because they’re following Jesus, the Light of the world.

And he who follows Jesus will never, ever walk in darkness.

Jesus is the Light of the world and he’s on the move.

Are we following him?

The Light testifies and judges truly

Not surprisingly, not everyone is convinced that Jesus is who he said he is.

Look with me at verse 13, “So the Pharisees said to him, “You are testifying about yourself. Your testimony is not valid.”

My son Ezekiel told me about this video clip about a very gullible man who saw a sign at a Café window: “worlds’ best cup of coffee”

When the man saw this, he was very excited and ran into the café and shouted.

“Congratulations! You did it! World’s best cup of coffee”

And everyone in the coffee just looked at him strangely.

They all know that the sign is not true; even the owners didn’t even pretend that the sign is true.

It’s a big claim but it’s probably not true that they have the best cup of coffee in the world.

When Jesus says that He’s the Light of the world, the Pharisees might have had a similar thought.

The Pharisees’ said Jesus was testifying about himself.

Just because Jesus said he’s the light of the world doesn’t necessarily mean it’s true.

But Jesus insists that his testimony about himself, what he says about himself, is true.

Jesus knows where he’s come from and where he’s going.

He is from heaven and he’s going back to heaven.

Jesus is the Light of the world!

He knows exactly where he is, where he was, and where he will be.

His words and his actions throughout his life has affirmed that he is who he said he is.

His opponents, however, had no idea who Jesus is and where he is going.

The problem is not with Jesus; the problem is with them.

They judge Jesus according to human standards; literally they judge Jesus according to the flesh.

They judge Jesus according to how humans judge things.

But not Jesus, he will not judge by human standards.

He judges with God the Father.

Jesus doesn’t make his judgement alone but with God the Father.

And so, we have two witnesses who can verify who Jesus is:

Jesus himself and God the Father, who’s sent Jesus.

And the testimonies of both the Father and the Son are true.

This has a very important implications to how we understand who Jesus is.

Our understanding of who Jesus is must be based on the testimonies of God the Father and God the Son.

They are foundational to us in understanding who Jesus is.

We don’t try to verify who Jesus is according to science or history or feelings or vibes.

We don’t verify who Jesus is by whether I think or whether I feel that Jesus is true.

The moment we let other standards determine who Jesus is, we’re relying on human standards.

Why shouldn’t we use human standards to determine who Jesus is?

It’s because our standards are insufficient and inadequate.

It’s like trying to measure the temperature of the sun using our thermometer.

The heat of the sun would melt the thermometer thousands of kilometres away before it gets anywhere near the sun.

No, the truth of Jesus is greater than any human standards we can use.

They might help us see him better, maybe a little clearer.

But ultimately, our knowledge of who Jesus is, the way we determine who he is, must be based on the testimony of Jesus and our Heavenly Father.

They are the most reliable witnesses of themselves.

Jesus and His Father are the most reliable judges of reality.

If you don’t rely on the testimonies of the Jesus and his Father, you will never come to know Jesus.

And if you don’t know Jesus, you will never come to know the Father.

Jesus and the Father are the most only reliable witnesses of who they are.

Later on, in the Gospel of John, we’ll see another who will come to testify truly about Jesus.

He is the Holy Spirit, whom Jesus will send from God the Father.

If you have a chance, you can read ahead about the Holy Spirit in John chapter 16.

But coming back to John 8, the important thing to remember for now is this: Jesus and His Father are the ultimate and the most foundational witnesses of who Jesus is.

Two days ago, the Sydney Morning Herald published an article about our Archbishop, Kanishka Raffel.

It mentioned that Kanishka was a Buddhist immigrant who moved to Sydney and was studying to be a lawyer when his friend Andrew spoke with him about Jesus.

Andrew then gave Kanishka the Gospel of John to read.

It would’ve looked something like this.

We’re reading it as a Church over the past few months at this church.

Kanishka promised his friend Andrew that he would read it.

Then on one hot summer night, he couldn’t sleep.

And so he got up and read the gospel.

Here’s a quote of Kanishka in the Sydney Morning Herald,

“You get a very strong sense of [Jesus’] personality. You don’t get that when you read Buddhist scripture ... [Jesus is] a very compelling character.”

And after he read the Gospel of John that night, Kanishka became a Christian.

He read the words of Jesus in the Gospel of John and trusted him.

And he’s been following Jesus ever since.

Friends, don’t underestimate what Jesus says about himself.

Ask your friends to read through the Gospel of John.

Take copies of the Gospel of John home.

Give them away!

We have them in English and Chinese.

Just get them to hear the words of Jesus.

Who knows, God might save another Buddhist friend who just so happens to read the Gospel of John, just like God saved Kanishka.

Sinners die without the light

I’m so thankful to God that Kanishka understood the urgency of following Jesus and decided to follow Jesus as soon as he could.

Some people, who takes Jesus’ offer to follow him for granted, might not get another chance.

Look with me at verse 21, “Then he said to them again, “I’m going away; you will look for me, and you will die in your sin. Where I’m going, you cannot come.”

Jesus says that there will come a time when he would go away and the Jews would look for him and, not finding him, they would die in their sins.

They’d look for Jesus and not able to find him; Jesus will go to a place they cannot go.

That’s a problem because the criteria for living, the criteria for life, is to follow him.

Jesus says those who follow him will never walk in the darkness but have the light of life.

Conversely, if you can’t follow Jesus, you can’t have the light of life, you won’t have the light of life.

Jesus says those who oppose him are in a separate realm from him.

They are from below and Jesus is from above.

They are of this world and Jesus is not of this world.

They will die in their sins because they will remain in their realm and they can’t follow Jesus.

Jesus puts it in another way in verse 24, “Therefore I told you that you will die in your sins. For if you do not believe that I am he, you will die in your sins.”

They will die in their sins because they don’t believe that Jesus is He.

That is, if you don’t believe that Jesus is He, that Jesus is the One, you can’t follow him to where he is.

And if you don’t follow him, you will die in your sins.

To not follow Jesus is to keep walking, to keep living in darkness, not having the light of life.

It’s like driving in the Blue Mountains on a dark and foggy night, with lights off, with GPS off, going at full speed.

The danger of falling off the cliff or crashing into another car is very high, very high.

The risk of not follow Jesus is not just very, very high.

It’s certain. The risk is 100%.

There is no soul in heaven who got there by not following Jesus.

There is no soul in hell who got there by following Jesus.

If you don’t follow Jesus, you will die in your sins.

If you don’t believe that Jesus is He, you will die in your sins.

Who is “He”?

Literally in the original language, Jesus says, “For if you do not believe that I am, you will die in your sins.”

In some translations, like the Chinese version, the word Christ (基督) to try to make it clearer.

But there’s a strong reason to leave it as it is, without adding the word Christ to explain it.

The phrase, “I am” or “I am He”, is often how God describes himself in the Bible.

In Isaiah 43:10, we read, ““You are my witnesses”—this is the Lord’s declaration— “and my servant whom I have chosen, so that you may know and believe me and understand that I am he. No god was formed before me, and there will be none after me.

When Jesus uses the phrase “I am He”, he’s referring to himself using the same phrase that God uses to refer to himself.

Jesus is the God from everlasting to everlasting.

There is no god formed before him or after him.

When will they know that Jesus is He?

Look with me at verse 28, “When you lift up the Son of Man, then you will know that I am he, and that I do nothing on my own. But just as the Father taught me, I say these things.”

When his enemies lifted Jesus on the cross, then they’d know that Jesus is He.

Then they will know that Jesus is God.

If you are to follow Jesus, you will only recognise who he is as you come before him at the cross.

For it’s at the cross that the glory of Jesus is revealed.

It’s at the cross that you’ll see a glimpse of the severity of the judgement that God has set on the sins on the world.

It’s also at the cross that you’ll the glory of the love of God who paid for our sins in the person of the Lord Jesus Christ.

At the cross, you’ll see Jesus exalted.

You’ll see that Jesus is He!

Two days ago, I was reminded that my grandmother died thirteen years ago.

She was a Buddhist until the last week of her life.

I still remember praying for her because she was going to spend her eternity in utter darkness and pain since she didn’t follow Jesus.

We thank God that in the last week of her life, she became a Christian.

She followed Jesus in the very last week of her life.

And now she’s in heaven. She’s in the light.

We look forward to seeing her again one day.

Friends, if you have a grandmother, a mother, a brother, a friend, who’s not following Jesus, they are still walking in darkness.

If they don’t follow Jesus, they will die in their sins.

It’s a 100% certainty.

And so, don’t stop praying for them.

Please don’t stop praying for them.

If you love them, you should be worried about them.

If they don’t have Jesus, they will keep walking in darkness, they will die in their sins.

Only Jesus, the Light of the world can save them.

Don’t stop praying for them.

Don’t miss the chance of telling them about Jesus.

How about us, in the congregation here present?

Do we know whether we’re going?

Are we walking in darkness? Or are we following Jesus, the light of the world?

If you follow Jesus, the light of the world, you will know where you’re going.

Jesus is the Light of the world, who testifies and judges truly, without whom sinners will die.

There are no exception.

But thank be to God, through Jesus Christ our Lord!

He’s sent Jesus, the Light of the world, to save us from our darkness.

And so, follow Jesus, the light of the world.

Earlier this week, I heard a great phrase that helps us remember what it means to follow Jesus.

As Christians, we follow Jesus, on bended knees.

We know that we can’t follow Jesus on our own and so we pray, we come before him in bended knees.

We need his help.

And the people around us can’t come to Jesus on their own.

They need God the Father to move them to Jesus so that they can come to know him.

Follow Jesus, the light of the world, on bended knees.

Come to Jesus on bended knees to our prayer nights.

Ask Jesus, “Lord, what do you want me to do? Lord, where do you want me to go?”

No matter where he sends you, it’s always worth it.

It’s always worth it because you’ll never be alone; he will be with you, always.

On the other hand, no matter how much the world offers you, walking in the darkness is never worth it.

No matter how much the world offers you, the result will always be death.

And so, friends, follow Jesus the Light of the world.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How am I following Jesus and walking in his light?

Do I come to understand Jesus according to his words or human standards?

Who do I know are in danger of dying in their sins?

Friends, Jesus is the Light of the world.

Follow him on bended knees.
