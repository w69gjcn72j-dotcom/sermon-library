Many years ago, my family took me to see a movie called Aladdin.

Has anyone seen it?

The movie begins with the villain, Jaffa, who wanted to go into the Cave of Wonders.

He’s got the magic key to go in but he couldn’t because the guardian to the cave had a very specific instruction about who’s allowed to go in:

“Know this: only one may enter here, one whose worth lies far within. A diamond in the rough.”

Not only does Jaffa need the key, he also needs the right person, a diamond in the rough, in order to go into cave.

In today’s passage, we’ll see what has been known as the Greatest commandment.

It’s a command not just for Christians but for everybody.

If everyone in the world keeps this command, it will be a far better place.

More than that, if we keep this command, we’ll be in a good relationship with God.

But just like the key to the Cave of Wonders, by itself, it won’t be enough.

And just like the Cave of Wonders, as well as the treasure that is the Great Commandment, we need the right person to lead us in.

We’ll need our diamond in the rough.

Who is our diamond in the rough?

That’s what we’ll see in today’s passage.

The Greatest Command

Firstly, the Greater Command.

Mark 12:28, “One﻿ of the scribes﻿ approached. When he heard them debating and saw that Jesus answered them well, he asked him, “Which command is the most important of all?”’

The scribe were teachers in Jesus’ days who were experts in the Bible.

They studied the Bible and would often have debates over it.

One of the debates that they have was over the commands of God.

They went through all the commands of God in the Old Testament and found 613 altogether.

Of these, 365 were prohibitions, things that you shouldn’t do and 248 things that you should do.

These experts would talk about which of these commands is the most important command of all.

Which command is the most important commandment to keep?

It’s not that if you keep this commandment, you don’t need to keep the others.

Rather, they were looking for one command that’s foundational to all the others.

One command which would summarise all of God’s law.

They were looking for one command that would in one sense rule the rest of the commands; a command that makes sense of the others.

It’s a little like this.

A while ago, Jenny and I went to a café to have coffee and tea.

When the waiter came, and asked what we wanted, Jenny’s said that she wanted some Earl Grey Tea.

He told us that he hadn’t heard of the tea before, and he went into the kitchen to make some inquiry.

We thought that he’s misheard us, but genuinely had never heard of Earl Grey Tea.

And I got a little worried.

If he does not know something as basic as Earl Grey Tea, what are the chances of him knowing the more advanced things in the café?

It’s like going to Yumcha and the waiter not knowing about Pu erh tea or going to McDonald’s and the person serving at the counter not knowing French fries.

Knowing what Earl Grey Tea is, is basic knowledge in a café.

In the same way, the experts in the law believe that there are commands in God’s laws that are absolutely foundational to understanding the others.

The expert in the law in today’s passage heard how Jesus had answered well and asked Jesus, “Which command is the most important of all?”

In other words, which command is absolutely foundational to understanding the others.

Listen to what Jesus say, in verse 29, “The most important﻿ is Listen, Israel! The Lord﻿ our God, the Lord﻿ is one. Love﻿ the Lord your God﻿ with all your heart, with all your soul, with all your mind, and with all your strength.” The second is, Love your neighbour﻿ as yourself. There is no other command﻿ greater than these.”

There are two parts to Jesus’ reply.

The first part is what has been known as the Shema

The word Shema, means to listen in Hebrew.

The passage that Jesus quotes is from Deuteronomy 6:4, where the people of Israel are called to Shema, or to Listen.

The Lord our God, the Lord is one., Love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your strength.

The Shema is something that every devout Jew will say every morning and every evening.

It’s a summary of what a devout Jew believes, much like the Apostles’ Creed is a summary of what Christians believe.

Jesus says that what the Shema teaches is foundational to the rest of the commandments in the Law.

To love God with all of your heart, with all of your soul, with all of your mind and with all of strength is what the summary of the law.

The law of God teaches us how to love God completely, with every fibre of our being.地

We’re to love God with all of our heart, with all of our emotions.

We’re to love God with all of our soul, with all of our spirit.

We’re to love God with all of our mind, with all of our intelligence.

We’re to love God with all of our strength, with all of our will.

We’ve to love God with everything within us; we keep nothing back in our love for God.

That’s foundational to understanding God’s laws.

But Jesus then does something very interesting, something which has not been done by other teachers before his time.

He combines this command to love God with all of our being with the command to love our neighbour as yourself.

It’s a command found in Leviticus 19:18.

The Law of God teaches us how to love God AND how to love our neighbour as ourselves.

The law is both vertical – it shows us how to love God AND it’s horizontal – it shows us how to love our neighbour.

Jesus says love of God and love of neighbour goes together.

Love of God and love of neighbour are complementary.

You cannot truly love God without loving your neighbour and you cannot truly love your neighbour without loving God.

If you truly love God, you will love your neighbour.

As Bishop Paul Barnett puts it, “Our love for God is expressed through our love for our neighbour.”

Once we understand that the law of God is designed to teach us how to love God and our neighbours, the rest of the laws will fall into place.

The rest of the laws show us in more details what it means to love God and to love one another.

Just a little over two weeks ago, Jenny and I celebrated our twentieth anniversary.

We thank God for bringing us through the highs and lows of marriage.

Through the years, we faced different challenges.

And there are still many challenges ahead!

There will be many different ways to deal with the various challenges.

How do we deal with these challenges? We need to come back to what’s foundational in the marriage.

We’ve made a promise before God to have and to hold from this day forward,

for better for worse, to love and to cherish, as long as we both shall live.”

There are many ways to approach our challenges but foundational to these must be our commitment to love one another under God.

Once we get that right, the other things will, under God, fall into place.

When we read the commands of God in the Bible, we must think about how these commands teach us to love God with our whole being and to love our neighbour as ourselves.

Why shouldn’t we make idols? Because we love God with our whole being.

Why shouldn’ t we steal? Because we love our neighbour as ourselves.

At the heart of all of God’s commands is love of God and love for our neighbours.

Jesus says there is no command greater than these.

The Greater Son

And the expert in the law agrees with Jesus!

Here in this passage is one of those rare moments when a Scribe, a group that’s often seen to battle with Jesus, actually agreeing with Jesus.

He says that Jesus is right!

That Jesus is correct to say that there is no one but God, that to love him with all your heart, understanding and strength, as well as loving your neighbour is far more important than sacrifices.

This Scribe gets it! He agrees with what Jesus is saying!

That’s fantastic, isn’t it!

Jesus then says something quite unexpected.

Instead of saying, “This man gets it! We’ll see him in heaven.”

Jesus says, “You are not far from the Kingdom of God.”

You are not far from the Kingdom of God?

What does that mean?

That means the Scribe is near but he’s not in God’s Kingdom.

Why isn’t he in the Kingdom of God?

It’s because he doesn’t know Jesus, the Greater Son.

There’s a saying in the corporate world that goes like this, “It’s not what you know, but who you know.”

Have you heard of it?

It’s the idea that it’s not your degrees, or your knowledge, or your competence, that gets you up the corporate ladder.

It’s the people you know, your relationships with them, that gives you access to success.

Of course, it’s only partly true.

If you don’t have any competence, if you don’t have any knowledge of the work, you’ll soon be found out.

But what the saying emphasises is the importance of knowing the right person.

Though you may have the skills and the knowledge, you need to know the right person to get you to the right place.

There’s a sense in which this is what happened to the Scribe here.

The Scribe gets what Jesus is saying.

He understands it intellectually.

He understands that loving God wholeheartedly and loving your neighbour as yourself is more important than sacrifices.

He understands how the most important command in the Law works!

He’s a long way further than many people; he’s probably even further than most of his fellow scribes.

Even Jesus sees that he has answered wisely.

But here’s the thing: Even though he understands Jesus’ teaching about the law, he’s not saved.

He understands Jesus’ explanation of how the law works, he agrees with Jesus’ teaching, And Jesus himself sees that he understands, but this man is not saved.

He’s like the man who might score 100% in a quiz on the Bible, he knows all the right answers in the Bible, answers that Jesus agree with, but he’s not saved.

He is not far from the Kingdom of God, but he remains outside the Kingdom of God.

Why is that?

Why does he understand the law but isn’t saved?

It’s because knowing the facts of the Bible alone doesn’t save you, if you don’t know Jesus.

It’s no good just knowing the “what” of the Bible; you must come to know the “who”, the One whom the Bible points to, that is, Jesus Christ.

Look with me at Mark 12, verses 35, “While﻿ Jesus was teaching in the temple, he asked, “How can the scribes﻿ say that the Messiah﻿ is the son of David?”

The Scribes say that the Messiah, the Christ, is the son of David.

And they’re right! Jesus Christ is from the line of David.

And there are Old Testament references pointing out the promises that God made to the David’s descendants.

But Jesus said the Messiah, the Christ, is bigger than just being the Son of David.

In Psalm 110, David says that the LORD, that is the LORD God, declared to my Lord, the Messiah, the Christ, ‘Sit at my right hand﻿ until I put your enemies under your feet.’”

David calls the Messiah, the Christ, my Lord.

And so Jesus asks, “David himself calls him ‘Lord.’ How, then, can he be his son?”

Yes, the Christ, the Messiah, is the Son of David but he’s more.

He’s also David’s Lord.

More than that, he’s the Son of God.

A few days after Jesus taught this, he would be raised from the dead, to prove that he’s the Son of God, that He is David’s Lord.

Why does Jesus teach about the Son of David here?

It’s because the scribes, experts in God’s laws, had got it very wrong when it comes to their understanding of who Jesus is.

One of the scribes got it right when it comes to understanding the importance of the Greatest Commandment,

And it’s important that he got this right.

But even more crucial than understanding the Greatest Commandment of God is to understand who Jesus really is.

Jesus is not just the Son of David, he’s David’s Greater Son.

If a person is to enter God’s kingdom, understanding the Greatest Commandment, as great as it is, is not enough.

To enter God’s kingdom, you must come to know the Greater Son, Jesus Christ.

One person who came to know Jesus was a man by the name of John Wesley.

John Wesley was born in a family of an Anglican Minister, Samuel Wesley.

As a boy growing up in a Christian family, taught mainly by his mother Susannah, John would’ve known all the Bible stories when he was young.

When he grew up, he went to Oxford and even became a short term missionary to America.

However, even though he was a missionary, he had doubts about his own salvation.

It wasn’t until he returned to England that he began to think more seriously about his own relationship with Jesus.

On May 24, 1738, after reading about how God works in the heart through faith in Christ, he said that he felt his heart strangely warmed.

A short time later, he shared with his brother, Charles, and declared, “I believe”.

John knew about Jesus Christ, but he didn’t know Jesus Christ.

It wasn’t until he came to know Christ that he entered into God’s kingdom.

We can be like John too, can’t we?

We can know a lot about the Bible, and know it correctly, just like the scribes and John Wesley, but do we know Jesus?

The Bible is full of wonderful truths but foundational to them all is Jesus Christ.

Just as knowing the Greatest Commandment is key to understanding the rest of the commandments,

Knowing Jesus is key to knowing God.

If you don’t know Jesus, you won’t know God, you’ll never enter God’s kingdom.

But if you know Jesus, you will know God and you’ll enter into God’s kingdom.

How do we come to know Jesus?

That’s who the Bible is all about.

Keep reading the Bible until you come to meet Jesus.

And pray to him, ask Him to show you what it means to trust in him.

Ask him to help you develop a genuine relationship with him.

Ask him to help you get to know him.

And as you come to know him, give him the honour that’s due to him.

He’s the Son of David; He’s the Lord of David; He’s the Son of God.

Give him the greatest honour!

The Greatest Honour

When we honour Jesus, we honour God.

How do we honour Jesus? How do we honour God?

In the last part of today’s passage, we see that we honour God by how much we don’t keep.

In verses 38-40, Jesus points out another fault of the Scribes.

They loved to go around wearing long robes and be greeted when they go shopping.

Here’s a clerical garment that I found in one of the cupboards in the Church.

It’s a very expensive looking robe.

Ministers in the past have worn it but I haven’t.

Now, imagine wearing this and going to shopping at Woolworth’s.

That’s what the scribe were doing; they were drawing a lot of attention to themselves.

The love the best seats in the house; if there’s a group photo, they want to be right in the middle of the shot.

They love the place of honour.

They love to receive honour from people.

But they’re phonies; they’re fakes.

Jesus said that they devour widow’s houses and say long prayers just for show.

They were using their religious status for financial gain.

They use their religious position to take advantage of poor widows.

Jesus says these people love honour and take advantage of others will receive harsher judgements from God.

God will judge people who abuse their religious position for their own honour and gain.

Though these people might talk about how they honour God, their actions show that they were far more concerned about how people honour them.

What does genuine honouring of God look like?

In Mark 12:41-42, Mark records an incident where Jesus was sitting﻿ across from the temple treasury and he watched how the crowd dropped money into the treasury.

Many rich﻿ people were dropping a lot of money in.

But a poor﻿ widow came and dropped in two tiny coins.

These two coins are the smallest coins in circulation.

It’ll be like dropping two five cent coins.

Financially speaking, it’s hardly anything.

But listen to what Jesus says in verses 43-44, “Summoning his disciples, he said to them, “Truly I tell you, this poor widow has put more into the treasury than all the others. For they all gave out of their surplus, but she out of her poverty﻿ has put in everything she had﻿—all she had to live on.”

Jesus says the rich people all gave out of their surplus; they use the money they have on themselves, then they give anything they have leftover to God.

But the widow, who didn’t have a lot of money, gave everything that she had – all that she had to live on.

Though she only has two five cent coins in her bank account, she gave it to God to be used for God’s purposes.

That’s what true sacrifice look like.

True sacrifice is not how much you give but in how much you keep for yourself.

The poor widow kept nothing for herself but gave everything, she gave her all to God.

That’s what it means to love God with all your heart, all your soul, all your mind and all your strength.

The widow loved God and gave Him her all.

That’s how she honoured God; that’s how she loved God.

She gave God her all.

A while ago, I spoke with a Church planter who met up in a small Bible study of around 15 people many years ago.

They wanted to start a Church for God but have no money.

An opportunity then came up for them to buy a Church building.

Since they have only a small group, they prayed and they gave generously.

Praise God, they got enough money and they paid off the loan over a few years.

They’re now a Church of over 400 people and have even started a daughter Church elsewhere.

The Bible study that first met up would not have known that God would bless them so abundantly.

But they trusted God and honoured God by giving Him their all.

They wanted to give God the greatest honour.

How about us? What would be like if everyone in our Church gave God our all and give him the greatest honour?

Talk with the person next to you.

Think big!

What would it look like if we gave God the greatest honour?

Text and message your answer in if you’re watching from home.

You have 20 seconds.

Friends, wouldn’t it be wonderful if we did give God the greatest honour?

Wouldn’t it be great if we give God our all?

But that’s what we’re called to do.

We’re to give God the greatest honour by obeying the greatest command through his Great Son Jesus Christ.

We can’t honour God without his Great Son Jesus Christ.

We can’t honour God if we don’t obey his Greatest command of loving him completely and loving our neighbour as ourselves.

The cost is great, the cost is our all, every part of being, but it’s worth it because it’s God we’re honouring.

Jim Elliot, a young missionary who died on mission, wrote this in his diary:

"He is no fool who gives up what he cannot keep to gain that which he cannot lose"

No matter how great the cost you think you give for Jesus, what you gain for God is of far greater value than you can ever imagine.

Here are the three questions for the week:

How am I loving God with all of my heart?

How am I loving my neighbour sitting next to me or living next to me, as I would love myself?

What do I need to stop keeping for myself and give to God instead?

Friends, give God the greatest honour by obeying the greatest command through his Great Son Jesus Christ.
