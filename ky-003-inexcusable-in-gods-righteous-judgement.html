Inexcusable in God’s Righteous Judgement

Romans 2:1-16 · Sunday 12 July 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

Big Idea: God’s judgment is perfectly fair — He judges by truth, by works, and by light — so no one can plead “I’m a good person.” Our only escape is Jesus.

Aim: by the end, students can (1) explain why being “good” can’t save anyone; (2) spot their own judge-others-excuse-myself habit; (3) treasure God’s kindness that leads us to repentance — and want their friends to know it.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, this sheet · about 60-75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • 1. Referee moment: share a time you called someone out for something you also do — roasting a mate for being late when you’re always late. Why is it so easy to see other people’s faults?
  • 2. Imagine every private comment you’ve ever made about someone — every group-chat message, every eye-roll, every “did you see what she did?” — got projected on the screen at assembly. 1-10, how cooked are you? What does that reveal about the standards we use on others?

Read — Romans 2:1-16 (CSB)

1 Therefore, every one of you who judges is without excuse. For when you judge another, you condemn yourself, since you, the judge, do the same things. 2 Now we know that God’s judgment on those who do such things is based on the truth. 3 Do you think—anyone of you who judges those who do such things yet do the same—that you will escape God’s judgment? 4 Or do you despise the riches of his kindness, restraint, and patience, not recognizing that God’s kindness is intended to lead you to repentance? 5 Because of your hardened and unrepentant heart you are storing up wrath for yourself in the day of wrath, when God’s righteous judgment is revealed.

6 He will repay each one according to his works: 7 eternal life to those who by persistence in doing good seek glory, honor, and immortality; 8 but wrath and anger to those who are self-seeking and disobey the truth while obeying unrighteousness. 9 There will be affliction and distress for every human being who does evil, first to the Jew, and also to the Greek; 10 but glory, honor, and peace for everyone who does what is good, first to the Jew, and also to the Greek. 11 For there is no favoritism with God.

12 For all who sin without the law will also perish without the law, and all who sin under the law will be judged by the law. 13 For the hearers of the law are not righteous before God, but the doers of the law will be justified. 14 So, when Gentiles, who do not by nature have the law, do what the law demands, they are a law to themselves even though they do not have the law. 15 They show that the work of the law is written on their hearts. Their consciences confirm this. Their competing thoughts either accuse or even excuse them 16 on the day when God judges what people have kept secret, according to my gospel through Christ Jesus.

Context

Paul has just shown a world running from God (Romans 1:18-32) — and it’s ugly. Now he turns to the person clapping along: the “good person” who thinks, “Well, I’m not like that.” In Paul’s day that was often the religious person; today it’s anyone who thinks being decent is enough. Paul’s point: everyone stands guilty before God, so everyone needs the gospel.

Discuss

  1. Read verses 1-3 again. What does Paul say we are actually doing every time we judge someone else?
  2. Verse 2 says God’s judgment is “based on the truth.” What does that mean — and why is that bad news for someone whose life looks great on the outside?
  3. Verse 4: what is God’s kindness meant to lead us to? How do people misread His patience (“He hasn’t zapped me, so I must be fine”)?
  4. Verses 6-11 say God “will repay each one according to his works.” Does that mean we’re saved by being good? How do faith and works fit together? (Hint: how do you know a tree is an apple tree?)
  5. Verses 12-15: how can God fairly judge people who’ve never read the Bible or heard the Ten Commandments? What has He given every single person?
  6. Verse 16: God will judge “what people have kept secret.” Why is the private stuff (group chats, search history, thoughts) the real test of who we are?
  7. We love to “rename” our sins: “it’s just banter”, “I’m not gossiping, I’m just saying”. Where do you see this at school? Where do you do it?
  8. If even “good people” can’t survive judgment day, what is our only hope? How does Romans 2 make Romans 1:16-17 (the gospel is God’s power to save everyone who believes) even better news?

Live It Out — KOG 3·2·1·0

God’s kindness is meant to move hearts — starting with ours. Keep the rhythm going this week:

  • PRAY for 3 friends — especially ones who’d say “I’m a good person, I’ll be fine.” Write their names down tonight.
  • CONNECT with 2 of them this week — a real conversation, not just a like on a post.
  • INVITE 1 of them to KOGYouth or church — God’s kindness probably reached you through someone’s invite.
  • 0 left out — at school and at youth, nobody sits alone.

Weekly challenge: every time you catch yourself about to judge someone — out loud or in the group chat — stop, and pray for them instead. One prayer per almost-judgment.

Memory verse: “God’s kindness is intended to lead you to repentance.” — Romans 2:4 (CSB)

Pray

  • Thank Jesus that He faced the judgment we deserve, so we can meet Him now as Saviour and not later as Judge.
  • Pray by name for your 3 people — that God’s kindness would lead them to repentance and faith.
  • Ask God to make us honest about our own hearts — quick to repent, slow to judge, glad to point people to Jesus.

Leader’s Notes

Tone: judgment is heavy, so keep joyfully pointing to Jesus — Romans 2 makes the gospel land as good news. Some students may not yet be Christians: never corner or shame anyone. The invitation is “meet Jesus as Saviour now”, not “behave better.”

  • Q2: “based on the truth” = God judges reality — motives and secrets included — not the highlight reel. That flattens the “I look fine” defence.
  • Q4: we are saved by faith and judged by works — works are the fruit that show the tree. You change because you’re saved, not to get saved.
  • Q5: everyone has a conscience and a God-given moral code (vv.14-15), and nobody keeps even their own standards — so God’s judgment is fair.
  • Q8: the only hope is Jesus. Sunday’s big line: “Either we face Him as Saviour now, or we face Him as Judge later.”

Vision: this is “from faith to faith” (Romans 1:17).
