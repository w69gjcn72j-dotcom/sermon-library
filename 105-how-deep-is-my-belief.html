How Deep Is My Belief

John 4:46-54 · 2020
Big IdeaMove from believing Jesus for his signs to taking Jesus at his word.

Sermon Title: How deep is our belief?

Passage: John 4.46_54

Big Idea: Jesus’ word transformed a desperate official into a believing father.

Belief Level 1: A desperate father seeks Jesus’s help

Belief Level 2: A man believed in Jesus’ word

Belief Level 3: A father leads his family to belief

How deep is our belief?

An ordinary testimony about an extraordinary Jesus

Jesus is gracious even to the pragmatists

Belief in Christ spreads wonderfully in families

The internet is an incredible, isn’t it?

With the internet, we have so much knowledge at our disposal.

However, the problem with people searching a wide range of topics is that it leaves the person with knowledge that’s a mile wide and an inch deep.

They might cover many topics, but they don’t have go deep in any topic.

This can also happen when it comes to Christian belief.

With the internet, many people can access many Christian speakers and many Christian books.

But how deep will their faith be?

Can saving faith be a mile wide but an inch deep?

That’s what we’re going to see in today’s passage.

If you have your Bible there, please open up with me to John 4, beginning from verse 46.

Belief Level 1: A desperate father seeks Jesus’s help

Last week, we read an interesting statement about what Jesus thought about fellow countrymen.

If you have your Bible there, go back with me just a couple of verses back to John 4:44, “(Jesus himself had testified that a prophet has no honour in his own country.)”

While Jesus’ fellow countrymen might welcome him with their lips, they won’t honour him.

It’s with this reception in mind that we come to verse 46.

“He went again to Cana of Galilee, where he had turned the water into wine. There was a certain royal official whose son was ill at Capernaum.”

Will this royal official from Capernaum be another example of someone who welcomes Jesus but doesn’t honour him?

This royal official would’ve worked for King Herod Antipas.

He lives in Capernaum, which is around 26km away from Cana.

This royal official’s son is sick and is about to die.

And this father, like any good father would, runs desperately to find help for his boy.

If this happened today, it will be like a father whose son had contracted the coronavirus and is on ventilator.

He hears about a miracle worker who heals people with coronavirus and so he rushes down to this miracle worker, hoping that he’ll come.

This royal official is desperate for Jesus to come and help his son.

But as he comes to Jesus, he hears an unexpected response.

It sounds as if Jesus is disappointed that he’s come.

Look with me at verse 48, “Jesus told him, “Unless you people see signs and wonders, you will not believe.”

Jesus is saying that the royal official and those in Galilee are not interested in what Jesus teaches about God or about who Jesus is.

They only wanted to see Jesus do miracles.

For this royal official, he’s not interested in Jesus; he’s only come for the healing.

He sees Jesus merely as a service provider.

Look with me at what he says to Jesus in verse 49, “Sir, come down before my boy dies.”

He’s very pragmatic; he’s come for the benefits that Jesus can offer him.

There are still many people in the world like that today, isn’t there?

I have known people who’ll only come to Church when we have lunch on.

Sometimes these people will pay but often they don’t.

I have met people who only come to Church if they have recognition; others will only come if their friends come.

They come to Jesus to get some benefits from him.

Do people like the royal official and others who come to get some benefits from Jesus, actually believe in Jesus?

They must, to an extent.

This royal official and others like him might believe that Jesus can perform miracles,

But they don’t actually believe Jesus; they don’t necessarily trust him.

This kind of belief in Jesus won’t save them.

It’s a superficial belief, a very thin layer of belief.

It’s a belief that just scratches the surface.

To have a belief in Jesus that saves, one needs to go deeper.

This takes us to Belief Level 2: a belief in Jesus’ word.

2. Belief Level 2: A man believed in Jesus’ word

Look with me at what Jesus tells the royal official in verse 50, “Go, your son will live.”

In one sense, this is a rather unspectacular turn of events.

As far as we know, there is no sudden rush of wind.

The father doesn’t have a sudden sense of feeling that his son was healed.

The royal official had to choose whether to believe in Jesus’ word or not.

Now notice how the royal official is described in this verse.

Can you see it? He’s not described as the royal official here, but as the man.

From here on, this man is no longer described as the royal official anymore.

It’s a subtle change but I think it’s an important one.

This man believed what Jesus’ said to him.

This man was a desperate royal official who’s only interested to have his boy healed but now he’s the man who’s come to believe in Jesus’ word.

Has something now changed within this man?

What does it mean now for this man to believe in Jesus?

This man’s belief in Jesus now looks more promising.

We’ve seen that there are different depths of beliefs in the Gospel of John.

There are people like the Jews, who believe in Jesus on the basis of his miracles.

These Jews are said to believe in Jesus, but Jesus would not entrust himself to them.

And then there are other people like the Samaritans that we’ve been reading about.

They believed in Jesus on the basis of his words.

In John 4:41, we read that many Samaritans believe in Jesus because of what he said.

Would this royal official’s belief in Jesus go deeper than his fellow Galileans?

Would his belief in Jesus be deeper than just seeing what Jesus can do in miracles?

This takes us to a deeper level, Belief level 3: a father leads his family to belief.

Belief Level 3: A father leads his family to belief

As this man walks back down to Capernaum, his servants meet him along the way to tell him that the boy’s going to make it.

The boy’s going to live!

The man then asks his servants “what time?

What time did the boy got better?”

To which the servants reply, “At one in the afternoon”.

Look with me at how the man responds in verse 53, “The father realized this was the very hour at which Jesus had told him, “Your son will live.” So he himself believed, along with his whole household.”

The man realises that at the very hour that Jesus’ said, “You son will live”, his son was healed.

It isn’t a coincidence.

It was Jesus who healed the boy, remotely, with his word, while he was still 26km away.

Now notice that this man is referred differently again in this verse.

How was he referred in this verse?

As the father.

This is now the third description that this man has in this short passage.

He was introduced to us as the royal official, then simply as the man who believed in Jesus’ word and finally as the father.

This is a very relational term.

He is the father to his household; he is the father to his family.

As the father to his family, he leads them to believe in Jesus.

He would’ve told them that it was Jesus who had healed the boy

And now, whenever a family member sees the boy, they see someone who was on the brink of death and brought back to life by a word of Jesus.

As a family unit, as a household, they testify to who Jesus is and to what he’s done.

They would’ve told many people about what happened.

The story eventually came to the Apostle John, the writer of this Gospel.

And now this story has been passed on to us.

Here, the father has gone to a deeper level of belief.

He’s taken Jesus at his word and now he brings Jesus’ word to his family.

It’s a belief that he wants to share with those closest to him.

It’s a belief that he shares with his family, his household.

The belief that he’s sharing here confirms that the belief he has back in verse 50 is genuine.

That is, this royal official, this man, this father, has taken Jesus at his word.

He is a genuine believer of Jesus.

What can we learn from today’s passage?

Firstly, we need to ask ourselves, ‘how deep is our belief?’

JI Packer, the world-renowned Anglican theologian, said in his article, “It has often been said that Christianity in North America is 3,000 miles wide and half an inch deep. Something similar is true, by all accounts, in Africa and Asia, and (I can testify to this) in Britain also.”

I think the same can be said of Australia and China as well.

There are many Christians in these countries, but not many Christians who have a deep understanding, deep faith in Jesus Christ.

There are many who might be like the royal official at the beginning of today’s passage, who believe some things about Jesus, who might even believe that he’s a miracle worker.

But they only have a level 1 level of belief; they don’t see him more than a service provider.

Their belief in him won’t be enough to save them.

The belief that Jesus wants for us is a deeper belief, a belief that takes him at his word.

That’s what the royal official did; he took Jesus at his word.

And more than that, his did something about it.

He started walking back home to see his son.

This man’s faith journey took a great leap forward when he trusted Jesus’ word.

That man’s faith is confirmed when he sees his son healed.

In seeing how Jesus’ word can be trusted, he puts his faith in Jesus.

He also leads his family to put their trust in Jesus.

Which level of faith are you at?

Are you at level 1, only believing Jesus at a superficial level, coming to him only for what he can give you?

Are you at level 2, where you sincerely trust Jesus and take Jesus at his word?

Or are you at level 3, do you trust Jesus enough to share your faith with your family?

In reality, of course, you can’t separate level 2 and level 3.

When you do trust Jesus and take him at his word, and you see how Jesus is trustworthy, you will want to share Jesus with your family and your friends.

This takes us to the second point.

What we have here is an ordinary testimony about an extraordinary Jesus

In one sense, this is a testimony from a very ordinary man.

He wasn’t serious about Jesus.

He saw Jesus face to face and he still didn’t care who Jesus was.

All he cared about was what he can get from Jesus.

It’s not until he hears the word of Jesus that he believes in him.

After that, he’s a changed man.

He didn’t have some special feeling and from the outside, nothing spectacular happened to him when he trusted in Jesus’ word.

But he did believe in Jesus’ word and something spectacular happened.

His son was healed; the sign confirmed that Jesus can be trusted.

This doesn’t mean that Jesus will answer every call for the sick to be healed or every prayer for the dead to be raised.

Christians still get sick; Christians still die physically.

But the word of Jesus is just as powerful.

It still transforms ordinary lives into extraordinary ones.

Though we may not be able to see anything spectacular on the outside, when someone believes in Jesus’ word, the spiritually dead are brought back to life.

The spiritually sick are healed.

You might not see it straight away, but it’s a spectacular thing to behold.

If you haven’t been listening to Jesus’ words, then pick up a Bible, read through the rest of the Gospel of John.

I’ve read it many times and I’m always amazed at what Jesus says in there.

If you’re not sure where to start, drop me a message and I would love to read / listen or arrange someone to listen / hear Jesus’ words with you.

Finally, we see that belief in Christ spreads wonderfully in families

There are many Christians who believe because someone in their family, someone in their household led them to Jesus.

For my wife Jenny, it was her sister who first became a Christian.

She then led Jenny and her brother Ken to become Christians.

Later on, their mother became a Christian.

Many years later, their father became a Christian.

And we thank God that her children, along with our children, are Christians.

How about you? Who led you to Christ?

According to research, 65.4% of Church attenders said that their mother was the most significant person to show them what faith was about.

46.6% said that their father was the most significant person to show them what faith was about.

A parents’ faith can have a long and lasting impact on their children.

In some cases, other family members can have lasting impact as well.

Especially in a time like ours, when we’re not able to meet physically together as God’s family, the family will be the place where we’ll continue to grow in our faith.

What we’re hoping to do in the coming months is to equip families so that they can continue to grow in Christ together.

In time, we hope to bring two or more families together so that they can encourage one another to grow in Christ.

As well as fathers and mothers, uncles and aunties will be involved in seeing their children grow in Christ.

We want you and your household to believe and grow in Jesus, just like the man and his household in today’s passage.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How deep is my belief in Jesus? Is it broad and shallow? Or is it deep?

What is my testimony about the extraordinary Jesus Christ?

How can I encourage my family to grow deep in their faith in Christ?

Let’s not be content with a superficial knowledge of Jesus.

Let’s dive deep as we grow in our faith of Jesus Christ.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you that you know us and yet you still love us. Thank you that you’ve sent Jesus to reveal yourself to us. Please forgive us when we come to him only when we’re desperate and we want something from him. Please forgive us for our lack of faith. By your Spirit, please give us a faith that takes Jesus at his word. Enlighten our hearts and our minds so that we can see the wonder of your word. And please use us to bring your word to our families, our friends, and anyone that you’ve placed in our paths this coming week. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”.
