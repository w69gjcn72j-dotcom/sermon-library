Today, I want to share with you and all of us here a passage from John 7.

Our Church has been going through the Gospel of John for most of this year and one of the most striking things we see here is the fickleness of people’s faith.

We see this especially in chapter 6.

Not long after Jesus has miraculously fed thousands of people with five loaves of bread and two fish, many of them were offended by Jesus’ teaching and left Jesus.

Even among those who have stayed with Jesus, the Gospel of John has an ominous note for one of them:

“Jesus replied to them, “Didn’t I choose you, the Twelve? Yet one of you is a devil.” 71 He was referring to Judas, Simon Iscariot’s son, one of the Twelve, because he was going to betray him.”

Throughout the Gospel of John, we as readers are challenged to ask ourselves:

Have I truly been freed from sin or am I still a slave to sin?

Am I a genuine disciple of Jesus or am I a fake?

That’s what we’re going to see here again in today’s passage.

The Son confronts believers who won’t listen

The passage begins on what seems to be a pretty positive note: “As he was saying these things, many believed in him.”

But we know from the rest of the Gospel of John that this may not be a good thing.

Jesus himself certainly wasn’t convinced that these were genuine believers.

Most of us would be happy for people to say that they are believers.

But Jesus pushes further.

Jesus confronts the Jews who had believed him with the requirement of what makes a person a true disciple.

Look with me at verse 31, ‘Then Jesus said to the Jews who had believed him, “If you continue in my word, you really are my disciples.”’

Our criteria for what it means for person to be a believer is so low compared to Jesus.

For us, if the person ticks yes on the census form, or comes to Church on special occasions, or comes to Church when they have free time, then we call them believers.

But not Jesus; these criteria fall woefully short.

Jesus says, if you continue in my word, you really are my disciples.

In other words, if you don’t continue in Jesus’ words, you’re not his disciples.

Jesus continues, in verse 32, “You will know the truth, and the truth will set you free.”

Not only is continuing in the word of Jesus the requirement of what it means to be a true disciple.

Continuing in the word of Jesus is the requirement of being set free.

At this, the Jews who were listening to Jesus were offended.

They objected what Jesus’ said about them.

Basically, he said that they were slaves who need to be set free by the truth.

But they argued that they were not slaves.

They argued that Jesus was offering something they didn’t need.

It’s like this ad that I saw on the internet.

It’s for New York air.

It says that it’s air from New York, trapped in a can.

It’s a prank, of course! Who needs the polluted air from New York?

The Jews in Jesus’ days said that they’re not slaves and so they were implying that they have no use for Jesus’ offer, just like we have no use for air from New York.

These Jews saw themselves as believers in Jesus.

They were the good guys!

But as long as they refuse to continue in the words of Jesus, they remain in their sin.

Look with me at verse 34, “Jesus responded, “Truly I tell you, everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin.”

And as someone who commits sin, they remain slaves of sin.

Even though they might not admit it, they were slaves to the ultimate slave master.

They were slaves to sin itself.

The best slave masters are those who have manipulated the slaves so effectively that they don’t even know they’re slaves.

The slaves freely obey the slave master, thinking that they’re free when they’re in fact serving their master.

Sinners might think that they’re in control of their sin when it’s sin that’s in fact in control of them.

What’s the clearest indication that they were hopelessly enslaved to sin?

It’s their refusal to listen to the word of Jesus.

Jesus spoke God’s truth to them, but they refused to listen.

They called themselves sons of Abraham.

Abraham was someone who trusted in God, listened to and obeyed God.

Abraham was willing to leave his country and his family behind because God had told him to go.

The Jews in the passage claimed to be Abraham’s sons in that regard.

Not only were the sons of Abraham by blood but they were also sons of Abraham in the way they obeyed God.

However, Jesus refuted by saying that Abraham wouldn’t do what they did, if he were alive in Jesus’ days.

The Jews in Jesus’ days were trying to kill Jesus, the One who spoke the truth about God; Abraham would’ve never done that.

They then claimed to be sons of God.

However, Jesus refuted them again by saying that if they were truly sons of God, then they would love him.

They would’ve recognised that Jesus was from God.

But they haven’t listened to Jesus.

In fact, Jesus went further.

Look with me at verse 43, “Why don’t you understand what I say? Because you cannot listen to my word.”

They didn’t listen to Jesus because they couldn’t listen to Jesus’ words.

They were incapable of listening to Jesus; it was beyond their ability to listen and obey Jesus’ words.

Why not?

Ultimately, it was because their father was the devil.

Look with me at verse 44, “You are of your father the devil, and you want to carry out your father’s desires. He was a murderer from the beginning and does not stand in the truth, because there is no truth in him. When he tells a lie, he speaks from his own nature, because he is a liar and the father of lies.”

The devil is the father of lies.

In the beginning, he challenged God’s authority by asking, “Did God really say that?”

He even lied by directly contradicting God, “You will not surely die” when God said “on the day you eat of it (fruit of the knowledge of good and evil), you will surely die.”

The devil’s mother tongue is lies; when the devil speaks lies, he’s speaking his native language.

He speaks lies about God, lies about our world and lies about you!

He tells us, “You’re free to do what you want” when it’s an outright lie.

And all of us, before we follow Jesus, by default follow the devil.

There’s no middle ground; there’s no neutral ground from which we choose to follow Jesus or the devil.

By default we follow the devil and unable to believe the words of Jesus.

We don’t get to choose because we can’t choose, we’re not able to choose.

That’s what Jesus tells the Jews in his days in verse 45, “Yet because I tell the truth, you do not believe me.”

Jesus was not simply saying that the Jews didn’t believe him.

He was saying, it’s because I’m telling you the truth that you don’t believe me.

We, like the Jews in Jesus’ days, are so caught up in the devils’ lies that we don’t believe in Jesus because we can’t accept the truth

We are not capable of believing the truth and so we don’t believe in Jesus.

Last week, Greg Sheridan from The Australian, wrote this about Australia.

“We are on the way to becoming, for the first time, an avowedly anti-Christian nation. Not just non-Christian, but anti-Christian. The census tells us. The culture tells us. The law tells us.”

He cited an example of an art exhibition in Tasmania, support by the Tasmanian government, that openly and explicitly mocks Christianity.

And it’s only one of hundreds of examples where our country openly mocks Christianity.

In our national anthem we sing, “Australians all, let us rejoice,

For we are young and free”

That’s how the Jews in Jesus’ days saw themselves; that’s how many Australians today, whether born here or migrated from elsewhere, see ourselves.

And so, when Jesus confronts us with our sin, we object and we resent.

How dare Jesus challenges on our freedom!

Freedom comes from remaining in the Son’s word

But as long as we commit sins, we are slaves of sin.

Sin isn’t just one or two big bad things that you do.

We’re sinners through and through.

As sinners, we live and breathed sin.

Sin is our desire to love ourselves above God.

It’s a desire that ensnares us, without us realising it.

Whenever we think we can disobey God and not suffer judgement from God, we fall for the same lie that the devil had told the first people in the beginning.

There’s no denying it; we’re slaves of sin.

However, Jesus has come to set us free.

Jesus is the Truth; he’s not just a Teacher of Truth but Truth itself.

It’s only as we keep his word that we can truly be his disciples.

And it’s only as we’re his disciples that he sets us free from our sin.

Jesus uses the example of slaves and sons to prove his point.

Look with me at verse 35-36, “A slave does not remain in the household forever, but a son does remain forever. 36 So if the Son sets you free, you really will be free.”

A slave does not truly belong to the household.

They’re not permanent; they come and go.

But the son, the Son remains forever in the household.

The Son doesn’t stop being the Son.

And he has the authority to set slaves free.

Slaves can’t set themselves free; no one but the master can set the slaves free.

But if the Son sets you free, you really will be free.

Likewise, as slaves of sin, we can’t set ourselves free; no one can.

However, Jesus, the Son of God has the authority to set us free from sin.

Only he can set us free.

And how do you know whether you’re free?

If you remain in Jesus, if you continue in his word.

It’s not a once-off event; it’s not a matter of you simply ticking off a box to say that you’re a Christian.

You are a Christian, you’re a true disciple of Christ, if you continue in his word.

That’s the mark of a true Christian.

Fake Christians will say that they believe in Jesus but don’t follow Jesus’ words.

Fake Christians are still slaves of sin, much like the Jews in today’s passage.

True Christians, however, will continue in Jesus’ words.

They will listen and they will obey.

It’s a little bit like this.

Two weeks ago, my family and I were in Movie world on the Gold Coast.

There were a lot of merchandise and souvenirs that mega fans can buy.

If you are a Batman fan, you can go to the Batman store where you can buy batman shirts, batman hat, batman towels.

I am an OK fan of the Batman but there are people who will live and breathe all things Batman.

As Christians, we’re called to live and breathe the gospel.

This doesn’t mean that we buy all sorts of merchandise to show that we’re Christians but it does mean that Jesus is what we’re on about.

When the world sees us, they should see us striving to listen, to understand and to obey Jesus, no matter how poorly our attempts might be.

When the world sees us, they should see that despite all our weaknesses, despite all our imperfection, despite all our trials, we have a joy that comes from being free in Christ.

That’s why at St Paul’s we have this motto:

See the Gospel, Hear the Gospel, Believe the Gospel, Tell the Gospel.

That’s what we want our Church to be about.

We want to be a Church that that remains in the word of Jesus; we want to be a church where true disciples of Christ are nurtured.

And we want to be a Church that proclaims that lives and breathes the gospel of Jesus Christ.

3. Live and breathe the Son’s word

And so, Hien and Rita, live and breathed the Son’s word.

Here are four things for you to do as you begin your ministry with us:

Live a life that remains in the Son’s word.

We’re so glad to hear that you’re moving into our area very soon.

As you do, share your life with other members of our Church.

By the way you live, show your friends and your families and your new neighbours what following Jesus is about.

Don’t go beyond the word of Jesus; always remain in the word of Jesus.

Show people what it means for people to believe the gospel.

In your life, may people see the gospel.

Don’t stop breathing out the Son’s word

Keep proclaiming the good news of Jesus.

The world will tell you to keep the gospel to yourself.

The world will tell you to calm down when you tell them they’re sinning (didn’t someone write a song about that?)

But don’t stop proclaiming the gospel.

Tell the Gospel.

In your conversations, in your sermons, in your Bible studies, tell the gospel.

Pray for opportunities for people to hear the gospel through you.

Don’t lose the opportunities that you’ve been given.

Keep teaching the gospel to us

It’s not people who aren’t believers who need to hear the gospel.

From today’s passage, it’s people who think they are believers who need to be reminded that we need to remain in the gospel.

Keep challenging us, keep confronting us with God’s word.

Help us to praise and rejoice in the freedom we have in Christ

Even though following Jesus is costly, and it will cost us everything we have,

it’s nothing compared with the freedom that’s ours in Christ.

Christ’s truth is greater than anything that we can ever own.

Christ’s freedom is far better than anything this world has to offer.

Help us to rejoice in the freedom that’s ours in Christ as we grow in the word of Christ together.

Of course, this is not just for Hien and Rita, but for all of us here present.

May all of us here, no matter where we gather, continue to rejoice in the freedom that’s ours in Christ, as we continue in the word of Christ.
