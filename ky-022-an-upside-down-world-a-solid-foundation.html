An Upside-Down World, a Solid Foundation

Luke 6:20–49 · Sunday 22 November 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah · Sermon by Rev Jerry Zhu

Big Idea: The kingdom of God does not belong to the so-called “self-sufficient”, but to those who know they have nothing and can only depend on God; so build your identity and security on Jesus Christ, and let love, forgiveness, generosity and obedience flow from the grace you have already received.

Aim: by the end, students will

see that Jesus turns the world’s idea of “blessed” upside down, and ask what they are leaning on instead of God;

understand that we love enemies, don’t judge and give generously because we are already God’s children, not to earn his love;

know that the only foundation that stands in life’s storms is Jesus, not how good we are.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • If you scrolled your feed right now, what would it say makes someone “blessed”? Who looks like they’ve “made it”?
  • Someone shoves past you in the canteen line, or roasts you in the group chat. What’s your first reaction? Be honest.

Read: Luke 6:20–49 (CSB)

Context

This is Luke’s “Sermon on the Plain”, Jesus’ big talk to his followers after he chose the Twelve. Matthew has a similar one (the Sermon on the Mount), but Luke’s version is short and sharp: blessings and woes side by side. Jesus is telling his disciples what following him really means, and it turns the world’s values upside down.

Discuss

  1. (v.20) Who is Jesus looking at when he speaks? Why do you think Luke tells us that?
  2. (vv.20–23) Jesus says the poor, the hungry and those who weep are “blessed”. What do you think he means? Why might it be easier for someone with nothing to admit they need God?
  3. (vv.24–26) Is Jesus saying it’s wrong to be rich? What does “you have received your comfort” mean? What do people our age lean on instead of God: likes, marks, sport, looks, being popular?
  4. (vv.27–30) What does “offer the other also” mean, and what doesn’t it mean? Read John 18:22–23. When is it right to tell a teacher or parent, or set a boundary?
  5. (v.31) How is this different from “if you’re nice to me, I’ll be nice to you”? What would it look like at school this week?
  6. (v.35) Do we become God’s children by loving our enemies, or love our enemies because we are already his children? Why does it matter which way round?
  7. (vv.37–38) What’s the difference between judging someone and knowing right from wrong? Where do you see judging in group chats? What does the overflowing measure of grain tell you about God?
  8. (vv.46–49) Both houses look the same until the flood. What makes the difference? What is your life being built on right now?

Leader’s Notes

Framing. Joyfully point to Jesus. Some students may not yet be Christians; never corner anyone. Teens live under constant comparison and quick online judgement. The sermon’s key is that identity comes before behaviour: we love, forgive and give not to earn God’s love, but because we have already received it. Be alert to anyone who is being bullied or hurt at home; this passage never means they must stay silent.

Two likely wrong turns

  • Wrong turn 1: “So I should just let people walk all over me.” No. At his trial Jesus asked, “Why do you hit me?” (John 18:23). Turning the other cheek is about a heart free of revenge, not a rule. Telling a trusted adult and setting boundaries is right.
  • Wrong turn 2: “If I try hard enough to love and give, God will accept me.” No. We love because God first loved us (Romans 5:8). Our foundation is what Jesus has done, not our performance.

Question helps

Q1 — Jesus is speaking to his followers, not just the crowd. He looks at them with love and seriousness: “You need to know what following me really means.”

Q2 — The poverty includes having nothing and knowing you can’t save yourself. People with nothing to lean on often find it easiest to say, “I need God.” The kingdom belongs to them.

Q3 — No: Abraham and Job were rich. The woe is on people who make money, likes or praise their comfort. The sermon told of a young man who kept comparing himself online and it never felt like enough. That fullness is fake and doesn’t last.

Q4 — It means not joining the cycle of revenge. But Jesus himself asked “Why do you hit me?” at his trial, so it isn’t a rigid rule. If someone is being hurt, getting help and setting boundaries is right.

Q5 — The world says: you go first, then I’ll be nice. Jesus says: be kind first, expecting nothing back. That’s grace.

Q6 — We don’t earn being God’s children; in Christ we already are, so we can start to look like our Father, who is kind to ungrateful people. If we get the order wrong, it turns into trying to earn God’s love.

Q7 — We still say some things are wrong (John 7:24), but we don’t act like the judge while everyone else is on trial, because we need the cross too. The seller pressed the grain down until it spilled over: God gives us far more than we deserve.

Q8 — You can’t see a foundation until the flood. Building on the rock means building on Jesus, not on being good. The sermon told of a guy who couldn’t forgive until he started each day remembering that Jesus died for him while he was still an enemy.

Church vision tie-in. “From faith to faith”: the faith passed on to KOGYouth becomes real when each student builds their own life on Jesus.
