The Folly of Ambitious Disobedience

Genesis 10:1-11:32 · 2023
Big IdeaA life of ambitious disobedience will end in failure and judgement; a life of faithful obedience to Christ will outlast even the heavens and the earth.

Sermon Title: The folly of ambitious disobedience

Passage: Genesis 10:1-11:32 (Focus on 11:1-9)

Big Idea: A life of ambitious disobedience will end in failure and judgement; a life of faithful obedience to Christ will outlast even the heavens and the earth.

A wayward stopover leads to ambitious disobedience

A divine visit confuses ambitious sinners

Build a house that outlasts the heavens and the earth

A story was told of a man called 朱泙漫 (Zhu YuMan) in ancient China.

朱泙漫 was a very smart man and also very rich.

He would sell all that he had to learn new skills.

One day, someone said to him, "There is a strange man named 支離益(Zhi Liyi), who lives in the deep mountains and valleys far away, who can kill dragons with one hand.

It's just that this person is difficult to find, and he doesn't accept apprentices easily.

If you learn the skill of dragon-slaying, you will be the best of the best.”

And so 朱泙漫 Zhu YuMan travelled far and wide to train under 支離益(Zhi Liyi).

朱泙漫 Zhu YuMan worked hard and spent three years to master the skill of dragon-slaying.

He was very excited when he got back home and couldn’t wait to show his friends his new skills.

But when the villagers heard it, they all just laughted.

They said to 朱泙漫 Zhu YuMan, “Have you even seen a dragon before? You know they are no real dragons around, right?”

朱泙漫 Zhu YuMan then realized that even though he was ambitious and had trained so hard to be the best dragon-slayer in the world,

it was a complete waste of time because there are no dragons to kill.

Since then, a phrase was coined after 朱泙漫’s journey: it’s called 屠龍之技 (tú lóng zhī jì)

It describes people who are highly skilled at something completely useless.

Many of us applaud people who are driven, people who are ambitious.

We congratulate people who worked hard to achieve great things in life.

But what happens when they’re ambitious about the wrong things?

What happens when we don’t examine our ambition?

That’s what we’ll see in today’s passage.

Please open up to Genesis chapter 10, beginning from verse 31.

We’ll see three things:

A wayward stopover leads to ambitious disobedience

A divine visit confuses ambitious sinners

Build a house that outlasts the heavens and the earth

Before we begin, let’s pray,

“Father, thank you for your word, the Bible. By your Spirit, please help us to listen, trust and obey Jesus even more. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

A wayward stopover leads to ambitious disobedience

Over the past two months, we’ve been looking at what happened to mankind after they have sinned against God.

Let’s see how much you remember from the last eight weeks..

After God created the heavens and the earth, he told the first man, (Adam) and the first woman, (Eve), to be fruitful and multiply and to fill the earth.

However, Adam and Eve sinned against God and they were exiled from the east of the Garden of (Eden).

The story continued with their three sons, Cain, Abel and (Seth).

Cain, out of his jealousy, (killed) Abel his brother.

Cain himself was exiled from his family, and lived in the land of Nod, East of Eden.

Adam and Eve had Seth afterwards and the story continues with his line, as well as Cain’s line.

Ten generations later, God looked at the earth and saw that every inclination of the human mind was (evil) all the time.

And so, God sent a flood to destroy all humans.

However, he found favour with Noah and rescued him his wife, and his (three) sons, along with their wives.

They took pairs of every (animal) and God kept them safe.

After they came out of the ark, God gave the sign of the (rainbow) to remind himself that he will never destroy the world by flood again.

God also told Noah’s sons to be fruitful and (multiply).

Things were looking good but Noah got (drunk) and his son Ham mocked his Dad for being drunk.

How will they go from here?

Well, the three sons of Noah did multiply.

In Genesis chapter 10, we see many sons and people group that grew from these the families of these three brothers.

In fact, there are 70 nations listed in chapter 10.

The world’s population was increasing again.

Look with me at Genesis 10, beginning from verse 31,

“These are Shem’s sons by their clans, according to their languages, in their lands and their nations. These are the clans of Noah’s sons, according to their family records, in their nations. The nations on earth spread out from these after the flood.”

Shem, along with his brothers, were spreading across the world according to their languages, lands, and nations.

Wait, hold up, languages?

How did they have different languages when they all came from the same family?

Didn’t the family speak the same language?

That’s what we’ll find out in Genesis 11.

Genesis 11 records an event that has shaped the movement of the whole human population back then and continues to have a profound effect on us today.

Look with me at Genesis 11:1-3, “The whole earth had the same language and vocabulary. 2 As people migrated from the east, they found a valley in the land of Shinar and settled there. 3 They said to each other, “Come, let’s make oven-fired bricks.” (They used brick for stone and asphalt for mortar.)

Everyone originally spoke one language since they were all from the same family.

And they moved East.

Adam and Eve were exiled from the East of the Garden of Eden, Cain was exiled and went to Nod, East of Eden.

And here they are, migrating from the East.

It doesn’t look good for them!

They found a valley in the land of Shinar and settled down there.

Once they’ve settled there, they decided to build a city and a tower together using oven-fired brick.

Now, we got some lego packs here.

If you’re in KOG, and you’re sitting next to a responsible adult, like your parent, you can get some legos and start building a tower.

You can listen to the talk as you build the tower.

Let’s see who can build a big one!

When the people were doing at the Valley of Shinar.

They were making bricks to build a city and a tower.

Why?

Look with me at verse 4, “4 And they said, “Come, let’s build ourselves a city and a tower with its top in the sky. Let’s make a name for ourselves; otherwise, we will be scattered throughout the earth.”

They wanted to build a legacy for themselves.

They want to be united as one people, so that they won’t be scattered.

Separately, they were insignificant individuals, but together, they can do something great.

If they put their minds together, they can achieve great things, even building a tower that reaches the heavens, where God is!

They were ambitious!

Doesn’t that sound familiar?

It’s a very modern thing to say!

Today, it might sound like this, “If you put your mind to it, you can accomplish anything” or

“Alone, we can do so little; together we can do so much.”

And as humans, we like work together to achieve great things.

That desire for greatness as described here in Genesis 11, is still seen in man’s desire to build impressive sky-scrappers.

The taller the sky-scrapper, the greater you are in the eyes of the community, the greater you are in the eyes of the world.

But even the tallest sky-scrapper in the world is not tall enough.

We wanted to go even further, and so we worked together to go to the moon.

We might even work together to go to Mars.

But even that’s not far enough!

Many scientists want to take us back to the very beginning of the universe.

They present a lot of theories of how the universe began and came up with ideas like the Big Bang.

Many scientists have come up with many ideas about what the first milliseconds, first nanoseconds, were like.

Sometimes, they were so sure that it sounds as if they were there!

The Bible tells us that in the beginning was the Word, and the word was with God and the Word was God.

But the scientists want to tell us that in the beginning was Big Bang, and I, man, can show you what it’s like to be there, at the Big Bang.

Like those in Genesis 11, they want to take us to the heavens, to where God is, and maybe, to try and show us that God wasn’t there.

As a side note, if you want to find out more, Dr Peter Chen will be sharing with us his thoughts on this in a few weeks’ time.

As humans, we haven’t changed, we haven’t evolved to become better.

Just like those in Genesis 11, we still want to make a name for ourselves, we still want to make our way to the heavens by our hard work and by our ambition.

But is it wrong to be ambitious?

Don’t we congratulate people who achieve great things?

It’s not just non-Christians who congratulate people on achievements, Christians do as well.

We also congratulate people who have overcome great obstacles and achieve great things.

We don’t read biographies about normal people; we read biographies about extraordinary people.

Is it wrong to be ambitious and make a name for ourselves?

2. A divine visit confuses ambitious sinners

Look with me at verse 5, “Then the LORD came down to look over the city and the tower that the humans were building.”

God had to come down from heaven to look at this great tower that the humans were building.

It’s not because God is short sighted and needs glasses.

Rather it’s so small that he had to go down to see this tower that they were boasting about.

You call that your great tower, the tower that will reach my throne? No, it’s a little tower, a tiny tower.

Their arrogance has blinded them to how insignificant they were.

Even so, God responds to them by going down to them and confuse their language.

Look with me at verses 6-7, “6 The LORD said, “If they have begun to do this as one people all having the same language, then nothing they plan to do will be impossible for them. Come, let’s go down there and confuse their language so that they will not understand one another’s speech.”

God wasn’t threatened by them.

Together, having the same language, they can achieve the impossible.

However, when they do these things, it’s beyond what God has set for them.

Just as God had given Adam and Eve the command to not eat the Forbidden fruit,

God had commanded their sons and daughters to multiply and spread across the world.

The people were deliberately disobeying God by not spreading but staying in the Valley of Shinar.

God is the Ruler and Judge of all; there are consequences and punishments when we don’t obey Him.

Far too often, we applaud ambitions and achievements without examining whether it’s what God wants.

When a group, a community, a nation is ambitious about the wrong thing, horrible things, utterly destructive things can happen.

We’ve seen wars start because of the ambition of one man, as well as the ambition of the party he’s leading.

We’ve seen generations of families suffer, because of the ambition of one nation to rule over others.

Ambitions can lead us achieving difficult things, even seemingly impossible things, but those things might not be good.

To curb the ambitions of man in Genesis 11, God frustrated their plans by confusing their language.

I don’t need to tell you how difficult it is to try to communicate with someone who doesn’t speak your own language.

Our Church service this morning has people who have different languages from each other.

It’s not easy!

As a result of God’s intervention, the Babel Tower Project was abandoned.

They stopped building and they started spreading.

Despite man’s ambitious disobedience, God is still in control.

We think we have a plan for a better humanity, and we want to do this without God.

It’s sheer arrogance to think that we can be better human beings, better images of God, without God.

It’s so foolish to think that our tiny effort can bring us before God’s throne.

It’s embarrassing.

But God, in his kindness and love, sometimes intervene to stop us going down this path of ambitious disobedience.

He stops us not because he doesn’t love us, but precisely because he does!

He doesn’t want us to descend further into sin, to waste our lives in sin, thinking we are achieving something when we are in fact just wasting our time.

And so, yes, the fact that God had confused the languages of mankind was an act of judgment,

but it was also an act of kindness.

God intervened to thwart man’s march to sin.

A few years ago, we planned for a big mission to the kids in our area.

We printed many, many flyers, like this one, all ready to distribute to all the children around our area.

But COVD struck and we had to cancel our plans.

And all these brochures were going to the recycling bin (I kept this as a reminder for myself)

I don’t know about you, but every now and then, our plans don’t work out.

Sometimes, even our plans for God, which we prayed for, don’t work out.

And we ask God why?

Why didn’t God allow our plans to happen?

There are at least three reasons why God says No to our plans.

It’s not a straight No. It’s a not yet.

It’s the right plan, but it’s the wrong time. Try again. Don’t give up!

It’s a No; because there’s something better.

It’s the good thing, but it’s not the best thing. Find the best thing and pray for that!

It’s a No because it’s the wrong thing to ask for.

There might be sins in our lives that we need to repent of.

We need to ask ourselves whether we have prayed from the right heart.

We need to ask ourselves was our ambition for God’s glory or was it for our glory?

After Babel, we see this in verse 10, “These are the family records of Shem. Shem lived 100 years and fathered Arpachshad two years after the flood.”

It sounds a lot like the beginning of today’s passage, in 10:31.

Man has gone off the path God has set and decided to build Babel.

Now after Babel, God returns man back to his path and the focus of the Bible continues in the lie of Shem.

As we go down the line of Shem, we see a reference to another set of three sons.

They were Abram, Nahor and Haran.

This group of three sons prepares us for the next chapter of God’s dealings with mankind.

Here’s what God says to Abram in 12:1-2, “The LORD said to Abram: Go from your land, your relatives, and your father’s house to the land that I will show you. I will make you into a great nation, I will bless you, I will make your name great, and you will be a blessing.”

God himself will make Abram’s name great.

We don’t take the initiative to make our name great; it’s God who makes anyone’s name great.

And though Abram’s name was great, his name wasn’t the greatest.

The man with the greatest name is Jesus Christ, the Son of God.

3. Build a house that outlasts the heavens and the earth

Look with me at Philippians 2:9-11,

“For this reason God highly exalted him

and gave him the name

that is above every name,

so that at the name of Jesus

every knee will bow—

in heaven and on earth

and under the earth—

and every tongue will confess

that Jesus Christ is Lord,

to the glory of God the Father.”

It’s Jesus who has the greatest name of all.

It’s Jesus whom God honours above every other name.

What man was trying to do by our ambitious disobedience, Jesus has accomplished by his trust and faith in God the Father.

From now on, anyone who wants to true security in eternity, need to find their identity in the Lord Jesus.

How do you know whether you are secure in Jesus?

Jesus tells us in the parable of the Wise and the Foolish.

Jesus says, ““Therefore, everyone who hears these words of mine and acts on them will be like a wise man who built his house on the rock. 25 The rain fell, the rivers rose, and the winds blew and pounded that house. Yet it didn’t collapse, because its foundation was on the rock.”

When you hear the words of Jesus, here at church, in your Growth Groups, in your own home, and you act on it, you will be safe.

That’s what it means to trust and obey Jesus.

It’s no good just listening to Jesus’ words but don’t act on it, even if you listen to it every Sunday.

Jesus says that person is like someone who builds on sand.

It’s a crash waiting to happen.

And it’s easy to do that; it’s easy just to listen to the Bible and not do anything about it.

In fact, almost by default, if we don’t do anything to try to remember God’s words, if we don’t take written notes, or mental notes, we will build our life on the sand.

You don’t need to do anything to make your life crash.

It will eventually happen if you listen to Jesus’ words but don’t do anything about it.

Your life won’t last on the Day the world ends, on the day when God comes in judgement.

But if we take the effort to listen, and take the effort to do change the way we live after hearing God’s word, we will survive God’s judgement,

Not because we have achieve any good things by ourselves,

But because we trust Jesus and what he has done for us.

If we trust Jesus, then we will obey him, we will do what he say.

If we don’t do what he say, it shows that we don’t trust him.

Your life reflects your faith.

It takes ambition to trust and obey Jesus; it takes hard work and sacrifice.

But we do it because we truly believe that Jesus is the Lord.

We do it because we trust that he has died for us and has risen from the grave.

We do it because He’s God and we’re not.

We do it because he has the words of eternal life, and if we trust and obey him, our life will outlast even heaven and earth.

That’s a life that’s worth living!

As a minister, I feel nervous for people who come and sit to listen to the Bible with no intention to obey, and no intention to change.

I feel nervous for them because it’s dangerous.

It’s like watching an adult build a house of made from Lego thinking it can survive a nuclear explosion.

It won’t stand a chance when God’s judgement comes.

Even though they heard Jesus’ teaching many times, and hear me warning them many times, they remain stubborn and refuse to obey.

They remain stubbornly disobedient, even ambitiously disobedient, sometimes to the point of embarrassment.

If God doesn’t intervene, they will die.

And so, sometimes I pray that God will intervene, that God will frustrate their plans, so that they’ll turn back to God.

And sometimes God listens!

If you’re planning to do something ambitious but disobedient and it fails, it might had been an answer to someone’s prayer for your salvation.

It might had even been God’s answer to mine!

What happens when we don’t examine our ambition?

By default, we as men and women are ambitious for the wrong thing, which ultimately lead to our destruction.

Instead, obey and trust Jesus for a life that will outlasts heaven and earth.

Instead, be ambitious for Christ’s glory, be ambitious for his fame!

And so, examine your ambitions in the light of Christ.

The greater Philosopher Socrates once said, “An unexamined life is not worth living”

It’s true, but I like to add that “An unexamined life, untouched by the glory of Christ, is not worth living.”

By all means, be ambitious but be ambitious for the glory of Christ.

Be ambitious to live a life of faith and obedience to Christ.

Think about the three questions that I ask at the end of the sermon.

Ask those questions of each other in your Growth Group and in your family during the week.

Repent of any wrong and selfish ambitions that you have.

Also repent of having no ambitions to grow in Christ.

Trust and obedience in Jesus Christ is a hunger, a longing to seek His Kingdom and His righteousness.

Be ambitious, be hungry to obey and trust Jesus.

Don’t waste your life on dragon slaying skills (屠龍之技 (tú lóng zhī jì).

Instead, be ambitious to live a life of faith and obedience to Christ.

Examine your ambitions in the light of Christ.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What might ambitious disobedience look like in our lives?

Has God said ‘No’ to our prayers recently? Why?

How can I intentionally trust and obey God when I listen to his words?

Jesus says, “But everyone who hears these words of mine and doesn’t act on them will be like a foolish man who built his house on the sand. 27 The rain fell, the rivers rose, the winds blew and pounded that house, and it collapsed. It collapsed with a great crash.”

Let’s pray,

“Father, thank you for stopping us from descending to our own destruction by your divine intervention. Thank you for sending Jesus to save us. Forgive us for our ambitious disobedience. Forgive us when we present our plans as godly ambitions when it was really all about our own glory. Lead us to repentance. Please by your Spirit, give us the hunger, the ambition, to trust and obey Jesus in all that we do, for your glory, and the glory of Jesus Christ and the glory of the Holy Spirit, Amen.”
