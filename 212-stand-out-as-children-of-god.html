What kind of Christians do we want to be?

What kind of Christians do we want to be known as?

A common response may be that we want to be loving Christians.

To see whether this is true, I went to one of the biggest local Christian online bookstore and searched for titles that have the word ‘love’ in it.

There are up to 4370 books in this Christian bookstore that has the word ‘love’ or something similar in it.

I then tried the word ‘obey’.

Guess how many entries came up?

Six.

Up to 4370 books on love and six books on obey.

It seems like obedience is not very marketable among Christians

If the Apostle Paul wrote a Christian book today, it might not sell many copies because he spends a lot of time talking about obedience.

Why does Paul talk to Christians about obedience?

Aren’t Christians saved by faith already? If so, why does he still talk about obedience?

Obey by working out your salvation

If you have your Bible with me, open your Bible with me to Philippians 2:12, “Therefore, my dear friends, just as you have always obeyed, so now, not only in my presence but even more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling.”

Last week, we read about the humility of Jesus, from verses 5-11.

Jesus, who is in very nature God, took on the very nature of a servant.

Jesus humbled himself by becoming obedient.

Jesus’ obedience is Jesus’ humility in action.

It’s in light of Jesus’ humility and obedience that we come to verse twelve.

‘Therefore, my dear friends, (in light of Jesus’ humility, in light of Jesus’ obedience)… just as you have always obeyed, so now, not only in my presence but even more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling.’

what does Paul say to them?

As you have always obeyed, continue to work out your salvation.

Continue to work out your salvation?

What does it mean to continue to work out your salvation?

Before we look at what it means, we’ll look at what it doesn’t mean.

It doesn’t mean, “God has done his bit of your salvation; now you’ll need to do your bit.”

And it certainly doesn’t mean, “Let go and let God! You don’t have to do ANYTHING to continue as a Christian.”

The word translated ‘work out’ in the phrase means to bring about, or to effect.

It’s the idea of bringing your salvation out in the open; it’s working to bring your salvation out.

Peter O’Brien, a very well-respected New Testament scholar, said that the phrase means to bring out your salvation with ‘continuous, sustained, strenuous effort’.

It’s applying continuous, sustained, strenuous effort to bring your salvation out in the open.

It’s like mining.

As some of you know, for a number of years, Australia has been in a mining boom.

For a long time, Australia has been able to mine and export a lot of minerals to other countries.

While it was a boom, the minerals weren’t just growing on the side of the road.

No, mining is hard work; it takes a lot of effort.

The miners have to dig caves and go deep into the ground or in mountains to get the minerals.

A lot of times, the minerals are really hard to get to and so the miners would often use explosives to get to the minerals.

You can’t see it from the outside, but the miners knew that there are precious minerals on the inside.

And so they work hard, they apply continuous, sustained, strenuous effort to get the minerals out.

That’s what Christians are called to do.

God has saved them; it’s a spiritual reality that’s happened to them.

And so, they are called to apply continuous, sustained, strenuous effort to bring that salvation out in the open.

Now note that it’s not about bringing you out.

The Bible is not saying that you are a diamond in the rough and that you’ll need to bring your special qualities out.

No. The thing you need to show the world is not how special you are but how great God’s salvation is!

You are bringing God’s salvation out; you are showing the world what salvation looks like in your life.

And it will be hard, strenuous work.

You will be asked to make tough decisions on the way you spend your time, on the way you spend your money, on the way you relate to your friends.

You may feel like you’re missing out on precious study times, and even career opportunities because you are doing things at Church.

Working out your salvation, bringing the effects of your salvation into your everyday life, is hard work and requires continuous, sustained and strenuous effort.

And so, we’re to work out our salvation, with great effort.

And we’re to work out our salvation with fear and trembling.

Fear and trembling?

Why fear and trembling?

Look with me at verse 13, “For it is God who is working in you both to will and to work according to his good purpose.

It’s God who works in us.

When we work out our salvation, when we apply your continuous, sustained and strenuous effort to apply our salvation in our lives, it’s actually God doing his work in us.

When we work out our salvation, it’s God’s Spirit moving our minds, our hearts and our hands to live out our salvation.

We don’t merely work out our salvation in the presence of God’s Spirit.

We work out our salvation by God’s Spirit.

It’s God’s Spirit who works in our minds and in our hearts, who move our hands to make us want to know Jesus, love Jesus and to serve Jesus

It is God’s Spirit who humbles us so that we can obey Jesus.

We can’t obey God without God’s help.

When we read the Bible, when we pray, when we come to Church, when we love one another – it’s all God’s work.

People often ask for evidence of God’s work in our lives.

Here they are!

Whenever we obey God, they evidence God’s work in our lives!

Whenever we see ourselves working out our salvation by obeying God, we ought to be filled with fear and trembling,

Because we see God’s work in our lives!

Some people associate God’s presence with a Church building.

Other people associate God’s presence with traditional Church music while others associate God’s presence with modern Church music.

But according to the Bible, you witness God’s presence when you see a Christian’s obedience.

You witness God’s presence when a young man consciously, intentionally chooses to have a job with a smaller income,

so that he can teach children about Jesus.

You witness God’s presence when an older woman intentionally, consciously, gives to God’s work

So that more people will hear about Jesus.

When we see others in Church obeying God, indeed, when we see ourselves obeying God, we should tremble because we stand in God’s presence.

Our obedience shows that God is at work among us.

Obedience is the fruit of salvation.

If a person is saved, he will obey Jesus, not perfectly, but that’s what his heart desires, that’s what his actions will reflect.

If a person has not been saved, he will not obey Jesus.

He’s not interested to obey Jesus.

And so, as children of God, obey by working out your salvation.

Secondly, as children of God, obey by shining like stars

Obey by shining like stars

Look with me at verse 14, “ Do everything without grumbling and arguing,”

We complain because we don’t get what we want; it’s because our interests aren’t served.

We grumble about how people and how our leaders don’t meet our expectations.

The world is a place full of complainers.

It’s a place where self-interest rule and because of that, it’s a very dark place.

But Christians are to be different; we’re to do everything without grumbling and arguing.

15 so that (verse 15) you may be blameless and pure, children of God who are faultless in a crooked and perverted generation, among whom you shine like stars in the world,”

God’s children are to be blameless, flawless and faultless.

Christians are to be in stark contrast with the rest of the world.

The Church, where Christians meet, is to be a place where humility rules, because their King, their God did not consider equality with God to be grasped.

It’s where people are less concerned about their self-interests and more concerned about their self-sacrifice.

To put it simply, the difference between the Church and the world is light and darkness, day and night.

And in this world, in this darkness, Christians are to be like stars in the universe, showing the world around them that God’s shown us a different way to live.

Friends, that’s what we’re called to be: stars in the world.

In Matthew chapter 5, Jesus calls us to be light of the world; we’re to be a city on a hill, visible to everyone around us.

You can only live a life that shines in the darkness if you hold on to the word of life.

It means keeping a firm gaze on the word of life.

The word of life is the gospel.

The word of life is the message of Jesus, the Servant King.

We hold on to the word of life as we obey the word of life, the words of Jesus.

We hold on to the word of life as we obey Jesus.

As we come in humility and obey Jesus, we will shine in this dark world as children of God, blameless, flawless and faultless.

Our God has given us a wonderful Church building here in Kogarah.

We thank God for it but more importantly I want people to know who we are.

I want people to come and know the people of St Paul’s, not because I want us to be popular, but because I want them to see the relationships that we have with one another.

I want them to see that we’re different, that we don’t come here to serve our self-interests.

I want them to see that we don’t complain nor argue against one another, but that we love one another.

I want them to see how we bring out our salvation in our lives.

I want them to see us shining like stars in the world.

If someone comes to St Paul’s, what will they see?

Will they see people complaining and arguing?

Or will they see people living in humility, serving one another and loving one another?

When people look at us, will they see darkness, or will they see light?

As God’s children, obey by working out your salvation, obey by shining like stars and finally, obey by rejoicing at being spent.

Obey by rejoicing at being spent

Look with me from verse 16, “by holding firm to the word of life. Then I can boast in the day of Christ that I didn’t run or labour for nothing. ”

Paul has worked really hard for the sake of the Philippians.

He worked hard to bring the gospel to them and to nurture them in the gospel.

He’s invested so much time, and effort, and love on them!

He’s worked so hard for them because he wants to see them holding on to the word of life for all their lives.

He’s worked so hard for them so that on the day of Christ, the day when Jesus comes back, he may boast that he did not run or labour for nothing.

Paul knows what it means to labour in vain.

What does it mean to labour in vain?

To support his work, Paul would often work by making tents.

Tents in Paul’s days aren’t like ours, made by a machine in a factory.

Back in Paul’s days, he would have to manually sew the cloth of the tent together.

Now, I’m not an expert on knitting, but some people in our Church are.

Last week, Sheryl and her daughter Jassinta made many scarves for the fathers among us.

I’m thankful for the time that they spent making these scarves.

It would’ve also taken Paul quite a long time to sew a piece of cloth for his tents.

Now imagine Paul sewing away, starting from morning, through lunch until evening.

Now imagine that just as he was applying the finishing touch, he notices what he thought was a tear.

As he looked closer, he notices that it’s a pretty big hole, possibly caused by a rat.

The cloth is not good as a tent anymore.

It would’ve been a waste of Paul’s time.

Paul would’ve laboured in vain.

While it would’ve been tragic for a tent to be a failure, it would’ve been a lot worse for a life to be a failure.

The most tragic failure for a person is for them to be rejected by Jesus Christ on the Day of Christ.

And so Paul tells the Philippian church, “hold on to the words of Jesus for me.

Please tell me that I didn’t waste my time with you.”

Even so, Paul was quite certain that his time with the Philippian Church was not a waste of time.

Look at verse 17, “But even if I am poured out as a drink offering on the sacrificial service of your faith, I am glad and rejoice with all of you.”

Paul sees himself as the drink offering and the Philippian church as the main offering.

Now that’s not to say that they are offering sacrifices to save themselves.

No, Jesus has died, once for all, the righteous for the unrighteous, to bring us to God.

However, as Christians, we’re called to be living sacrifices, offering our lives to God.

That’s what Paul says that the Philippian church has been doing;

They’re offering their lives as living sacrifices to God.

To see them serve Jesus brings Paul joy.

He is happy, he rejoices, when he sees people serving Jesus, sacrificially and faithfully.

Even if he’s like a drink offering that’s being poured out, even if he feels like he’s spent everything emotionally and physically, he’s glad, because he sees their sacrificial service.

Long before studied full-time at Bible college, Jenny and I started a ministry called Junior Worship.

It was made up of mainly high school students.

In fact, I met with someone from Junior Worship just three days ago.

It was hard at the beginning because we didn’t know what to do.

We just taught them the Bible and we taught them how to serve.

Still we stuck with it for about two years.

While it was great work, it was a hard ministry to pull off.

That was more than twenty years ago.

Throughout the past twenty years, I heard how this group continued to grow and how they have continued to serve Jesus.

Two are now working full time as Anglican ministers.

Another two have been studying at Moore College.

The man I met with the other day, his wife, is going to Moore College.

It gives me so much joy to see that many of them are serving the Lord still.

Friends, I don’t know where you’ll be in ten years’ time, in twenty year’s time.

Some of you might still be at here at this Church.

Some of you will move to a different suburb, others will move to a different city.

No matter where you are, no matter what you become, hold on to the word of life, work out your salvation in Jesus.

When other leaders and I see you again in ten years, we will ask you, “Are you still following Jesus? Are you still serving Jesus?”

Don’t tell us on that day that we’ve wasted our time.

Don’t tell us that we’ve laboured in vain.

Be a sacrificial offering, a living sacrifice for the sake of Jesus.

We long to see you living sacrificially for Jesus.

To see you living sacrificially for Jesus is our joy.

It’s something worth spending our life for.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How is God working in you?

How have you applied continuous, sustained, strenuous effort in obeying God this past week?

How are you, as a member of our Church, shining like a start this week?

Obedience is vital to all Christians.

No one is exempt. God has called all of his children to be obedient Christians.

And so friends, by God’s Spirit, obey by working out your salvation, obey by shining like stars and obey by rejoicing at being spent.

Let’s pray, “Father, we thank you for the joy that comes from knowing you. We thank you that it is you who has saved us in Jesus and it’s you who leads us to have knowledge of Jesus. Father, by your Spirit, work in us to will and to act according to your good purpose and good pleasure. Let us produce fruits of obedience. Let us shine like stars in the light. Let us rejoice at the growth of others, even if it means us being and feeling spent in the process, because it’s your name that we want to glorify. Father, make us humble and obedient like Jesus, who reigns with you and the Spirit in glory and in whose name we pray, Amen.”
