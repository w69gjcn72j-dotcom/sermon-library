The Good Shepherd's Own

John 10:1-21 · 2022

Sermon Title: The Good Shepherd’s own

Passage: John 10.1_21

Big Idea: The Good Shepherd is the Beloved Son who calls his own sheep, lays down his life for his own, that they may have life in abundance.

Jesus the Shepherd calls his own

Jesus the Gate gives abundant life to his own

Jesus the Good Shepherd lays down his life for his own

This is a label maker.

Our family uses it to put label on our things so that we know it’s ours.

Here’s a label I made for my book. This book is mine; it’s my own book!

Later in KOG junior today, we’re going to give the children a label of their own.

They can stick it on their favourite toy or favourite book.

They can tell others that these things are their very own.

As Christians, we often come before Jesus and praise him as our Lord, and that we are his people.

But how do we know?

How do I know that I am Jesus’ very own?

How do I know I belong to Jesus? Why do I want to belong to Jesus?

This is what we’re going to see in today’s passage.

If you have your Bible there please open up to John 10, beginning from verse 1.

We’ll see three things:

Jesus the Shepherd calls his own

Jesus the Gate gives abundant life to his own

Jesus the Good Shepherd lays down his life for his own

Before we look into it, let’s pray,

“Father, as we come before you and your word, please still our hearts and incline them to listen to your word. Keep us away from distractions and let our hearts and our minds hear what you have to say to us, for Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

Jesus the Shepherd calls his own

Jesus’ own know his voice

They follow his lead out

In today’s passage, Jesus uses the imagery of the shepherd and the sheep to describe his relationship with his people.

He starts by describing himself as the shepherd who comes to the gate to call his own (sheep).

Look with me at 10:1,

“Truly I tell you, anyone who doesn’t enter the sheep pen by the gate but climbs in some other way is a thief and a robber. 2 The one who enters by the gate is the shepherd of the sheep.”

Being a shepherd is not an easy profession to be in.

You must look after the sheep as well as keeping an eye out for dangers from wild animals, thieves, and robbers.

And so, what some of the shepherds in Israel did was to gather as a group of shepherds.

They would then put all their sheep in one pen and hire people to look after the sheep.

This would mean that the didn’t have to stretch their resources looking after separate sheep pens.

When the shepherd of a particular flock of sheep come, they call for their own sheep and these sheep would follow their shepherd out of the sheep pen.

That’s the picture that Jesus describes here.

He’s the shepherd, calling them out by name.

It wasn’t a generic whistle or a loud grunt.

The shepherd calls them his own sheep out by name.

It’s very personal.

And after the shepherd calls them, they follow him out, leaving the communal sheep pen behind them.

Now of course, at this point, it can be safely assumed that the sheep already belonged to the Shepherd beforehand.

They hear his voice, and they instinctively follow him.

That’s what happened to the first disciples who followed Jesus.

Jesus called them in the life that they were in, and they followed him.

They left their life, their lifestyle, their sheep pen, as it were, to follow Jesus.

They don’t follow the stranger’s voice; they follow the shepherd’s voice.

When Jesus the shepherd calls them by name, they follow him.

Jesus’ message, the gospel we talk about every Sunday, is for all people.

Anyone who comes to Church on Sunday, who hears and believes the gospel of Jesus, will be saved.

It’s a message for everyone, whether you’re a man or woman, boy or girl.

At the same time, it’s a message that is very specific, personal message.

Jesus says that he calls his own sheep by name.

They aren’t just any sheep that he leads out.

They are his own, his sheep, that he calls by name.

It’s his sheep, those who know Jesus’ voice, that follows Jesus out.

It’s Jesus’ own, those who belong to him, those who are his, who follow him out.

They leave their life behind and they follow Jesus out.

Just as the sheep who recognises and follows the shepherd’s voice show that these sheep belong to the Shepherd,

Those who recognise and follow Jesus’ voice show that they belong to Jesus.

They had belonged to Jesus even before he called them.

The fact that they followed his voice shows that they indeed belong to him.

In Ephesians 1:4 we read, “For he chose us in him, before the foundation of the world, to be holy and blameless in love before him.”

Jesus has chosen those who belong to him before the foundation of the world.

We were his before we answered his call; and so, when he calls, we instinctively follow.

The reverse is also true.

Those who aren’t his, who were not chosen by him, won’t respond to his call.

That’s what we see in verse 6, “Jesus gave them this figure of speech, but they did not understand what he was telling them.”

They hear his voice, they hear his words, but they don’t understand Jesus.

They don’t understand Jesus, they don’t follow Jesus, because they are not his own.

If you do belong to Jesus, if you are Jesus’ own, you will respond to his voice by following him.

Even if he takes you to an unfamiliar place, a place away from home, a place outside of your comfort zone, you know that everything will be OK you don’t need to be afraid, because he’s leading you.

Jesus the Gate gives abundant life to his own

We don’t need to be afraid because Jesus provides safety.

In fact, true and ultimate safety, only comes through Jesus.

For Jesus is the Gate that gives abundant life to his own.

Look with me at verses 7-9, “Jesus said again, “Truly I tell you, I am the gate for the sheep. 8 All who came before me are thieves and robbers, but the sheep didn’t listen to them. 9 I am the gate. If anyone enters by me, he will be saved and will come in and go out and find pasture.”

Jesus says, “I am the Gate”

This is the third “I am” statement that Jesus makes in the gospel of John.

Do you remember the first two?

Here are two drawings to jog your memory:

Jesus say, “I am the Bread of Life”, “I am the Light of the world”.

Now he says, “I am the Gate”

There were those who came before Jesus who were thieves and robbers.

They either took things from that didn’t belong to them by stealth or by violence.

The sheep weren’t safe with them.

But they’re safe with Jesus, who is the Gate that provides safety and security for the sheep.

What does it mean for Jesus to be the Gate?

Some ancient sources tell us that to protect their sheep, shepherds in Jesus’ time would sometimes lay themselves on the ground across the opening of the sheep pen.

That is, they use their body as the gate for the sheep pen.

He puts himself between the sheep and the wild, providing safety for the sheep within.

As the Gate for the sheep, Jesus gives them safety and security.

Look with me at verse 10, “A thief comes only to steal and kill and destroy. I have come so that they may have life and have it in abundance.”

Jesus has come to give life in abundance; he comes to give his own, his sheep, a full and abundant life.

There are others who made similar promises like Jesus throughout history.

There have been individuals who made promises to his people, to his nation, that should they follow him, they would have life in abundance.

If they follow him, they will have the best life.

And throughout history, we’ve seen how this has failed, time and time again, whether it’s dictators in the West or the East.

Rather than giving abundant lives to the people, they’re thieves who take life away.

They steal and they confiscate properties for their own benefits.

They kill and disregard human lives for their ideologies and their economy.

They’ve come to destroy all that is valuable.

They are the thieves who come only to steal and kill and destroy.

They pretend to be a Jesus-like figure.

They promise utopia but delivered dystopia.

The real Jesus, however, has come to offer life to the full, a life in abundance, to those who are his very own.

Again, it’s an expansive offer; it includes a all types of people.

However, it’s also a restricted offer; it only applies to those who come to him.

The offer of an abundant life is only for those who come through the door, it’s only for those who are his.

If you do come to Jesus, if you do come to the door, you will experience life to the full.

Jesus is not here talking about material wealth; he’s not even talking about unfailing health.

The world sees an abundant life as someone who has both wealth and health.

Over the past two years, I’ve heard many people say that in light of the pandemic, the most important thing that you can have is health.

Sadly, even some Christians say that.

But that’s not what Jesus is saying here.

Having good health and being comfortably wealthy are not the most important things that you can have.

For what good is it, if you have health and wealth, and yet forfeit your very soul?

Jesus has come to give life to your soul.

Once you have the life that he gives you in your soul, you will know what’s truly life.

You will enjoy your life even if you don’t have a healthy bank balance.

You will have an abundant life even when you’re in poor health.

You will have life that is truly life; you will have life in abundance.

When Pastor John Piper was 28 years old, his parents were both leading a tour in Israel.

Then John got a phone call, “Johnny, your parents were in a bus accident, and your mother is dead, and we don’t know if your dad is going to make it”.

It was a time of great sorrow for John.

He wept for his mother.

And yet, he was also joyful.

He prayed, ““Thank you, Father, that she was a spectacularly good mother to me.”

“Thank you, Father, that I had her for 28 years.”

“Thank you, Father, that evidently she did not suffer long but died quickly.”

“Thank you, Father, that she is happier right now in the presence of Jesus than I am sad.”

That’s the hope that Pastor John had, even in the depths of his despair.

Even in the death of this mother, he has hope for her.

In fact, it’s only because of John knows that his mother has followed Jesus that he has any hope for her at all.

If you belong to Jesus, if you are Jesus’ own, you will find life as you enter through Jesus the Gate.

You will have an abundant life, a thriving life, a life that’s not diminished because of life’s circumstances.

A life that continues to thrive because you have Jesus.

Are you Jesus’ own?

Jesus the Good Shepherd lays down his life for his own

This abundant life that Jesus gives us is free for us, but it came with a high price for Jesus.

To give us our abundant life, Jesus has laid down his own life for us.

Look with me at verse 11, ““I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep.”

He lays down his life for his own, for his sheep, because he is the Good Shepherd.

This is the Fourth “I am” statement of the Gospel of John.

Jesus is the Good Shepherd, who lays down his life for his sheep.

Jesus died for those who are his own.

This is not a random, pointless death.

It’s not someone who needlessly throw themselves off a building to show someone else that he loves them.

No, Jesus, the Good Shepherd, lays down his life for or on behalf of the sheep.

When Jesus died on the cross, he has his own in mind.

He was dying for those who are his own, those who are his sheep.

If you belong to Jesus, then your name, your face, was on Jesus’ mind when he died on the cross.

If you’re Jesus’ own, then he’s died for you.

The Shepherd cares for his sheep.

Here, Jesus pushes the imagery beyond its typical use.

While a shepherd may love his sheep a lot and take care of them, he probably wouldn’t lose his life for them because that would mean the other sheep would be left in danger after the shepherd dies.

No, here, Jesus applies this case specifically to himself.

As the Good Shepherd of people’s soul, he would lay down his life for them, in order to save them, in order to put them forever out of harm’s way.

Again, he pushes the imagery beyond its normal use to describe his relationship with his people.

Look with me at verses 14-15, ““I am the good shepherd. I know my own, and my own know me, 15 just as the Father knows me, and I know the Father. I lay down my life for the sheep.”

Jesus know the sheep and they know Jesus.

This is not the textbook knowledge they have of one another.

It’s not a matter of the sheep doing an exam to show that he know all the facts about Jesus.

No, Jesus is talking about a deep, relational knowledge.

It’s a knowledge that comes from knowing the person, not just facts about them.

To show what this knowledge between Jesus and his sheep are like, he uses the relationship that he has with his Father.

Jesus has known God the Father forever and God the Father has known Jesus forever.

They know everything there is to know about one another.

Jesus says that that’s the kind of knowledge he has with his own.

Jesus knows everything there is to know about those who are his own.

He knew about us before the foundation of the world.

He committed to knowing and loving us before we did anything bad or good.

He committed to loving us so much that he was willing to lay down his life for us.

It’s a sacrifice that he’s made for those who are his own.

But death is not the end.

Jesus lays his life down so that he may take it back up again.

Death cannot keep him down.

God the Father has given him the authority to lay down his life and the authority to take it back up again.

That’s what God the Father had commanded Jesus to do.

And Jesus has done exactly what God the Father has asked.

That’s why the Father loves the Son; Jesus, God the Son has always obeyed God the Father.

And it’s out of the Father’s knowledge and love of God the Son, as well as the love and knowledge the Son has for the Father, that we are saved.

And so, the order goes like this: The Father knows and loves the Son, the Son knows and loves the Father.

The Father commands the Son to save those who are the Son’s own and the Son obeys.

The Son obeys by laying down his life and taking his life back up.

In the process, those who belong to Jesus hears the call of Jesus, come to know Jesus, find safety and security in Jesus, have an abundant life in Jesus, as they come to know and love both God the Father and God the Son.

The first disciples responded to the call of Jesus by following him.

But it’s not just the first disciples.

Look with me at verse 16, “But I have other sheep that are not from this sheep pen; I must bring them also, and they will listen to my voice. Then there will be one flock, one shepherd.”

There were other sheep in Jesus’ time who were outside of the first group of Jewish disciples.

Jesus must go and tell call them also.

Like the first disciples, they, too, will listen to his voice.

We thank God that after many centuries and crossing many countries, it’s reached us, here at Kogarah.

And it mustn’t stop with us.

There are other sheep of Jesus who are not here with us at Church on Sundays.

We must go to them also.

Did you know that in Kogarah, there are different 89 languages are spoken at home?

We thank God that we are a bilingual congregation, able to gather and worship in at least two different languages.

But there are many more who needs to hear the gospel of Jesus in their own language.

In particular, there are many Nepali speakers who have moved into our area in the last five years.

They need to hear the call of Jesus as well.

As a Church, we need to think about how to bring the gospel to them as well as those who speak other languages at Kogarah.

If we can’t do it ourselves, we’ll need to think about how to support others who can.

As those who belong to Jesus, those who are Jesus’ own, we are thankful, always thankful that Jesus has laid down his life for us.

As Jesus’ own, we also share his longing for the other sheep who have not yet heard the call of Jesus.

When you walk around Kogarah, or Hurstville, or whatever suburb God has placed you, do you share in his longing for his sheep?

If you do belong to Jesus, you will also long for his sheep who are still lost.

You will long for them with the heart of Jesus, you will long for them with the heart of a shepherd.

So, how do you know whether you belong to Jesus? Whether your Jesus’ own?

You will hear his voice and you will follow Him.

He will be your Gate: You will find your safety, your security, and your life in Him.

He will be your Shepherd: you will be thankful that he’s laid down his life for you and you will long for his lost sheep.

You will long for them with a shepherd’s heart.

Jesus said, “I am the Good Shepherd. I know my own, and my own know me.”

If you are his own, you will know him when he calls.

But are we listening?

He’s speaking but have we been listening?

Friends, Jesus tells us that he knows us and that he’s laid down his life for us.

And so be comforted in the fact that if you’re Jesus’ own, you have a shepherd who knows you.

He knows all the bad things about you, he knows all your failures, and yet he still loves you and calls you by name.

But at the same time, don’t remain in your comfort zone.

Long for those who are still outside, long for those who are lost and don’t know Jesus yet.

Share in Jesus’ longing for his sheep; have the shepherd’s heart for his sheep.

And finally, be hungry in your knowledge of Jesus.

Jesus knows you, but do you know Jesus?

Not just the facts about Jesus but Jesus himself.

Do you know him? What is he telling you to do now?

Where is he telling you to go?

Friends, if you are Jesus’ own, then hear his voice, follow him.

For Jesus said, ““I am the Good Shepherd. I know my own, and my own know me.”

Here are the three questions for this week:

Have I responded to Jesus’ call?

Do I have the abundant life that Jesus promises, despite the circumstances I’m in?

How do I cultivate and nurture a shepherd’s heart, like Jesus?

Friends, hear Jesus’ call, for he is the Good Shepherd. He knows his own, and his own know him.

Let’s pray, “Father, we thank you for the love that you have for Jesus. You have known him and loved him in eternity past. We thank you that in Jesus, you have brought us to you. You commanded Jesus to lay down his life for us and to rise again so that we can come to know both him and you. Father, by your Spirit, please give us a heart like Jesus’. Pray that you will fill us with a longing for the lost. By your mercy and grace, please use us to proclaim the gospel to lost sheep around us, that they may hear Jesus’ call, that they may have the abundant life of Jesus, so that together, we may praise and magnify your holy name. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
