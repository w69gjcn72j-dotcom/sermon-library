The Holiness of God: God is incomparably holy

Passage: Isaiah 40:18-31

Additional passages: 1 Peter 1:15-17, Matthew 11:28-30

Big Idea: The incomparably holy God strengthens weary Christian saints who patiently wait for him.

God is incomparably holy

God strengthens saints who are weary

God’s saints soar after they wait

Sam is a Chinese-Australian and he tells a story about him and his brother.

Both he and his brother played in the same rugby team at school.

And people thought they were amazing.

“Who is this Chinese player? He’s everywhere!”

That’s because they didn’t know there were two of them.

They thought they were the same player.

Why? Because … and we’re allowed to say this … “all Chinese look the same!”

But of course in reality this is not the case!

If you get to know a Chinese person, you will quickly realise we are all so very different, not just with looks.

A lot of times, we hear people say that all religions are the same.

But are they, really?

Does it matter what God you believe in?

When we say all religions are the same, it’s like saying all Chinese are the same.

On the surface they look the same, but once we get to know them, we realise they’re so very different.

That’s what we’re going to see in today’s passage.

If you have your Bible there, please open up to Isaiah 40, beginning from verse 18.

We’re going to see three things:

God is incomparably holy

God strengthens saints who are weary

God’s saints soar after they wait

Before we look into it, let’s pray,

“Father, please help us see a glimpse of your greatness today as we hear from your word. Please remove anything that hinders, any distractions, any temptations, that we stop us from hearing your word. For we pray in Jesus’ name, Amen.”

God is incomparably holy

Here’s where the book of Isaiah sits in the overall timeline of the Bible.

You’ll have the timeline in your handout.

God’s people had been living the Promised land of Canaan for over 500 years.

However, even though God has saved them and placed them in the promised land, they continued to sin against God.

God’s sent prophets after prophets to tell them to turn back to him, but they refused.

And so, God punished them by sending them to exiles, by kicking them out of the Promised Land.

This happened to the northern kingdom of Israel in 722BC (Before Christ) and the southern kingdom of Judah in 586BC (Before Christ).

The Prophet Isaiah was one of the prophets in the southern kingdom from 740BD to 681BC who foretold that the Israelites would be exiled if they kept disobeying God.

The first part of Isaiah was the warning; here in the second part of Isaiah, from chapter 40 onwards, Isaiah prophesied to the Israelites as if they were already exiled to Babylon.

Isaiah was very clear on the danger that the Israelites were in because God, the holy God, has appeared to Isaiah in the temple.

In Isaiah chapter 6, Isaiah saw the Lord seated on a high and lofty throne and the angels were singing to one another, “Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of Armies; his glory fills the whole earth.”

In response, Isaiah said, “Woe is me for I am ruined because I am a man of unclean lips and live among a people of unclean lips, and because my eyes have seen the King, the LORD of Armies.”

God is holy, but Isaiah is unholy and unclean; he has no right to be alive in God’s presence.

It’s a little bit like this.

One of the ways modern science deals with cancer is to use radiotherapy.

I’m not an expert on radiotherapy; there are a few experts at Church so you can ask them about it.

But as far as I can understand, radiotherapy uses high doses of radiation to kill cancer cells.

Radiotherapy uses the particle nature of radiation to destroy bad cancer cells.

Friends, we are like the cancer cells.

We are impure, we’re sinful and we’re bad.

Not only do we have no right, we have no power to stand in God’s pure and holy presence.

God is far too good, far too pure for us.

That’s the first thing we learn about God’s holiness.

His holiness is dangerous to unholiness; his holiness is dangerous to sinners.

His pure, beautiful, good and perfect holiness cannot tolerate the presence of sin (Habakkuk 1:13)

That’s we, as impure, ugly sinners dread his holiness.

And so, firstly, God’s holiness is dangerous to unholy sinners.

Second His holiness in incomparable.

There is no one, nothing, like God, not even close.

As humans, we learn by comparing the unknown with the known.

We go from what we know, and learn by comparing the unknown with what we already know.

For example, how would you describe what spaghetti is like to someone who’s never head spaghetti?

You can give them the dictionary meaning, which is “pasta made in solid strings, between macaroni and vermicelli in thickness.”

Of if you’re Chinese, you already know what it is by reading the translation: Italian noodles.

That’s the literal translation of spaghetti in Chinese.

Spaghetti is like noodles, except it’s Italian.

We compare to get from the known to the unknown.

And we tend to do the same thing when we to describe God; we compare the unknown God, with what’s known to us.

In our world we see that it’s a good thing to have power and so God must be powerful.

We project what we know of God to who we think God is; we use our imagination to define God.

When we try to define God on our terms, we are in fact trying to make God in our images.

Any image of God that we make using our imagination is what the Bible called Idols.

Idols are our images of God that we made for ourselves.

The Greeks have Greek gods, the Canaanites in the Promised Land had Canaanite gods, many of our Indian friends have Hindi gods, the Chinese have Chinese gods, and even the first people of Australia have their own indigenous gods.

These are gods of our imagination; these are what the Bible call idols.

But the prophet Isaiah asks in verse 18, “With whom will you compare God? What likeness will you set up for comparison with him?”

How about an idol?

Verse 19, “An idol? (you can almost hear Isaiah say, “An idol, seriously?”)” an idol? —something that a smelter casts and a metalworker plates with gold and makes silver chains for?

Would we really compare God with something that can ask a metalworker to make?

Now, not all of us can afford metal workers.

But even if you are a poor man who can’t afford metalworkers, at the very least, you still need to find some wood to make a stand for the idol, so that the idol won’t fall over.

Isaiah was saying, that, that, you’re going to compare God with that?

That’s a cheap view of God.

The real God doesn’t need help to stand up; he’s the one who’s made the foundations of the earth.

He makes the whole earth stand; He keeps the world from falling.

That’s the true God.

He’s the God who’s enthroned above the circle of the earth.

The first thing that comes to my mind is a sphere, which as we know today, is true - the earth is a sphere! But that was probably not what Isaiah had in mind.

The point that Isaiah wants to make is that God is above the earth; he’s in charge of the earth.

And we’re nothing compared to him; we’re like grasshoppers.

Has anyone seen one? Children, go with your parents this holiday to look at nature.

If you don’t see a grasshopper, look for the ants; take a photo of ants and send it to the Church.

That’s how small we are to God!

God is described to stretch out the heavens like a thin cloth; again, a beautiful imagery.

It’s the language that many modern scientists use to describe the heavens.

They talk about the fabric of space and time and they talk about how reality stretches.

That’s what God’s been does; God makes reality.

God can’t be compared to idols.

How about those in power? How does God compare with those in power?

Verse 23, “He reduces princes to nothing and makes judges of the earth like a wasteland.”

Those who are in power are not really all that powerful in God’s eyes.

They are like plants that are barely sown, plants that had barely grown roots into the ground.

When God blows on them, they are gone.

For example, who’s the current prime minister of Australia? Anthony Albanese.

Who’s PM before him? Scott Morrison

And before him? Malcolm Turnbull.

Let’s go all the way back.

Who’s the first Prime Minister of Australia, Edmund Barton.

All these people were once one of the most powerful people in Australia, but now, many of them are forgotten.

And so, don’t compare God with powerful men and women; God is simply far more powerful.

He’s incomparable.

How about the stars of the universe? They’re pretty powerful.

And yes, they are powerful in their own rights – they release massive amount of energy in the form radiation through space.

When the ancient people saw them at night, they literally worshipped them.

Even today, people look for guidance in the stars in things like horoscopes.

But God owns and names them.

Just like a shepherd who calls his own sheep by name, God names each star by name since they are all his.

And so don’w worship the stars, don’t read the horoscopes.

Reply on the God who made them instead.

God is simply incomparable.

It’s easy to follow the rest of the world and agree with what they say about religions.

They want to say the God we worship is only one of many hundreds, one of many thousands of religions in the world

They want to say, and they want us to say, that our God is neither incomparable nor holy.

That’s nothing new.

The Israelites were unique in that they worshipped one God, even though people around them worshipped many gods.

The first Christians in the first century, also, worshipped one God, even though the Romans and everyone around them worshipped many gods.

In fact, Christians were called atheists for not participating in the worship of other gods.

Many of them suffered and even killed because they stood firm on the belief that there is one holy and incomparable God.

And he’s come to us in the Person of Jesus.

Christians throughout the ages and even today will face the same temptation to be one of the crowd, because we know we’ll be rejected if we say that there is one God and one way to God.

However, we can’t agree with the rest of the world, because there is only one God.

Our God is incomparable and utterly holy.

We can’t put him as only one god among many idols, one powerful being among many powerful figures, even one powerful spiritual being among the heavens.

No, we can’t compare God with anything in all of creation, for he is no creation.

He is the Creator; He is God.

God strengthens saints who are weary

Since God is so holy and we’re so unholy, since he’s incomparable and we’re just like ants, we might be tempted to think that he doesn’t care about us.

Why would he care about us?

Most of us don’t care about ants, why should God care about us?

That’s what Isaiah prophesied the people in exile would be saying, in verse 27, “Jacob, why do you say, and Israel, why do you assert, “My way is hidden from the LORD, and my claim is ignored by my God”?

We’re utterly insignificant.

Does what we do, whether good or bad, really matter?

Does the holy, incomparable God even care about what unholy insignificant sinners say or do?

Over the past two weeks, Peter, Stephen and I have been representing you, the Church, at the Sydney Anglican synod.

During synod one of the ministers who had worked faithfully for 30 years, he felt disappointed because the overall number of Christians going to Churches is decreasing.

Even though he poured his heart into ministry, not a lot seems to be happening.

He looked back over the time he was in ministry and wondered whether he had made any difference at all?

He was clearly disappointed, and many people who went to synod felt the same.

We live in a place where many people don’t care about Christianity or are actively hostile towards Christianity.

Even within Churches, there are many Christians who are less likely to give their resources and time sacrificially.

In light of what’s happening, Christians can feel insignificant and tired, faint and weary.

Even though we are physically insignificant in God’s eyes and don’t deserve any attention from God, our God cares for the faint and the weary.

Listen to the prophet’s answer in verse 28, “Do you not know? Have you not heard? The LORD is the everlasting God, the Creator of the whole earth. He never becomes faint or weary; there is no limit to his understanding.”

The one and only God, the Holy God, is an everlasting God.

He is God of the ages; he’s always there.

He never becomes faint and weary. We might grow faint and weary, but not God.

There is no limit to his understanding; there is nothing is hidden from him, not even what you do in your life.

God knows you.

You might feel insignificant, but he knows you, he knows your weakness, he knows the times when you feel like you’re about to give up.

He knows your disappointments and your heartaches, even when no one else does. He knows you.

This is especially true for Christians.

Just as God knows each of his stars by name, the Lord Jesus knows each of his people, each Christian, by name.

This is how Jesus describes his relationship with Christians, those who belong to him:

10:3 The gatekeeper opens it for him, and the sheep hear his voice. He calls his own sheep by name and leads them out.

Jesus knows each of his own by name.

He knows each of them and loves them.

He loves them so much that he died for them to save them and to make them holy.

That’s what the death of Jesus does.

He died for sinners like you and me to make unholy sinners holy.

For those of us who trust in what Jesus has done, we can now come before God as a holy son, as a holy daughter.

In Christ, we come before God as a holy one, a saint.

For those of us who belong to Christ, for those of us who had been made holy by trusting in Jesus, we can claim this wonderful promise in verse 29, “He gives strength to the faint and strengthens the powerless.”

That’s a good thing for the faint and the powerless saints to hear.

We have an infinitely, incomparable God, who never grows faint or weary, who will give strength to the faint and weary.

This promise is not for every person who’s faint and weary, but those who recognise that they are faint and weary, and has humbly before God, who gives rest to the faint and weary.

Though we are weary and faint, God gives strength to his saints.

Jesus has said something very similar in Matthew 11:28, “Come to me, all of you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest.”

In Isaiah, we read that God the Father gives strength the weary.

In Matthew, we read that God the Son gives rest to the weary.

Well which is it?

Does the weary get rest or strength?

When Jesus God the Son says he gives rest to the weary, he’s not talking about lying down on a lounge and not doing anything.

If we keep reading the passage in Matthew, Jesus gives his yoke to the weary and burdened.

Jesus, the very Son of God, will yoke himself with us and walk with us because he’s lowly and humble in heart.

The path of discipleship will be hard, and the work will be difficult, but he’s with us.

It is Jesus himself who gives us the strength to do his work; it is Jesus himself who gives us strength to walk with him.

It’s Jesus himself who gives us strength to rest with him. To do his will.

Even though we might feel insignificant, even faint and weary, when we come to serve the one and only God, Jesus promises to be with us. He’s yoked himself with us.

He gives strength to the faint and weary, by being there with us, every step of the way.

One of the ministries that we’ve been doing regularly is Tuesday Prayer nights.

Often, many of us who go are tired and weary.

We have so many other things that we could do instead of coming to prayer.

But we made prayer our priority, as we humbly pray to God together.

Though I might feel tired and weary when I go into the prayer meeting, I always feel lifted up afterwards.

And I don’t think I’m the only one.

In prayer meetings, we can express our humble dependence on God.

In prayer meetings, we come knowing God hears us, knowing that God answers us in his wisdom, and we come celebrating his answers to our prayers.

I know we can pray individually at home, but God does something wonderful to us as a Church when we come together in humble prayer before God.

Sometimes, we might think that rather than praying, we just get on with the work.

But to pray is often the heart of the work, for it takes great strength, both mental and spiritual, and often spiritual, to humble ourselves, get on our knees to pray.

God the Father gives strength to the weary. Jesus, God the Son gives rest to the weary.

God in his kindness gives us strength so that we can rest. For it takes great strength to rest and trust in God.

But when we do rest, we will soar.

God’s saints soar after they wait

Look with me at verses 30-31. These are two of the most famous verses in the Bible.

“Youths may become faint and weary, and young men stumble and fall, but those who trust in the LORD will renew their strength; they will soar on wings like eagles; they will run and not become weary, they will walk and not faint.”

There are a lot of young people under 50 here and you’re full of energy.

I look at what you do and I get tired just from looking!

But even you grow faint and weary, even you stumble and fall.

There’s a limit to human strength.

But those who trusts in the Lord will soar like eagles.

The word translated trust can also be translated as ‘wait’.

Those who trust in the Lord will wait for the Lord and they will soar like eagles.

How do eagles soar?

Here’s a clip to show you how an eagle soar: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tM2bC6qNksM

“ I can just feel the wind beginning to build now and the first thing that Sasha does is going from sitting vertically on my fist to her body going horizontal and the wings spreading okay now what we're seeing really is the kind of classic pose of an eagle gliding the wings are almost at full stretch now you can see the flight feathers the primary flight feathers almost like fingers at the end of the wings and these feathers here called a lunar feathers they're the ones making them micro adjustments to keep him stable so he has to use no force no effort at all he hasn't even once had to flap his wings to keep in this position and now even though I've still got a hold of him he's totally weightless right let's see him in action.”

In nature, eagles wait for large gusts of wind called wind thermals.

It might wait for a long time until the right gust of wind comes.

When it comes, the eagle fly straight into it and let the wind carry them.

That’s why eagles are the only birds I know that will deliberately fly into a storm.

Most birds fly away but eagles fly into the storm because that’s when wind thermals are formed.

It would fly into the storm which will help it soar far above the storm.

That’s what God’s people are called to do;

Like the eagle waiting for the wind thermal to come, we’re to wait on the Lord, we’re wait for his command.

If you know who really God is, it makes perfect sense to wait on him.

After all, He is the Creator of all, He knows how everything work and he arranges how everything to happen, it makes perfect sense to trust him and his commands.

The God whom we serve is the Triune God - God the Father, God the Son, God the Holy Spirit.

Throughout the Bible, and as we’ve read last week, the Holy Spirit is like the wind.

The Holy Spirit leads and guides us to follow Jesus as we read the Bible.

Like the eagle, Christians wait for the Holy Spirit’s timing.

It might be a few seconds, it might be a few years, but we need to wait for his call to move.

When the Spirit does call us to move, we obey by moving and we soar.

We soar as we do incredible things that he’s asked us to do and as we celebrate what He’s done through us.

Like the eagles, when we soar, we don’t need to flap our wing, we don’t try to add to what the Holy Spirit has asked us to do.

We let him guide us and lead us to where he wants us to be.

He’s more than capable of leading us to exactly where he wants us to go.

And so, study the Holy Spirit, study the Breath of God, study the Wind of God, and wait on him.

When he calls, when the moment is right by him, soar!

But until then, we must ask God for the strength to do this often overlooked but incredibly hard task.

We must wait on his command.

It often takes a lot of hard work to wait.

Are we willing to persevere and stay we are until he tells us to go?

Are we willing to remain strong, to remain faithful, to stand our ground, even when we’re tempted to shift without the Spirit’s lead?

And when the Holy Spirit calls us, are we ready to go?

Waiting is hard work, but leaving what we have behind can also be hard work.

Are we willing to leave behind what we have to go where the Spirit wants us to soar?

Do you feel weary? Do you grow faint?

If you do, then join me as we humble ourselves before the one and holy God.

Join me as we ask him to renew our strength in the Lord Jesus so that we can do his will.

Let’s ask God as a Church, as households, as individuals to us wait for him and to soar when he calls us.

So, does it really matter what God you believe in?

Yes! There’s only one true God. He’s incomparable.

He is all powerful and never become faint and weary, and he wants to strengthen the faint and weary who have come before him in Jesus.

God wants to strengthen the weary and faint Christian saints.

So wait for the Lord’s leading, study the Wind of God by reading the Word of God, and wait for the Holy Spirit’s lead and he will renew your strength and help you soar like an eagle.

Friends, the incomparably holy God strengthens weary Christian saints who patiently wait for him.

For those of us who have been coming to the prayer meetings, you might remember that I’ve asked you to pray for an old Anglican minister by the name of Rev Wilfred Chee (朱樹慶牧師).

Jenny and I visited Wilfred a few weeks ago.

Sadly Wilfred died last week.

He was the Senior minister of Chinese Christian Church and Western Sydney Chinese Christian Church, two of the largest Chinese Churches in Sydney, and served at churches in Malaysia, Taiwan and Sydney Anglican churches as well.

His daughter Denise shared that ministry was hard for her Dad, and also hard for the family.

However, she also said that Wilfred kept pointing people to Jesus, to keep trusting him.

She also mentioned that he has a big smile.

Wilfred always has a big smile.

Even the last memory that Jenny and I have of him was a big smile.

He was obviously very sick and very weak, but boy, he still had a big smile.

It takes great strength to smile when things are hard.

It takes great strength to smile when you’re weak and when you have hardly any strength left.

God gave him the strength to smile.

And he’s now smiling with the biggest smile in God’s presence.

What’s wearing you down?

Humbly turn to the everlasting God.

He has more than enough strength to give you.

Ask him for the strength to keep trusting in him and to wait on him. Study the Wind of God as you study the word of God.

At his timing, when he calls you, soar like an eagle, soar with renewed strength.

Soar with a smile.

Friends, the God we serve is the incomparably holy God who strengthens weary Christian saints who patiently wait for him.

When you do call us to go, let us step out in faith, that we might soar to places where you want to take us, that your name will be glorified, for we pray in the name of Jesus, Amen.”
