Preparing to meet a dead Jesus

Please open up your Bible with me to Mark 16, beginning from verse 1, “When the Sabbath was over, Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James, and Salome bought spices, so that they could go and anoint him.”

These three women witnessed the death of Jesus from a distance.

And now, they’re going to the burial place of Jesus to anoint him.

What’s fascinating is that while the women prepare to anoint Jesus, the men, the disciples are nowhere to be seen.

The last we’ve heard of them in the Gospel of Mark was back in chapter 14, when Jesus was betrayed and captured.

The last time we heard of Peter was at the end of Mark chapter 14, when he denied Jesus not once, but three times.

Here, at the beginning of Mark 16, it’s as if the whole landscape of Jesus’ followers has literally become a no man’s land.

The women prepare to meet a dead Jesus while the men are too afraid to come out for fear of getting captured.

The women have come to show their last respects to Jesus.

And so, they got up very early in the morning and got to the tomb at sunrise.

Already here, there’s a change in the tone of the Gospel.

For the past few chapters, it’s been heavy and dark.

Jesus was talking about the signs of the end, Jesus was captured at night, he was put on trial at night, darkness came over all the land in the middle of the day.

But now, the women comes to the tomb at the start of a new day, on the first day of the week.

After Jesus died, they placed his body in a tomb.

It would’ve looked a little like this.

This picture was taken outside of Old Jerusalem in a place called the Garden Tomb.

It’s not actually the place where Jesus was buried but it would’ve looked similar.

In Jesus’ case, there would’ve been a large stone over the entrance to keep thieves and wild animals from entering and to prevent the bad smell from escaping.

As they come, they ask one another, “Who will roll the stone from the entrance to the tomb for us?”

They aren’t strong enough.

All the disciples are in hiding; there’s no man around to give them a hand!

They’re feel powerless.

But at least they’re not dead, like Jesus!

Jesus is dead, powerless to do anything, or so they thought.

And so, they’ve come to do something for the dead Jesus.

They’ve come to anoint Jesus with spices so that he won’t smell as bad as his body decays.

But to do something for the dead Jesus, they need someone to move the stone away and there’s no one to help them.

They need someone else’s help, so that they can help the now dead Jesus.

That’s what some people think when they come to Church.

When they come to Church, they think that they’re here to honour a dead Christ.

If not dead, then someone weak, someone who needs our help.

Like the women, they’ve come to attend to a dead, powerless Christ.

In their minds, Jesus Christ is as powerless as he was when he was nailed to the cross.

In popular culture, there are many fictional movies and comic books that describe gods as beings who need people’s attention to survive.

One such movie is the Clash of the Titans.

The movie describes the relationship between people and gods.

If people pray to them, they’ll grow in power but if they stop paying attention to them, they lose their power.

These are weak gods, pitiful gods, that depend on people to survive.

Without people, these gods wither and die.

What are your expectations when you come to Church?

Have you come to worship a weak God? A dead God?

Are you here to give God to help God, because he needs it?

That’s what the women at the tomb has come to do.

They came to the right place, they’re looking for the right person.

And to an extent it’s good that they’ve come to show their respects to Jesus.

But when it comes to their understanding of Jesus, they got is so wrong!

Theirs was a simple mission; their mission was to anoint a dead man.

But their mission was doomed from the start.

Because Jesus is not the pitiful God that they were looking for.

He’s not here in the tomb; he’s risen!

Shocked to hear about a risen Jesus

As they come to the tomb, they’re shocked to hear about a risen Jesus.

Look with me at verse 4-5, “Looking up, they noticed that the stone—which was very large—had been rolled away.

5 When they entered the tomb, they saw a young man dressed in a white robe sitting on the right side; they were alarmed.”

As they come to the empty tomb, they’re met with a messenger who gave them a fright!

I still remember that a few years ago, when we visited my sister’s in San Jose in America,

We wanted to give our nieces a surprise at their school.

And so, my family and I went with my sister to visit them after school.

When they saw us, they gave a loud scream and went to hug us as hard as they could.

That was one of the best surprises we’ve ever pulled off.

The young man dressed in white robe was an angel.

It should’ve been a delightful surprise for the women, except they weren’t happily surprise.

They were frightened.

They were expecting to see a dead body but they a living, speaking angel instead!

The word angel literally means a messenger.

The angel in the passage is God’s messenger, with a message from God for the women: Don’t be alarmed.

He then tells them a summary of what happened to Jesus is a short, precise sentence.

Jesus was crucified and buried but he’s not here. He’s risen!

That sums up the incredible events that’s happened in the past 40 hours or so.

They’re here to look for a dead man but he is risen!

They’re preoccupied with death; they’re here to see Jesus one last time.

But Jesus is not here, he’s risen!

James Edwards in his commentary sums it up well, “the living are consumed with death, but the Crucified One is consumed with life.”

That’s so true for us, is it not?

We work hard to survive because if we don’t, we will die.

Our default is death.

If we don’t do anything, we will die.

But not so the Crucified but Risen Christ.

His default is life.

He doesn’t have to do anything to stay alive.

Jesus just keeps on living.

That’s the risen life!

And it’s here, in the empty tomb where we see the dawn of this risen life.

A dead man enters this tomb but he comes out a risen man, a man who will never die again.

It was dark when Jesus’ body entered the tomb but when Jesus comes out in his risen body, it’s the beginning of a new day.

A new dawn has just begun.

It’s not just the dawn of that particular day but the dawn of a new era.

And the women are now given a new mission: Go tell those men missing in action, those disciples.

Tell the disciples and Peter, “He is going ahead of you to Galilee; you will see him there just as he told you”.

He’s already gone ahead of them; now go tell them!

That’s the new mission! Tell the disciples that Jesus’ risen.

Tell them that the new dawn has come!

In the arctic circle and the Antarctic circles of our world, there are often long periods of time when there’s no daylight for long periods of time.

When a place goes through long periods without sunlight, it’s known as the polar night.

In some places, a polar night can last for 30 days.

If you’re at the North or South Poles, the polar night can last for around 11 weeks.

Our world has been in a spiritual polar night for many years.

We’ve been in living in spiritual darkness, we’ve been living in the shadow of death for thousands of years.

But when Jesus rose and walked from the grave, we see the beginning of the end of the long night.

In Jesus, we see what life beyond death can look like.

In Jesus, we see the dawn of God’s kingdom.

The dawn of the Kingdom of heaven

Almost as soon as Jesus walks out of the tomb, as soon as dawn breaks over the kingdom of heaven,

Jesus regathers the lost disciples.

The disciples have failed him, the fellowship between him and the disciples have been broken.

But with the dawn of the Kingdom of heaven, on the top of Jesus’ to-do list, is the regathering of these weak, powerless, broken disciples.

A few weeks ago, I was watching a Youtube video by two guys called the Two-sets.

These two guys make funny videos about being classical violinists.

They joke about the difficulties of songs,

the ridiculous skill sets of prodigies and virtuosos

as well as well as making fun of what musicians go through,

often poking fun at themselves while doing it.

But in this particular video, they were drawing attention to the stress and distress that many musicians, particularly classically trained musicians go through.

For many classical musicians, they practice for perfection.

They would spend hours to play pieces perfectly.

And sometimes the pursuit for perfection would take a heavy toll.

It’s been particularly hard for many musicians around the world because many, many tours were cancelled.

All the hours of training seem to have gone to waste.

Some have suffered such trauma that they couldn’t touch, let alone play their beloved instruments once again.

That’s a little like what’s happened to the disciples.

They’ve followed Jesus for a long time.

They’ve heard him preached and they themselves would’ve share the gospel on number of occasions.

Like Jesus, they’ve cast out demons, they’ve healed the sick though to a smaller extent.

They were disciples of Jesus and they were following what Jesus did.

But then they’ve failed spectacularly.

They all left him when he needed them the most.

And now, they’re afraid to even come out in public, let alone do what they did when Jesus was around.

But like a conductor who gathers and leads the musicians to play music again, Jesus gathers the disciples to tell them to follow him again.

Though they’ve failed Jesus, Jesus is gracious to them and wants to restore them.

That’s the command that the angel gives the women.

Tell these weak, broken men to follow Jesus.

Jesus has risen from the grave and has ushered in the dawn of the Kingdom of Heaven.

They’re to follow the risen Saviour.

They’re to follow the Risen One who’s consumed with life.

They’re to look forward to a life consumed with life.

That the new mission for these women at the tomb.

The old mission was to pay respects to a dead man.

They failed that mission because the man was no longer dead.

Their new mission now is to tell the disciples to follow the risen man, Jesus of Nazareth, the Son of God!

What a great mission to receive!

What an honour it is to receive this mission at the dawn of the kingdom of heaven!

And so the women goes and the rest is history…right?

No. That’s not what the Bible says.

Look with me at verse 8, “They went out and ran from the tomb, because trembling and astonishment overwhelmed them. And they said nothing to anyone, since they were afraid.”

They were given two commands: Don’t be alarmed. Tell the disciples.

But they failed on both counts; they were afraid, they stayed afraid and they said nothing to anyone, including the disciples.

That’s how the Gospel of Mark finishes.

There are more verses beyond verse 8 but as you’ll see from your Bible, “some of the earlier manuscripts (mss) conclude with 16:8”

Most scholars around the world rightly believe that the Gospel of Mark finishes at 16:8.

And it’s a surprising ending.

It’s not the victorious, happy ending that we are used to.

The mission was given to women who seemed to have already failed their mission before it began.

Why does the Gospel of Mark finishes like this?

It’s because it wants to remind us that Jesus’ mission, Jesus’ Gospel, the Good News about the Kingdom of Heaven,

Is given to ordinary, fallible men and women, like you and me.

The mission wasn’t given to these men and women because they were super spiritual or super capable.

By themselves, it would fail the mission; they would not be able to spread the Good news of Jesus.

But Jesus has promised that he would go on this mission alone.

Jesus would give them the Holy Spirit.

The Holy Spirit would help them with the mission.

And the Holy Spirit will help us to, as we continue with the mission of telling others that the dawn of the Kingdom of Heaven has come.

And so at the end of the Gospel of Mark, we’re given a chose.

Lile the women, we’ve been given a great mission, an important mission.

What would we do with the mission?

Will we tremble and not tell others about Jesus?

Or will we obey our mission and tell others about Jesus, despite our weaknesses?

A few days ago, I read a short tribute given to a Christian woman called Glenda Weldon.

Glenda used to live in Rockdale.

While she was there, she met a group of young teenagers.

For many afternoons, she would prepare hamburgers for them.

While they ate, she would patiently explain the gospel to them.

Now, being teenagers, they didn’t care much about the stories; they only wanted to eat the hamburgers.

They were there to obviously take advantage of her kindness and generosity!

But she persisted because she loves Jesus and she loved them and wanted them to know Jesus too.

Eventually, they became Christians and loved Jesus too.

One of them became a pastor and got a PhD and had become an internationally known writer and speaker.

His name is John Dickson and he’s share the gospel with thousands of people.

And it all began with Glenda sharing the gospel with teenager boys over hamburgers.

Though we might be weak, the Lord has given us a wonderful message to proclaim.

Jesus’ died, buried and risen.

He’s regathering a people; a weak people and he’s tasked us to tell others about him.

He’s asked us to be his witnesses; witnesses of the dawn of the kingdom of heaven.

And so, how do we respond?

Jesus has risen, so rise up!

What does life after death look like?

Life, true, everlasting, eternal life, looks like Jesus.

Jesus is the Resurrection and the life; Life after death looks like the Risen Christ.

However, this life is only for those who are in Christ, who have put their trust in him.

For those who don’t have Jesus, life after death is death in the worst possible sense.

It’s being abandoned by God, forever under the judgement of God.

That’s why Jesus came and died.
