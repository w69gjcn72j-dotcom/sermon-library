Intro:

A few years ago, one of my cousins told me about a new sickness.

It’s called Princess Sickness or Gun Jue Beng (公主病)

Have you heard of it?

It describes girls who think that they are princesses and can make really crazy demands of their boyfriends.

The famous singer Jay Chou even wrote a song called Gun Jue Beng公主病.

As well as Gun Jue Beng公主病, I found out recently that there’s now an equivalent for men.

It’s called Wong Ji Beng王子病, Prince sickness.

It’s similar to Gun Jue Beng公主病, except the men with Prince Sickness王子病’s focus is less on his girlfriend and more on himself.

Here are some symptoms of someone who has Prince Sickness:

They think that they are like a prince and look down on everyone else 以為自己是貴族少爺

They don’t help and serve other people because they are too good for that.

They think that they are geniuses 覺得自己是被埋沒了的天才

Sure, their parents might have said that they were smart when they were little boys, but they haven’t really grew smarter since then.

They’re always right. 自己永遠是對的

They don’t mistakes, but when they do, it’s always somebody else’s fault.

That is, they don’t take responsibility for their actions.

Like their princess equivalents, the men with Prince sickness only cared about themselves and how others can make fit in.

They only see the faults in others and not the faults in themselves.

That’s why you often hear them criticise about other people, as if they are somehow superior to everybody else.

They would be very difficult people to live with.

But before we point our fingers at the princes and princesses around us, we need to look at ourselves first.

When things don’t go our way, when life doesn’t turn out the way we want to, when we don’t get what we want, don’t we complain?

That’s what we mean by discontent; it’s being unhappy with our present situation.

How should Christians face discontent?

Should Christians feel discontent?

How do we avoid become someone infected with Prince sickness Huang Ji Beng and Princess Sickness Gun Jue Beng?

Before we look into the passage, let’s pray, “Father, please open our minds by your Spirit as we look into your words today. And by your Spirit, enable our lives to be shaped into the likeness of Your Son. In his name we pray, Amen.”

1. Christian contentment depends on Christ

Turn with me to Philippians 4:10, ‘I rejoiced in the Lord greatly because once again you renewed your care for me. You were, in fact, concerned about me but lacked the opportunity to show it. ’

Paul’s really happy that the Philippians have renewed their concern for Paul.

The Philippians have been concerned for Paul all along but they just haven’t had the opportunity to show it.

Now that the opportunity has finally come up, they made the most of it by sending their friend Epaphroditus to Paul along with some gifts that they’ve prepared.

Over the past few months, because of COVID restrictions, we haven’t had a lot of opportunities to show our concern for one another.

But gradually, over the past few weeks, many of our members took the opportunity to show genuine concern for one another.

My family and I are thankful to the Lord for the concerns that many of you have shown us over the past few weeks.

A number of sisters have brought us food, like eggs, and fish, and their cooking.

A number of brothers have helped us with maintaining the Church property.

We thank God for the concern you’ve shown us!

And more than that, I’ve seen how you’ve taken the opportunity to show your concern for one another as well, as you start to invite each other to dinners, when another family couldn’t cook for the night.

It’s a joy to see you showing concern for one another.

Praise God! Keep showing concern for one another whenever you get the opportunity!

Paul rejoices in the Lord that the Philippians have once again renewed their concern for Paul.

However, Paul makes it that he’s not doing this because he’s looking for another gift from the Philippians,

He’s not saying ‘now that you’ve finally renewed your concern for me, don’t make it too long before the next one’

No, Paul will do just fine even if they don’t send another gift ever again.

Why?

Look with me at verse 11, ‘I don’t say this out of need, for I have learned to be content in whatever circumstances I find myself.’

To be content means to be in a state of peaceful happiness or satisfaction.

It’s being happy with who you are and where you’re at.

The opposite of contentment is discontent, that is, getting agitated for not getting what you want.

I think for most of us, there will be times when we feel contented and there will be times when we feel discontent.

And generally speaking, we don’t like being discontent.

When we are discontent, our tendency is to change our situation.

I remember when I was a young student, I saw a picture of a young boy looks to a young teenager and says, “I want to be like him”

The young teenager looks to a young worker and says, “I want to be like him.”

The young worker looks to a family man and says, “I want to be like him.”

The family man looks to an old retired man and says, “I want to be like him.”

And the old retired man? He looks to the young boy and says, “I want to be like him, again.”

It’s a cycle of discontent that doesn’t lead to satisfaction.

How about Paul?

How does he feel with discontent in his life?

Look with me at verse 12, ‘I know how to make do with little, and I know how to make do with a lot. In any and all circumstances I have learned the secret of being content—whether well fed or hungry, whether in abundance or in need. ’

Paul says that he has learnt to be content whatever the circumstances.

It didn’t matter whether Paul had money to pay the rent, or whether he had enough money to buy several houses,

It didn’t matter if he didn’t have a decent meal for days or whether he had a full meal five times a day,

In every situation that he’s faced, Paul was content, he was happy, he was satisfied.

Paul’s learnt the secret of being content in any and every situation.

Paul’s contentment doesn’t depend on the situations that he’s in.

We often think that if we change our situation, if we change the way we live, we’ll be content.

When we don’t feel content, when we don’t feel happy or satisfied with our lives, we often look for a reason or a cause in the circumstances that we’re in.

We might say that we’re unhappy because we’re poor, or that we’re unhappy because we don’t have a stable job or that we’re unhappy because we don’t have many friends.

If we can fix the state we’re in, if we can change our circumstances, then we’ll be content, then we’ll be satisfied.

Until then, we remain discontent; we remain unsatisfied.

And so, our contentment, our happiness is often very fickle.

For many of us, our contentment depends heavily on our circumstances.

We’re content when things go our way; we’re discontent when things don’t go our way.

But the contentment that Paul describes is a constant contentment.

In any and every situation, whether well fed or hungry, whether rich or poor, Paul is content.

His contentment does not depend on his circumstances.

He is always content.

How does he do it?

The secret to Paul’s contentment is found in verse 13:

‘I am able to do all things through him who strengthens me.’

Paul’s secret to his contentment is that he has someone who strengthens him to face every situation in life, whether he’s rich or poor, whether he’s hungry or well fed.

This person is none other than Jesus Christ.

Paul is not the strong one; he’s not the strong willed man who can face all of life’s problems.

It is Jesus Christ who strengthens Paul.

It is Jesus Christ who gives Paul the strength to face the challenges of each new day.

For Paul, his contentment is no longer dependent on what circumstance he is in.

Rather, his contentment is that in whatever circumstance he is in, he will face it with Christ.

He knows that Christ will give him the strength to face it.

He’s dependent on Christ to give him the strength to face his circumstances, whatever they might be.

As the Bible Commentator puts it, “The secret of Paul’s independence was his dependence upon Christ.”

Paul’s contentment is independent of his circumstances because he is dependent on Christ.

Paul’s contentment is not based on what circumstances he’s facing but who is facing it with.

It’s a contentment that says ‘All I have Christ and that is enough”.

We see this kind of contentment illustrated in the life of a Christian man by the name of George Matheson.

George was once engaged to a young lady but during the engagement, he found out that he was going blind.

When his fiancé found out she told him that she could not go through life with a blind man and so she left him.

George has a sister.

When George became blind, she went to take care of him.

He went on to study in a bible college and regularly preached to over to over 1500 people each week.

But he was only able to do this because his sister took care of him.

After a while, his sister became engaged and was about to marry.

Who will care for him now?

On his sister’s wedding day, his whole family went to the wedding and had left him alone.

How terrible would he have felt?

Pretty bad, pretty sad.

But it was during this time that he wrote this song.

“O Love that will not let me go, I rest my weary soul in thee; I give thee back the life I owe, That in thine ocean depths its flow May richer, fuller be.”

George knew that even though his fiancé might have left him, even if his family has left him, there is still one who will not let him go.

He knew that God will not let him go.

He knew that God loved him so much that sin, suffering and death cannot stop God from loving him.

He knew that nothing can stop God from saving him.

This is God’s love, a love that will not let me go.

All George had was Christ, and it was enough.

How about us?

Do we have the kind of contentment that the apostle Paul and George Matheson have in Jesus?

Or are we still discontent with our lives, hoping that our lives will be better by being richer, being smarter, or by having a girlfriend or boyfriend in our lives?

Don’t look for contentment by changing the circumstances in your life.

Look for contentment in Jesus who will give you strength to face every circumstance in your life.

Jesus is the secret of contentment.

Now, if you’re one of those in the Philippian church listening to this letter being read, you might be a little confused at what Paul’s been saying.

Paul just said that he rejoiced at the fact that the Philippians remembered him and gave him a gift.

But now, in his response, he is saying that he is content in whatever circumstance that he was facing.

Does this mean that Paul didn’t really need their gift in the first place?

Is this Paul’s way of telling them that they’ve wasted their time and effort, since he’s content in whatever circumstances he’s in?

While it is true that Paul has learned the secret of being content in whatever circumstance that he faces, he also wants the Philippians to know that he’s grateful for what they’ve done.

What he shows in the last part of the letter is God-Centred courtesy.

2. Christian courtesy is God-centred (vv14-20)

Look with me at verse 14-16, ‘Still, you did well by partnering with me in my hardship.15 And you Philippians know that in the early days of the gospel, when I left Macedonia, no church shared with me in the matter of giving and receiving except you alone. 16 For even in Thessalonica you sent gifts for my need several times. 17Not that I seek the gift, but I seek the profit that is increasing to your account. ’

It was good for the Philippians to share in Paul’s troubles.

But Paul is saying here again that he’s not saying this to get another gift.

Instead of focussing on himself as the recipient of the gifts, Paul turns our gaze heavenward.

Look with me at verse 18, ‘But I have received everything in full, and I have an abundance. I am fully supplied, having received from Epaphroditus what you provided—a fragrant offering, an acceptable sacrifice, pleasing to God.”

The gifts that the Philippians gave were not simply gifts given to Paul.

They are a fragrant offering, an acceptable sacrifice, pleasing to God.

God is the Ultimate Recipient of the Philippians’ gifts.

And so, while Paul is grateful for what the Philippians have done, he wants them to know that these gifts aren’t just gifts between friends;

These gifts are sacrifices, acceptable to God.

It’s a beautiful way to express God-centred courtesy.

Showing God-centred courtesy is not always an easy thing for Christians to do.

Some will go really overboard in thanking somebody.

“Oh, sister, you are the most amazing, friendliest, kindest, wisest, loveliest and most loving Christian woman the world has ever seen!”

Maybe this Christian woman is amazing, but the most amazing, ever?

Maybe a little overboard.

On the other end, some people don’t thank other Christians at all.

They say that our focus should be God and not people.

And so, these people avoid thanking anybody.

They don’t thank the preacher, the musicians, the Bible reader, the Prayer-leader, ever.

Now you think that might sound a little extreme, but I’ve met some Christians who was almost horrified when someone in the church thanked another Christian.

It was almost as if it was a sin to thank a Christian in public.

And so, should we thank Christians or not?

Yes, we should show courtesy and thank our Christian brothers and sisters for what they’ve done.

But more importantly, when we show our gratitude, we need shift both their gaze and ours heavenward.

We need to show that it’s God who is the Ultimate recipient.

We need to let them know that what they did for us is precious, because their gifts are fragrant offerings, acceptable sacrifices, pleasing to God.

It is beautiful when Christians show God-centred courtesy to each other, recognising that what they’ve done to each other is precious while at the same time giving the glory to God.

What does this mean for us?

How can we be courteous Christians who are content?

Firstly, be content with what you have.

Don’t envy what other people have, whether it’s a bigger house, a nicer car, or a better job.

Don’t envy other people’s achievements or other people’s approvals.

Don’t be discontent because of what you don’t have.

Rather, be content because of who you have.

Be content that you have Jesus Christ.

Is Christ enough for you?

Second, be generous with what you have.

Don’t hoard what God’s given you; give them away to those in need.

Show genuine concern for one another and give to one another.

You might share some of your possessions with another brother or sister.

You might share your precious time with another brother or sister.

Being gracious and generous with what’s God given us shows that we’re content in Christ.

It shows that we have understood the secret of contentment.

Finally, have God-centred courtesy.

Instead of criticising other Christians, be thankful to God for them.

Pray for them and let them know that you’re praying for them.

Let them know that you are thankful to God for what them.

I’m thankful to God for our Bible study leaders, our youth leaders and our SRE teachers.

Over the past few months, I’m especially thankful to God for those who help out at the Mobile Community Pantry.

And I’m thankful for many more who have been serving God among us.

Who are you thankful to God for?

How have you shown them God-centred courtesy for what God’s done through them?

Can I give you a challenge this week?

Make it your goal this week to show God-centred courtesy to someone who’s made an impact in your life.

Tell them that you give God thanks for them, that what they’ve done for you is something precious to God.

Tell them that you praise God for what they’ve done in your life.

3 questions

Are you content?

Are you generous?

Are you grateful?

And so, brothers and sisters, remember that you are children of God the King.

You are precious in God’s eyes.

But that doesn’t mean that we have Prince Sickness and Princess Sickness.

Instead, be perfect as your Father is perfect.

Be contented Christians.

Find your contentment in him who gives you the strength to face whatever circumstances you are currently facing in your life.

Also, be Christians who show Christian courtesy

Show someone who’s supported you that what they’ve done is a beautiful and precious thing in the sight of God.

And we’ll finish with the last two verses of today’s passage.

“And my God will meet all your needs (i.e. he will give you everything you need to face whatever situation you’re facing) according to his glorious riches in Christ Jesus. To our God and Father be glory for ever and ever. Amen.”
