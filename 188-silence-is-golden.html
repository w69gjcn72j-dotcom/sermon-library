Silence is golden

Passage: 1 Corinthians 14:26-40

Big Idea: Silence is essential when gathering before the God of peace

Silence is essential for building up

Silence is necessary to prevent disgrace

It’s so good to start to see people at Church again!

I missed seeing everyone at Church and I can’t wait to see more people coming back over the coming weeks!

When lockdown began last year, we scrambled to get our meetings happening online.

Now, I’m not an engineer nor the son of an engineer and so we just took whatever we have and put it together.

Thank you for all those who have helped!

As we were researching on how to get livestream right, we’ve learnt that generally speaking, getting the sound right is more important than getting the video right.

People won’t mind too much if the video is a little fuzzy or not in high resolution.

But if the sound is wrong, people will turn off.

And looking at our experience, it’s true!

When people give feedback, they often say things like, it’s too soft, there’s a humming sound, there’s feedback and so on.

I remember hearing a humming sound in the video and I couldn’t work out what it was.

As a result, I took out all the cables to isolate the sound but it was still there.

And so, I’ve set up a second mixer, to get rid of the humming.

The result? Silence.

That silence was beautiful!

The silence was golden!

There are times when silence is both important and appropriate.

In today’s passage, we’ll see that there are times when it’s important and appropriate to be silent at Church.

In an always switch-on society like ours, where we hear many voices, often very loud noises, all at once,

Being silent seems out of place.

Being silent seems like we’re missing out.

What if we’re not heard?

But there are times when silence is golden and being at Church is one of those times.

Today, we’re going to read from 1 Corinthians 14, beginning from verse 26.

We’re going to see:

Silence is essential for building up

Silence is necessary to prevent disgrace

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Father, just as Jesus has calmed the storm with his still, small voice, calm our hearts now as we come before you, that we may hear and follow your still small voice. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

Silence is essential for building up

As you know, our vision statement is we gather to build and to nurture a nation which lives and breathes the gospel of Jesus Christ.

That’s basically what the Apostle Paul is telling us to do.

Look with me at 1 Corinthians 14:26,

“What then, brothers and sisters? Whenever you come together, each one has a hymn, a teaching, a revelation, a tongue, or an interpretation. Everything is to be done for building up.”

We come together, we sing, we speak, for the building up of the Church.

We do all things so that we’ll build one another up in Christ.

And we need to do it in an orderly fashion.

When new teachers begin teaching, one of the things they need to learn is classroom management.

Things might start out quiet at the beginning of class.

But after a few minutes, one student might begin to speak with someone.

Then another starts.

Then someone will ask you a question and two more conversations start behind your back.

Before you know it, the whole class is talking and not doing their work.

And no one learns anything!

Has that ever happened to you?

This doesn’t just happen in classrooms.

It can happen in Churches as well.

Look with me at verses 27-28, “If anyone speaks in a tongue, there are to be only two, or at the most three, each in turn, and let someone interpret. 28 But if there is no interpreter, that person is to keep silent in the church and speak to himself and God.”

Paul reminds the Corinthian Christians that if someone at Church is to speak in a tongue, very likely a language that only God understands,

They will need to organise an interpreter first, someone who’s been gifted with the gift of interpretation to understand the tongue.

How can the Church be built up if they have someone who speaks in tongues but no interpretation?

Also, if they are going to have tongue and interpretation at Church, they shouldn’t have more than three people do it.

As helpful it might be for the person speaking in tongues, if the Church doesn’t understand the person, then it’s not helpful for the Church.

It would have been better for the person to stay silent and speak to himself and God, presumably at home rather than at Church.

Now notice here that Paul’s telling person speaking in tongues to remain silent not because he doesn’t have anything good to say,

Or that he’s in any sense bad or inferior.

Paul’s telling this person to remain silent because speaking in tongues without interpretation will cause disorder in the Church,

Much like the children who speak out of turn in class.

It’s not going to build up the Church.

Paul then uses a similar reasoning for those who prophesy at church.

Those who prophesy at Church are those who speak a timely message from God’s word for God’s people.

Look with me at verses 29-30, “Two or three prophets should speak, and the others should evaluate. 30 But if something has been revealed to another person sitting there, the first prophet should be silent.”

Here, unlike what he’s said about tongues, Paul’s not saying if there is anyone who wants to prophesy.

He’s saying that on any given Sunday, let two or three prophets speak.

He’s expecting that the prophets will minister at Church more regularly than those who speak in tongues.

When they do speak, others, presumably other prophets or teachers, will evaluate what they say.

When another prophet rises to speak, the first prophet should be silent.

Speaking God’s word to God’s people is important, but even the prophet speaking God’s word need to recognise that there are other prophets in the Church as well.

They need to recognise that they are not the only ones speaking God’s word in God’s Church.

God gives multiple prophets to God’s Church so that everyone may learn, and everyone may be encouraged.

As we’ve seen in 1 Corinthians 11, both men and women prophesy.

Both men and women speak God’s word to God’s people.

1 Corinthians 11:5 tells us that both male and female prophets need to present themselves in an appropriate way, lest they dishonour their heads, namely Christ and man, respectively.

Here in 1 Corinthians, both male and female prophets will need to be respectful of other prophets,

Ready to step down and be silent so that other prophets can speak.

Both those who speak in tongues and those who prophesy will be silent when it’s appropriate for them to do so “since God is not a God of disorder but of peace”, verse 33.

Over the past few years, I’ve been asked a number of times about the order of our Church service.

Why do we have prayers, the Lord’s prayer, confessions, absolutions, saying the creed together?

We have an order in our Church service because God is not a God of disorder but of peace.

We want to have order in the Church so that people who come to Church are built up.

As Anglicans, we have a wonderful heritage of an ordered church service.

It’s a service order that reminds people of our sins and of our need to turn back to God and find forgiveness in the gospel of Jesus Christ.

It’s a service order that reminds people that this is not a show for people to passively watch but it’s a service order that actively invites people to join together in an orderly manner.

It’s a service order that’s gospel-focussed and actively engages people.

Also, while we might not have people who are formally called prophets, we do have people who speak God’s word to us.

When preachers preach, like I do now, we teach God’s people God’s word in a timely manner, just like the prophets.

When Growth group leaders teach, we also teach God’s people God’s word, just like the prophets.

When service leaders lead, they help the congregation reflect on God’s word and think about how we apply it to our lives.

In that sense, they too, act like the prophets.

On our Church service, we encourage people to speak and to be silent at different parts of the service,

so that there’ll be order in the service, which leads to the Church being built up.

For those who speak in tongues and those who prophesy, there will be times when they will make a conscious decision to be silent,

Even if they think they have something important to say, they will intentionally choose to be silent

So that others might be built up.

To be silent is not simply just not doing anything.

To be silent doesn’t mean that you stop engaging the service, or to stop listening to the sermon.

Rather, to be silent here is a conscious decision to both respect and to worship the God of order as well as to build up those who are at Church.

To be silent at the appropriate time in Church is both a godly thing to do and a respectful thing to do.

It’s with that in mind that we look at the next section of the passage.

Silence is necessary to prevent disgrace.

Silence is necessary to precent disgrace

Look with me at the end of verse 33 to verse 35, “As in all the churches of the saints, 34 the women should be silent in the churches, for they are not permitted to speak, but are to submit themselves, as the law also says. 35 If they want to learn something, let them ask their own husbands at home, since it is disgraceful for a woman to speak in the church.”

Here, Paul’s saying that silence is necessary to prevent disgrace.

If we just look at the verse in isolation, we might be horrified at what Paul seems to be saying.

Isn’t Paul telling women to be not speak at Church?

Isn’t he telling the women to press on the mute button at Church?

One of the errors that people not familiar with the Bible make is that they look at a Bible verse in isolation.

They look at the one verse in isolation and they’re horrified at what the Bible is saying.

It’s like looking at this picture.

Taken in isolation, it looks like a blanket of some sort.

But what is it?

Message your answer to me via whatsapp, wechat or Zoom.

What is this?

You have twenty seconds to work out.

It’s the pages of a book!

Sometimes, we can get things wrong when we look at it in isolation.

People unfamiliar with the Bible often do that with the Bible

Sadly, some Christians do that as well.

That’s why we must take care to read the Bible in its context, especially when we come to passages like 1 Corinthians 14.

Is Paul here really telling women at Church not to speak?

Well, all you need to do is to turn back to 1 Corinthians 11.

I had already mentioned that Paul expects female prophets to be in the Corinthian Church as well.

And to prophesy, you will need to speak.

And so, in context, the command here to not speak mustn’t be an absolute command.

Indeed, we see from Joel 2 in the Old Testament that God will pour out his Spirit so that sons and daughters will prophesy.

The fact that the women in the Corinthian Church prophesied is a fulfilment of the Old Testament.

Praise God!

Also, when Paul talks about speaking in tongues, he doesn’t mention any difference between men and women.

The presumption would be that men and women are permitted to speak in tongues, and presumably interpret as well.

And so, the command here for women not to speak is not absolute.

To understand what it does mean, we need to look at the wider context.

From 1 Corinthians 11, Paul begins his section on worship and here in 1 Corinthians 14, he ends his section on worship.

On the bookends of this passage are his instruction on the relationship between men and women.

In chapter 11, we read that the Christ is the head of every man, and the man is the head of the woman, and God is the head of Christ.

That’s the order that God’s established in the creation and the Church.

However, when humanity rebelled against God, we rebelled God’s order so that the woman was leading the man, and the man was overriding a direct command from God, even though he had no authority to.

The reversal of God’s creation order is what led men and women to disobey God.

As part of the new beginning in the Lord Jesus Christ, we don’t want to live according to this reversed order anymore.

We want to live according to God’s way for us, as he’s shown in the creation and as he’s now re-established in Christ.

And so the order that we have at Church matches that of creation: Christ is the head of every man, and the man is the head of the woman, and God is the head of Christ.

And so, with regards to speaking, the Bible is not against women speaking in Church absolutely.

However, the Bible is here commanding our sisters to be silent when it comes to speaking authoritatively.

It’s about speaking authoritatively as the leader of the Church.

This is reinforced by the fact that there seems to be a progression on the significance of the three modes of speech from verse 26 onwards.

With speaking in tongues, Paul says if someone can speak in tongues, and if there’s someone to interpret, and as long as you have a maximum of three, why not?

With prophesying, Paul says that two or three prophets should speak, as long as they’re ready to step down for others to speak, and as long as they’re willing to be evaluated.

Prophesying seems to be a step up from speaking in tongues.

When it comes to speaking, Paul begins by saying that as in all the churches of the saints, the women should be silent.

Also, verse 36, “Or did the word of God originate from you, or did it come to you only?”

Paul is more serious about not having the women speak at Church than for women not to prophesy nor to speak in tongues.

And so, it seems to suggest that the speech that Paul is thinking of is an authoritative speech that one would expect a leader of a Church to make.

Also, Paul says that if the women have questions, they should ask their husbands at home.

This may be questions that someone in authority ask to see whether someone is legitimate, for example, asking questions to see whether the person is teaching the truth.

Or it may simply be questions of curiosity about what was said in the service.

Either way, there’s an appropriate place where women ask these questions, namely, the home.

And there’s an appropriate person to whom women are to ask these questions, namely, their own husbands.

The home is the appropriate place to ask these questions, not the Church.

What would happen if a woman were to speak (I think authoritatively) at Church?

Paul says it would be a disgrace.

The word disgrace was used last in 1 Corinthians 11.

There it talks about how disgraceful it would be for a woman to not cover her head while prophesying (which involves speaking).

In the Corinthian Church, when a woman doesn’t cover her head, she shows that she doesn’t respect God’s created order.

To avoid bringing dishonour and shame to her head, namely the man, and out of respect of God’s created order, she should prophesy with her head covered.

Here in 1 Corinthians 14, if the woman speaks authoritatively, she similarly shows that she doesn’t respect God’s created order.

She, too, brings dishonour and shame to her head, namely the man, which ultimately brings dishonour and shame to his head, namely Christ.

This isn’t just for the Church in Corinth.

Paul includes all the churches of the saints in this command.

In fact, it’s not just something that Paul commanded; it’s the Lord’s command.

Look with me at verse 37, “If anyone thinks he is a prophet or spiritual, he should recognize that what I write to you is the Lord’s command.”

If you belong to the Lord Jesus, if you think you’re spiritual or a prophet, you will recognise this as the Lord’s command.

It’s what Jesus commanded.

And he follows this with a stern warning, “If anyone ignores this, he will be ignored.”

For any man or woman to ignore this command will lead to that same man or woman being ignored, not by Paul but by the Lord.

It’s a very stern warning!

And as the Church of the Lord Jesus, we take the Lord’s command very seriously.

That’s why we invite both men and women to read the Bible and to pray.

I’m glad that our Bible readers take reading the Bible seriously for they are reading to us the very words of God.

There’s a sense in which they are doing the function of a prophet as they God’s word to us.

Throughout the Bible, we’ve seen men and women praying to God and as a Church we affirm that it is good for men and women to pray to God.

We have men and women teaching our children in KOG ministries and in Scripture in schools.

We have men and women involved in each of our 5G teams.

We have men and women involved with our AV team.

We invite both men and women to our Growth groups where we learn God’s word together.

But we reserve the authoritative preaching of God’s word to the men of the Church.

We also reserve the authoritative teaching of God’s word in Growth groups where there are men or mixed men and women to the men of this Church.

Whenever possible, we hope to start a Growth group led by women for women.

We believe that we celebrate God’s created order in the Christ’s Church by following the order he’s given us in the Bible.

We want to follow God’s word, not only when it’s convenient for us, not only when the world agrees with us,

But at all times and in all places.

There will be some, in fact, there will be many who would disagree.

Some would say that Paul’s way was ancient and not applicable in the 21st Century.

Some would say that while they agree with most of the Bible, they would leave this section and other difficult sections out.

How should we respond to these charges?

Kathy Keller, wife of the well-known Dr Timothy Keller of Redeemer Presbyterian Church in New York, wrote a little book called Jesus, Justice and Gender Roles.

In it she responds to these charges with a question and a statement:

﻿“What other of the Lord’s commands are you willing to sit in judgment upon?”

If you think you have the authority to leave out one part of the Bible, which other parts of the Bible don’t you think belong there?

The second is more of a statement that a question: “﻿Consider the enormous hubris in appointing our present cultural moment as the yardstick against which God’s Word must be measured.”

Allow me to rephase it as a question: Why do you measure God’s word using your worldview as standard? Could it be that your worldview is an inadequate way to measure God’s word?

Don’t be foolish and judge God’s Word according to your standards.

Rather, be judged by God’s Word as we realise how far we have failed his standards.

Paul concludes with these words in verses 39-40, “39 So then, my brothers and sisters, be eager to prophesy, and do not forbid speaking in tongues. 40 But everything is to be done decently and in order.”

When we come to Church, make sure everything is done decently and in order.

When everything is done decently and in order, the God of peace is glorified and the Church of God is built up.

Part of making sure everything is done decently is to make sure silence is observed appropriately when we gather.

Silence is essential when we gather before the God of peace.

What does this mean for us in today’s Church?

There’s a time to be silent and a time to speak, Ecclesiastes 3:7

When I’m on social platforms like WeChat, there’s a temptation to respond quickly because it only takes a few seconds, right?

However, as many of us will realise, being on social media can quickly take up minutes, even hours of your time, especially when you’re in the middle of a discussion!

I remember seeing something that I didn’t agree with on WeChat and there was a temptation to send a message quickly to tell the person that they’re wrong.

However, I paused and waited, as Jenny had wisely counselled me.

In the end, I’m glad that I didn’t reply because as it turns out, the person was right.

If I had replied too quickly, I would have regretted soon afterwards and what’s worse, it will be stored somewhere on the internet, forever.

There’s a lot of wisdom in staying silent on social media at the appropriate time.

Of course, this doesn’t just apply to social media.

It’s also applicable to general speech as well.

There’s a time to be silent and a time to speak.

In the context of today’s passage, we need to think about what it means for us to speak at appropriate times at Church, especially when it comes to speaking authoritatively.

Firstly, a word to the brothers.

Brothers, we need to step up.

As men, we have a responsibility to teach God’s word to our families.

To do that, we need to grow in our understanding of God’s word.

In fact, if you are a follower of Jesus, you must grow in your knowledge of God’s word.

You must make it a priority.

Train yourself in God’s word; join a Growth Group. There are four on during the week, in both Chinese and English.

Take up your responsibility to teach God’s word authoritatively to your family.

I’m glad that a number of you already do that at home.

Keep it up and keep getting better at it.

If you are single, why wait until you get married?

Start growing in your knowledge of God’s word now!

Now a word to our sister.

If you are married and have a husband who believes in Jesus, praise the Lord.

It’s a blessing to have a believing husband.

Ask them questions of faith.

If they don’t know, get them to read the Bible and do the research.

Don’t let them off the hook – it’s their responsibility.

If your husband is teaching God’s word to the household, keep encouraging them to do it!

For those sisters whose husbands are not Christians yet, keep praying for the,

When you get the chance, ask them questions about faith as well.

Share with them what you’ve learnt in the Bible.

Don’t give up on your husbands; keep praying that they’ll turn to Christ.

There’s nothing more important right now, then for them to have Jesus Christ.

If you haven’t yet, join a Growth group; I know that many sisters have joined.

Also, join one-two-ones’ with other sisters; I know that Jenny has worked very hard to get them going.

If you want to find mutual encouragement with other sisters, please contact Jenny.

As we begin Church again, think about what it means for us to build someone up.

How am I speaking to build someone up?

How am I using my silence to build someone up?

May God give us wisdom to know when to speak and when to be silent.

For there is a time to be silent and a time to speak.

Here are the three questions for this week:

When am I tempted to speak when I should be silent?

When am I tempted to be silent when I should speak up?

How can I use my speech (and my silences) to build up the Church?

Friends, there is a time to be silent and a time to speak.

May we use our speech and our silences to glorify the God of peace.
