Home Groups (adults)

Doctor Jesus, heal yourself

Luke 4:16–44 · Sunday 25 October 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

For adult home groups and midweek fellowships · Sermon by Rev David Yung

Big Idea: God sent Jesus to tell the gospel, and he would not waver before contempt or confusion. Now that you have heard it, respond with consecrated service.

Aim: by the end of the study, group members will

see how Jesus did not waver before contempt from Nazareth or from demons, nor linger with a confused Capernaum;

name where they meet contempt for the gospel, or treat Jesus as the supplier of their needs;

respond to the gospel with consecrated service: one area of service, and one stranger invited, this week.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

1. Connect (10 min)

  • In Australia we talk about Tall Poppy Syndrome — when someone succeeds and, instead of being celebrated, is resented or cut down. Have you seen it at work, in the family or at school pick-up? Have you ever felt it, or been tempted to do it?
  • Think of a time someone did something for you that you could never repay — a doctor, a friend, a neighbour. What did gratitude look like in practice, beyond saying thank you?

2. Read: Luke 4:16–44 (CSB)

16 He came to Nazareth, where he had been brought up. As usual, he entered the synagogue on the Sabbath day and stood up to read. 17 The scroll of the prophet Isaiah was given to him, and unrolling the scroll, he found the place where it was written: 18 The Spirit of the Lord is on me, because he has anointed me to preach good news to the poor. He has sent me to proclaim release to the captives and recovery of sight to the blind, to set free the oppressed, 19 to proclaim the year of the Lord’s favor.

20 He rolled up the scroll, gave it back to the attendant, and sat down. And the eyes of everyone in the synagogue were fixed on him. 21 He began by saying to them, “Today, as you listen, this Scripture is fulfilled.” 22 They were all speaking well of him and amazed by the gracious words that came from his mouth. They said, “Isn’t this Joseph’s son?”

23 Then he said to them, “No doubt you will quote this proverb to me: ‘Doctor, heal yourself. What we’ve heard that took place in Capernaum, do here in your hometown also.’” 24 He also said, “Truly I tell you, no prophet is accepted in his hometown. 25 But I say to you, there were certainly many widows in Israel in Elijah’s days, when the sky was shut up for three years and six months while a great famine came over all the land. 26 Yet Elijah was not sent to any of them—except to a widow at Zarephath in Sidon. 27 And there were many lepers in Israel in the prophet Elisha’s time, yet not one of them was healed—only Naaman the Syrian.”

28 When they heard this, everyone in the synagogue was enraged. 29 They got up, drove him out of town, and brought him to the edge of the hill that their town was built on, intending to hurl him over the cliff. 30 But he passed right through the crowd and went on his way.

31 Then he went down to Capernaum, a town in Galilee, and was teaching them on the Sabbath day. 32 They were astonished at his teaching because his message had authority. 33 In the synagogue there was a man with an unclean demon’s spirit, and he cried out in a loud voice, 34 “Leave us alone! What do you have to do with us, Jesus of Nazareth? Have you come to destroy us? I know who you are—the Holy One of God!” 35 But Jesus rebuked him. “Be silent and come out of him!” he said. And throwing the man down before them, the demon came out of him without hurting him at all. 36 They were all struck with amazement and kept talking to each other, saying, “What is this message? For he commands the unclean spirits with authority and power, and they come out!” 37 Reports about him went out to every place in the vicinity.

38 After he left the synagogue, he entered Simon’s house. Simon’s mother-in-law was suffering from a high fever, and they asked him about her. 39 So he stood over her and rebuked the fever, and it left her. She got up immediately and began to serve them.

40 When the sun was setting, all those who had anyone sick with various diseases brought them to him. He laid his hands on each one of them and healed them. 41 Demons also came out of many, shouting, “You are the Son of God!” But he rebuked them and would not allow them to speak because they knew he was the Messiah.

42 When it was day, he went out and made his way to a deserted place. The crowds were searching for him, and they came to him and tried to keep him from leaving them. 43 But he said to them, “It is necessary for me to proclaim the good news about the kingdom of God to the other towns also, because I was sent for this purpose.” 44 So he kept on preaching in the synagogues of Judea.

3. Context

Luke 4:16–44 opens Jesus’ public ministry. In his home synagogue at Nazareth Jesus reads Isaiah’s promise of good news for the poor and says it is fulfilled in him. The people who admire his gracious words turn violent when he says God’s favour has gone to outsiders — a widow in Sidon and a Syrian general. In Capernaum he teaches and casts out demons with authority and heals many, and when the crowds try to keep him he moves on. Three responses: contempt, confusion, and the one right response — consecrated service.

4. Discuss

  1. (vv.16–21) Jesus reads Isaiah’s promise and says, “Today, as you listen, this Scripture is fulfilled.” What is he claiming about himself and his mission? Why does it matter that the promise is fulfilled in a person?
  2. (vv.22–23) “Isn’t this Joseph’s son?” … “Doctor, heal yourself.” Why does contempt hurt most from the people who have known us longest? How did Jesus respond — and what did he not do?
  3. (vv.24–27) Why does Jesus remind Nazareth of the widow at Zarephath and Naaman the Syrian? What is the heart of the problem — not that they rejected Jesus, but that…? (Compare Luke 3:8.)
  4. (vv.28–30) The crowd tries to throw Jesus off the cliff; he walks through them. When family or colleagues roll their eyes at our faith, where are we tempted to retaliate or retreat? Who beyond our friends and family — a stranger, a foreigner — might God have prepared to hear the gospel?
  5. (vv.31–37) The demon cries, “I know who you are—the Holy One of God!” Jesus commands him with authority. When we hear Jesus’ words, do we recognise his authority or sneer? Where, even at church, do we look down on gospel work others have done?
  6. (vv.38–44) Capernaum is amazed and tries to keep Jesus; he leaves (v.43). What is the difference between amazement and understanding? How can a church that only asks “are we well fed?” mistake Jesus for its Doctor?
  7. (vv.38–39) Simon’s mother-in-law “got up immediately and began to serve them.” What does this show about saving and serving? How is her knowledge of Jesus different from the demons’ (James 2:19–20)?
  8. (application) “Grace comes first and service follows.” What hours of your week could you offer in consecrated service? What holds you back from stepping out of admiring Jesus from a familiar distance?

5. Live It Out

KOG 3·2·1·0 — pray for 3, connect with 2, invite 1, 0 left unloved.

Pray for 3 — three people at work, in your street or in your extended family who don’t yet know Jesus. Pray by name daily.

Connect with 2 — a coffee, meal or walk with two of them this week.

Invite 1 — invite one of your three, or a neighbour or stranger, to the Welcome Home course, the Discover Course or the Christmas concert on 13 December (we are praying for 120+ people).

0 left unloved — at morning tea, find the person on the edge: new, alone or drifting.

This week’s challenge: Stop admiring Jesus from a familiar distance. Offer yourself for one area of service — hall set-up and pack-down, Safe Ministry helpers, music or Welcome Home hosting — using the Giftedness team’s link. Serve not to be healed, but because you have been healed.

Memory verse: “Today, as you listen, this Scripture is fulfilled.” (Luke 4:21)

6. Pray

  • Thank God for sending Jesus to tell us the gospel, and for not wavering even on the cross. Confess where you have treated Jesus and his word with contempt, or wanted the gifts without the Giver.
  • Ask the Father to consecrate our lives with acts done in faith, so we carry Jesus’ good news to the poor, the captives, the oppressed and the blind.
  • Pray by name for your 3 — that they would hear the gospel and meet Jesus, the Holy One who came to serve.

7. Leader’s Notes

Framing. Joyfully point to Jesus. Assume some aren’t yet Christians or are unsure. The good news is not “try harder to serve” but that the Holy One has already served us. Guests may be present; never corner anyone.

Q1 — God has anointed Jesus with his Spirit to preach good news to the poor, release captives, give sight to the blind and free the oppressed. It is fulfilled “today” because he is here.

Q2 — They had known him twenty years: “Who does he think he is?” Jesus did not feel sorry for himself; his answer turned the situation upside down.

Q3 — The heart of the problem was not that they rejected Jesus, but that God had rejected them: the year of favour was not for Nazareth. It is like giving the birthday cake meant for my child to a stranger. Favour rests on those who confess Jesus as the Son of God, not merely Joseph’s son (Luke 3:8).

Q4 — Contempt for your gospel work is a sign they are not favoured by God and need the gospel more urgently than they realise. Don’t retaliate, retreat or be discouraged. Strangers are often more open, because God has prepared them; most baptisms this past year were strangers.

Q5 — The demons know exactly who Jesus is, but theirs is not a saving knowledge. He speaks with unmatched authority, so don’t show contempt for his words or for those who do his work.

Q6 — Capernaum wanted a free doctor with a 100% success rate, but amazement is not understanding. Jesus came to be the Saviour, not the Doctor. Don’t reduce him to a supplier of your needs; he is firstly your Lord, and he sends us to every household in our parish.

Q7 — She was saved when Jesus served her, and her service arose from that: a saving-serving relationship (James Edwards). Faith without works of service is useless, demonic, dead.

Q8 — “Consecrate” means to make holy: we serve because the Holy One came to serve us. About 410 hours a month make church happen; pray that God moves more of us to give our hours. Not contempt, not confusion, but consecration.

Our vision. “From faith to faith”: faith is handed on, but real only when each of us hears the gospel and responds.
