Have you heard of the term ‘Asians don’t raisin’?

Raisins are dried fruits and wrinkly and so when people say Asians don’t raisin, they’re saying some Asians still look young for their age.

Of course, that’s a very general comment and Asians do raisin.

But I do remember one time taking our children to their new school and we were met by this very excited, very welcoming young lady.

She was about Zoe’s height or even a little shorter than Zoe so I thought, “She’s probably a senior student but she just looks young for her age.

You know what they say, ‘Asians don’t raisin.’”

But after we talked for a while, I realise she wasn’t a student.

She was teacher, the year advisor no less, looking after Zoe’s year.

Yeah, Asians don’t raisin!

The fact that she wasn’t wearing the school uniform probably was a give-away.

But in my defence, Miss C carrying backpack makes her look a lot like a student.

Miss C was a wonderful teach to Zoe and her grade and I was very glad that she was Zoe’s year advisor for a number of years.

To help us identify students, schools in Australia require our students to wear uniforms.

But how do we identify Christians?

How do we know who’s a Christian in our community? Should we be wearing uniforms too?

How does anyone know that you’re a Christian?

What’s the mark of a Christian?

That’s what we’re going to see in today’s passage in Genesis 17 beginning from verse 1.

We’re going to look at the topic of circumcision and we’re going to see three things:

The covenant of circumcision magnifies God’s promises

The covenant of circumcision marks Abraham’s sons

The covenant of circumcision foreshadows God’s Son

Now for some of our parents, if you feel like you would need to cover your children’s ears at certain parts of the talk, I’ll understand.

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Dear Lord, by your Spirit please open our eyes as we read your word, that we might once again marvel with thanks and humility for all that you’ve done for the Father’s glory, Amen.”

The covenant of circumcision magnifies God’s promises

When we started reading about Abram, he was already an old man.

Do you remember how old he was?

75 years old.

God promised to make him into a great nation.

He waited a long time, but nothing was happening.

After ten years, Sarai his wife couldn’t wait any longer.

And so, she asked her maid to sleep with Abram so that even though she couldn’t have a son, Abram would still have a son.

As we read last week, Hagar and Abram did as they were told and eventually Ishmael was born to Abram, about one year afterwards.

As we begin today’s passage, Abram is now 99 years old.

Thirteen years have now passed since Ishmael’s birth and God comes to see Abram again.

Look with me at Genesis 17:1, “When Abram was ninety-nine years old, the LORD appeared to him, saying, “I am God Almighty. Live in my presence and be blameless.”

Last week, we read that Hagar gave God the name El-Roi, which meant God see me.

This week, we’re introduced to another name of God that we see in the Bible - God Almighty- El Shaddai.

He gave Abram two commands: to live in his presence and be blameless. (In Chinese: “我是全能的 神, 你要在我面前行事為人；)

We read two chapters ago, in chapter 15, that Abram has been counted righteous because he believed in God.

Now, he’s to live out that righteousness by living in God’s presence and being blameless.

God then says that he will set up his covenant between him and Abram, that God will multiply him greatly.

Didn’t God already set up a covenant with Abram already, back in Genesis 15?

Do you remember when he asked Abram to cut the animals in half and God walked through them to say that he will be broken like these if he were to break his promises to Abram?

Why is he making a covenant with him again?

God’s making his covenant with Abram to magnify his promises to Abram.

Why do we use magnifying glasses? To see things that are too small for our eyes to see.

Sometimes, these things might be really small, and we have to look through microscopes to see what they are.

Other times, these things might be enormous, like stars, which seem small to us because they’re far away and we must look through telescopes to see they look like.

When God magnifies his promises, he’s helping us to see them not because they’re small.

These promises are massive and they’re still far away.

However, as they come, God begin to show even more clearly how incredible these promises really are.

If we use the telescope example, it will be like watching a comet, hurtling towards us.

It starts off as a speck of light in the sky but as we use our telescope to look at it more closely, we begin to see the brilliance of the comet.

That’s what God’s promises are like in the Bible.

It starts off beautiful and simple, but they slowly unfold into something magnificent and glorious.

In Abram’s case, we might summarise God’s promises to Abram in Genesis 12 like this:

From Genesis 12-16, we read that God will make Abram into a great nation (God’s people); God will one day give the land of Canaan to Abram (God’s place); and God will bless those who bless Abram and curse those who show him contempt (God’s rule).

God’s people, in God’s place under God’s rule.

In Genesis 17, in this covenant, God magnifies these already magnificent promises.

Verses 3-4, “Then Abram fell facedown and God spoke with him: 4 “As for me, here is my covenant with you: You will become the father of many nations.”

God will make not just one nation from Abram but many nations. God’s people.

Verse 8, “And to you and your future offspring I will give the land where you are residing—all the land of Canaan—as a permanent possession, and I will be their God.”

God will give the land of Canaan to Abram’s descendants, as a permanent, everlasting possession. God’s place.

Verses 6-7, “I will make you extremely fruitful and will make nations and kings come from you. 7 I will confirm my covenant that is between me and you and your future offspring throughout their generations. It is a permanent covenant to be your God and the God of your offspring after you.”

Kings and nations will come from Abram and God will be the God not only of Abram but his children as well. God’s rule.

And from now on, Abram will no longer be known as Abram but Abraham; he will be the father of many nations.

From this one man, Abraham will come many nations, living in a place permanently under God’s rule.

The LORD Almighty, El-Shaddai, will be the God of the children of Abraham, just as the LORD Almighty had been Abraham their father’s God.

There’s no point being a great nation, to having kings and nations, to even having permanent land, if you don’t belong to God.

To be identified as God’s very own, to be able to call God and live in God’s presence, is greater honour than all of these.

As well as magnifying the covenant in Genesis 15, the covenant in Genesis 17 also highlights how God’s people are to live in response to what God’s done.

Whereas in Genesis 12, Abram was mainly a passive recipient of God’s covenant, in Genesis 17, God commands Abraham to live in God’s presence and be blameless.

Abram is to actively engage in and take part in the covenant.

By implication, this applies to all the children of Abraham.

As people of the LORD Almighty, they, too are to walk blamelessly in his presence.

The way they live their life blamelessly is how people know that they belong to the LORD Almighty.

I remember going to a restaurant many years ago with Jenny and I noticed that one waiter was very friendly and happily doing his job.

And so, I was wondering whether he was a Christian.

How did I find out for sure? I just asked him.

I figured, if he said yes, then I’ll ask what church he goes to.

And if he said no, I could ask him whether I can talk with him about Jesus.

I did ask him as I was paying the bill and I’m glad to tell you that he was a Christian.

And no, he didn’t get tips for answering.

Of course, no Christian is perfect; in fact, there are some fake Christians living such bad lives that they give the love of Christ a bad name.

That’s why we need more Christians who will live blameless lives, not to be noticed and be praised, but to be noticed and to point to the One who should be praised.

In Matthew 5:16, Jesus says, “In the same way, let your light shine before others, so that they may see your good works and give glory to your Father in heaven.”

Be that little light that shines so that somehow, through our lives, we may magnify God’s glory, and help them see Jesus better.

Point out God’s glorious promises in the Bible to those around us.

Be a telescope! Walk in God’s presence and be blameless!

2. The covenant of circumcision marks Abraham’s sons

As well as blameless living, there’s another very distinctive marker for God’s people.

It’s the mark of circumcision.

It’s a procedure done to the boys and men who belong to Abraham’s household.

It’s something only done to the guys, not the girls.

Parents, have I given enough warning?

Look with me at verses 9-12,

Vv9-12, “God also said to Abraham, “As for you, you and your offspring after you throughout their generations are to keep my covenant. 10 This is my covenant between me and you and your offspring after you, which you are to keep: Every one of your males must be circumcised. 11 You must circumcise the flesh of your foreskin to serve as a sign of the covenant between me and you. 12 Throughout your generations, every male among you is to be circumcised at eight days old—every male born in your household or purchased from any foreigner and not your offspring.”

Every boy, every man in Abraham’s household is to be circumcised.

Circumcision is the mark that you belong to Abraham’s family.

In New Zealand, many men and women have Ta Moko, tattoo that they put permanently on their faces and other parts of the body.

These Ta Moko aren’t just for decoration; they tell you what tribe you’re from.

They tell you where you belong.

For the Jewish men, they don’t get a tattoos; they get circumcised.

They have to cut their foreskin off.

That’s God’s requirement for belong for every Jewish boy and every Jewish man.

Even four thousand years later, we feel uncomfortable and embarrassed to talk about it.

God makes a permanent mark on a very intimate part of the men and the boys.

It’s a procedure so it’s bloody and painful.

It’s not nice to talk about it.

In the Bible, there had been several accounts that describe how grown men couldn’t move much for a few days after they’ve been circumcised.

But that’s God’s requirement for every man in Abraham’s household.

If you’re a baby boy, you must be circumcised eight days from birth.

If you started working for Abraham and you’re a man, you’ll be expected to be circumcised on your first day of work.

If you don’t get circumcised and you’re a man, you’ll be cut off.

How ironic! You don’t want to be circumcised, if you don’t want to go under the knife, well, you’ll be cut off.

What about Abraham’s wife Sarai?

She will have a new identity as well. Instead of being known as Sarai, she’s now known as Sarah.

She will have her own son from her own body.

They will both actively be involved in God’s covenant.

Together, this 100-year-old man and this 90-year-old woman will have a son.

From their son, kings and nations will rise.

When Abraham heard it, he thought it was so funny that he laughed.

Would God really give a baby son to this old couple, seriously?

And so, rather than focussing on a son who hasn’t been born yet, Abraham talks to God about the son he already has.

Verse 18, ““If only Ishmael were acceptable to you!”

Basically he’s saying, look, God, here’s my son Ishmael.

Let the covenant come through him. He’s already here!

But God says no.

God’s covenant won’t come through Ishmael but Isaac.

God’s covenant will come through Isaac, the son of promise.

But even for Ishmael, there will be blessings.

Look with me at verse 20, “As for Ishmael (remember the name meaning God heard in Hebrew), I have heard you. I will certainly bless him”.

Ishmael will also be the father of a great nation, but the covenant will come through Isaac.

And so, Abraham obeyed God and circumcised all the men in his household.

He was circumcised; Ishmael was circumcised.

All the men were circumcised.

All these men, both old and young would’ve been in a lot of pain for the next few days.

It would not had been a pretty sight, but that was God’s command and so Abraham obeyed and circumcised.

Why would God ask Abraham to do that?

Why not do something else?

Hands up those who would’ve preferred a tattoo over circumcision.

Hands up those who would’ve preferred a tattoo on the face instead of circumcision.

Of all the markers God could’ve chosen, why did he choose circumcision?

What is it about circumcision that led God to choose it as the distinctive physical marker for Abraham and his children?

Surely there are less painful ways, less embarrassing, and dare I say, less shameful ways that the God Almighty could’ve chosen to identify his people.

Why circumcision?

3.The covenant of circumcision foreshadows God’s Son

God chose circumcision as part of the covenant because circumcision foreshadows God’s Son.

In time, the practice of circumcision comes to define the men of the nation of Israel.

Circumcision serves as a sign that that every man in the community is here only because of God’s faithfulness to the old man Abraham.

The whole community of circumcised Jewish men has been a testimony of God’s faithfulness to Abraham for the past four thousand years.

However, as good as that is, it’s eclipsed by the coming of the Son of God, who came as a Jewish boy, who himself was circumcised and was himself what every act of circumcision ultimately points to.

Look with me at Colossians 2:11-12,

“You were also circumcised in him with a circumcision not done with hands, by putting off the body of flesh, in the circumcision of Christ, 12 when you were buried with him in baptism, in which you were also raised with him through faith in the working of God, who raised him from the dead.”

The covenant of circumcision is ultimately a sign that points to Jesus.

A few years ago, our family drove up to Queensland to meet with Zoe, who had just finished a conference there.

It’s a long way from here, over 861km.

As we drive along the way, we see signs that tell us how far we’re away from Queensland.

800km to go, 700km to go, 600km, all the way until we’ve crossed the border and now, we’re down to 30km.

We keep following the signs until, finally, we reach our stop.

Every sign that tells you how many km you have to your destination.

And that’s what circumcision is; every circumcision points you to its ultimate destination, namely, Jesus Christ.

For a Jewish man, physical circumcision is the mark that he is a son of Abraham, according to the Old Covenant.

For the Christian, the circumcision of Christ is the mark that he or she belongs to the New Covenant.

That’s what the apostle Paul means when he says in Col 2:11, you were also circumcised in him with a circumcision, not done with hands (that is not the physical circumcision).

No, you were circumcised in him in the circumcision of Christ.

Do you remember what God said as he was about to make the covenant with Abraham, back in Genesis 17:1?

Live in my presence and be blameless.

However, even the great Abraham himself couldn’t do that.

He could not live a completely blameless life in God’s presence.

But Christ did; He was perfectly blameless, fully obedient to God.

He was and is the one person who truly belonged to God the Father.

He succeeded where we all have failed.

As a result of our failure, we deserved to be cut off from God.

To be cut off from God is to lose our relationship with him.

But it’s more than just a relationship, to lose relationship with God is to lose relationship to life itself.

And yet, for our sake, Jesus was willing to take what we deserved.

Jesus took our punishment from God and experienced being cut off from God.

He experienced death, physically a horrible experience, and spiritually an unimaginable torture.

Jesus was cut off to save people like us.

On the cross, Jesus was cut off.

The death of Jesus Christ on the cross is the circumcision of Christ.

Circumcision is embarrassing; it’s bloody, it’s ugly, it’s shameful.

That’s what the cross of Jesus is.

Those who belong to Christ are no longer required to have a physical circumcision because Jesus Christ was cut off for them.

Their sign is not a physical circumcision but the circumcision of Christ, that is, the cross of Christ.

Look with me at Galatians 6:14-15, “But as for me, I will never boast about anything except the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ. The world has been crucified to me through the cross, and I to the world. 15 For both circumcision and uncircumcision mean nothing; what matters instead is a new creation.”

Trusting in what Jesus has achieved on the cross, the circumcision of Christ, is what counts.

And it’s not merely trusting the fact that Jesus has died on the cross.

Believers are said to be circumcised in him.

If we trust in the circumcision of Christ, our hearts will be circumcised.

Look with me at Romans 2:29, “On the contrary, a person is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart—by the Spirit, not the letter. That person’s praise is not from people but from God.”

Our hearts will be transformed into one that wants to love God.

John Newton, the man who wrote Amazing Grace, also wrote these words about what it means to have a heart circumcised before God:

“Our pleasure and our duty,

Though opposite before;

Since we have seen his beauty,

Are joined to part no more:”

Those whose hearts are circumcised enjoy obedience.

They find their joy in obedience.

They learn to be like Christ, who would rather choose public shame if it glorifies God,

who would rather suffer and be blameless than to be happy at someone else’s expense.

Those who are circumcised in the heart, those who are in the circumcision of Christ, would choose to be poor in the eyes of the world, even shamed and ridiculed, just as Christ himself endured the shame that comes with circumcision.

For just as Christ knows the brilliant, blinding glory that lies behind the cross, who now lives and reigns in that glory, the glory he shares with God the Father and God the Spirit,

those who are his also put their hope in the same glory.

On 10 March 2020, a group of men with sledgehammers appeared at Pastor David’s (another Pastor David) church in Cuba at 9am.

That Pastor David said “These men wanted to demolish the church and everything inside, and [they] started smashing it with their 10-kilo sledgehammers.”

All that remains of Pastor David’s church building is the roof, yet his congregation continues to gather for worship and fellowship.

“Despite everything, the congregation continues to grow more and more.”

Would you still come to this Pastor David’s Church if our Church was ridiculed and attacked, and even destroyed?

Would you be embarrassed to talk about Jesus if it brings shame and suffering?

For those of us who trust in the circumcision of Christ, who trust what Christ has done for us on the cross,

For those of us who are circumcised in our hearts, who have been transformed in our hearts,

Our hope doesn’t depend on the physical.

Our hope depends on Christ.

Our hope is in the glory of Christ, the glory that we see glimpses of in his promises as described in the Bible.

It’s because we’ve seen the glory of Christ, we will also bear the shame and the suffering.

And so, what’s the mark of a Christian?

It’s the Circumcision of Christ – a life marked by the death of Christ and resurrection of Christ.

It’s also a heart that’s been circumcised – renewed and transformed; a heart that finds joy in obeying God.

It’s the heart of a people who find joy as we walk with Jesus, before his presence and be blameless.

Friends, remember: The covenant of circumcision magnifies God’s promises, marks God’s people, and foreshadows our circumcision in the circumcision of the crucified Christ.

By faith look through the telescope and see the glory of Christ.
