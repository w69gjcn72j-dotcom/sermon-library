A Dangerous Happiness

Passage: 1 Cor 10:1-13

Big Idea: Don’t desert your faith by desiring evil but depend on God instead.

We desire happiness

A dangerous happiness:

A redeemed people

Desert by desire

Happiness in God’s faithfulness

What makes you happy? What gets you excited?

When I was small, we didn’t go to theme parks all that often.

But when I was growing into my teenage years, I started trying out the rides in the amusement park.

I tried rollercoasters and even horror houses on the rare occasion.

Once Jenny and I even tried the catapult ride – that was probably the craziest one I’ve been on.

But I haven’t tried any of the big spinning one yet; I know I’ll get really dizzy afterwards.

Even though these rides are fast and exciting, and even though it appears to have an element of danger, I know that they’re pretty safe.

Though it looks like it’s dangerous, it’s still safer than activities like parachute jumping.

It’s a safe sort of happiness.

But can the pursuit of happiness be dangerous?

I’m not talking about the amusement ride or even parachute jumping.

I’m talking about pursuing happiness in the wrong place, for the wrong reason.

In today’s passage, we will see a certain kind of happiness as dangerous, fatal even.

Now of course, I’m not saying that all happiness is dangerous.

But there are pursuits of happiness that will lead us down a dangerous path.

Today, I want us to think about how some of our desires, how some of our pursuits of happiness will lead to our downfall.

Today, we’re going to look at 1 Corinthians 10:1-11.

We’re going to see three things:

We desire happiness

A dangerous happiness

Happiness in God’s faithfulness

Before we begin, let’s pray, “Father, we thank you for your word. Now, may the words of my mouth and the meditation of all our hearts, be acceptable to you, Lord, my rock and my Redeemer.”

We desire happiness

We like happiness, don’t we?

We want happiness. In a time like ours, we look forward to having something to smile about.

What was the last thing you’ve smiled about over the past day?

That’s the first question for today.

Talk with the person next to you. What got your smiling over the past day?

You can send me your answer via whatsapp, wechat, or Zoom.

What’s put a smile on your face?

You have twenty seconds.

It’s great to have something to smile about, isn’t it?

Over the past week, I got to smile a bit more when I watched the Olympics on TV.

Though it was delayed for a year, it was good to see the Olympics again.

We celebrated as Australia won gold in swimming and in rowing.

Ezekiel and I cheered as the Australian Men Hockey team won their game and advanced to the next round.

We don’t usually watch a lot of sport but it’s been good watching it.

It gave us something to smile about.

It’s good to be happy; it’s a nice feeling.

But is it always a good thing? Is it ever possible to be happy for the wrong reason?

I still remember watching the 1988 Seoul 100 metres sprint men’s finals.

In the race was an athlete called Ben Johnson.

Ben was representing his country Canada and he was the first Canadian to win the gold medal in the event since the last Canadian back in 1928.

Ben ran 100 metres in 9.79 seconds; it was an incredible run.

Everyone was so happy that he won!

However, that happiness was short-lived because Ben was tested positive for steroids.

He was disqualified three days afterwards.

The happiness that Ben Johnson was pursuing was the wrong type of happiness.

He chased after the happiness that comes from winning gold, even if it means cheating.

It’s a pursuit that led to his downfall.

Sadly, there are many people like Ben Johnson, who pursues the wrong type of happiness.

Some, like Ben Johnson, pursue happiness in fame and glory and cheats along the way.

Others pursue happiness in intimacy and, also, cheats on their spouse along the way.

Though we want happiness in our lives, not all pursuits of happiness are good.

Some are downright dangerous.

Dangerous Happiness

In today’s passage, we’ll see how God’s people in ancient Israel had pursued a dangerous happiness.

If you have your Bible with me, please open up to 1 Corinthians 10, beginning from verse one.

“Now I do not want you to be unaware, brothers and sisters, that our ancestors were all under the cloud, all passed through the sea, 2 and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea.”

The apostle Paul tells us that the ancestors, that is, the ancient Israelites were all rescued by God back in the time of the Exodus, around 1500 years before Paul’s time.

You might remember from reading Exodus earlier that God has rescued the Israelites from the Egypt and had opened up the red sea for his people to pass through.

God had miraculously and mightily saved the Israelites from their Egyptian overlords.

They were all baptised into Moses in the cloud and in the sea.

That is, they were all following what God has taught through Moses and would identify Moses as their leader.

Look with me at verses 3-4, “3 They all ate the same spiritual food, 4 and all drank the same spiritual drink. For they drank from the spiritual rock that followed them, and that rock was Christ.”

God didn’t just rescue them; God provided for the Israelites as well in the wilderness as well.

God miraculously gave them food to eat in the form of manna, a bread like substance that appeared every morning.

God also miraculously gave them water from the rock. Yes, a rock!

Paul calls these spiritual food and spiritual drink, not in the sense that they are not physical.

Rather it’s because they are food provided by God the Spirit who can create things out of nothing!

But more than that, can you hear Paul’s choice of words: baptism, spiritual drink, spiritual food.

It sounds like he’s talking about the Church!

He draws the ancient Israelites’ experience and the Church’s experience even closer by identifying the rock that provided the water as Christ!

That doesn’t mean that Jesus became (or transformed into) a rock that moved around with the Israelites.

What Paul means is that Jesus had provided for the Israelites and nourished them during their time in the desert.

What the Israelites were experiencing was a foretaste of what Christians would experience under Christ.

Like the Israelites, the Christians are saved, not from Egyptian slave masters but from the slavery of sin.

Like the Israelites, the Christians are baptised, not in the red sea like the Israelites but in the baptism of the name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit.

Like the Israelites, the Christians are provided with spiritual food, not manna or water from the rock but the bread and the wine that we share in the Holy Communion as we remember that Jesus has done for us.

And like the Israelites, Christ is provides and nourishes us, whether we’re in the Palestinian wilderness or here in COVID lockdown.

But it’s here that Paul wants the similarities to stop.

Look with me at verse 5, “5 Nevertheless God was not pleased with most of them, since they were struck down in the wilderness.”

God was not pleased with most of them.

In fact, all of them, with the exception of two men, died in the desert.

God was not pleased with over 99.99% of the Israelites.

God rejected 99.99% of them.

Most of the Israelites, whom God had rescued, whom Christ had nourished, were rejected and died in the desert.

How about us?

Is it possible for Christians, whom God has rescued, whom Christ has nourished, be in danger of being rejected and die in the spiritual wilderness?

Yes, and a resounding yes at that!

If it’s not possible, then Paul wouldn’t say what he said in verse 6,

“Now these things took place as examples for us, so that we will not desire evil things as they did.”

These things took place as examples for us, so that we won’t do what they did.

They were rescued by God, nourished by Jesus but they were rejected.

As a people rescued by God and nourished by Jesus, we must learn from their mistake and not follow their example.

They’re like an anti-example.

What was their mistake? What did they do that was so wrong?

They desired evil things; they pursued a wrong happiness.

They desired for and they craved for evil things.

They were looking for happiness in evil.

There are four things listed from verse 6-10, can you see them?

Take a moment to look for them in these verses.

Send your answers in by whatsapp, wechat or zoom.

I give you a bit more time; let’s make it 40 seconds.

There are four evil things that the Israelites craved for that we must avoid.

They worshipped idols.

Though God had demonstrated his powers to them over and over again, the Israelites participated in the worship of other gods by joining in their religious festivals.

This involved eating, drinking and partying!

God had given them spiritual food and spiritual drink but now they would eat food and drink drinks which had been offered to idols.

That’s how they repaid God for his kindness! It was like a slap in the face!

Over the past two chapters in Corinthians, Paul’s been talking about food being sacrificed to idols.

While idols are not real, the Corinthians need to do all they can to avoid being misunderstood as participating in idol worship or give the idea that it’s Ok to participate in idol worship.

Risking someone worshipping an idol to gain any sort of happiness is not worth it.

As we’ve learnt from the Israelites, any risk of idol worship is not worth it.

What about us? How might we be in danger or idol worship?

Brian Rosner and Ciampa made this comment about idols: a god is that which one loves, trusts, and serves above all else.

Who or what do we love, trust and serve above all else?

What do we give most of our time and our resources to?

If God is not the one you love, trust and serve above all else, which idol have you been worshipping?

Sexual immorality

The Israelites committed sexual immorality, that is, sleeping with someone who’s not your spouse, and in a single day, 23,000 people died.

In the Old Testament, idolatry and sexual immorality often go together.

And God hates them both. God hates sexual immorality and idol worship.

In a city full of sexual immorality, the Corinthian Christians are called to learn from the Israelites’ examples and not fall into sexual sins.

Our city, our world, is also full of sexual immorality.

What sexually immoral desires are plaguing us?

What sexually immoral desires are we craving for?

Don’t look for happiness there.

Don’t even make inappropriate jokes about sexual immorality; it has no place in a Christian’s life.

It promises happiness but it’s a dangerous happiness.

Testing Christ

The Israelites tested God in the desert by complaining about the food that God gave them.

They were really testing God’s patience.

And God responded by sending poisonous snakes after them and many of them were killed.

The Corinthians too, were testing God’s patience.

They were pushing the limits of what they thought they were allowed to do.

They allowed sexual immorality to go unchallenged in the Church, they were suing one another in the Church, they trivialised the danger of idol worship.

They were testing God’s patience.

Paul warns them against testing God by pointing them back to what happened to the Israelites.

How about us?

In what way are we pushing God’s patience?

Are we letting some sins fester in our lives, knowing full well that it’s against God’s will?

Don’t test God’s patience.

Whatever happiness you might think you get by testing God, it’s dangerous and it’s not worth it.

Complaining

Some people find happiness in finding faults in other people.

I’ve met some and I’m sure you’ve met them too.

Some even enjoy finding fault in God and what he’s said.

To be smug in thinking that you’ve found fault in God’s word is an utterly foolish and stupid way to find happiness.

That’s what the Israelites did, and they were killed by the destroyer.

It sounded a lot like the destroyer that destroyed the firstborns of Egypt.

The Corinthian Christians might have complained about their lack of freedom for the sake of their brothers and sisters.

It might seem like a trivial thing; nothing more than a bit of a whinge.

But make no mistake; to complain about God and his word is an evil thing in the eyes of God.

God’s sent his destroyer to kill the Israelites who complained.

We would do well to learn from the Israelites’ mistakes.

What do we complain and grumble about?

Do we complain about our lack of freedom for the sake of our brothers and sisters?

Do we complain when we serve them?

Paul says that these things that have happened to the ancient Israelites were written for our instructions.

They were written for us, on whom the ends of the ages have come.

The Old Testament is written to help us see what it means to live as God’s people.

And so, read it!

This is not just for those who are new Christians who aren’t sure about the Christian faith.

He has a special word especially for those who thing they are sure.

Look with me at verse 12, “So, whoever thinks he stands must be careful not to fall.”

To fall is to not make it to the end.

Just like the Israelites who began strong when God rescued them but fell in the desert,

There will be people who began well as a Christian, baptised, confirmed even, shared in the Lord’s Supper, who would fall along the way.

Just like the ancient Israelites, if someone pursues happiness in idol worship, in sexual immorality, in testing Christ, in complaining against God,

In any of these evil things,

They will fall.

Even if you have been baptised, take the Lord’s supper, come to Church on Sundays, you can still fall.

For God has not rescued anyone, Man or woman, boys or girls, in order to find their happiness in any of these evil things.

You can’t be a Christian and find your happiness in evil.

A while ago, I met someone who used to play cricket with local Churches in this part of Sydney.

When I told him that I’m from St Paul’s Kogarah, he told me that he remembered playing against our Church’s team.

However, sadly, he’s said that he was pretty sure from the way the players behaved that they were not Christians.

In fact, he was pretty sure that none of those who represented St Paul’s Kogarah actually went to our Church.

These people might have thought that they represented us, they might have thought that they were Christians, but their behaviour showed otherwise.

I don’t know who these Cricketers are; I’ve never met them.

And I hope that they were only having a bad day and that it was out of character for them.

However, I do hope that no one would delude themselves to think that you can call themselves Christians and behave in a way that brings shame and dishonour to Christ.

I hope that no one would be smug and think that they can pursue happiness in evil things and still be a Christian.

I’m not talking not just about the cricketers; I’m talking about all of us who call ourselves Christians.

For those who are Christians would learn from the Bible, they would not pursue happiness in idol worship, in sexual immorality, in testing God, in complaining.

They would not find joy in these evil things.

They would stay away from any forms of happiness that delights in evil.

Instead, they would find their happiness in God’s faithfulness.

Happiness in God’s faithfulness

Look with me at 1 Corinthians 10:13, “No temptation has come upon you except what is common to humanity. But God is faithful; he will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able, but with the temptation he will also provide the way out so that you may be able to bear it.”

Though God’s people may be faithless, God is faithful.

Though in and of ourselves we don’t have the strength to finish the race, God can carry us through.

As my child grow and begin to work with others in school, they introduced a term to me.

It’s called Team Carry.

It’s the person who carries the team even when the rest of the members falter.

It’s that dependable person that you know will help the whole team finish even though they’re not contributing.

Even though we might falter, even if our Church falters, there’s one who never falters.

He is God himself.

God is faithful and will carry us through.

The verse tells us that that the temptations we face are common to humanity.

In the context of this passage, the temptations to worship other gods, the temptation for sexual immorality, the temptation to testing God and complaining against God, is common to all humanity.

Some of us here watching online might be struggling with one of these temptations.

All these temptations promise to make you happy; all these temptations promise that they’re worth pursuing.

In fact, some of the temptations might be so tempting that they tell you that it’s futile to resist them.

The temptation might occupy you throughout the whole day.

It might be the temptation of greed and the craving for better things to own.

It might be the temptation of fame and the craving to be recognised.

These temptations promise happiness and claim to be irresistible.

But they are lies.

No temptation has come upon you except what is common to humanity.

They’ve come before and they’ll come again.

But the Christian is not alone when we face temptations.

We have a faithful God.

God hasn’t left us alone by ourselves to face the temptations; he will carry us through.

God won’t allow us to be tempted beyond what we’re able.

No temptation will prove too much for us.

However, when we are tempted, he will provide a way out.

Temptations are not irresistible; they can be overcome.

God will always provide an exit plan when we face temptations.

He will always provide an exodus.

Just as he’s led the Israelites out of Egypt, God can and will lead his people out of temptations.

Just as he’s provided water from the rock in the middle of the wilderness,

God will provide spiritual nourishment and his very own presence even as we struggle with our own private temptations.

That exodus, that Rock, is none other than Jesus.

Jesus has triumphed over Satan, the Ultimate Tempter.

By his strength, in his name, we will triumph over Satan’s temptations.

And so, friends, don’t desert your faith by desiring evil.

Don’t leave the race of faith in pursuit of an evil happiness.

Instead, depend on God.

God is faithful; he is the Ultimate Team Carry.

He will carry us through.

What does this mean for us?

Don’t be happy with evil but be happy in a faithful God.

In this season of lockdown, it’s a good opportunity for us to re-evaluate what’s valuable to us.

It’s a good opportunity to ask ourselves what makes us happy and why.

In our lockdown, what have we missed the most? Why have we missed it?

In our lockdown, have we not missed something that we should miss?

What godly things aren’t we missing that we should be missing?

Why aren’t we missing these things? Why don’t we yearn for these things, as we should?

Ask God this week to search our hearts with us.

Ask God to help us see what makes us happy.

Ask God to transform our hearts so that we won’t find our happiness in evil, but to find our happiness in him.

Ask God to open our eyes so that we’ll see how good and faithful he is so that we might fix our eyes on him, lest we should wander away and fall.

Friends, don’t be happy with evil but be happy in a faithful God.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What evil thing am I tempted to find my happiness in?

What am I more vulnerable to? Idol worship and sexual immorality? Or testing God and complaining against him?

How has God been faithful even when I’ve been faithless?

Friends, don’t be happy with evil but be happy in a faithful God.

Forgive us when we pursue happiness in other gods, in sexual immorality, in testing you and in complaining. Forgive us when we pursue happiness by craving evil things. Please transform our hearts by your Holy Spirit, that we might keep running the race that you’ve marked out for us. Please help us find our happiness in Christ and in Christ alone.
