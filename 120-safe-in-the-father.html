A special welcome to all the fathers who are at Church today!

I’m so glad you can join us this morning!

Hope you a wonderful Father’s Day!

One of the most unique features of Christianity is the fact that we call God our Father.

The Muslims don’t call God Father.

The Buddhists don’t call Buddha Father.

But as Christians, we celebrate God as our Father.

We haven’t simply decided by ourselves to God our Father.

Rather, it’s Jesus himself, the Son of God, who invites us to call God our Father.

We can only call God our Father because, Jesus, God’s one and only Son, invites his followers to do the same.

This relationship God the Father and God the Son is by far the most famous and most important Father-Son relationship in history.

There is no other Father-Son relationship that remotely compares!

It’s no exaggeration to say that the fate of our world and the destiny of all mankind depends on the relationship between the Father and the Son.

Why is this Father-Son relationship so important?

What does this Father-son relationship has to do with me?

That’s what we’re going to see in today’s passage.

If you have your Bible there, please open up to John 10, beginning from verse 22.

We’re going to see three things:

The Father and the Son keep Jesus’ own safe

The Father lives in the Son and the Son in the Father

Be a faithful witness of the Son

Before we do, let’s pray, “Father, please open our eyes so that we can see the beauty and glory of the relationship you have with your Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

The Father and the Son keep Jesus’ own safe

The Bible tells us that on a Winter’s Day, some of the Jews surrounded Jesus and asked, “How long are you going to keep us in suspense? If you are the Messiah, tell us plainly.”

It’s possible that some might of them might have genuinely wanted to know whether Jesus was the Messiah, the Chosen One of God.

But more realistically, it was probably their attempt to trap Jesus in his words, as they’ve been doing all this time.

That seems to be what Jesus was thinking too.

Look with me at verses 25-26, “I did tell you and you don’t believe, ”Jesus answered them. “The works that I do in my Father’s name testify about me.26 But you don’t believe because you are not of my sheep”

The reason they didn’t believe in Jesus wasn’t because there was a lack of evidence.

In both words and works, Jesus has provided plenty of evidence to show that He’s from God and that He is the Messiah, the Chosen One.

Let’s see if you can remember what Jesus has said about himself.

Jesus has said that I am the “Bread of Life” “The Light of the World” “Gate” “Good Shepherd”

Jesus has healed the blind, miraculously fed thousands of people, made the paralysed able to walk again.

It wasn’t because the evidence wasn’t there nor because the evidence wasn’t clear enough.

It was because they were not his sheep.

Since they were not Jesus’ sheep, since they didn’t belong to Jesus, they didn’t believe in Jesus.

In contrast, those who were his sheep know his voice.

Jesus knows them; they are his.

And they follow him.

Their belief in Jesus and their willingness to follow him show that they belong to him.

That is, belief is a sign of something far deeper.

Belief is a sign of whether you belong to Jesus or not.

I remember when we used to collect our children after they finished their Bible lessons at a Christian conference.

The leaders would take the children to an enclosed area and the fathers and mothers would come and collect them.

Though it can be quite chaotic, it worked remarkably well!

That’s because the children know their parents’ voice.

When the parents call, they would follow the voice and the fathers and mothers would collect them.

Jesus’ followers know Jesus’ voice.

They recognise Jesus’ voice, and they follow him.

For those who do follow him, Jesus will give them eternal life and they will never perish.

They will have a life that never fades away.

And more than that, Jesus promises that no one will snatch them out of his hand.

And not just Jesus’ hand; Jesus also promises that no one is able to snatch them out of the Father’s hand, the Father who is greater than all.

That’s the assurance that followers of Jesus, those who are Jesus’ own, can have.

Their life, their eternal life, is secure in the hands of God the Father and of God the Son.

This gives Christians an assurance for at least two reasons.

Our eternal life does not depend on our performance.

As Christians, we know that we’re not perfect.

We fail God daily, in our words, in our deeds and in our thoughts.

But we thank God that our eternal life does not depend on how good we are.

Rather, it all depends on how good and how kind God the Father and God the Son are.

Our security does not depend on how tight we hold onto God.

Rather, it depends on how tight God holds on to us.

Our eternal life does not depend on external pressures

As Christians, we will face pressure from the world to stop following Jesus.

A few nights ago, Professors Zehavit Gross and Suzanne Rutland published their decade-long study on SRE in the major faiths in Australia in 2021

Their ten-year study shows that around one quarter of all religious students in government schools suffer verbal and physical bullying for their faith.

Many of our children will find it very hard to openly follow Jesus without any pressure from their peers.

And it’s not just children who find it difficult; adult Christians in Australia will find it hard to openly talk about God.

And that’s not including the hardship and persecutions that many Christians face from all around the world.

However, though our world might pressure Christians to leave Christ, even though they may forcefully try to remove us from Christ,

The world will fail, for no one will snatch us out of Jesus’ hand.

No one is able to snatch us from the Father’s hand.

Christianity cannot be destroyed.

Friends, if you find your faith failing, don’t look for strength within yourself.

Find your strength in the Lord.

Find comfort in the words of Jesus.

Know that you are safe in his hands.

The Father lives in the Son and the Son in the Father

Jesus says no one can snatch those who belong to him from his hand.

And no one can snatch them out of the Father’s hand.

Well, whose hand are we in? The Son’s or the Father’s?

Jesus answers this question with one of the most profound statement about God the Father and himself ever recorded:

“I and the Father are one.”

Jesus is not just saying they’re one in action, like they’re united for a common course.

The Father and the Son are one; they identify completely with one another.

Jesus has referred to himself on numerous occasions in ways that the Bible has ever only used to describe God.

Jesus saw identifies himself with God.

That’s how the Jews understood Jesus.

And so they were ready to stone Jesus, for to them, Jesus has committed blasphemy.

They believed that Jesus, a mere man, identified himself as God.

It’s a little like this.

Over the past few years, I’ve seen many hacked into someone’s profile and pretended to be somebody else.

These hackers would lie about someone’s identity in order to scam money from you.

Has anyone else hear about these?

That’s what the Jews thought Jesus was doing.

They thought that Jesus was blaspheming by lying about being God.

Look with me at verse 33, ““We aren’t stoning you for a good work,” the Jews answered, “but for blasphemy, because you—being a man—make yourself God.”

However, they got it completely wrong.

Jesus didn’t make himself God; he was always God.

He’s always been God.

Rather, if anything, Jesus, being God, made himself man.

We see this in John 1:14 “The Word became flesh and dwelt among us.”

How is Jesus the real deal?

How is he truly not blaspheming or lying when he says that he and the Father are one?

Jesus responds in three parts.

Firstly, Jesus appeals to the law.

Look with me at verses 34 “Jesus answered them, “Isn’t it written in your law, I said, you are gods?”

Here, Jesus quotes Psalm 82:6. We’ll read both verses 6-7.

It reads, “I said, “You are gods; you are all sons of the Most High. 7 However, you will die like humans and fall like any other ruler.”

Here, the Bible refers to human beings, people, as gods.

It’s not saying that the humans became actual gods but that they were given the highest honours, being treated as if they were the sons of the Most High.

Jesus argues that if the Scripture is true, and it is, since it cannot be broken,

Then people have been addressed as gods in God’s word, the Bible.

Why should they be surprised if Jesus refers to himself as the Son of God?

In fact, this psalm sees its fulfilment ultimately in Jesus, who is the man who is God.

What we see as a description of the highest honour becomes a reality in the Person of Jesus, the one who can rightfully be called God.

So firstly, Jesus appeals to the law.

Second, Jesus appeals to his works.

Jesus has done many miracles; he has performed many signs.

Believe these good works that Jesus describes as his Father’s work.

It has God’s fingerprint all over them, as it were.

Thirdly, Jesus points to the very nature, the very personhood of God.

Here, Jesus has here in this passage a second profound statement about his relationship with God the Father.

Look with me at verse 38, “But if I am doing them and you don’t believe me, believe the works. This way you will know and understand that the Father is in me and I in the Father.”

If you believe the words and works of Jesus, then you will know and understand this incredible and glorious truth about God:

The Father is in the Son and the Son is in the Father.

That is, the God whom Christians believe, is a mutually-indwelling God.

The Father lives in the Son, and the Son lives in the Father.

You can’t separate the two of them at all.

You can’t take the Father away from the Son nor can you take the Son away from the Father.

They mutually live in one another.

That doesn’t mean that they are the same person.

They are two different Persons, but they’re described as one

There’s one God, not two.

But the two of them live in one another.

God the Father lives in God the Son, God the Son lives in God the Father.

I remember in my conversations with some young Muslims, one of them say that Christians believe in more than one God, God the Father, God the Son, and God the Spirit.

But I pointed this aspect of God to them.

I told him that there is one God, but the three Persons of God live in one another.

They are not different personalities; they are not different aspects of the one God.

Rather, God the Father, God the Son and God the Spirit are three distinct Persons, mutually living in one another.

That’s how Jesus describes who God is.

God the Father living in the Son and God the Son living in the Father.

And of course, later on, Jesus will talk about how the Holy Spirit lives in them as well.

And so, one God, Three Persons, mutually living in one another.

Now that can be very difficult for us to understand.

What does it mean for God to mutually live in one another? How does that work?

Since we don’t see that happen in nature, it’s very hard for us to understand it.

We can try to use different illustrations, but they will almost certainly fail to describe God.

For now, let’s take Jesus at his word and thank God that he’s revealed a glimpse of who he is, the triune God who lives mutually in one another.

Be a faithful witness of the Son

The Jews certainly didn’t get what Jesus was saying and they tried to seize Jesus.

Jesus escaped their grasp and went to where John was previously baptising.

By this point of the story, John had already died.

As Jesus continued to speak his word and to do his work in the region, the people who previously heard John remembered what John had said about Jesus.

As a result, many had come to believe in Jesus there.

John was like a farmer who had sown the seeds and then went away afterwards.

It wasn’t until after he was gone that the seeds began to grow.

Listen to how the people describe John’s ministry, in verse 41, “ Many came to him and said, “John never did a sign, but everything John said about this man was true.”

John didn’t do any spectacular signs, but he did speak truthfully about Jesus.

In fact, everything he said about Jesus was true.

While it’s good to see supernatural miracles from God, God doesn’t need to use them to bring people to faith in Jesus.

Instead, for the vast majority of the time, God uses ordinary people who speak truthfully about Jesus to bring people to faith in Jesus.

Don Carson is one of the most respected New Testament scholars for the past fifty years.

Many people from all around the world know about Don Carson.

But not many people know about Tom Carson, Don’s father.

Tom Carson was an ordinary pastor, not spectacular at all.

He didn’t have a lot of people in his ministry, but he spoke about Jesus truthfully and faithfully.

Don Carson wrote a book about his father and reminds pastor that, “the God of Augustine, Calvin, Spurgeon, and Piper is no less the God of Tom Carson, and of you and me”

We might not have spectacular ministries, we might not do miracles, but may everything, everything, we say about Jesus be true.

And may people come to Christ because of what we say about him.

And so, what does this Father-son relationship between God the Father and Jesus has to do with me?

If you believe God the Son and what he says about the relationship he has with the Father, you will have eternal life.

You will be secure.

No one can snatch you out of the Son’s hand and no one can snatch you out of the Father’s hand.

You will come to know the God who is One God in Three Persons.

You will know him as the Father who lives in the Son and the Son who lives in the Father.

You will speak truthfully about Jesus, and through what you say, by God’s grace and kindness, others will come to believe Jesus and have eternal life also.

And so, friends, this Father’s Day, remember what Jesus’ said, “No one will snatch them out of my hand.”

There can be no greater assurance than to know that no one is able to snatch us out of Jesus’ hands.

And so, find your security in Jesus.

Don’t judge your security base on your performance.

If you perform poorly, you might fall into despair and doubt your salvation in Christ.

If you perform well, you might become proud and think that you are entitled to be saved in Christ.

No, don’t rest your security in your performance but on what Jesus has already done for you.

As the Son who lives in the Father, and in whom the Father lives, Jesus holds you in his hand.

It’s a message too good to keep to ourselves.

Let’s be a church known for the truth that we proclaim.

My prayer is that this will how our speech and our deeds will be described when someone sees our Church, “St Paul’s Kogarah has never done a sign, but everything the Church said about Jesus is true”!

Here are the three questions for this week:

If your life should fall apart, where would you go to find your security?

How does Jesus say about his relationship with the Father shape the way we understand God?

Friends, as we celebrate the Father’s Day, may we celebrate our heavenly Father and for the greatest gift that he’s given us, his only Son our Lord.

May we remember that for those who believe in Jesus, he’s made this promise, “No one will snatch them out of my hand.”

Let’s pray, “Father, we thank you for holding us secure in your hand. We thank you that Jesus holds us secure in his hand. May we find our security in you alone. This week, may someone around us hear us speak truthfully about Jesus. Be merciful Lord, and save someone around us this week. This we pray in the name of Jesus, Amen.”
