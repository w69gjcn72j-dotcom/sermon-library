God With Us

Matthew 1:21-23 · Christmas 2020
Big IdeaIn Jesus, the Saviour and Immanuel, God promises to save us and be with us.

Walking with God

Passage: 1 John 1:5-10

Big Idea: Our claim to be in fellowship with God, who is light, must be tested by our walk and by our confession of sin.

God is Light

The holiness of God

The sinfulness of Man

Fellowship with God is shown through the way we walk

Liars walk in darkness

Cleansed ones walk in the light

Fellowship with God is shown through our confession

Liars don’t confess sins

Confessors are forgiven and cleansed by the holy God

Last week, we began our series in the letter of 1 John.

We saw that that John and the rest of the apostles told people about the Gospel because they want them to have fellowship with them and with God.

To have fellowship is to have something common or to share something in common.

What does that look like?

Before we look at what the Bible says, we need to know what it doesn’t look like.

There’s a mistake that we often make when we think about God and how we relate to God.

Our problem is that we start by thinking about God from our end.

We look at ourselves and we think that God is like us, only bigger.

We think of ourselves as a nice person, and so, God must be a nicer, even the nicest person.

We think of ourselves as a fairly kind and generous person, and so, God must be the kindest and most generous person.

The danger with this is that we simply think of God as an amplified version of ourselves.

We dial up our qualities to the highest factor and call that person God.

In biblical terms, this is what we call idols, a god that we make in our own image.

The same thing can happen when we think of our relationships with God.

Jesus call his disciples his friends, and so, my relationship with Jesus is like my relationship with my friends, only better.

The Bible calls God our Father and so, my relationship with God is like the relationship I have with my Father.

For some people, the experience may be good; for others, it may be not so good.

But here again, the problem lies with thinking about our relationship with God from our end.

Rather than beginning with us, the Apostle John begins our thinking about God from God’s end.

What does God say about himself and his relationship with us?

God is Light

Open your Bible to 1 John 1:5

“This is the message we have heard from him and declare to you: God is light, and there is absolutely no darkness in him.”

God is Light, and there is absolutely no darkness in him.

That’s the message that the Apostle John gives us.

God is Light.

Light can be used to as a metaphor for many things.

Light can mean enlightenment – “I see the light, I understand now!”

Light can also mean life – “I was in a dark place but now I’ve been rescued, I’ve been brought to the light, a better place”

While they both can be true in general, in the context of 1 John, Light seems to be pointing to the holiness of God.

God is Light, God is Holy; He has no darkness, he is not tainted.

In 1 Timothy 6:16, God is described as the one who lives in unapproachable light.

God is utterly pure in his holiness; he cannot even look upon sin.

God is absolutely pure in his righteousness and absolutely perfect in his justice.

Contrast this to the sinfulness of man.

Man is made in God’s image; God’s put us in a highly elevated place among all that he’s made.

And yet, man is not content.

We want to be God; we want to take his place.

That’s what sin is. Sin is not treating God, not honouring God, not loving God in the way that we should.

This desire to take God’s place is at the heart of all other sins.

How I relate to God, how I see God, will have an impact on all other areas of my life.

If I have a warped view of God, if I have a corrupt relationship with God, it will lead to a warped view of man and it will lead to corrupt relationship with other people.

That’s who we are in our nature; we’re enemies of a holy God.

He is Light; we are darkness.

That’s what the Bible teaches about sin.

But it’s a very unpopular thing to talk about, not only in Bible times but also in today’s world.

People today don’t want to hear about sin; they don’t want to hear about their darkness.

They want to hear that God is loving and kind and that God is pleased with them, because they are loving and kind, so they think.

They want to hear about a God who can’t wait for to be their best friend.

They want to hear a God that they’ve made in their own image.

The danger with that is that it grossly overestimates our worth and it outright underestimates the holiness of God.

They think that they can approach the God who lives in Unapproachable Light.

But that’s the whole point, God lives in unapproachable light – we can’t get there.

It’s like coming into an operating theatre.

Hopefully, you don’t have to do this much, unless you’re a Doctor, a nurse or some other medical personnel.

When you go into an operating theatre, many steps are taken to prevent germs from getting in.

You need to remove all jewellery, wash your hands with antimicrobial soap, wear the scrubs (special clothes for the medical personnel) and sterilise all the tools before the surgery.

All these have to be done to prevent contamination of the theatre.

When we come before God, we come before someone who is far purer, far cleaner than any operating theatre.

However, we carry with us contaminated hearts, contaminated minds, contaminated lives that have no place in God’s presence.

Unlike the operating theatre, our contamination is not a threat to God.

Rather, our contamination in the presence of a holy God is a threat to us.

God’s holiness will kill us.

God is perfect in his holiness and righteousness.

Sin has no place in his presence.

Since all of us are sinners from birth, his holiness will condemn us rightly to our death.

God is light and he won’t stand our darkness.

Just as light chases darkness away, God’s light will expose our darkness.

As sinners, we have no right to be in the presence of God.

As darkness, we can’t stand in the presence of the Light of God.

2. Fellowship with God is shown through the way we walk

What does light and darkness have in common?

Nothing. They don’t belong together.

That’s the emphasis that lies behind 1 John 1:6

“If we say, “We have fellowship with him,” and yet we walk in darkness, we are lying and are not practicing the truth.”

To be in fellowship is like seeing a group of people who are walking together.

Around election times, many of our political leaders walk together to show their unity.

You see them talking together, smiling, working for a better future.

Or closer to home, you might see a couple walking together.

They walk slowly, maybe even hand-in-hand, as they spend the afternoon together.

That’s what fellowship looks like.

People who are in fellowship are walking in sync.

What does it mean, then to have fellowship with the utterly holy God?

It means that we must walk in a way that is in sync with the utterly holy God.

If we’re to have fellowship with God, then the way we live, the way we behave must reflect his holiness.

In China, police are using a new form of surveillance tool called ‘gait recognition’.

Rather than recognising a person’s face or their finger prints, there are programmes which analyse a person’s shadows and create a model of the way a person walks.

By looking at how a person walks, the police can identify a person even if they are up to 50 metres away.

If you claim to have fellowship with God, the way you walk must show that you are walking with God.

The way you live must match the way God lives.

The way you walk must match the way God walks.

But therein lies the problem: we don’t walk the way God walks.

We’re utterly sinful people and we live sinful lives.

We aren’t holy like God; we aren’t perfect like God.

We walk according to darkness, not light.

And so, if we live according to darkness and claim to have fellowship with God, then we are lying and we are not practicing the truth.

This is the first lie that our passage exposes.

It’s the lie that we tell others about us having fellowship with God while still walking in the darkness.

Now notice that the people who say these things would call themselves Christians.

Christians are people who claim to have fellowship with God, and rightly so.

However, people who call themselves Christians and yet walk in the darkness are liars.

There are many types of Christians likes these.

Some are called Sunday Christians.

They come to Church on Sundays, say the right prayers, sing the right hymns.

But then they do whatever they want throughout the week.

They pretend to walk in the light on Sunday mornings and they start walking in darkness sometimes as early as Sunday afternoons.

That’s the Sunday Christians.

There are others who are called nominal Christians.

These are Christians who tick the Christian box in the census but only comes to the Church on special occasions like Christmas and Easter.

They may identify themselves as Christians but for most of their lives, they live in darkness.

The Apostle John doesn’t hold back; he calls these Sunday Christians and nominal Christians liars.

They claim to be in fellowship with God and yet walk in the darkness.

However, thankfully, not all Christians are liars.

Look with me at verse 7, “If we walk in the light as he himself is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin.”

If we walk in the light as God is in the light, then we have fellowship with one another.

When we walk in the light, when we have true fellowship with God, we’ll have true fellowship with one another.

We walk in unity because we are all walking in fellowship with God.

It’s like the marching band and their drum major.

Have you ever seen a marching band?

In a marching band, people with many different instruments march and play their instruments together.

They all follow the direction of the Drum Major.

The job of the Drum Major is to conduct the band by marching and playing the drums.

When the rest of the band follows the Drum Major, they all march in unity.

That’s what happens when genuine Christians walk in the light.

Genuine Christians obey and follow God’s teaching.

They live their lives and behave in a way that reflect what God’s will.

They care for the sick because God cares for the sick.

They help those in need because God is concerned with those in need.

Jesus says that they are like a city on a hill which can’t be hidden.

Jesus says, “In the same way, let your light shine before others, so that they may see your good works and give glory to your Father in heaven.”

When we live our lives, obeying God, doing what pleases him, shining out his light, others will give glory to God our Father.

That’s what walking in the light looks like.

It’s a life of obedience to God’s will.

To bring this to something that’s close to home, many of you would’ve received my message about a bill that’s about to be debated in the parliament.

It’s a bill that allows for abortion of babies, theoretically, even up to the day of delivery.

It’s a bill that had come into the parliament suddenly without public consultation.

As Christians, we must speak for those who can’t speak for themselves.

We must speak for the unborn babies, who aren’t able to speak or scream inside the womb.

In a while, we will pray for this.

If you’re able, would you please call your MP in your electorate and voice your concern?

We live in a dark world, and as Christians, we must walk in the light and protect the innocent, as God would have us.

God is Light; if have fellowship with God, we must walk in the light.

Also, if we have fellowship with God, we will confess our sins.

Fellowship with God is shown through our confession

Look with me at the second part of verse 7,

If we walk in the light, if we have fellowship with God, “the blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin.”

If we walk in the light, it shows that God has cleansed away our sin by the blood of Jesus.

It shows that God has cleansed us and qualified us to have fellowship with him.

However, before we’re cleansed by the blood of Jesus, we must avoid another lie that we keep telling ourselves.

Look with me at verse 8, “If we say, “We have no sin,” we are deceiving ourselves, and the truth is not in us.”

In verses 5-7, the contrast is between light and darkness.

In verses 6-8, the contrast is between those who claimed that they have not sinned and those who have confessed their sin.

Now notice the contrast is not between those who have sinned and those who have not sinned.

The Bible tells us clearly that we’re all sinners and that we have all walked in darkness.

All of us were born in darkness; all of us were born as sinners.

Romans 3:23 tells us that, “For all have sinned and fall short of the[ glory of God.”

We’re sinners by default.

If we think that we have not sinned, we’re deceiving ourselves.

That’s the second lie that this passage exposes

The first lie is the person who claims to be in fellowship with God and yet walk in darkness.

It’s a lie he tells others.

The second lie is the person who claims to have no sin.

It’s a lie that he tells himself.

If we say that we have no sin then we don’t understand ourselves, we don’t understand the darkness within us.

If we say we have no sin, we lie to ourselves and the truth is not in us.

The problem lies within us; we are the problem.

The way to solve the problem of sin is not to hide it but to confess it.

Those who confess their sins are those who recognise their sinfulness and acknowledge them before God.

When we confess our sins, we see the beauty of the gospel message.

Look with me at verse 9, “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and righteous to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”

This is what makes the Message of Jesus the Good New!

When we confess our sins, God the Light, the Holy One, is faithful and righteous to forgive us our sins and cleanses us from all unrighteousness.

God is faithful – he will keep the promises he made to us in Jesus.

God is righteous – he has already paid for our sins in the Lord Jesus.

We belong in the darkness but when we confess our sins, God forgives us, cleanses us and brings us safely into his light.

That’s the paradox of the Christian faith.

In order to have fellowship with the light, we must confess our darkness.

To come into the light, we mustn’t hide our sins, we mustn’t deceive others and we mustn’t deceive ourselves.

To come into the light, we must confess our sins, admit that we’re wrong before God, turn to Jesus, trust in him and walk in the light.

The reformer Martin Luther was reputed to have described the entrance of a Cathedral which had statutes of saints on one side of the entrance and statues of sinners on the other side.

The statutes of the saints appear to be serene and tranquil while the statues of the sinners appear down-hearted and defeated.

Luther says the reality is the reverse of what these statues represent.

The down-hearted and defeated statues ought to describe the saints as they come before God in humility.

They are the ones who confess their sins and will have them forgiven before God.

On the other hand, the serene and tranquil statues ought to describe the sinners because they don’t recognise their sins before God and they fail to confess them before God.

Having deceived themselves, they will remain in the darkness.

Those who hide their darkness are lying to themselves.

Those who confess their darkness, who bring their darkness before God will come into God’s light.

Those who are honest before God with their sins will be forgiven and cleansed.

In our Church, we have corporate confession every Sunday.

Every Sunday, we corporately acknowledge that we’re sinners before God.

While it’s a good thing, please don’t let it become automatic.

Don’t just say the words and don’t mean it.

Come before God, recognise your sins and ask God to forgive you.

Sometimes it is hard to think of sins when you say the corporate prayers on Sundays.

To give yourself more time to think through about how you can genuinely confess your sins to God, confess your sins to another Christian brother or sister that you trust, in your Bible study or in your Christian circle of friends.

Be honest with them; tell them, “Sister, can you pray for me? This is the sin I’m struggling with.”

And of course, confess your sins daily before God.

Ask God to forgive the sins you’ve committed against him, either straight after you’ve committed it or confess them at the end of the day.

Be honest with God, confess your sins and God will forgive you your sins.

If you refuse to believe that you’re a sinner, if you refuse to admit that you’ve sinned against God, then you make God out to be a liar.

Look with me at verse 10, “If we say, “We have not sinned,” we make him a liar, and his word is not in us.”

That’s the third lie that’s exposed.

The first lie is the one we tell others: that we fellowship with God even though we walk in darkness.

The second lie is the one we tell ourselves: that we have no sin.

The third lie is the lie we tell about God: God is a liar; we have not sinned.

Sadly, most people around the world believe the third lie.

They would rather believe that God is a liar then to admit that they’re sinners.

Friends, don’t buy that lie; don’t walk in the darkness.

Confess your sins before God, be honest with God and he’ll bring you safely into the light.

He’s sent Jesus so that through his blood, by his death, you can be forgiven and cleansed.

Come and have fellowship with God in Jesus Christ.

Come and walk with God.

The three questions I have for us this week are these:

God is light. What darkness has his word exposed in my life?

What sin do we find hard to confess before God?

How can I walk closer with God this week?

Let’s pray, “Father, you are Light, in you there is no darkness at all. Father, you know our darkness. We can fool everyone, we can even fool ourselves, but we cannot fool you. Father, we thank you that even though you know our deepest, darkest darkness, you still love us. You love us enough to send your only Son to die for us. Because of Jesus, we can now come before you and ask for forgiveness. Father, forgive us. Cleanse us, and by your Spirit, let us walk in the light, as you are in the light. We pray this in Jesus’ name. Amen.”
