Good morning and welcome back to St Paul’s Kogarah.

If I haven’t met you yet, my name is David Yung, the Senior Minister of St Paul’s Kogarah.

Today, we’re currently going through a series of talks on Thanksgiving.

The first one was given by Bishop Michael Stead, who reminded us to thank God for the conversion of believers, especially those who have demonstrated genuine faith.

The second one was given last week by our friend Arya, who reminded us to not love and thank God with empty word but with genuine, costly service to our Lord Jesus.

Today, we’re going to look at how we can possibly repay the Lord for all the good the has done for us.

For many of us from a Western background, we might think that it’s a strange concept to repay the LORD.

After all, he is the gracious and loving God; of course, he wants to do good to us!

Why would we need to repay him?

In fact, I suspect most of us in the West would think that we’re entitled to receive goodness from God,

and if God doesn’t give us good things, like wealth and health, then he’s in the wrong.

But for those of us from a Chinese background, many of us understand that it is a good thing to repay someone who has done good to you.

When someone’s given you a gift, it’s good to give a gift back.

In fact, there’s a very strong obligation to give a gift back.

It’s not just a nice thing to do; it’s something that you must do.

It’s how we establish ‘guanxi’ or ‘relationship’.

If you don’t repay the gift or the favour, you may damage your guanxi with the giver.

Sometimes, a gift is just a gift, but other times, when someone brings you a gift, especially if it’s a person you don’t know that well, you have to think hard.

Do you accept the gift and establish guanxi with them? Or do you not accept the gift in the first place because you want to keep your distance? Or do you accept the gift and give something equivalent back?

It can get complicated.

Which brings us back to today’s passage, especially verse 12: How can I repay the LORD for all the good he has done for me?

Do I accept the good God has done to me because I think I’m entitled to it?

Or do I repay it with a gift? Can I repay God with a gift?

Please open your Bible to Psalm 116.

In today’s passage, we’ll see two things:

1. Calling on the LORD for salvation and for thanksgiving

2. Repaying the LORD with love and vows

How can I repay the LORD? Before we look into it, let’s pray:

“Father, thank you for all the goodness that you give to us in Christ. By your Holy Spirit, please open the eyes of our hearts as we look into your words. Let us come before you with thanks, for we pray in Jesus’ name, Amen.”

1. Calling on the LORD for salvation and for thanksgiving

Psalm 116:1-2, “I love the LORD because he has heard my appeal for mercy. Because he has turned his ear to me, I will call out to him as long as I live.”

We don’t have the back story, but the Psalmist who wrote this psalm was in some sort of trouble and he called out to the LORD to save him.

The LORD has heard his cry and rescued him.

As a result, he loves the LORD.

He loves the LORD because God has rescued him when he cried out to God for help.

And so, he will now keep crying out to God, keep calling out to him, for as long as he lives.

The first time he cried out to God was to ask God to rescue him; it was as plea for mercy.

The second time he cried out to God was to praise God for rescuing him.

But it’s also possible that he’ll keep crying out to God for rescue whenever he gets into trouble because the LORD has rescued him before.

I think it’s both; he keeps calling out to God, for both mercy and for praise, for as long as he lives.

The psalmist said that the ropes of death were wrapped around him, strangling him.

The torments of Sheol, or the underworld, overcame him, literally found him.

And he encountered, literally found, trouble and sorrow.

He was in a very bad place in his life.

And so, he prayed, no, he desperately cried out to God, “LORD, save me!”

He would’ve known from reading the Bible that the LORD is gracious and righteous, that God is compassionate.

But now, he has experienced God’s grace, righteousness and compassion for himself.

God has rescued him; God has saved him.

He was inexperienced; he didn’t know how to handle the bad situation he was in.

He was helpless and God saved him.

It would’ve been like what happened to a 14 year old boy earlier this year.

A 14 year old boy was at a beach with his parents in a place called Anderby Creek when the sand underneath his feet suddenly gave way.

What the boy didn’t know was that the sand he was standing on was only a thin layer with a massive hole beneath it.

Only his head was visible above the sand.

It was a good thing his parents were nearby! As soon as they saw what happened, they dug the sand away from the son’s mouth and cried for help, as they waited for the fire fighters to dig the boy out.

The boy would not have survived if there was no one there to rescue him.

The psalmist was like the boy who fell into the hole.

He was utterly helpless and he cried out to the LORD to save him.

And the LORD did!

As a result, the psalmist can rest.

Look with me at verses 7-8, “Return to your rest, my soul, for the LORD has been good to you. For you, LORD, rescued me from death, my eyes from tears, my feet from stumbling.”

The psalmist would’ve been dead, but God saved him so that he can continue living.

The psalmist would’ve wept and sobbed but God rescued him from his tears.

The psalmist would’ve stumbled and fallen but the LORD rescued him so that he can walk in the land of the living.

His life will never be the same again.

He will walk before the LORD, he will walk in the presence of the one who saved him.

After experiencing his trauma, he can say what he said in verses 9-10, “I believed, even when I said, “I am severely oppressed.” In my alarm I said, “Everyone is a liar.”

What does he mean when he says that everyone is a liar?

While it’s true that everyone lies sometimes, I think the Psalmist means something deeper than that.

In the context of the psalm, he’s talking about how God saved him when he cried out to God.

The focus of the psalm is that God is the One who is both able and reliable to rescue the Psalmist.

Every man, without God, may offer all kinds of rescue.

They will make promises about how the man can be rescued, perhaps how he can save himself, or how he can be saved by some other means.

But all these offers, all these promises, are lies.

None of what man promises can truly save a man’s soul, can give a man the rest that he seeks.

Only the LORD can do that; only the LORD can rescue.

Like the psalmist, we Christians know that we too were helpless in our sin.

Death was chasing us, literally day by day, for each day we move one day close to our death.

And each day, we see the effect of our own sin on the world, as well as the sins of the world on our lives.

But in the LORD Jesus, God has saved us from sin and death.

When we call on the LORD Jesus, we were saved.

When we pray to LORD Jesus, desperately knowing that all men are liars and he’s the only way, he saves us.

Look with me at Acts 16:30-31,

“He escorted them out and said, “Sirs, what must I do to be saved?” They said, “Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved—you and your household.”

Only the LORD Jesus can save and he will save when you call on him.

And don’t think you can come to him half-heartedly, thinking that you can trick Jesus.

Those who come to him must come wholeheartedly.

All men are liars, and we’re prone to lie, even when we come before Jesus.

But we must come before him genuinely and truthfully because we would be fools if we think we can fool Jesus.

Those who genuinely recognise their need to be saved will be desperate.

They will cry out to God for rescue, asking God, “Please, LORD, save me.”

As a people, as a Church, we pray to God, but I’m afraid that we’re so used to praying that we’re not desperate in our prayers anymore.

We pray but we don’t expect any answers; we pray but we don’t think it will make any difference.

But to cry to the LORD for salvation is a desperate prayer, a prayer that’s urgent and expects longingly, humbly, for an answer.

Praying to the LORD for salvation is like a man banging on a door when you’re trapped, or when someone is sinking in the sand.

It’s calling for someone, anyone to hear, anyone to save.

It’s supposed to be desperate because it’s urgent.

May we have the same desperateness to pray for salvation, either our own or someone’s.

For anyone, anyone, without the LORD Jesus is one heartbeat away from eternal damnation.

Be desperate in your prayers.

Don’t waste your prayers. Make the most of it, God hears.

And so, pray, call upon the name of the Lord, for yourself and your loved ones.

Pray for your unbelieving husband, your unbelieving wife.

Pray for your unbelieving son, your unbelieving daughter.

Call on the name of the Lord!

And when you’re saved, don’t stop, but keep calling on the name of the Lord! Praise Him, love him, for he has heard your cry for mercy.

2. Repaying the LORD with love and vows

How do we respond to God for hearing our cry for mercy?

We repay the LORD with love and vows.

Look with me at verse 12 again, “How can I repay the LORD for all the good he has done for me?”

How can I repay the LORD? How can it possibly be done?

There’s a sense of course that it cannot possibly be done.

There’s nothing we can do to fully repay what the LORD has done for us.

Even if we give all of our lives to the LORD, it will still be a tiny drop in the bucket compared to all that the LORD has done for us.

Does that mean that we don’t do anything?

It’s a little like this:

As a child grows, the parents provide various things to provide for a child’s needs.

Things like a safe place to live in, food to eat, schools to go to, clothes to wear, and so on.

However, it doesn’t take long, usually before the child turns two, for the child to know that there are things that they want, which they don’t need.

It might be their favourite food, for example, and they will only have that food and nothing else.

Or it might be their favourite toy, and they will cry, throw tantrums, scream, and shout until they get that toy.

Parents, you know what I’m talking about.

And for some of us, especially if we have grandparents living with us, will be tempted to just give them whatever they want, as long as they stop their cries.

What happens as they grow older? They think they are entitled to the good things of life.

They think that they don’t need to work hard and take responsibilities for their actions, and they’ll get everything that they want.

That’s what their family has taught them; their family has taught them to be thankless and entitled.

In the end, they won’t do much good to the society, their community and even their family.

That’s what we are, thankless and entitled, if we don’t’ thank God for what he’s done for us in Jesus.

You know that God doesn’t have to save you, right?

He doesn’t have to send Jesus, paid the high cost of the death of his Son.

But God did it out of his great love and righteousness.

And we must be thankful for what he’s done.

We need to give genuine thanks; not giving thanks with empty words but genuine thanks from the heart which shows itself in love.

In the kids’ spot today, we saw Jesus’ parable of forgiveness.

Jesus says the woman who anointed Jesus and washed Jesus with her hair and her tears loved much, because she’s been forgiven much.

But the one who is forgiven little, loves little.

The woman couldn’t repay Jesus fully for what He’s done for her, but she gives as much as she could to show her love and thankfulness.

It was a public display of her love and thankfulness for Jesus.

We see this public display of love and thankfulness in the psalmist’s response as well.

He asks, “How can I repay the LORD?”

Look at his response in verses 13-14, “I will take the cup of salvation and call on the name of the LORD. I will fulfill my vows to the LORD in the presence of all his people.”

The psalmist publicly acknowledges that God has saved him.

God saved me! Let’s celebrate!

He performs some sort of ritual meal that involves drinks and meat.

The first part involves the drink – he takes the cup of salvation and call on the name of the Lord.

The second part involves a sacrifice of thanksgiving – he offers a thanksgiving sacrifice and call on the name of the LORD, he prays to God.

He will also fulfil his vows to the Lord.

Vows are formal promises or pledges that he makes to the LORD.

We don’t know what these vows are, but he makes them in light of what the Lord has done for him.

God’s been faithful to him and so he promises to be faithful to God.

Between these two ritual acts is the heart of his thankfulness.

Verses 15-16, “The death of his faithful ones is valuable in the LORD’s sight. LORD, I am indeed your servant; I am your servant, the son of your female servant. You have loosened my bonds.”

The psalmist is thankful to God because the death of his faithful ones are precious in the Lord’s sight.

Each death of his faithful one is precious because each life of his faithful one is precious.

We’re not just a number, but a person whom the LORD knows and loves deeply.

The LORD will do all that he can to save those who are His.

And he did that by sending Jesus, who came so that none of those who belong to Him will be lost.

Those who are saved now identify themselves as servants of the LORD.

A servant, and more than that, the son of the female servant, the maid.

God has loosened the ropes of death on the psalmist and brought him back to walk in his presence in the land of the living.

To express his thanks and to repay the LORD for what he has done, the psalmist calls to God in praise, making vows before him, and living as the servant, the son of the Lord’s maid.

We can’t repay the LORD for what he’s done for us in Jesus but that doesn’t mean that we don’t do anything either.

Those who have been saved would not be thankless and entitled.

Instead those genuinely saved would be humble servants of the LORD, doing whatever job, whatever ministry God has asked us to do,

And when we’ve done it, we will say we’re unworthy servants who have only done our duty (Luke 17:10).

When we give to God, we don’t do it to establish guanxi; we give because we’re unworthy servants who have been saved by the LORD.

There’s nothing we have or own that God needs; God owns everything that we have and he’s the one who has given everything to establish our relationship, our guanxi with him.

We will be loving servants who loves Jesus and will feed his sheep (John 21:17).

We will make vows before God, promising to love and serve him, promising to love and serve his people.

God has been faithful to us, in response, we want to be faithful to him.

We will be careful with what we promise before God, because we don’t want to lie to God.

We might start with something small but significant, like a promise to be at Church on Sunday.

Or a promise to be here on time so that you can serve others.

I have a friend who once came late to a Church meeting.

When he came, he said, “I said I’ll be here at 7:30pm but I didn’t. I’m sorry that I lied”.

I thought, wow, this man has integrity.

He doesn’t make excuses for himself, even for being late.

That’s a man that places faithfulness to God before his own personal honour; that’s a man that I can trust.

Can you start with a small promise before God?

What will you promise God in light of all that good that he’s done for you?

How will you repay the LORD for the good that he’s done for you?

We can’t repay God for what he’s done for us in Christ, but if we are saved, then we can’t help but give thanks to him by loving him, thanking him, calling out to him, serving him, making vows to serve him.

What good has God done for us?

If you are a Christian, then God has saved you from death to life, from stumbling in the darkness to walking in the light.

If you are a Christian, He’s saved you when you cried out to him.

And if you have been saved, you will be eternally grateful and will continue to call out to him as long as you live.

How can we repay the LORD for the good he has done for us?

Keep calling out to the Lord for salvation and praise – don’t stop praying to God.

Celebrate your salvation publicly – Don’t hide it.

Serve the Lord as the son of his maid.

Take the lowest job, the one with the least honour and serve the Lord gratefully, knowing how much he treasures you.

Thank him not with empty words, but with genuine faithfulness, making a vow to God that you will keep.

Don’t be a liar but be someone who promises truthfully.

And so, friends,

Love the Lord Jesus for he heard your cries, and thank him with truthful vows.

Maybe it’s the two areas of growth that you want to work on?

How are they going?

Our Lord is gracious and righteous, our God is compassionate.

He has saved us when we cried out to him.

And so, Love the Lord Jesus for he heard your cries, and thank him with truthful vows.

Friends, Love the Lord Jesus for he heard your cries, and thank him with truthful vows.

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you for hearing our cries to you. We love you, and we give you heartfelt thanks for all that you’ve done for us in Christ. Forgive us for our thanklessness. Forgive us for feeling entitled. Forgive us that we can ever pay you back adequately. By your Spirit, make us servants who delight in serving you, Make us more like Jesus, our Servant King, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
