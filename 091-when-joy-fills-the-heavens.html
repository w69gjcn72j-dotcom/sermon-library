When joy fills the heavens

Passage: Luke 15.1-7

Joy fills the heavens when one sinner repents, for Jesus has found and saved his lost sheep.

1. Is the one lost sheep worth it?

2. Jesus’ joy of bringing his lost home

3. What makes God celebrate

On Friday, we celebrated Ezekiel’s graduation from Year 12.

Ezekiel still has to finish his HSC exams, but he’s finished his academic year at school; he’s graduated!

And no, he’s not here this morning because he’s off to a Youth camp along with Caitlyn, Mikey and others in their Friday night Youth group.

He’s going straight to a study camp on Monday to prepare for is exams.

I was going through many of my friends’ post on Facebook and many of them celebrated the graduation of their children as well!

It was great seeing so many parents, Chinese, Indian, Australian, all celebrating the academic achievements of their children.

They celebrate because they know what their children had to overcome to get there.

It’s a great celebration to have; it’s a good thing to celebrate.

However, not all celebrations are necessarily good.

For example, when you’re in a game and your competitor suddenly gets sick, so that he abandons the game, it’s not a good thing to celebrate.

You shouldn’t say, “Yes, he just got sick at the right time. Now, I’ll win the match!”

The Germans have a word for this: Schadenfreude.

Schadenfreude describes your celebration at someone else’s misfortune.

When you celebrate schadenfreude, it shows that you’re not a kind person on the inside.

Celebrations are great, but what do celebrations say about you?

Today, we’re going to see what God celebrates and what that says about Him.

If you have your Bible there, please go to Luke 15, beginning from verse 1.

We’re going to see three things:

1. Is one lost sheep worth it?

2. Jesus’ joy of bringing his lost home

3. What makes God celebrate

Before we do, let’s pray, “Father of our Lord Jesus, the Great Shepherd of the Sheep, by your Holy Spirit please open the eyes of our hearts that we might see what brings joy to your heart. Let us long for that same joy, for we pray in the name of Jesus, Amen.”

1. Is one lost sheep worth it?

Look with me at Luke 15:1-2, “All the tax collectors and sinners were approaching to listen to him. 2 And the Pharisees and scribes were complaining, “This man welcomes sinners and eats with them.”

Tax collectors and sinners were drawn to Jesus’ teachings.

Now, these sinners were not upright citizens of the society.

They were the shady businessmen and the dodgy tradesmen of Jesus’ day.

You couldn’t trust these ‘sinners’; they were lost causes.

The Pharisees and scribes were the religious spokesmen of Jesus’ day, and they complained about Jesus eating with them.

Jesus welcomed them and ate with them; He was acting as if he was one of them!

Why would Jesus do that? Why would Jesus waste his time on these people?

In response, Jesus told them three parables.

They were the parables of the Lost Sheep, the Lost Coin and the Lost Son.

If you have time at home this week, you can read about the Parable of the Lost Coin and the Lost Son in the Gospel of Luke in the Bible.

If you don’t have a Bible, we have some with the reference marked out for you; please take one home!

We’re going to focus on the Parable of the Lost Sheep this morning.

Look with me at verses 3-4, “So he told them this parable: 4 “What man among you, who has a hundred sheep and loses one of them, does not leave the ninety-nine in the open field and go after the lost one until he finds it?”

I don’t know about you but when I read it, I have to ask myself, would I leave 99 behind for the one sheep?

Would you leave the 99 for the one lost sheep?

It seems to make a lot of sense to say ‘No’ doesn’t it?

There are 99 sheep already, and only one is lost.

It’s only a 1% loss; it’s bad but probably not worth our time looking for it.

In our neighbourhood here in Kogarah, we often find a Lost-Dog poster on the lampposts.

The owners of these dogs were worried because their dog went missing.

While some of them might have multiple dogs, in Kogarah, they probably can own only one.

It’s 100% of the animals that they own!

The costs to find their dog will involve making and printing the posters, putting these posters up, calling different agencies and they might even knock on the doors of the nieghrbourhood.

These owners might search for a few days, but they wouldn’t be able to keep searching for long.

If the dog is not found in a few days, they need to be ready for the fact that they might not see their dog again.

That’s a dog owner with one dog.

For the Shepherd in Jesus’ story, there’s a far higher cost, because he would have to walk through the dry wilderness of Israel, putting his own life at risk, and he could be gone for days.

Would the one sheep be worth it?

Now notice that this is not just any sheep; it’s his sheep that got lost.

The sheep belonged to him and he wouldn’t rest, he couldn’t rest, until he finds his lost sheep.

For a sheep is not like a dog.

When a dog gets lost, there’s a chance that he might find his way back.

But not the sheep.

When a sheep gets lost, it wanders from one place to another, looking for patches of grass in the wilderness.

It wouldn’t know how to get home.

In fact, it might not even know that it was lost!

And the Shepherd worries about his sheep for it’s his sheep that got lost.

The Bible tells us that Jesus is the Good Shepherd; Jesus is the Good Shepherd who knows his sheep.

Over the past few weeks in Ephesians, we learnt that God has chosen those who are his, before the foundation of the world.

Before the foundation of the world, God the Father predestined and gave these people to the Son.

These people are His to look after.

And Jesus tells us in John 10:28-29, “I give them eternal life, and they will never perish. No one will snatch them out of my hand. 29 My Father, who has given them to me, is greater than all. No one is able to snatch them out of the Father’s hand.”

Jesus will not lose anyone that God the Father had given him.

And so, Jesus goes out to look for his sheep.

It’s the job that God the Father has given Jesus God the Son to do and he will do it.

But it’s more than just a task.

Jesus loves those who are his; Jesus doesn’t want to see those who are his wander away from God, lost in their wandering, because he loves them.

I still remember losing Zoe in a toy shop when she was about five years old.

It was a massive toy shop and so I ran all around the Toy shop to look for her but I couldn’t find her.

I then ran to the entrance of the toy shop to see if I could see her.

After what must have been the longest five minutes of my life, Zoe came out of the toy shop and ran towards me.

And we hug each other for a very long time.

She wouldn’t rest until she found me; and I wouldn’t rest until I found her.

Charles Spurgeon, a preacher from almost two hundred years ago, said that Jesus’ “heart was filled with one burning thought; for first, the sheep is his own; next, he is full of compassion; and then again, it is his office to shepherd the flock.”

Jesus’ one burning thought was to find his lost sheep, because they are his, he loves them and it’s his job to save them.

That’s why Christians go to the furthest places to tell people about Jesus.

It’s because Jesus sends us there; it’s because Jesus wants to go there to save this lost sheep.

Last week, Peter, Stephen and I went to the Synod, which is like a parliament but for Anglican ministers of the Sydney diocese.

One of the presentations was on what our diocese has been supporting in the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

DR Congo is the fifth poorest country in the world.

Our Anglican Churches in Sydney have worked together to provide resources to help the country.

It’s a high cost; we won’t see any economic returns coming back to us.

But it’s where Jesus wants to go and save his sheep and so, we send missionaries, we send resources, we send Bibles to where Jesus leads us.

Today, the Anglican Church of Congo runs more than 1000 churches, 700 schools and 90 health centres.

There’s a high cost to supporting the Anglican Church of Congo, but the one who paid the greatest cost was Jesus himself.

What we give is significant, but what Jesus gave was significantly higher.

Jesus himself had paid the high cost of leaving heaven to save his sheep.

2. Jesus’ joy of bringing his lost home

In order to save his sheep, Jesus left the comforts of heaven a place with neither pain nor suffering, no sin and death, to come into our world of sin, suffering and death.

He not only came into our world to live but he came to our world to die.

Jesus the Good Shepherd laid down his life for his sheep.

We, the lost sheep, foolishly rebelled against God and we deserve to die in the wilderness.

We deserve to die in God’s judgement.

But Jesus died on the cross to bear what we couldn’t bear ourselves.

Look with me at verse 5, “When he has found it, he joyfully puts it on his shoulders…”

Just like the shepherd who bears the weight of the lost sheep on his shoulders, Jesus bears weight of our sin on his shoulders.

Just like lost sheep who’s too tired, too wounded to walk the journey home, Jesus lifts us up and carries us back home.

The weight of our sin and the weight of God’s judgement upon our sin would’ve been a terrible burden to bear.

And yet, Jesus bore this burden on our shoulders, and he did it gladly.

Imagine a young son who got lost and had to spend days in the Australian outback.

When all hope was lost, the father finds the boy, lifts him on his shoulders and carries him home.

And the boy asks the Father, “Aren’t you tired, Dad? You’ve been looking for me all this time. You must be exhausted.”

And the Father replies, “Yes, I’m exhausted, but I’m too happy to be tired.

I’m going to hold you tight and carry you home.

I’m just so glad to hold you again.”

That’s the joy that Jesus himself experienced at the cross.

Jesus endured great pain and suffering on the cross.

Physically it would’ve been horrible, as he bled on the cross and struggled for every breath.

Spiritually, it would’ve been even worse as his heart breaks at the thought of God the Father, the One who had known him forever, left him by himself on the cross.

Was the pain too much? Was the cost too high?

He could’ve got off the cross anytime if he wanted to; he could’ve come down, destroyed all his enemies, without any effort at all.

But no, he didn’t, because he loves His lost sheep too much to let the cross go

Listen to how the Bible describes the joy that Jesus felt even as he was suffering on the cross in Hebrews 12:2,

“keeping our eyes on Jesus, the pioneer and perfecter of our faith. For the joy that lay before him, he endured the cross, despising the shame, and sat down at the right hand of the throne of God.”

With his last breath, Jesus cried in a loud voice on the cross: “It is finished!”

Jesus was suffering tremendous pain when he cried out, and yet it wasn’t a cry of despair but a victory cry.

I dare say that it was even a joyful cry, because he was carrying us home.

He knew that by his death on the cross, those who are his, those who trusts him him, will be saved.

He knew that by his death on the cross, the debt we owed to God is paid for and that we will be safe from death, that we will be safe from God’s judgement.

But we also know that death couldn’t hold him down for he rose three days later.

Our Shepherd bore our sins to save us; our Shepherd rose from the dead to bring us home.

Again, from Spurgeon, “Some of the old writers delight to put it thus: in his incarnation he came after the lost sheep; in his life he continued to seek it; in his death he laid it upon his shoulders; in his resurrection he bore it on its way, and in his ascension he brought it home rejoicing.

Our Lord’s career is a course of soul-winning, a life laid out for his people; and in it you may trace the whole process of salvation.”

Those who trust Jesus, who have been saved by him, will know the safety they have in him because they’ve been saved.

A song that we’ve been singing for a while at Church, and which we’ll sing later on, is the song, “Jesus Strong and Kind.”

The song was written by Richard Thompson from St Paul’s Castle Hill.

Earlier this year, Rich shared that someone showed him a video of a group of girls who sang this song.

This group of girls in the music video were survivors of human trafficking.

They were sold as objects and treated in unimaginable horrible ways.

The girls in the video were rescued and were living in a shelter in the Philippines.

Yet, even after everything they had gone through, they sang these lyrics with certain joy:

“For the Lord is good and faithful, He will keep us day and night We can always run to Jesus; Jesus, strong and kind.”

These girls were saved physically, and they were physically carried home.

In Jesus, they’re saved spiritually.

They know that Jesus has carried their burden and they know that Jesus will joyfully, gladly, carry them home.

And because of that, they have great joy.

Jesus paid a high cost to save those who are his, but the joy that he has is higher because he will carry us safely home.

3. What makes God celebrate

This joy that Jesus has is not private, but one that he wants to share with everyone.

Look with me at Luke 15:6-7, “and coming home, he calls his friends and neighbours together, saying to them, ‘Rejoice with me, because I have found my lost sheep!’ 7 I tell you, in the same way, there will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous people who don’t need repentance.”

The shepherd wants his friends and neighbours celebrate with him for finding the lost sheep.

Jesus wants all of heaven to share with him for the one sinner who repents.

There’ll be more joy for one sinner’s repentance than for the 99 righteous people who don’t need repentance.

The joy of God the Father, God the Son and God the Spirit fills the heavens when one sinner repents and turns back!

There’s joy because the lost is now found, the blind can now see, and the dead is now risen.

That’s what gives God joy; that’s what God celebrates!

It pains him to see us lost in our sins and it pains him to see the hurt we’re causing one another.

God longs for us, but do we even notice him?

If only we have a glimpse of how much God longs for us, we would be humbled and even embarrassed by how much God has given to bring us home.

But God is not embarrassed!

He wants the whole world to know.

He wants heavens to know.

He celebrates the repentance of one sinner.

And in God’s joy, in God’s celebration, we see who God is.

For at the heart of God is love; God is love.

God loves the world, God loves his people, and God will do whatever it takes to save those who are his.

Do our hearts long for what God longs for?

Do we celebrate what God celebrates?

If we understand God’s heart, we will respond by repenting.

That is, we will turn back to him.

We will also celebrate when one sinner repents.

Like Jesus, we will welcome sinners and eat with them,

not because we want to sin with them

but because we want to speak Jesus’ words to them, so that they might be saved.

Today, as we celebrate our 154th Anniversary, we have many things to celebrate.

We celebrate the new facilities that we’ve upgraded this year.

We celebrate the new families who have been joining our Church over this past year.

We celebrate the new projects and new opportunities we have ahead of us.

But most of all, we celebrate the repentance of sinners who repent and turn back to God.

That’s what God longs for, that’s why Jesus came.

And so, that’s what we long for, that’s what we pray for.

In June this year, I’ve asked the Men’s Growth group to pray special requests that only God can do, things that are clearly beyond our control and capabilities.

And so, one prayed for five new families to join, one prayed for 100 families to walk into our Church, another prayed for one new member joining every fortnight, and still another prayer for ten conversions to Jesus.

By God’s grace, he’s answering these extraordinary prayer requests.

In the men’s group, we are still praying for these, but we’re also thanking God, celebrating at what he’s already given us.

Would you also pray that God will save even more people through us, as we share the gospel of Jesus with them and as they come to Jesus in repentance?

This is what God longs to see and so by faith we pray that we will celebrate what God celebrates.

As a Church, we want to celebrate with God for one more sheep to be found for one more sinner who repents.

What do our celebrations say about us?

What brings us joy?

God longs for those who are lost and so He’s sent Jesus to come and save those who are lost.

On the cross, Jesus bore our sins to save us.

God celebrates when one sinner repents and puts his trust in Jesus.

Our repentance, our salvation, brings God joy.

May we celebrate what God celebrates.

May the repentance of one sinner also bring us great joy!

And so, like Jesus, long for and celebrate the repentance of one sinner.

If you haven’t repented and turned back to Jesus yet, turn to him now.

If you have been sinning against God, repent, turn back to him.

Don’t remain lost in the wilderness, come back to him.

Jesus has borne the weight of your sin.

He will happily carry you home.

If you have repented, and have put your trust in Jesus, long for the repentance of your neighbours and friends.

How does Jesus want you to bring His word to them?

Jesus longs for your friend and your neighbours to repent.

Long for their repentance too!

Don’t be like the pharisees and the scribes who wouldn’t ‘waste’ their time and resources on the ‘lost’ causes.

Remember what Jesus did to save lost people like you and me.

Long for the day when you will celebrate their repentance.

Long for the joy of heaven on that day.

Friends, here are the three questions for this week:

1. How have you responded to what Jesus has done to save you?

2. Does your heart share God’s longing for the salvation of a friend or neighbour that you know?

3. What do your celebration say about you?

My prayer for all of us at St Paul’s is this, that like Jesus, we will long for and celebrate the repentance of one sinner.

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you for sending Jesus to save lost people like us. Thank you Lord Jesus, for the great price that you’ve paid, for the weight that you bore, for sinners like us. By your Spirit, let us celebrate what you celebrate. May what brings you joy, bring us joy. May we long for the salvation of one more sinner. Please equip us with what we need to bring your word to your people. Amen.”
