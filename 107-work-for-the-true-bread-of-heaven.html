Work for the True Bread of Heaven

Passage: John 6.22_35

Big Idea: Our God-given work is to believe Jesus, the true Bread from heaven.

What are you working for?

Our God-given work

Satisfied by the true Bread of Heaven

One of the questions that families and friends often ask each other is, “How’s work?”

It’s a fairly common question.

Usually, we might reply by saying, “good”, or “busy” or “tough”.

However, for many Australians last year, work was not great.

In fact, 1.3 million Australians, or about one in ten Australian workers, quit their job last year.

About one in ten Australians didn’t like their job last year.

For some, it was the added pressure that COVID brought; for others, it was the constant pressure of the job, regardless of COVID.

Interestingly, for some people, it’s not about the money.

Even if they were offered more money, they didn’t want to stay in their jobs anymore.

They were looking for satisfaction in their work, but they couldn’t find any.

And so, they quit with the hope of finding satisfaction in another job.

Maybe some of us here have had that experience over the past year.

We were looking for satisfaction in our job, but we found none.

As a result, we might ask ourselves, what’s the point?

Why am I slaving away at our work?

Is this job right for me, anymore?

Of course, it’s not just in our formal jobs that we look for purpose and satisfaction.

For those of us who don’t have formal jobs, we might look for the purpose and satisfaction in our work.

It might be housework, or the work of raising kids, or even work at Church.

Work at home and at Church can be hard too and we might ask ourselves, what’s the point?

What’s the purpose for our work?

In today’s passage, Jesus challenges the crowd to think about the purpose of their work.

Please open your Bible to John 6, beginning from verse 22.

In it, the Lord Jesus will challenge us to think about the purpose of our work.

We’ll see three things:

What are you working for?

Our God-given work

Satisfied with the true Bread of Heaven

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Father, please open our eyes so that by faith we might see the beauty and the glory of the Lord Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

What are you working for?

Here’s what’s been happening so far.

Jesus had miraculously fed 5000 men, not including women and children.

When evening came, the disciples got into the boat and went to the other side of the lake while Jesus stayed behind.

In the middle of the night, Jesus miraculously walked across the sea to be where the disciples were, in the middle of a ferocious storm.

The disciples were witness of these two miraculous signs of Jesus.

Meanwhile, back at where Jesus had fed the 5,000 men, they couldn’t see Jesus or the disciples.

There had been only one boat on this side of the lake and the disciples had already used it to cross the lake.

Since they didn’t know Jesus had walked on the sea, they would’ve thought that he was still around them, but they couldn’t find him.

When some boats from Tiberias came to the place where the crowd was staying, they got on the boat and went to Capernaum, looking for Jesus.

It was hard work!

Imagine the logistics of trying to fit hundreds, perhaps even thousands of people onto boats.

They worked hard looking for Jesus.

When they got there, they asked Jesus “Rabbi (teacher), how did you get here?”

Jesus didn’t answer their question, nor did he commend their effort either.

Instead, he made a damning comment about their motives.

Look with me at verse 26, “Jesus answered, “Truly I tell you, you are looking for me, not because you saw the signs, but because you ate the loaves and were filled.”

Why did they go through all the troubles looking for Jesus?

Because they wanted to be fed.

I still remember a few years ago, we were going down Cumberland Highway to Liverpool when we saw a lot of traffic on the left lane.

The cars were moving very slowly.

At first, we thought it was a car accident of some sort.

But after we overtook them, we saw that it was a long line of cars waiting to go to Krispy Kreme.

Krispy Kreme was giving away free donuts that day and many people in possibly hundreds of cars, were waiting to get their hands on free donuts!

They were coming for free food!

That’s what the crowd was doing.

They travelled all the way by boat across the Sea of Galilee, not because they understood the significance of Jesus,

But because they wanted to get free food!

They experienced the sign; they saw the sign.

They’ve seen how Jesus miraculously fed 5000 men, possibly up to 20,000, including women and children.

However, they didn’t understand how it pointed to the significance of Jesus.

Instead, they came to Jesus for something far more pragmatic reason.

They wanted a free meal.

Before we start pointing the fingers at them, we need to think about ourselves.

Why do we come to Jesus? Why do we come to Jesus’ Church?

Do we come because the Church can give us some material benefits?

As a Church, we do give material benefits to those in need.

That’s’ why we have the Mobile Community Pantry; we have it to support those in our community who are in need.

Before COVID, we had discounted lunches after Church.

Before COVID, we even had free bread!

And it’s not wrong for the Church to give material benefits.

Jesus himself gave out free bread!

But the bread and the material benefits are signs pointing to the Person of Jesus.

They are good gifts, only as they point toward Jesus.

We miss the point of the gifts if we only care about the gifts but forget about the Giver.

Look with me at verse 27, “Don’t work for the food that perishes but for the food that lasts for eternal life, which the Son of Man will give you, because God the Father has set his seal of approval on him.”

The material gifts that Jesus and his Church gives, are perishable.

But the Giver is far superior to the gifts.

The Giver wants to give you something that’s imperishable; he wants to give you food that last for eternal life.

And so, if you’re going to work, don’t work for what’s perishable, even if they are good gifts from the Giver.

Rather work for what’s imperishable, work for what lasts for eternal life, life that never ends!

What does that mean, not working for what’s perishable?

It doesn’t mean that you absolutely stop working at all for all food.

The Bible commends us to work for food and not be a free-loader, living off someone else.

You should work hard at your job, always seeking to be improve and be the best worker you can be.

You should work hard at your job to solve the community’s problems, to solve the world’s problems.

You should work to provide for you and your family.

However, as good and as noble these aims may be, if you are working only to feed yourself and your family, if you are working only for this world and this moment,

Then you are missing out on food that truly lasts.

These aims are noble, but they are not eternal.

Like the rest of the world, these aims will one day perish.

They are not works commended by Jesus; it’s work for food that perishes.

Don’t work for these; that is, don’t make working for these your life’s work.

There is a superior work, a work that leads to eternal life.

You should do this work.

This superior work will lead to food that leads to eternal life, which only the Son of Man can give.

God the Father had given his seal of approval on the Son of Man.

If we are to work for food that leads to eternal life, we must come to the Son of Man.

When I started working, I begin to think of how much meals are in terms of how many hours I worked.

When I started, a fast-food meal was about an hour’s work.

I needed to work for an hour to get a burger meal.

When I worked for a little longer, I needed to work for an hour to have a nice meal at a restaurant.

To afford a dinner for two in the city, I needed to work a few hours, double pay and overtime!

Some meals are more memorable than others, of course, but it’s still a meal.

It lasts only until the next meal.

The satisfaction lasts only until the next meal.

The same can be said of our achievements at work and our achievements in life.

We might work hard to accomplish a goal in our lives but the satisfaction of completing it is usually short-lived.

Before you know it, you want to achieve the next target, the next goal.

When does it stop? When will we be truly satisfied?

That was Jesus’ point.

Don’t work for things that don’t satisfy.

Instead, work for things that truly satisfy.

Work for the bread that lasts for eternal life.

Our God-given work

And so, naturally, we want to ask, what kind of work is that?

What kind of work leads to eternal life?

Listen to Jesus’ response to that question, in verse 29, “This is the work of God—that you believe in the one he has sent.”

Notice Jesus is not talking about some vague notion of faith.

He’s not saying as long as you have faith, you’ll be right.

No, it’s not just any faith but faith in one person.

It’s faith in the one whom God has sent.

To have faith in Person is to trust that Person.

It’s to have confidence in that person.

Some of us at Church are very much involved with the Children Centre.

Some of us look after the long day care and others look at the after-school care.

The parents of the children need to trust our teachers and carers before they hand their precious children over to us.

They needed to be confident that their children will be looked after.

That’s why we want to make sure that our teacher and carers are well-trained and that they can competently look after the children.

The parents have put their confidence and their trust in the teachers and carers.

I thank God for the teachers and carers that the Children Centre have.

They’re doing a great job!

What does Jesus say is our job? What must we do if we’re to have food that lasts for eternal life?

Our job is to trust in Jesus, the One whom God’s sent.

The parents of the Children Centre trusts in the teachers and carers to look after their children;

We’re to have an even greater trust in Jesus, who takes care of us!

That’s our work! That’s our job!

Our work is to trust in the One whom God’s sent.

And it’s hard work!

We are to trust Jesus not only when things are easy but when, and especially when, things are difficult.

We’re to trust him when we’re about to make a difficult decision.

We’re to trust him when we’re asked to make a sacrifice, whether it’s in what time or resources.

If you think trusting Jesus is easy, then you probably have never genuinely trusted him.

You probably have never sacrificed something precious in your life for his glory.

You probably haven’t obeyed God’s command to love him with all of your heart, all of your mind, all of your soul and all of your strength.

You probably haven’t loved your neighbour as yourself.

For if you have trust in Jesus, you will know that it’s not easy.

However, if you do believe in Jesus, you will know that it is hard, very hard, to trust him at times.

But you will also know that it’s a joy to trust him, even when it’s hard.

You will delight to trust in him because you love him.

It’s hard but it’s good.

It’s satisfying because you know that you are doing what God’s tasked you to do.

It’s a joy because you know that what you are doing is not in vain.

Whenever you trust in Jesus and do what he’s asked you to do, you know for sure that you’re not wasting your time.

It’s always right to trust in Jesus above all things; it’s always right to believe in him and to have confidence in him.

When you do trust in him, labouring for him, you know that you are working for food that won’t perish in a day or two.

Rather, you’re working for food that lasts because you trust in Jesus, the very thing that God has tasked you to do.

About a month ago, Rev Albert Leung came to share God’s word with us.

In a few weeks, Rev Leung will come back to share Gods’ word with us again.

When Rev Leung was a young minister, he ministered to a man who had autism.

Let’s call this man Andy (安仔).

Andy didn’t have many friends because of his condition.

However, Rev Leung shared the gospel with him over thirty years ago.

They lost contact but through a TV show, they were reunited.

Here’s a snippet.

Though it might not have seem like the best use of time back then, Rev Leung trusted God and shared the gospel with Andy.

As a result, Andy heard the gospel and still remembers what Rev Leung had said, as well as memorising some Bible passages as well.

Rev Leung’s work had lasted for many years, and by God’s grace, it will last for eternal life.

What a wonderful testimony of God’s grace!

Isn’t that something worth working for?

The work of God is to trust in Jesus. Nothing more and nothing less.

How do we trust Jesus with our time and with our resources?

Or don’t I trust Jesus and withhold my time and resources from him, working for perishable food instead?

Do I find satisfaction in perishable things? Or do I find real satisfaction in Jesus?

Satisfied by the true Bread of Heaven

At this, the crowd wanted Jesus to give them some verification.

Jesus had made some bold claims; can he back up what he’s said about himself?

They referred to another great one from God, Moses the Prophet.

While the crowd’s ancestors were in the wilderness, He (referring to Moses), gave them bread from heaven.

What’s Jesus going to give? What sign will Jesus give to prove himself?

Instead of giving them another miraculous sign (as if the miraculous feeding of 5000 men weren’t enough),

Jesus compared the superiority of the Bread of heaven to the manna that Moses allegedly gave.

Look with me at verses 32-33, “Jesus said to them, “Truly I tell you, Moses didn’t give you the bread from heaven, but my Father gives you the true bread from heaven. 33 For the bread of God is the one who comes down from heaven and gives life to the world.”

The Bread that Jesus gives, the Bread from heaven, is superior to the manna that God gave through Moses.

This Bread of God is a Person who comes down from heaven,

And gives life, not only to the Jews but to the whole world.

Who is this Bread of God?

Look with me at verse 35, ““I am the bread of life,” Jesus told them. “No one who comes to me will ever be hungry, and no one who believes in me will ever be thirsty again.”

The Bread of Life is a Person; Jesus is the Bread of Life.

This doesn’t mean that Jesus is made of dough and you can just take bits of him to eat.

Jesus is not literally bread!

Rather, to have him, to have Jesus, is to come to him and to believe him.

It’s to find satisfaction in him.

There’s life in Jesus; eternal life lies in him.

He has life that truly satisfies.

Whoever goes to him will never go hungry again; whoever believes in him will never thirst again.

This doesn’t mean that Christians don’t get physically hungry or thirsty.

However, it does mean your ambition and your desires will find their satisfaction in Jesus.

It doesn’t mean that Jesus will grant your every wish but it does mean that your wishes and desires will no longer be for perishable things like wealth and health and pleasure, as good as these things may be.

Rather your wishes and desire will be for the imperishable thing, namely the eternal life of God.

You will long to spend your eternity with God the Father, God the Son and God the Spirit and that desire will be fulfilled in the Person of Jesus.

You will still have temptations for those perishable things, but in your heart of hearts, your true desire will be for Jesus Christ.

In the Gospel of Luke, there’s a passage about a man called Zacchaeus.

Zacchaeus was a tax collector who was despised by his countrymen because he was collecting taxes for the Romans, the invaders.

However, when Jesus invited himself to Zacchaeus’ home, he willingly gave half of his possessions to the poor and willingly gave back four times as much to anyone whom he’s cheated.

He was happy to give away his perishable wealth because He has the Lord Jesus, the Bread of Heaven.

Because Zacchaeus was satisfied in Jesus, he no longer needed his wealth to keep him satisfied.

That’s the testimony of many Christians since.

I’ve seen many Christians who could’ve had a better paying job, a bigger house, a nicer holiday, a romantic relationship but instead, they would rather trust in Jesus.

Because it’s in Him that both you and I find true, joyful, everlasting satisfaction.

There is nothing happier for a Christian than having Jesus.

Christians are people who long for eternity.

They hunger for eternal life.

It’s a hunger that’s satisfied in the person of the Lord Jesus, the Bread of Life.

Friends, if you want true satisfaction, do the task that God the Father has given you to do.

Believe Jesus, the Bread from Heaven.

To believe Jesus is our God-given work.

It’s the only work that will bring true satisfaction.

What does this mean for us?

Be satisfied with Jesus the Bread of Heaven.

A few weeks ago, I was at a look out in Copacabana in the Central Coast (North of Sydney).

I just came back from a conference and was reflecting how we could possibly apply what we’ve learnt at Church.

And then I looked and saw this in the sea.

It was a cold and cloudy day but in the middle of the clouds, the sun shone through.

I was reminded that no matter how difficult things are, no matter how dark, God’s behind the clouds and his glory will shine through.

He’s in control.

And because he’s in control, I’m happy, I’m satisfied.

Friends look for your satisfaction in the Lord Jesus.

Find satisfaction as you work to put your trust in him.

Be satisfied with Christ as you serve him in ministries at Church.

Be satisfied with Christ as you spend time reading the Bible with your family.

When you welcome a new person, when you give them a call, when you come to Church on Sundays, attend Growth groups, serving the children – when you do all these because you trust in Jesus, it‘ll never be time wasted.

When you trust in Jesus, you are doing the very work God’s tasked you to do.

However, if you spend your resources and time to try to find joy in anything or anyone other than the Lord Jesus,

You would have wasted your time and resources.

You would have wasted your resources on food that perishes.

And so, don’t work for food that perishes.

Rather do the work that God’s tasked you to do.

Trust Jesus.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What are you working for? Perishable things or things that lead to eternal life?

How are you trusting Jesus with your time? With your resources?

What gives you true satisfaction?

Friends, don’t waste your life working for perishable things.

Be satisfied with Jesus the Bread of Heaven.

Forgive us when we don’t trust him as we should. Please turn our hearts to him. Let us find our joy and our satisfaction in him and him alone. Help us to put our trust in him, especially during the difficult times. Help us to find satisfaction in him and him alone. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”
