Growth Groups (adults)

The Foolish Wisdom of the Cross of Christ

1 Corinthians 1:16–25 · Sunday 16 August 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

For adult home groups · preached by David Yung

Big Idea: Don’t be a wise-sounding fool but a foolish-sounding sage, for God is pleased to save all who believe his message of Christ crucified.

Aim: By the end of tonight, we want each person to:

Name one place where they have been embarrassed by the message of the cross.

See from verses 18–25 why God chose a foolish-sounding message to save.

Write down three names to pray for and one person to invite before Father’s Day.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, index cards for the three names. Allow 60–75 minutes.

1. Connect (10 min)

  • Tell us about a time you backed something publicly — a team, a product, a point of view — and it turned out badly. How did it feel to be the last one still cheering?
  • At work, at the school gate, or over morning tea, which part of being a Christian do you mention most easily — and which part do you quietly leave out?

2. Read — 1 Corinthians 1:16–25 (CSB)

16 I did, in fact, baptize the household of Stephanas; beyond that, I don’t know if I baptized anyone else. 17 For Christ did not send me to baptize, but to preach the gospel — not with eloquent wisdom, so that the cross of Christ will not be emptied of its effect.

18 For the word of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but it is the power of God to us who are being saved. 19 For it is written, I will destroy the wisdom of the wise, and I will set aside the intelligence of the intelligent.

20 Where is the one who is wise? Where is the teacher of the law? Where is the debater of this age? Hasn’t God made the world’s wisdom foolish? 21 For since, in God’s wisdom, the world did not know God through wisdom, God was pleased to save those who believe through the foolishness of what is preached.

22 For the Jews ask for signs and the Greeks seek wisdom, 23 but we preach Christ crucified, a stumbling block to the Jews and foolishness to the Gentiles. 24 Yet to those who are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God, 25 because God’s foolishness is wiser than human wisdom, and God’s weakness is stronger than human strength.

3. Context

Corinth was a busy port city that loved a clever speaker. Paying to hear a polished orator was entertainment, and your status rose or fell with the teacher you were seen backing.

The church there had caught the same bug, splitting into fan clubs behind their favourite preachers (1 Cor 1:10–17).

So Paul refuses to compete on eloquence. The message itself — a crucified Messiah — is the power of God, and dressing it up in impressive wisdom would empty the cross of its effect.

4. Discuss

  1. In verse 18 Paul divides everyone into two groups. What are they, and what is the one thing that separates them?
  2. Verse 17 says eloquent wisdom can empty the cross of its effect. What do you think Paul means, and how could that happen in a church like ours?
  3. Verses 22–23: the Jews wanted signs, the Greeks wanted wisdom. What do the people around you — neighbours, colleagues, family — actually want from God before they will believe?
  4. Verse 21 says God was pleased to save through the foolishness of what is preached. Why would God choose preaching rather than something more impressive?
  5. Verses 24–25 call the same message power and wisdom. What changed — the message, or the hearer? What does that mean for how we pray for people?
  6. Be honest: when have you softened, delayed or skipped the message of the cross because it would be awkward? What were you protecting?
  7. We are good at renaming sins — “I’m just being honest”, “righteous anger”, “sharing it as a prayer point”. Which one is most at home in your circles, and what does it cost the gospel?
  8. If the cross really is God’s power, what is one thing you will do differently this week at work, at home, or with a neighbour?

5. Live It Out — KOG 3 · 2 · 1 · 0

  • Pray for 3 — Write down three people — a colleague, a neighbour, a family member — who do not yet trust Christ. Pray for them by name daily until we meet again.
  • Connect with 2 — Take a real step toward two of them this week: a coffee, a meal, a lift, a hand with something practical. Connection earns the right to be heard.
  • Invite 1 — Invite one of them to something — a Sunday service, a midweek fellowship, or the Father’s Day celebration on 6 September, a natural door for the dads in your street.
  • 0 left unloved — Ask your group: who at church has gone quiet lately? Nobody at St Paul’s should be left unnoticed. Take a name each and follow up.

This week’s challenge: Say one plain sentence about Jesus’ death for you to someone this week — without decorating it, apologising for it, or changing the subject afterwards.

Memory verse: “For the word of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but it is the power of God to us who are being saved.” (1 Corinthians 1:18, CSB)

6. Pray

  • Thank God that he was pleased to save people like us through a message the world calls foolish, and that our salvation rests on his mercy and not our cleverness.
  • Pray by name for your three — that God would call them, and that he would give you courage and plain words when the moment comes.
  • Pray for our preachers, our home groups, and the new midweek fellowships being planted this term, that Christ crucified stays at the centre of every one of them.

7. Leader’s Notes

Aim for joy tonight, not guilt. The passage exposes real embarrassment about Jesus, but it lands as good news: God delights to save, and he does it through a message any of us can carry. Point people to Christ, not to their own performance.

Assume guests and not-yet-Christians are in the room. Never corner anyone into confessing unbelief or naming a sin publicly. “Some of us find this hard” is safer than “who here is guilty of this?”

Brief answers to the key questions

  • Q1: Those perishing and those being saved. The line is not intelligence or morality but response to the word of the cross. Both verbs are present tense — nothing is final yet, which is why we keep praying and inviting.
  • Q2: Paul is not against clear speech; he is against packaging that makes the speaker impressive and the cross incidental. Our version: great music, warm morning tea, slick production — and the crucified Christ as background music.
  • Q4: Preaching is deliberately unimpressive — a messenger proclaiming news he did not invent. That way the power is plainly God’s and nobody can boast. Verse 19 quotes Isaiah 29:14, where God demolishes the self-confident wisdom that keeps people from him.
  • Q5: The message is identical; God’s calling changes the hearer. That is freeing — we are not responsible for producing faith, only for telling faithfully and praying persistently.

From faith to faith

Our vision is faith passed on household by household — 50 of our 75 households now serving, on the way to 500 by 2035. That growth will not come from out-impressing the world; it comes from ordinary adults plainly preaching Christ crucified over kitchen tables and back fences.

Live initiatives to weave in (by 30 September)

  • Three new midweek fellowships (two Chinese, one English). Home groups are the engine — ask who in your group could host, lead or invite.
  • Focus on Dads: five new regular dads, culminating in the Father’s Day celebration on 6 September. A concrete, low-threshold invitation for the men you are praying for.
