Hope for hopeless reoffenders

Passage: Genesis 9.1_28

Big Idea: God, the Life-demander and the Covenant-keeper, graciously gives hopeless sinners hope in Jesus

Blood – the symbol of God’s life

Rainbow – the symbol of God’s covenant

Empty wine glass – the symbol of our repeat offence

Cross – the symbol of hope for the hopeless

Last Sunday, a TV show called the Project aired a segment which featured our Church’s Candidate Forum.

In the segment, they interviewed Kate Seselja, was a mother with six children from Canberra.

Kate also played the pokies for 12 years.

She said over that time, she hated who she’s become, living with the shame of someone who kept returning to the pokies.

When she was pregnant with her sixth child, she was given a check for her business.

But rather than putting it the bank, she took it to the pokies and lost thousands of dollars.

She felt horrible afterwards and thought about how everyone, including her babies, would be better off without her.

What a horrible state to be in!

She knew it was wrong to gamble and lose so much money, but she kept going back to it.

She was ashamed of it but she hopelessly kept falling back into the vicious cycle of gambling, loss, shame, and then a return to gambling.

While we may not all experience the same thing as Kate, we know what it is to get into a vicious cycle of sin.

We might know in our minds that something is bad, and yet once we had a taste of it, we keep falling back into it.

We know it’s shameful, and yet we keep doing it.

What hope is there for those of us who keep returning to sin?

What hope is there for repeat offenders like us?

That’s what we’ll see in today’s passage.

Open your Bible to Genesis chapter 9 beginning from verse 1.

We’re going to see four things:

Blood – the symbol of God’s life

Rainbow – the symbol of God’s covenant

Empty wine glass – the symbol of our repeat offence

Cross – the symbol of hope for the hopeless

Before we begin, let’s pray, “Almighty God, our heavenly Father, by your Spirit please open our hearts to the wonders of your word. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

Blood – the symbol of God’s life

After Noah, his family and the animals with him survived the flood that killed all people, land animals and birds on the planet, God told Noah and his sons to repopulate the world again.

Genesis 9:1, “God blessed Noah and his sons and said to them, “Be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth.”

God placed all the animals on the earth, in the sky and in the sea under man’s authority.

And just as God had given man green plants for food, He has now given men all creatures that move for food.

Man is now allowed to eat meat and vegetables!

But there is one thing that man is forbidden to eat.

Verse 4, “However, you must not eat meat with its lifeblood in it.”

Man can’t eat meat that still has blood flowing in it.

It’s strictly forbidden.

In the Old Testament, if you ate unclean food, there were certain rituals you do to cleanse yourself from the uncleanness.

But if you eat blood, you’re cut off from God’s people.

Look with me at verses 5-6, “And I will require a penalty for your lifeblood; I will require it from any animal and from any human; if someone murders a fellow human, I will require that person’s life. Whoever sheds human blood, by humans his blood will be shed, for God made humans in his image.”

God demands blood for blood.

What is blood?

Blood is a fascinating part of the body.

It’s the substance in the body that brings oxygen and nutrients to the rest of your body.

It’s essential to life.

It’s often seen as the symbol of life.

If you lose blood, you will lose life.

While your whole body belongs to God, God singles out blood, our blood, as his property.

Our blood belongs to God.

No one has the right to take our blood away from us; only God does.

The commandment to not eat meat with blood flowing through it is a reminder that blood belongs to God, that life belongs to God.

No one has the right to take your life away from you; only God does.

Neither do we have the right to take away life from someone else.

If an animal takes the life of a man, take his blood as it were, God will require the blood of the animal, the life of the animal.

The animal will be put to death.

The same goes for a man.

If you kill a man, if you take his blood from him, then God will require your blood from you.

God demands blood for blood; life for life.

For when one man kills another, he is killing a man made in God’s image.

He is dishonouring God by killing God’s image.

The literal translation here in verse 5 is that he’s killing a brother.

Since all of us can be traced back to Noah and his sons, to kill a person is to kill a distant brother or sister.

And the person who kills a brother or sister deserve the death penalty themselves.

Now this doesn’t mean that everyone can go and take revenge on anyone who had killed anybody.

God has given the authority to administer justice to people like the police.

If a gunman is causing serious harm to the public, the police are given the authority to use their guns to shoot the gunman.

Hopefully it doesn’t have to come to that.

But for the rest of us, we don’t have the right to kill.

Life is God’s and neither you nor I have the right to take it from another.

We don’t have right to kill someone we hate; that is, we don’t commit homicide.

We don’t have the right to kill a baby, whether it’s inside or outside of the womb.

We don’t commit infanticide.

We don’t have the right to end the life of a person who is old.

We don’t commit senicide.

And we don’t kill ourselves; we don’t commit suicide.

We don’t take another person’s life, we don’t shed another person’s blood because we are not God.

Only he has the right to take the life of a person.

And God will demand from anyone who has taken the life of another their life.

God will demand the blood of the killer, who took something that belonged to God alone.

Do not take another person’s life; instead be fruitful and multiply.

Rainbow – the symbol of God’s covenant

God then makes a covenant with Noah and his sons, as well as with all the creatures that came out of the ark with them.

Look with me at verse 11, “I establish my covenant with you that never again will every creature be wiped out by floodwaters; there will never again be a flood to destroy the earth.”

A covenant is a formal promise between two parties.

Here, God is making a formal promise, an everlasting promise to all mankind and to all the animals: God won’t destroy the world by floodwaters again.

The word covenant appears in the passage seven times.

God wants to make sure we heard him right!

It’s a covenant that goes one way, from God to us.

Noah and his sons weren’t required to do anything except to accept God’s promise to them.

It was a covenant that was unconditional.

Even though the inclination of their hearts may still be evil, God will keep his covenant with them.

The covenant was not dependent on how good they were; it was dependent on how good and faithful God is.

And the covenant is global.

It’s a covenant that God made to everyone and every living creature.

God will not destroy the world by floodwaters again.

And there’s a sign that comes with God’s covenant.

It’s the rainbow.

The rainbow is a beautiful sight to behold.

Sometimes, when the angle of the sunlight and the rain droplets is just right, we will see rainbow in the sky.

I am sure God has a very good memory, but to emphasise how serious God is about keeping his covenant, God has set the rainbow as a reminder for him!

Look with me at verses 14-15, “Whenever I form clouds over the earth and the bow appears in the clouds, 15 I will remember my covenant between me and you and all the living creatures: water will never again become a flood to destroy every creature.”

When I try to remember something, I put it in my diary or on a post-it note.

God doesn’t need to try to remember anything, but he puts the rainbow in the sky as his giant post-it note.

Whenever he sees the rainbow, he will remember the covenant that he had made to his people.

And God has been keeping his covenant to his people all these years.

No matter how bad things get here on earth, no matter how many wars, how many murders, how many catastrophes man has brought to God’s creation,

God remains faithful.

When God sees the rainbow, God will remember his covenant.

Lately, the rainbow has been taken by our society to mean something else.

Our society has twisted the rainbow as a sign of God’s faithfulness and turned it to a sign of human unfaithfulness.

Our society calls what God declared to be good as something bad.

God says sexual relationship is reserved exclusively between a man and a woman inside a marriage.

But our society doesn’t want sexual relationship to be only inside a marriage between a man and a woman.

It calls it bad.

Instead, what God declared to be evil, that is any and every sexual relationship outside of a marriage between a man and a woman, our society calls good.

To promote their cause, our society use the rainbow as their sign.

They make rainbow flags, make rainbow clothes, draw rainbow crosswalks, to make their point.

But the true rainbow, the one not made not by human hands, but by God himself, reminds God of his covenant.

God remains faithful even when human beings twisted his beautiful sign of the rainbow to make fun of God.

What an amazingly patient God we have!

He remains faithful and patient even when we sin against him time and time again!

Empty wine glass – the symbol of our repeat offence

Our tendency to sin time and time again is nothing new.

We see that tendency even righteous Noah.

After Noah landed on dry ground, he planted a vineyard.

He made some wine from it and became drunk.

He was the first drunk recorded in history!

He was so drunk that he uncovered himself in his tent.

Ham, Noah’s youngest son, saw that his father was naked and told his two brothers about it.

Both Noah and his son Ham had both seen that man’s sin can lead to an extinction level event like the flood.

They both survived the flood by God’s mercy and yet they both still fell back into sin.

Noah’s sin was being too indulged in wine, getting drunk as a result.

God’s people have been warned time and time again not to get drunk; Noah, after walking with God for over 600 years, ought to know better.

Ham’s sin was in dishonouring his father by seeing his father’s nakedness and by shaming and dishonouring his father in front of his brothers.

Even though the flood has washed away so many sinners, it hasn’t washed away sin itself.

Sin remained in the hearts of men.

We remain sinners; we are repeat offenders when we sin and offend God.

Like Kate who kept returning to the pokies, even though she knew it was bad for her,

We keep returning to our sins, even though we might know it’s bad for us.

We can’t help it!

Jesus himself has said that the problem lies deep within our hearts.

He said in Mark 7:20-23, “What comes out of a person is what defiles him. 21 For from within, out of people’s hearts, come evil thoughts, sexual immoralities, thefts, murders, 22 adulteries, greed, evil actions, deceit, self-indulgence, envy, slander, pride, and foolishness. 23 All these evil things come from within and defile a person.”

The sin that we keep returning to may not be gambling or drunkenness, but it may be envy of what other people have, or pride over what you have and what other people don’t have.

Shem and Jepheth realised what happened and covered their father’s shame with respect.

After he realised what had happened, Noah spoke for the first time in the Bible.

He cursed Canaan, the son of Ham, to be the lowest of the slaves to his brothers.

He blessed the God of Shem, for God is the focus of the story, not Shem himself.

He also asked God to extend Japheth so that both Japheth and Shem can live in the tents of Shem, to share God’s blessings over Shem.

The story of Noah and his son Ham serves as a warning for both the old and the young among us.

For those among us who are old or getting older, take care not to become complacent, take care not to get overly comfortable.

You don’t get better at avoiding sin just because you’re getting older.

Sin is still crouching at your door, just as it had been when you were younger.

Don’t think that your experience means that you are now safe from sinning.

Noah had spent over 600 years walking righteously with God and yet he still failed.

As we’ve seen time and time again, when our lives are in danger, we walk more closely with God.

But when we’re comfortable, when we take life easy, that’s when the danger of leaving God comes.

Noah trusted God in the flood but failed to trust God on dry ground.

Don’t let your experience give us a false sense of security.

For those who are younger among us, respect your elders!

Don’t dishonour them by exposing their shame, even if it’s true.

You might be right and they might be wrong.

But don’t be like Ham who exposed the shame of his father.

Don’t be a fool who look down on the weaknesses of others, especially those older than you.

That’s the great sin that many young (and not so young) people fall into.

It’s the sin of pride.

Don’t look down on your elders.

Instead honour them, cover them.

As 1 Peter 4:8 tells us, “Above all, maintain constant love for one another, since love covers a multitude of sins.”

Like Shem and Japheth, respectfully, place a cover over their sins with love.

That doesn’t mean to let them keep on sinning against God and other people.

But it does mean to limit the dishonour and the shame they had already exposed themselves to.

And more importantly, we need to take them to Jesus, who alone can deal decisively with our sin.

Cross – the symbol of hope for the hopeless

For in the cross of Jesus, hopeless reoffenders like us have a symbol of hope.

Look with me at 1 Thessalonians 5:7-10, “For those who sleep, sleep at night, and those who get drunk, get drunk at night. 8 But since we belong to the day, let us be self-controlled and put on the armour of faith and love, and a helmet of the hope of salvation. 9 For God did not appoint us to wrath, but to obtain salvation through our Lord Jesus Christ,”

If we belong to Jesus Christ, we belong to the day, not the night.

We don’t live as drunks stumbling through the night but we live as self-controlled soldiers, living for Jesus.

How do you know you belong to the day?

We will believe in Jesus Christ.

God owns our life, God owns our blood.

But we took that life that God rightfully owns for ouselves, living our own way, living our own life.

As sinners, God demands our life; God demands that we die for our sins.

But Jesus Christ gave his life, his lifeblood, to satisfy God’s demand for your blood.

And so from now on, anyone who trusts in Jesus can know for certain that God’s demand for our blood had been paid for.

And Jesus made a promise.

He said, “I am with you always, to the end of the age.”

We’re hopeless without Christ.

But if we have Christ, we will always have hope.

When God looks at the rainbow, he remembers the covenant that he has made with mankind and the earth, that he won’t destroy it.

When God looks at the cross of Jesus, he remembers that Jesus has paid for our sins.

When God looks at the cross of Jesus, he remembers that those who have turned and trusted in Jesus are his very own.

When God looks at the cross of Jesus, He remembers that he’s promised to never destroy us nor punish us, because he’s already punished our sins in the death of Jesus.

If you haven’t turned back to God in Jesus yet, then when God sees you, he still sees your sin.

He still sees your darkness, your drunkenness, your repeated offences.

He has not promised to save anyone outside of Jesus and so all your sin remains before him.

He will demand your life from you.

And so, please, turn to God now, before it’s too late.

What are you waiting for?

You are not safe until the day you turn to Christ.

And so to conclude, are you a repeated offender?

Of course you are! And of course I am too!

We are hopeless sinners, we are hopeless reoffenders.

But God has given us hope in Jesus Christ.

And so, fellow hopeless re-offenders, turn to Christ and stop offending God.

Stay away from the sin that you’re prone to fall in to.

If you’re an alcoholic, stay away from pubs.

If you are greedy, stay away from pokies and other lures to get rich quick.

If you’re prone to watch ungodly images and ungodly videos online, stay away from opportunities where you will be alone with your phone or the computer.

Mean it when you pray, “Lead us not into temptation.”

And Confess.

Confess your sins when you come to Church on Sundays.

When we confess our sins in the service, don’t just utter the words.

What sin are you re-offending?

What sin are you confessing to God when you pray the prayer of confession?

Confess also to one another.

Bring your sin to your brothers and sisters who care for you and love you.

Privately share your struggles against sin with them, knowing that “If we confess our sins, he is faithful and righteous to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.” (1 John 1:9)

Fellow hopeless re-offenders, turn to Christ and stop offending God.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What can you do to help others value other human beings as brothers and fellow bearers of the image of God?

When you see the rainbow, the real one and the man-made ones, are you reminded of God’s kindness and patience?

How can we help one another from different generations at church to turn to Christ and stop offending God?

Fellow hopeless re-offenders, turn to Christ and stop offending God.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for your kindness and patience towards us. Even though we have offended you time and time again, you remain patient with us. Thank you for the covenant you’ve made with Noah and us, the sons and daughters of Noah, to never destroy the world again by flood. Thank you most of all for the new covenant that you’ve made in Christ, that those who believe in him will have salvation and life eternal. Please by your Spirit, turn our hearts towards you, that we might not offend you by our thoughts, words and deeds. Let us walk in the day, as people belonging to the Day of Christ, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
