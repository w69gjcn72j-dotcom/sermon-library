God's Gracious Masterplan

Ephesians 1:1-10 · 2020
Big IdeaPraise God for his glorious grace to us in Christ!

Sermon: God’s Masterplan in Christ

Passage: Ephesians 1:1-10

Big Idea: Praise God for his glorious grace to us in Christ!

Praise the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ!

Praise God for choosing us in Christ!

Praise God for redeeming us through the blood of Christ!

Praise God for revealing his masterplan to us in Christ!

On Wednesday, 30th January 2020, the European Parliament met together to vote on the Brexit deal. PPT

The Brexit deal, of course, has been on the agenda for the United Kingdom for the past four years.

In June 2016, there was a public vote asking the people whether they wanted to leave the European Union.

52% said yes and 48% wanted to stay.

After many years of debates and a few change of Prime Ministers, the UK was finally ready for Brexit.

On Wednesday, after the European Parliament formally voted to accept the Brexit deal, the Members of the European Parliament stood up and burst out in song. PPT

They sang Auld Lang’s Syne (Should auld acquaintance be forgot, and never brought to mind? Should auld acquaintance be forgot, And auld lang syne?)

They were holding hands, hugging and singing this farewell song.

Some will be disappointed, others happy, but most would've been relieved that it’s finally over.

To express their feelings, they burst out in song.

Have you ever done that? Bursting our in song to express your feelings?

In the Letter to the Ephesians, the Apostle Paul bursts out in song as he thinks about God!

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Father, as we come to your Word, may your Spirit so move us that we too will burst into song in thankfulness. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.” PPT

Praise the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ!

Please open to Ephesians 1:3, “Blessed is the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us with every spiritual blessing in the heavens in Christ.”

Here, Paul bursts into song for God, as it were, as he begins the letter to the Ephesians.

He has so many things to sing about God!

Not only does he have many things to sing about God; it’s as if he can’t wait to tell us how good God is.

We can’t see this in our translation of the Bible, but there’s only one sentence in the original language from verse 3 to verse 14.

In the Greek, there’s one sentence with 202 words.

Paul’s very excited about who God is and what he’s done.

The focus of the passage is on God!

God’s the one who sets everything in motion.

It’s God who takes the initiative; it’s God who’s the Master-planner.

However, as we read about God in this passage, we’ll realise that it’s also all about the Lord Jesus Christ.

God is the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ.

Everything that God does, he does in and through the Lord Jesus Christ.

In these 14 verses, Jesus Christ is mentioned fifteen times.

Jesus is the focus of God’s masterplan.

And yet somehow, we are swept into God’s masterplan as well.

Blessed is God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us with every spiritual blessing in Christ.

We are the recipients of every spiritual blessing that God gives in Christ.

We didn’t do anything that makes us deserve what God gives to us in Christ.

God freely gives every spiritual blessing to us, in Christ.

But he does give them to us in Christ.

We receive all these spiritual blessings in Christ.

God doesn’t give any spiritual blessings apart from Christ.

It’s only in Christ that we receive God’s blessings.

As you know, here in Kogarah, we have the St George Hospital. PPT

I’m sure that the hospital Is excellent, but for argument’s sake, let’s say that it’s ranked the best hospital in the world.

That it provides the best practice of medicine in the world

It has all the facilities to treat all types of diseases and all types of conditions.

The doctors and the medical personnel there are all world-class and there are plenty of surgeons so that you don’t have any waiting period for operations and procedures.

And best of all, it’s free of charge!

How do you receive the treatment? You simply walk into the hospital and you will be looked after.

Our God provides spiritual blessings that are far better than anything that this world can give.

He has more than enough spiritual blessings to meet the needs of every man, woman, boy and girl.

However, to receive these blessings we must come to Christ because they are only available in Christ.

God has blessed us. Who are we? We are people who are in Christ.

We are the people who have believed in Christ.

We are the communion of the saints; and here in Kogarah, we are the Church.

With Christ, we have every spiritual blessing.

Without Christ, there will be no blessings.

And so, in this verse, we have God as the Master-planner, Jesus as the focal point of the Master plan and the Church swept up in God’s blessings. PPT

The commentator, Dr Peter O’Brien, my former lecturer at Moore College, wrote this about this verse: ‘God as the origin and goal of salvation, Christ as its mediator, and believers as its recipients – these themes give the passage a threefold theocentric, Christological and ecclesiological focus.’

What a rich verse!

Blessed is the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ who has blessed us with every spiritual blessing in the heavens in Christ!

What spiritual blessings has he given us?

Many! But we’ll look at three in particular from today’s passage:

God chooses us in Christ; God redeems us through the blood of Christ and God reveals his masterplan to us in Christ.

All of these are worthy of our praises! PPT

Praise God for choosing us in Christ!

Firstly, we praise God for choosing us in Christ!

Look with me at verse 4, “4 For he chose us in him, before the foundation of the world, to be holy and blameless in love before him.”

God has chosen us before the foundation of the world!

God chose us, not because we have done anything good, not because we are better than everybody else.

God chose us before he made the world, before we were born, before we’ve done anything good or bad.

God’s choice of us doesn’t depend on us.

God’s choice of us depends on him.

How does God choose us? What does the verse say?

He chose us in him, in Christ!

Jesus Christ is the Chosen One par Excellence.

God has chosen Jesus Christ; Jesus is the Chosen One.

And God chooses his people who are in his Chosen One.

That’s very important to the way we understand what it means for God to choose us.

God doesn’t just choose people at random.

God doesn’t roll a dice and decide who will be saved and who won’t be.

God chooses his people in His Chosen One, in Christ.

It’s a very personal decision; he chooses people who are in his Son.

God’s choice of us is tied to our relationship to Christ.

That’s the assurance for those who are in Christ; we’ve been chosen by God in Christ before the beginning of the world.

If we are not in Christ then we’re not chosen.

As people chosen in Christ before the beginning of the world, we’re to be holy and blameless.

To be chosen in Christ is both a privilege and a responsibility.

God chose us to be holy and blameless before him.

While we’re not there yet, we can look forward to the day when we’ll be holy and blameless, free from sin and blame.

That day will come when Jesus comes back.

But before that day, let’s towards the goal of being holy and blameless.

That’s what we’ll be one day; let’s live our lives in light of what we will be.

Let’s live our lives to be holy and blameless, like our heavenly Father.

Look with me at verses 5-6, as Paul describes our relationship with our heavenly Father: “5 He predestined us to be adopted as sons through Jesus Christ for himself, according to the good pleasure of his will, 6 to the praise of his glorious grace that he lavished on us in the Beloved One.

God has predestined us to be adopted as his sons and daughters.

God has predestined us to be in a personal relationship with him!

To be adopted is for a foreigner, a stranger, to receive the love and the privilege of being a son or daughter in a family.

It’s a beautiful description of what happens when a person is in Christ. PPT

I have a good friend by the name of Alan.

Alan married a few years ago to Beatrice; in fact I was the one who married them.

A few years into their marriage, Alan and Beatrice adopted a lovely young baby into their family, Jayzelle.

Jayzelle is now a part of their family; she’s now the daughter of Alan and Beatrice.

That’s what it’s like for us to be adopted into God’s family.

If we’re in Christ, we’ve been adopted in God’s family.

We’re now sons and daughters of God the King.

We’ve got royal privileges now because we’re children of the King.

And it’s all according to the good pleasure of his will.

It’s all out of his glorious grace.

What a great phrase, in verse 6, “To the praise of his glorious grace”

God’s grace is glorious!

God’s grace is a like a bright light that shines out in the deepest darkness.

God’s grace shows us God’s character, God’s power, God’s love.

And God lavishes his glorious grace on us in his Beloved.

God lavishes his grace; he doesn’t hold back.

And it’s in his Beloved that he gives his grace.

In the Chinese translation, it’s translated the Beloved Son, which is right in that the Son is who Paul refers to.

But in the original, it’s just Beloved.

God gives his grace to us in his Beloved.

The word beloved is a term for someone who’s dearly loved, like Darling, or Sweetheart.

In Christ, the Beloved, we’ve become God’s beloved.

We are now dearly loved by God because we’re in Christ.

We’re beloved because Christ is the Beloved.

We praise God for choosing us in Christ and we praise God for redeeming us through the blood of Christ. PPT

Praise God for redeeming us through the blood of Christ!

Look with me at verses 7-8, “In him we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of our trespasses, according to the riches of his grace 8 that he richly poured out on us with all wisdom and understanding.”

To be redeemed is to be freed from bondage and slavery.

A person might be imprisoned but is now set free because someone had paid the price for their release.

We see this in Exodus, when the people of God were slaves in Egypt.

God rescued them, redeemed them, by his mighty power.

In Jesus, we have a new Exodus.

But rather rescuing slaves in Egypt, Jesus has redeemed us from sin, by his blood, shed on the cross.

Jesus has paid a great price, his own life, to free us from sin.

As a people rescued and redeemed by the blood of Jesus, we now have forgiveness of sins.

As people who have disobeyed God, we were sinners living in bondage to sin.

Sin was our master, whether we acknowledge it or not.

But because Jesus has died on the cross, redemption and forgiveness of sins is now available for all who come to him.

Jesus has come to free us from the bondage of sin and by his death on the cross redeem us for God.

We don’t deserve to be redeemed or forgiven, just as we don’t deserve to be chosen in Christ.

But once again, it’s all because of God’s lavish, extravagant grace on us.

We are saved according to the wealth of God’s grace. PPT

Earlier this week, on 27th January, the world celebrated the 75th Anniversary of the liberation of Auschwitz.

Auschwitz was a German concentration camp in occupied Poland during World War 2.

Over one million Jews were killed at Auschwitz.

Many prisoners spent months, even years, in the horrible condition at Auschwitz.

Many of them saw their friends and families died there.

It was a horrible place to be.

Then on the 27th January 1945, the Soviet Army came and liberated the Jews who were there.

Georgii Elisavetskii, a Soviet soldier who entered one of the barracks, said in 1980 that he could hear other soldiers telling the inmates: "You are free, comrades!"

But they did not respond.

It’s only when he said to them: 'Do not be afraid, I am a colonel of Soviet Army and a Jew. We have come to liberate you'

Finally, as if the barrier collapsed ... they rushed toward the Soviet Army shouting, fell on their knees, kissed the flaps of our overcoats, and threw their arms around our legs." PPT

The Jews couldn’t believe that they were finally freed!

After many years as prisoners in Auschwitz, they were finally freed!

Brothers and sisters in Christ, you were prisoners in sin and Jesus has come to set you free!

We were in the prison of sin, separate from God, awaiting God’s judgement for our sin.

But in Jesus, God had redeemed us, rescued us, saved us from darkness to light.

As a people saved by Jesus, we should be even more grateful to God than the Jews to the Soviet Army for what God for saving us.

We should be the ones throwing ourselves as God’s feet, as it were.

God deserves all our praise!

We praise him for redeeming us through the blood of Christ. PPT

Praise God for revealing his masterplan to us in Christ!

Finally we praise God for revealing his masterplan to us in Christ.

This is where every spiritual blessing lead to.

This is the climax of God’s blessings.

Look with me at verses 9-10, “He made known to us the mystery of his will, according to his good pleasure that he purposed in Christ as a plan for the right time—to bring everything together in Christ, both things in heaven and things on earth in him.”

From eternity past, God had a masterplan.

It’s a mystery that has been hidden since the beginning of time.

However, he has now revealed this mystery to us.

Again, it’s not because we’re smarter or more intelligent than other people.

He chose to reveal his mystery to us according to his good pleasure that he purposed in Christ.

He’s the one who has given us the wisdom and the understanding to understand the mystery.

What’s God’s mysterious masterplan?

Here is God’s Masterplan: to bring everything together under Christ.

That’s been God’s Masterplan all along: to bring everything under Christ.

That’s God’s plan for everything, in heaven and on earth.

That’s God’s plan for the world; that’s God’s plan for you; it’s God’s plan for me; it’s God’s plan for everyone who has ever lived.

If you want to know what God’s plan for you, we can safely say, “It’s to bring you and everything under Christ.”

Jesus is the focal point of God’s Masterplan.

He’s not just one part of the masterplan; he is the Masterplan. PPT

Everything in the universe will ultimately find their place in him.

God’s Masterplan is all about Jesus.

This hasn’t happened yet; not everything is restored under Jesus yet.

But that day will come; at the right time, all things will be restored in Jesus Christ.

God has already planned for that day to come when he chose us in Christ before the foundation of the world.

God has confirmed that that day will come with the first coming of Jesus, when he redeemed us through the blood of Jesus.

And in Jesus, God has revealed to us his masterplan, his goal of placing all things, uniting all things, under Jesus.

What does this mean for us?

Two words: Plans and Praise

Firstly, plans.

Since we know that God has a Masterplan for everything, we need to place our plans under God’s Masterplan.

We need to place our plans under the lordship of Jesus Christ.

Here at St Paul’s, we want to place the plans for our Church under the Masterplan of God.

Our vision for St Paul’s is: We gather to build and to nurture a nation which lives and breathes the gospel of Jesus Christ.ppt

We want the gospel of Jesus Christ to be at the centre of all that we plan, all that we say and all that we do.

And so here at St Paul’s we want people to see the Gospel in our lives, to hear the Gospel in our speeches and to tell the Gospel to those who haven’t heard.

To see this happen here, we have four goals.

Do you know them? PPT

150 families in our 150th year

Produce campus feasibility masterplan

Have exemplary Intergenerational ministry by 2020

Become a Chinese Discipleship Centre in the south

Over the past two weeks, I’ve shared a number of goals with you.

Here’s how they fit in the four main goals of the Church: PPT

Our regional 3:16 mission is part of what we do to reach 150 families for our Church.

Our Holiday Kids’ Club is part of what we do to have an exemplary intergenerational ministry.

Our mission to Nowra and our support of overseas missionaries is part of what we do to train people for ministry beyond Kogarah.

And we’re currently looking at how to make the most of our land for the sake of the gospel.

These are broad goals that we have; next week, we’ll finish by looking at how each one of us can contribute to each of these goals.

But it’s not just the Church’s plan that needs to come under God’s Masterplan.

Our personal plans for our lives need to come under God’s Masterplan as well. PPT

Generally, we tend to think how God can fit in my plan.

We think about how God can fit around our jobs; we think about how God can fit around our lives.

But according to God’s Masterplan, everything is going to come under Christ.

And so, we need to turn our mentality about our plans around.

We need to think about where our jobs fit under God’s plan.

We need to think about how we order our time, how that fits, under God’s plan.

Rather than trying to fit God into our lives, we need to realign our lives to fit under God’s plan in Christ.

And finally, we need to praise God. PPT

We’re to praise God for his glorious grace.

God’s grace is glorious; it’s outstanding, it’s bright, it’s beautiful.

When we truly understand God’s lavish and extravagant grace, we will respond in praise and thanksgiving.

We too will burst into song!

Of course, you can do that in your own private time.

But when we come together at Church, when God’s chosen and redeemed people come together, we ought to burst into praise.

I like the band and for the way they serve us; they’re doing a wonderful job.

But it’s not just the band that ought to sound well.

We ought to be praising God at the top of our voices, thanking him for all that he’s done for us.

Don’t waste the opportunities to praise God that you have here on Sundays.

When you have the opportunity, praise God for all that he’s given us in Christ.

Praise him according to his glorious grace!

Here are the three questions for this week:

What are my plans for following God this year?

How can I contribute to help people see God’s grace in Jesus Christ?

What can I do to praise God more?

In a little while, we will celebrate God’s redemption and God’s revelation.

When we celebrate the Lord’s Supper, we give God thanks for redeeming us through Christ’s blood.

When we celebrate the Lord’s Supper, we look forward to his coming to fulfil all things according to God’s will.

So in a while, as you come, remember how God’s chosen you in Christ, how God’s redeemed you in Christ and how God has revealed his masterplan to us in Christ.

Let’s pray,

We thank you that in Jesus you have chosen us, you have redeemed us and you have revealed your Masterplan to us. Father, we thank you for your glorious grace. We pray that you will forgive us for not being as thankful as we should. Please forgive us if we’ve ever taken your grace for granted. By your Holy Spirit, please use us for your glory. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”
