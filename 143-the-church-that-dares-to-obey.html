In case you haven’t noticed, the Olympics is on again.

Can anyone tell me where’s the Olympics held this year?

Paris!

There are so many movies and songs about Paris.

Who can tell me what’s Paris famous for?

Eiffel Tower, the Louvre Museum, the Arc de Triomphe and streets filled with many cafes.

It’s a lovely city for Olympics!

However, as well as being a city full of beautiful buildings and tourists who came for the Olympics, Paris is also a city full of police.

Last Saturday, there were over 63,000 police and military personnel patrolling Paris on Opening Day.

They had to step up the security because there were possible security threats from outside that could’ve slipped through, as well as unrests that were brewing just a few weeks ago can come up again.

Paris, a city full of beauty and a city full of security, at least for the time being.

You know what’s another place that’s like Paris? Kogarah!

A few years ago, the University of Melbourne used Artificial Intelligence to see which city around the world looked like Sydney and Melbourne.

The result? Railway Parade North, Kogarah is the second place in Sydney that most strongly resembles Paris!

We were beaten by Lower Bent Street, Neutral Bay.

We got silver!

What’s Kogarah filled with? We have three hospitals, six schools, six BBQ chicken shops and also a lot of cafes.

What do you want Kogarah to be filled with?

More importantly, what do you think God wants Kogarah to be filled with?

The city responds to a Spirit-filled Church

Earlier in Acts, we read that the first Christians faced threats from the authorities and so they responded by praying to God for boldness.

Here’s what they prayed in Acts 4:29-30, “And now, Lord, consider their threats, and grant that your servants may speak your word with all boldness, 30 while you stretch out your hand for healing, and signs and wonders are performed through the name of your holy servant Jesus.”

God answered their prayer, and they spoke God’s word boldly.

God also used them to performs signs and wonders in Jesus’ name.

Look with me at Acts 5:12, “Many signs and wonders were being done among the people through the hands of the apostles. They were all together in Solomon’s Colonnade.”

Those who were sick and couldn’t walk were carried to them.

Some were so desperate to be healed by the first Christians that they would just want to be near them so that at the very least, Peter’s shadow might fall on them so that they might be healed.

They all wanted to be near the apostles; they wanted the apostles to heal them in the name of Jesus.

And being good neighbours, they started telling everyone about the free healing and so crowds from all around Jerusalem took the physically sick and the spiritually sick to them.

A while ago, on one of the KOGMen’s nights, Peter invited the whole group to go to Ikea.

Why? Because when you buy furniture from Ikea on that day, you get a food voucher at the same cost as your furniture.

Basically, you get your dinner for free!

And since Peter wanted to buy some furniture for the Children Centre, he basically shouted all of us to dinner!

When you have good news, especially good news about free stuff, you tell your friends about it.

That’s what the people of Jerusalem did!

But they got more than just free healing; they also heard about Jesus.

They heard that that it was in Jesus name that they were healed.

How did the city of Jerusalem respond to this?

Look with me at verses 13-14, “No one else dared to join them, but the people spoke well of them. 14 Believers were added to the Lord in increasing numbers—multitudes of both men and women.”

There were two types of responses:

Firstly, there were those who dared not.

They spoke well of the apostles, they saw the apostles as nice people, even good people.

But they were afraid to join them. Why?

Perhaps they heard the content about their teaching and got scared.

They would’ve heard that Jesus, the Son of God, was crucified and rose from the dead, and that his followers must take up their cross daily and follow him.

They liked the apostles, but they didn’t like them enough to follow their Lord.

Or perhaps they dared not to join the apostles because they heard about what the High Priests and other religious leaders thought of the apostles and their message.

Whatever it was, these dared not follow the apostles and kept their distance.

The second group of people were those who dared to believe.

They most probably would’ve seen exactly the same issues as the first group.

However, instead of staying away, they dared to believe.

They might had known about the high calling of Christians; they might had even known about the fact that some of the apostles had already been in jail because of their belief.

And still, they dared to believe in the apostles’ Lord, the Lord Jesus Christ.

How does the Bible describe those who dared to believe in Jesus?

They were described as those who were added to the Lord.

Isn’t that an awesome description?

While true, they weren’t described as added to the Church.

When you dared to believe in Jesus, you are added to the Lord; that is, you are now the Lord’s.

You’re one of the Lord’s own; you are added to his family.

Meanwhile, those who dared not were not added to the Lord.

This passage helps clear up a misconception about what it means to believe in Jesus.

It’s a misconception that we, modern day Christians in the West, had probably caused.

As a Church in a society that’s getting further away from the Bible, we want them to come to church and so we make things easier and more comfortable for them.

We want to make music that they enjoy, cook food that they enjoy, give talks that they enjoy.

We even want them to meet people that they enjoy meeting.

We want to them to find the Church experience to be lovely and comfortable place.

We want to make it easy for them to become a Christian.

But that’s not what the Bible teaches.

Jesus said in Mark chapter 8 verses 34 to 35, “If anyone wants to follow after me, let him deny himself, take up his cross, and follow me. For whoever wants to save his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life because of me and the gospel will save it.”

Jesus said, follow me, and you might get jailed; follow me, you might lose your home and all your family; follow me and you might be killed because of me.

To follow Jesus you must be daring, you must have courage.

We were reminded last week that in North Korea, many people are becoming Christians, even though Christianity is banned.

Many, up to 400,000 in North Korea dared to become Christians even though they would be sentenced to jail.

Unlike many of us in the West, they understood that being a Christian is hard, but these but these brothers and sisters dared to believe in Jesus.

They dared to have trust in the Lord alone and they are added to the Lord.

Genuine Christians dare to believe.

You only dare to believe if there is a risk in believing.

If there’s no risk in believing, if you can comfortably become a Christian, can you really still call it a dare?

Friends, please take the dare: Do dare to take the risks for Jesus, do dare to carry the cross daily, do dare to believe in Jesus.

2. God’s command to a church in danger

As well as the daring and the dare-nots, there’s a third group.

Look with me at verse 17, “Then the high priest rose up. He and all who were with him, who belonged to the party of the Sadducees, were filled with jealousy.”

There is the daring, the dare-nots and the jealous.

The Bible tells us that it’s the jealousy of the high priest and those who were with him that landed the apostles in jail.

Unlike most people in jails, the apostles didn’t go to jail because they killed someone or stole something precious.

The apostles were in fact doing the opposite: They healed people, they gave to those who have needs among them, and they talked about Jesus.

It was the jealousy of the high priest and those with him that landed them in jail.

What were the chief priests and his team jealous about?

They were jealous of all the attention that the apostles receive from the people because the apostles were doing God’s good work.

What is jealousy?

There can be good jealousy.

For example, it is right for you to be loyal to your spouse and for your spouse to get jealous if you turn to someone else.

The Bible says God is a jealous God because he wants us to be faithful to him only.

There’s also bad jealousy.

According to the dictionary, to be jealous is to have resentment against a rival, a person enjoying success or advantage, etc., or against another's success or advantage itself.

To be jealous of someone is wishing that the person who is succeeding stop succeeding, because he or she is more successful than you.

And in many cases, someone who is jealous will act to stop another person from succeeding.

In Australia, you might have heard of the term “Tall Poppy Syndrome”

This is something that happened when people criticise or undermine those who have achieved success.

The tendency for many Australians is to bring down those who have excelled.

While some people will argue that it keeps success in check, social psychologists say that this is really the behaviour of insecure individuals.

And so, instead of celebrating someone’s achievement, they tend to downplay these achievements, make sarcastic remarks, point out flaws, spread rumours, in order to bring them down.

The high priest and his team had a very bad case of tall poppy syndrome.

The apostles were telling people about Jesus, the very Jesus that the high priest and his people helped put to death.

But now the apostles said that Jesus rose from the dead and that it’s by his power in his name that these people are healed.

In response, out of jealousy of these apostles, the high priest and his team put the apostles in jail; they wanted to see these good, godly Christians fail.

They locked up the apostles in a public jail, so the public could see what happens to those who dare to believe.

But the Lord sent an angel and freed the apostles.

What did the Lord freed the apostles to do? To go back to bed? No!

Look with me at verse 20, ““Go and stand in the temple, and tell the people all about this life.”

God didn’t save them freed them to take them away from danger; rather, God saved them and placed them back in danger, if it means people will hear about the good news of Jesus.

And that’s exactly what they did.

They dared to obey God, even if it meant going back to the middle of danger.

When the priests and the Sanhedrin, the full council of the Israelites, sent for the apostles, they were missing.

They weren’t locked up in jail.

They were confused until someone said, “Look! The men you put in jail are standing in the temple and teaching the people!”

Look at verses 27-28 with me, “After they brought them in, they had them stand before the Sanhedrin, and the high priest asked, 28 “Didn’t we strictly order you not to teach in this name? Look, you have filled Jerusalem with your teaching and are determined to make us guilty of this man’s blood.”

What was the city of Jerusalem filled with?

They were filled with the apostles’ teaching, and they were saying that the high priest and his people were guilty of the death of Jesus.

Now notice what’s not there:

The high priest didn’t say anything about the fact the apostles were miraculously healing crowds of people.

They didn’t even question these apostles miraculously got out of a secure jail.

They weren’t interested in the significance of these extraordinary miracles or even the remote possibility that Jesus really is who he said he was.

Their focus wasn’t on the events, or even the truth behind the events, but only on themselves.

Their concern was that the apostles filled Jerusalem with their teaching, which made them look bad.

They were upset because it’s as if the disciples were trying to make them look like murderers.

But the disciples didn’t have to try; they were murderers.

Their jealousy had blinded them so much that they couldn’t see God’s hand at all, that they didn’t care about the truth anymore.

They couldn’t see that they were commanding the disciples to disobey God’s clear command.

Why were they so blind to what they were doing?

They were jealous; their jealousy blinded them.

That’s the terrible, blinding power of jealousy.

It’s not just high priest and his group that are vulnerable to the sin of jealousy.

We can very easily fall into this sin of jealousy as well.

We fall into the sin of jealousy when we compare ourselves with other Christians who seemed to be doing better, when we compare ourselves with other Churches who seem to be doing better, and we wish that they were not as good, that they were not as successful.

We fall into the sin of jealousy when we are resentful at their success, even if we know that it’s God himself who has been blessing their work.

This leads to a very unhealthy and a very ungodly mindset.

Don’t do it; don’t fall into the sin of jealousy; don’t fall into the same trap as the high priest and his group.

Rather than being resentful, thank God for his blessings on them, pray that the Lord Jesus will continue to be glorified through them.

God has blessed them, but that’s not his end goal.

God’s end goal is for them to glorify Jesus, to do his will.

That’s why God freed the apostles from jail.

God has saved the apostles, but that’s not his end goal for them.

God saved them, to do his will.

God freed them to fill the city with Jesus’ teaching; God freed them to obey and to testify.

3. The Spirit-filled Church dares to obey and testify

The genuine Christian Church is to be a Spirit-filled Church that dares to obey and testify.

In response to the high priest’s two part question, namely, how dare they disobeyed the high priest and how dare they fill the city with the teaching about Jesus.

Peter’s answer is also in two parts.

Firstly, Peter tells the high priest why they disobeyed the high priest’s order.

Look with me at verse 29, “Peter and the apostles replied, “We must obey God rather than people.”

Peter and the apostles must obey God first. There are no if’s or but’s.

You gave a strict command, but we must obey God’s stricter command first.

Second, Peter explains the Gospel in four steps.

Step 1: Man killed Jesus

Verse 30, “The God of our ancestors raised up Jesus, whom you had murdered by hanging him on a tree.”

The disciples didn’t make the high priest look bad. He and those with him were bad.

They murdered this Jesus by hanging him on a tree, that is, on the cross.

That was their verdict on Jesus.

Step 2: God exalts Jesus

Verse 31, “God exalted this man to his right hand as ruler and Savior”

God overturned man’s verdict by raising him from the dead.

More than that, God exalted Jesus to his right hand, to be the ruler and the Saviour.

From God’s right hand, the place of authority, Jesus gives repentance to Israel and forgiveness of sins.

Even here, even as he clearly shows that the high priest and those with him were guilty of the death of Jesus, Peter offers the gospel of hope.

Even now, in the middle of their trial against Peter and the apostles, there is an offer of repentance and forgiveness.

Though they had murdered the very Son of God, even now, they can be forgiven in the name of Jesus.

That’s such a beautiful picture of God’s grace, isn’t it?

The very people who murdered the Son of God, was offered repentance and forgiveness.

Here, the Bible tells us that even the repentance we need to come before God, even the very thought that we have sinned against God and need his forgiveness, comes from Jesus.

It’s completely out of God’s mercy that Jesus gives anyone repentance.

That’s why the first apostles, and all Christians, dare to believe.

We repent, we are forgiven, we are saved, all because of the mercy of Jesus.

It’s Jesus who gives us the faith, to dare to repent, to dare to believe.

It’s only as we come to Jesus, weak as we are, foolish as we are, that we receive repentance, that we receive forgiveness, that we dare to believe.

Step Three: Witnesses of the gospel

Verse 32, “We are witnesses of these things, and so is the Holy Spirit whom God has given to those who obey him.”

How do you know these things are true?

Because God has given us the Holy Spirit to believe him and to obey him.

We also have the testimony of the first apostles, the eye-witnesses of Jesus.

We believe, we witness, we dare to obey, only as God the Holy Spirit enables us to believe the testimony of the apostles.

That’s why we pray.

It was great to spend time on Monday night with the men of our Church at KOGMen to read the Bible and to pray together.

Then on Tuesday night, we had our Chinese prayer meeting, where we had the most people coming to pray, in a very long time.

(By the way, our aim is to have at least ten people coming to prayer meeting every Tuesday, in both Chinese and in English)

On Thursday night, I had the opportunity to pray online with Christians all around Australia for the upcoming Hope25 outreach.

We pray because we know we’re not worthy or good.

We pray because God is powerful and good, and He is worthy of all praise.

We pray that he will give us the faith to obey, especially when things are hard.

We pray not for the removal of danger to our Church but the boldness to face up to them.

We pray not for our own comfort but for God’s glory.

And so, we pray for God’s will to be done, we pray for God’s kingdom to come, even if it means hardship, even if it means ridicule, even if it means carrying the cross.

For that’s what a Spirit-filled Church does.

The Spirit-filled Church dares to obey and dares to witness.

God has given us his command.

Do we dare to obey him? Or do we dare to disobey him?

What does God want to see our city, our town of Kogarah filled with?

God wants to see Kogarah filled with the teaching of the Lord Jesus Christ.

God wants all Sydney, every suburb, to be filled with the teaching of Jesus.

He wants to see the Church as places where the world can see the Gospel, hear the gospel, believe the gospel and tell the gospel.

And so, friends, even in danger, be the Spirit-filled Church that dares to obey God, filling your city with the gospel of Jesus.

Earlier this week, Jenny and I visited an old ministry couple.

They’ve been serving churches in Sydney for many years.

The husband has been a minister and has been in hospital many times throughout the past year.

What did they pray for?

They prayed that they might be healthy so that they can continue to serve God.

The only reason for them to be healthy is to serve God.

That’s the God whom they dare to believe in; that’s the God whom they dare to serve.

Friends, I thank God for the risks that some of you dare to take to come here this morning.

Some of you risks upsetting your family, others risk losing the opportunity to earn more money, and still, you dared to come this morning.

That’s an important first step to take.

Do you dare to take the next step? What’s the next step of faith that God wants you to take?

It might be a step that will lead to ridicule, or sacrifice, or even being the target of someone else’s jealousy.

If we are going to publicly proclaim God’s will, if we are going to fill the city with the gospel, we will become the target of attack.

Do we dare to take the next step?

We dare, because God has put his Spirit in us, to receive repentance and forgiveness, and to proclaim the gospel of his glory.

Because God’s Spirit is in us, we dare to obey God and we dare not disobeying God.

For, like Peter, like all the apostles, and like all genuine Christians before us and around us, we must obey God, rather than man.

Here are three questions for us to think about this week:

When was the last time you dared to obey God, even when you might be in danger?

Is the end-goal of your prayer your comfort or God’s glory?

What would a city filled with the gospel of Jesus look like?

Friends, even in danger, be the Spirit-filled Church that dares to obey God, filling your city with the gospel of Jesus.
