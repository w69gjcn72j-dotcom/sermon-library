Young Adults (19–30)

From Paradox to Doxology

Luke 5:1–26 · Sunday 1 November 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

For young adults (19–30) · Sermon by Rev David Yung

Big Idea: Glorify God for the incredible Jesus, who recruits the unworthy, cleanses the unclean, honours the faithful friends and forgives the immovable sinner.

Aim: by the end of the study, group members will

see how Jesus recruits the unworthy Peter, cleanses the unclean leper with a touch, and honours the faithful friends by forgiving their paralysed friend;

name where they feel too good for Jesus, or not good enough for him, and where their prayers are safe and vague;

say yes to Jesus’ recruitment — one area of service — and plan with others to bring one friend to Jesus.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

1. Connect (10 min)

  • You pull an all-nighter on an assignment or a work deadline and it still flops. Then someone in the group chat tells you how you “should” have done it. What’s your first reaction?
  • Our feeds are curated to show everyone at their best. Where do you feel you don’t quite belong — at uni, at work, in your share house, or even at church?

2. Read: Luke 5:1–26 (CSB)

1 As the crowd was pressing in on Jesus to hear God’s word, he was standing by Lake Gennesaret. 2 He saw two boats at the edge of the lake; the fishermen had left them and were washing their nets. 3 He got into one of the boats, which belonged to Simon, and asked him to put out a little from the land. Then he sat down and was teaching the crowds from the boat.

4 When he had finished speaking, he said to Simon, “Put out into deep water and let down your nets for a catch.” 5 “Master,” Simon replied, “we’ve worked hard all night long and caught nothing. But if you say so, I’ll let down the nets.” 6 When they did this, they caught a great number of fish, and their nets began to tear. 7 So they signaled to their partners in the other boat to come and help them; they came and filled both boats so full that they began to sink.

8 When Simon Peter saw this, he fell at Jesus’s knees and said, “Go away from me, because I’m a sinful man, Lord!” 9 For he and all those with him were amazed at the catch of fish they had taken, 10 and so were James and John, Zebedee’s sons, who were Simon’s partners. “Don’t be afraid,” Jesus told Simon. “From now on you will be catching people.” 11 Then they brought the boats to land, left everything, and followed him.

12 While he was in one of the towns, a man was there who had leprosy all over him. He saw Jesus, fell facedown, and begged him: “Lord, if you are willing, you can make me clean.” 13 Reaching out his hand, Jesus touched him, saying, “I am willing; be made clean,” and immediately the leprosy left him. 14 Then he ordered him to tell no one: “But go and show yourself to the priest, and offer what Moses commanded for your cleansing as a testimony to them.” 15 But the news about him spread even more, and large crowds would come together to hear him and to be healed of their sicknesses. 16 Yet he often withdrew to deserted places and prayed.

17 On one of those days while he was teaching, Pharisees and teachers of the law were sitting there who had come from every village of Galilee and Judea, and also from Jerusalem. And the Lord’s power to heal was in him. 18 Just then some men came, carrying on a stretcher a man who was paralyzed. They tried to bring him in and set him down before him. 19 Since they could not find a way to bring him in because of the crowd, they went up on the roof and lowered him on the stretcher through the roof tiles into the middle of the crowd before Jesus.

20 Seeing their faith he said, “Friend, your sins are forgiven.” 21 Then the scribes and the Pharisees began to think to themselves: “Who is this man who speaks blasphemies? Who can forgive sins but God alone?” 22 But perceiving their thoughts, Jesus replied to them, “Why are you thinking this in your hearts? 23 Which is easier: to say, ‘Your sins are forgiven,’ or to say, ‘Get up and walk’? 24 But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins”—he told the paralyzed man, “I tell you: Get up, take your stretcher, and go home.”

25 Immediately he got up before them, picked up what he had been lying on, and went home glorifying God. 26 Then everyone was astounded, and they were giving glory to God. And they were filled with awe and said, “We have seen incredible things today.”

3. Context

Luke 5:1–26 follows straight on from Jesus’ teaching and healing in Capernaum (4:31–44), as his popularity grows. Luke gives us three encounters. Jesus calls the fisherman Simon Peter after a huge catch of fish; he reaches out and touches a man covered in leprosy, someone the law kept apart from all human contact; and, with Pharisees and teachers of the law watching, he forgives and heals a paralysed man whose friends lower him through a roof. Each encounter is a “paradox” — something hard to believe — and each leads people to glorify God.

4. Discuss

  1. (vv.1–5) Simon had fished all night and caught nothing; now the Teacher tells the fisherman how to fish. What do you hear in “But if you say so”? Where are you tempted to think Jesus is fine for Sunday but doesn’t know better than you about your degree, career or relationships?
  2. (vv.6–9) Why does an enormous catch make Peter say, “Go away from me, because I’m a sinful man, Lord!”? What has he realised about Jesus — and about himself?
  3. (vv.10–11) Jesus doesn’t leave the unworthy Peter; he recruits him. How is that different from a job interview or a graduate program? What does “catching people” mean, and what did it cost Peter to “burn his boats”?
  4. (vv.12–13) Lepers were cut off from human contact and had to cry “Unclean!” when anyone came near. Why is it so striking that Jesus touches him? In a call-out culture, who gets treated as “unclean” today?
  5. (vv.12–16) The leper was clear about what he wanted, but left it to Jesus: “if you are willing”. What safe, vague prayers do you pray without expecting God to act? How can you know God’s will?
  6. (vv.17–20) The crowd blocked the door, so the friends broke the roof, and Jesus “saw their faith”. What does that kind of faith look like for a friend at uni or work? What stops you bringing friends to Jesus?
  7. (vv.20–24) Why did the scribes call Jesus’ words blasphemy? Which is easier — to say “Your sins are forgiven” or “Get up and walk”? What does the healing prove?
  8. (vv.25–26, application) Everyone said, “We have seen incredible things today,” and glorified God. Why do we so often see failures, outcasts and the powerless in church? How could young adults show newcomers the paradox of grace?

  • Pray for 3 — three friends, housemates, colleagues or family members who don’t yet know Jesus. Pray clear prayers for them by name every day.
  • Connect with 2 — a coffee, a meal or a walk with two of them this week (not just a reaction in the group chat).
  • Invite 1 — invite one of your three to the Welcome Home — Discover course or to our Community Christmas Concert on 13 December (we are praying for 120+).
  • 0 left unloved — at church, at work and in your share house, notice the person on the edge and include them.

This week’s challenge: Jesus recruits the unworthy. Say yes to one area of service: an assistant for KOGKids, KOGMini or KOGYouth, an accompanist for Chinese Parallel, or helping at the Christmas concert. And with one or two friends, plan how you will bring one person to Jesus this month — when one door closes, break the roof.

Memory verse: “Don’t be afraid,” Jesus told Simon. “From now on you will be catching people.” (Luke 5:10)

6. Pray

  • Praise God for the incredible Jesus, who recruits the unworthy, cleanses the unclean and forgives sinners. Confess where we have thought ourselves too good for Jesus, or too good for his church.
  • Ask God for courage to say yes when Jesus recruits us, and for clear, faithful prayers according to his will — for the Discover course and the Christmas concert.
  • Pray by name for your 3 — that they would see that their greatest need is forgiveness, and that God would bring them before Jesus.

7. Leader’s Notes

Framing. Joyfully point to Jesus. Some may not yet be Christians or may be unsure; never corner anyone. Young adults are often told they must curate a better version of themselves. The good news is that Jesus recruits the unworthy and is not afraid to touch the unclean.

Q1 — Fishing was Simon’s profession and he had failed at it all night. His “if you say so” is reluctant respect. We too doubt that Jesus knows better than us about our own work and life.

Q2 — Not normal, not coincidence: God at work. Peter thought Jesus wasn’t good enough to tell him about fishing; in fact Peter wasn’t good enough to be near Jesus. To come before God, you must first realise you are not worthy to come before God.

Q3 — Employers recruit the worthy; Jesus recruits the unworthy — the paradox of grace. Catching people means proclaiming the good news of God’s kingdom, to save people before they are lost. Peter didn’t wait to be ready or happy; he left everything (Cortes burned his ships, 1519). Jesus is worth everything we lose, be it wealth or health, family or fame.

Q4 — Lepers were outcasts, not by choice; Josephus called them “in no way differing from a corpse”. Jesus’ touch is a reverse contamination: touching him makes us clean.

Q5 — The leper knew exactly what he wanted and that it was a gift, not a transaction. If we don’t expect God to act, is it really prayer? We know his will by his word and by prayer; when unsure (a big decision, or how to respond to his recruitment), pray. Jesus withdrew to pray (v.16).

Q6 — Giving up isn’t faith; breaking the roof is. “Their” faith is plural. Not “God closes a door and opens another”, but: when the crowd blocks the door, make an opening through the roof.

Q7 — Blasphemy is saying something false about God. Only the one sinned against can forgive (the stolen phone). Both are easy for God, but only healing can be seen — so Jesus heals to show the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins.

Q8 — Paradoxa (incredible) leads to doxazo (glorify). We see failures and outcasts in church because Jesus is willing to heal them. At the cross the rejected, betrayed Jesus took our uncleanness and sin so we could hear, “Your sins are forgiven.”

Our vision. “From faith to faith”: faith is real only when each of us comes to Jesus and follows him.
