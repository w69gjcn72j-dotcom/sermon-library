Teach us to number our days

Passage: Psalm 90.1_17

Big Idea: In our brief and condemned lives, pray for the satisfaction that only God’s faithful love can give in Jesus Christ.

The everlasting God and insignificant specks of dust

God’s anger and judgement against insignificant dust

A speck of dust’s prayer and the faithful God’s answer ‘

It’s great to see you all here on the first Sunday of 2023!

I can hardly believe that Christmas was only seven days ago!

What did you do after Church last week?

For our family, we had a Christmas lunch followed by opening our presents.

One of the presents I received was from Ezekiel.

It’s a physical, hard copy, diary for 2023.

I haven’t used one of these in ages; I love it!

This diary begins with something quite unique that I haven’t seen in other diaries before.

After the page with name details it has two pages that would’ve been blank except for these words: Resolutions I won’t be keeping.

I don’t know about you, but it’s kind of discouraging to make New Year resolutions over the past few years.

I still remember at the beginning of 2020, we were full of confidence of trying new things, reaching new goals, going to new places.

However, many plans were disrupted since then due to a little thing called COVID and some of us might be a little reluctant to make any long-term goals.

We’ve all experienced it: all our grand plans, all our strategies, might suddenly change with a rise in COVID infections.

That’s how fragile our plans are; a tiny virus, invisible to the eye, can derail them.

Our plans are fragile because we are fragile.

A disease can stop us in our tracks, a poor decision can damage family relationships, a poor decision from someone else can make our life miserable.

And even if we are strong and can somehow overcome all these things, what will we achieve if we did achieve all that we’re set out to do?

Will the world be a better place, even for a moment?

Aren’t we merely insignificant specks of dust in the sands of time and space?

In Psalm 90, the answers is Yes, No, and Please.

Here are the three expanded headings:

The everlasting God and insignificant specks of dust

God’s anger and judgement against insignificant dust

A speck of dust’s prayer and the faithful God’s answer

Before we look into it, let’s pray,

“Father, thank you for your word, the Bible. Please open our eyes, that we might see your work and that our children will see your splendour. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

The everlasting God and insignificant specks of dust

Open your Bible with me to Psalm 90, beginning from verse 1,

“A prayer of Moses, the man of God.”

This is a prayer from Moses the man of God, but it’s also a lesson on the difference between the everlasting God and insignificant specks of dusts that we are.

Firstly, listen to how Moses addresses God.

“Lord, you have been our refuge in every generation. Before the mountains were born, before you gave birth to the earth and the world, from eternity to eternity, you are God.”

God is from eternity to eternity.

He was there before the mountains were born; he made the earth, and he made the world.

He’s the Creator God.

This eternal, Creator God has been the refuge for Moses and his fellow Israelites from generation to generation.

How about us?

Verse 3, “You return mankind to the dust, saying, “Return, descendants of Adam.”

Some of us might have seen this video before.

It shows how insignificant we are, we’re really like dusts; we’re tiny!

God made everything, including the mountains, the world, the solar system, the milky way, and every constellation in the universe.

When we sang earlier that My God is so big, he is indeed very big.

We’re nothing compared to the rest of the world;

We’re less than nothing compared with all that he’s made.

We are insignificant specks of dust.

God made us from dust, and he tells us to return to dust, that is, he’s the One who made our life and he’s also the one who ends our life.

That’s what it means to be mortal.

To be mortal means that one day we will die.

God, on the other hand, is immortal; he’s from eternity to eternity.

There’s no beginning and no end for God.

Verse 4, “For in your sight a thousand years are like yesterday that passes by, like a few hours of the night.”

For us, a thousand years is a long time but for God, it’s like a short time, as if it was yesterday.

Not only are we insignificantly small but we exist in an insignificant short period of time.

One blink from God and we’re gone.

Verse 5-6, “You end their lives; they sleep. They are like grass that grows in the morning— in the morning it sprouts and grows; by evening it withers and dries up.”

We’re like this plant that grows in the morning – full of life, and full of growth.

But in the evening, we become like this plant – dead and dried up.

We have very short lives in the grand scheme of things.

No matter how significant or how important we think we are, at the end of the day, we’re mortal, we’re small and we have short lives.

It’s a little like this.

Who watched the World Cup final?

Can anyone tell me who won? Can anyone tell me who won in the World Cup before that?

How about the one before that?

Just as we have forgotten what happened in the 2014 world cup, the 2022 world cup will one day also be forgotten.

Whatever significant thing we might consider doing, we must remember two things:

We are small and our time is short.

We are not the centre of the world, God is.

God made all things and God’s from everlasting to everlasting.

As we think about what we do this year, and indeed, as we think about what we do with our lives, we must remember who we are and who God is.

As we plan for things, we mustn’t begin with us, we mustn’t think about how God fits in our plans.

Instead, we must think about where do we fit in his?

And so, are we mere specks of dust in the space of time and space?

Yes, yes we are, especially in light of who God is!

But we’re not merely specks of dust.

We’re worse than that.

God’s anger and judgement against insignificant specks dust

We’re insignificant specks of dust that are facing the anger and judgement of the eternal God.

Look with me at verse 7, “For we are consumed by your anger; we are terrified by your wrath.”

We’re insignificant specks of dusts that are facing the terrifying wrath of God’s anger.

We’re about to be consumed by the anger of the Almighty, Creator God!

What is God angry about?

Verse 8, “You have set our iniquities before you, our secret sins in the light of your presence.”

The almighty God’s anger is directed towards our iniquities and our secret sins.

Why is God so angry at our sins, the sins of insignificant specks of dusts.

It’s a little like this.

Many years ago, Michael, a good friend of mine was playing soccer with some youths.

But a few days afterwards, the friend felt that there was something wrong with one of his eyes.

And so, he went to see the Doctor.

What the Doctor found was that my friend had an infection inside the cornea of this eye.

This isn’t the usual conjunctivitis that you can treat with normal eye drops.

Somehow, a bacteria got inside my friend’s cornea, a place where it didn’t belong and where the body has very little defense against it.

As a result, my friend had to have months of treatment and his wife banned him from playing soccer with youths ever again.

Friends, as sinners, we’ve placed ourselves in a place where we’re not supposed to be.

As sinners, we’ve placed ourselves in the centre of the universe.

We think that we’re the ones to determine what the world is supposed to be like.

We think that we’re the ones to set the rules.

But God, and God alone is the legitimate ruler of the universe.

To take his place as the centre of the universe is to sin before him.

In response, God’s right to be angry at us.

He’s angry at what these little dusts of human beings who wrongly claim his creation for themselves,

who steal the honour that belongs to him alone.

What does God’s judgement look like?

God judges us by condemning us to mortality.

We will die; we will ebb away.

Look with me at verses 9-10, “For all our days ebb away under your wrath;

we end our years like a sigh. Our lives last seventy years or, if we are strong, eighty years. Even the best of them are struggle and sorrow; indeed, they pass quickly and we fly away.”

We will end our years like a sigh.

We might live to seventy, even eighty.

And some of us have lived beyond 80. Praise God!

But even the best of our years, the best of our years, are struggle and sorrow. Contrary to what we might be told, life is hard.

A trouble-free life is merely a dream, not reality.

Someone who tells you that they have a trouble-free life is either lying to you or worse, lying to themselves.

The life that we live, as short and as insignificant as they are, will be hard.

They are hard because of our sins and iniquities before God.

How was your year last year?

Was it a good year? What kind of troubles did you face last year?

I thank God that I had a great year, but it wasn’t without its troubles and sorrows.

There were times when it was a struggle; there were times when it brought sorrow.

How should we face our times of troubles and sorrows in our very short and insignificant lives?

We need to understand them in light of God’s judgement.

We need to see them as the power of his anger at work.

It’s because of God’s anger against our sins, it’s because of our secret sins, that we are living short, sorrowful lives.

To understand our mortality, our impending death, our short and insignificant lives in light of God’s judgement is where true wisdom starts.

Look with me at verses 11-12, “Who understands the power of your anger?

Your wrath matches the fear that is due you. Teach us to number our days carefully so that we may develop wisdom in our hearts.”

If you want wisdom in your hearts, ask God to teach you to number your days carefully.

Ask God to teach you to consider your days in light of God’s wrath – that’s how we have wisdom in our hearts.

God is rightfully angry at your sins and we should live in fear of him, we should serve him, we should worship him.

But we don’t; we fall woefully short of what we owe him.

And so, no, we’re not merely insignificant specks of dust.

We’re insignificant specks of dusts, we have sinned against God, we placed ourselves in the centre of the universe instead of worshipping the God who is rightfully at the centre of the universe.

We are rightly condemned to a life of sorrow and trouble, which will one day with absolute certainty end in death.

An insignificant speck of dust’s prayer and the faithful God’s answer

But that’s not how the psalm ends.

There’s hope for these insignificant specks of dust.

These insignificant specks of dusts prays and the faithful God answers.

Look with me at verse 13, “Lord—how long? Turn and have compassion on your servants.”

Those who have wisdom in their hearts will pray to God.

They know that God is the one who condemns but he is also the only One who can save.

There’s a play on words here:

God’s judgement to us is, “Return to dust”

Our prayer to God is, “Turn and have compassion on us, how long O Lord?

We are numbering our days, but for how long?

How long before you will have compassion on us?

How long before we find relief from your judgement?

How long before we find hope in our hopelessness?”

O Lord, how long?

God’s answer, “Until Jesus comes”

For Moses, it was around 1,500 years.

In God’s timing, God sent Jesus who turns to us and have compassion on us.

Jesus himself says this at the beginning of his earthly ministry, “The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God has come near. Repent and believe the good news!” (Mark 1:15)

Now’s the time of God’s compassion; now’s the time of God’s salvation!

Look with me at verse 14, “Satisfy us in the morning with your faithful love

so that we may shout with joy and be glad all our days.” What a beautiful verse!

What can give us meaning in our short and insignificant lives?

Or better still, who can give us meaning in our short and insignificant lives?

God!

The eternal One, the one who made the heavens and the earth; he is the only one who can give significance to our insignificant lives.

Earlier in the psalm, we’re described as the plant who spouts and grows in the morning but fades away at night.

Here, there is a new morning – new start, new beginning.

But this morning is one that continues.

For those who have been satisfied by God, there’s a new and glorious morning.

How will God satisfy us? With his faithful love.

For those who have his faithful love, we know for sure that he will love us always.

His love is faithful.

And he offers his faithful love to those who are in Christ Jesus, his one and only Son.

Jesus, who is the Eternal One, became temporal.

That’s what we celebrated last week at Christmas.

More than that, Jesus gave up his life to save us from our imminent death and judgement.

That’s what we’ll celebrate in a few months at Easter.

Jesus, the Eternal One died for us so that we, the temporal, insignificant ones can live forever.

Jesus also rose again to show us that this hope that he offers is real.

Jesus died and rose to show that God is satisfied with what Jesus has done for us.

Jesus died and rose from the dead to show that all of God’s anger against our iniquities and our secret sins have been completely satisfied.

Since God is satisfied with us, we can now find satisfaction in him.

We can ask God to satisfy us because Jesus has satisfied God.

For we are satisfied only when God is satisfied with us in Jesus.

And so, Moses’ prayer for God to satisfy us is answered in Jesus.

It’s because we have satisfaction in God, we can shout and be glad, all our days.

Our years may still be full of struggles and sorrows, but because of the satisfaction we have in God through Jesus, we can ask God to make us glad even in those days.

For even in those days of struggle and sorrow, we look forward to seeing God’s work and his glory.

And God’s work and God’s glory is shown most clearly and shown most brightly in the Person of Jesus.

We long for God’s work to be seen and his glory shown by his servants and to their children.

That’s who the gospel of Jesus is for!

It’s for families, for the extended families, the whole clans.

The good news of Jesus is for those who are near and for those far away.

It is in light of God’s work that we pray with Moses at the end of the Psalm, “Let the favor of the Lord our God be on us; establish for us the work of our hands—

establish the work of our hands!”

We are small and insignificant in the grand scheme of things, but God has chosen us in Jesus to do his will.

God’s chosen his people, the Christians, to do something significant for him.

Only God himself can bring significance to the works of our hands.

The works of our hands, our achievements, our goals, will only have significance if they somehow reflect the works of God’s hands.

That’s why Paul tells us in 1 Corinthians 15:58, “You know that your labor in the Lord is not in vain.”

As we begin the new year, I don’t know whether you were satisfied with last year.

Maybe you wished you had achieved more of your goals, or that you had a better time at school, or that you enjoyed yourself more.

But all these will pass away.

They won’t bring true satisfaction.

Only Jesus can truly satisfy.

And so, pray to God, “Please, God, satisfy us with your faithful love in the morning.”

It’s a bold prayer – satisfy us, God.

It’s also a humble prayer, for we know that we can’t find true satisfaction in ourselves on in our world.

Only God’s faithful love in Jesus can satisfy us.

And so, are we insignificant specks of dust floating through space and time?

Yes, we’re small and short-lived. We’re but a blip in history.

But in another sense, no.

We’re not merely small and insignificant specks of dust.

We’re worse than that!

We’re small and insignificant specks of dust that have sinned against the almighty, eternal and just God.

We’re small and insignificant specks of dust that deserve God’s wrath.

And yet, we’re small and insignificant specks of dust that God has compassion upon.

God’s sent His own Son to save small and insignificant specks of dust like us.

For those who trust in him, who has responded to his call, we’re no longer small and insignificant specks of dust but sons and daughters of God, precious in his sight, looking forward to eternal life with him.

And so we pray, “Please God, Satisfy us in the morning with your faithful love.

Satisfy us because Jesus has satisfied you.

Satisfy us according to your faithful love in Jesus.”

And so, friends, make this your prayer this year: Satisfy us in the morning with your faithful love.

Pray that you will come to know God’s faithful love for you and his church throughout this year.

Read up on God’s faithful love in his word, the Bible.

Ask God to teach you to number your days carefully, so that you might develop wisdom in your hearts,

Wisdom to distinguish sift what only appears to be significant from what’s truly significant.

Pray that you will plan and work this year so that in 1000 years’ time, in a blink of God’s eyes, God will say to you, ‘Well done, good and faithful servant! You were faithful over a few things; I will put you in charge of many things. Share your master’s joy.’

It might be nurturing young ones here at Church to grow in Christ,

It might be supporting Christian missionaries overseas,

It might be sharing the good news of Jesus in a conversation with someone at home.

Be faithful in these things, find your satisfaction in them for they bring glory to God.

Find your satisfaction in the faithful love of God in Jesus Christ.

And when you’re tempted to find satisfaction outside of Christ, pray, “Satisfy us in the morning with your faithful love.”

Here are the three questions for this week:

What temporal goals am I tempted to spend my life on?

Are there areas in my life where I want to rule instead of letting the almighty God rule?

What will satisfaction with God’s faithful love in Christ look like in my life?

Friends, let’s pray,

“Father, please, satisfy us in the morning with your faithful love so that we may shout with joy and be glad all our days. As we face this new year Lord, please turn our eyes firmly on Jesus, who has satisfied you by his death and resurrection and who alone can satisfy us. We bring before you all that we plan, all that we say, all that we do. Please establish the work of our hands so that you will be glorified among the nations, among our community, among our lives, here at St Paul’s Kogarah. This we ask in the name of Jesus, Amen.”ß
