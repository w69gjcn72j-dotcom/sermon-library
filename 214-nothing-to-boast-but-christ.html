Nothing to Boast but Christ

Philippians 3:1-11 · 2020
Big IdeaNothing compares with gaining Christ, in whom we have righteousness from God.

Nothing to boast except for Christ

Passage: Philippians 3:1-11

Big Idea: Nothing compares with gaining Christ, in whom we have righteousness from God.

1. Nothing to boast but Christ (3:1-6)

Boasting in the flesh vs glorying in Jesus Christ

Paul’s blameless righteousness in the law

2. Everything a loss compared with Christ (3:7-8)

Recalibrating our values

The surpassing worthy of knowing and gaining Jesus

3. Having the righteousness from God in Christ (3:9-11)

Righteousness not from the law

Righteousness through faith in Christ

Righteousness that leads to knowing Christ and the power of his resurrection

This time last year, we’ve celebrated out 150th Anniversary.

It was amazing to see so many of our friends and families gathered together to celebrate our Anniversary!

There aren’t too many churches in Sydney that can claim they’ve been meeting for the past 151 years!

That’s a great thing to claim!

But as a Church, we’ve got something even bigger to boast about.

It’s a boast that I hope we’ll always be known for.

Before we look into it, let’s pray.

“Father, by your Spirit, please open our eyes and our hearts so that we may understand your word. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”

1. Nothing to boast but Christ (3:1-6)

Look with me at Philippians 3:1, ‘In addition, my brothers and sisters, rejoice in the Lord. To write to you again about this is no trouble for me and is a safeguard for you.’

Paul asks the Philippian church to rejoice in the Lord.

Paul wants them to be happy in the Lord.

Despite being in prison, Paul rejoices in the Lord.

Last week, we read about Epaphroditus.

He was sick and was about to die.

But God was merciful to Epaphroditus and healed him.

And now, Paul will send Epaphroditus back to the Philippians.

When they see him, they are to receive him with great joy.

Paul wants both the Philippian Church and Epaphroditus to rejoice, to be happy.

Paul longs to see them smile.

But then, there is a change of tone.

Look with me at verse 2.

“Watch out for the dogs, watch out for the evil workers, watch out for those who mutilate the flesh.”

Mutilators? Evil workers? Dogs?

That sounds rather harsh!

Back in Paul’s days, there was a big divide between the Jews and the Gentiles, that is people who are not Jews.

The Jews saw themselves as the special people of God.

And as a people chosen by God himself, they would say that they were the ones who were right with God.

As long as they were Jews, following God’s laws, they saw themselves as being right with God.

But over time, some of them began to think very highly of themselves and they started to look down on people from other nations.

That’s when they started calling non-Jews ‘dogs’.

Unlike the Jews, the Gentiles were not special; they were inferior, like dogs.

No one likes to be called a dog.

It was an insult to the non-Jews, the Gentiles.

Even when some of the Jews believed in Jesus, they believed that the Gentiles must become Jews first before they can belong to God.

If a Gentile wants to belong to God, then they need to identify themselves with the Jews, who have been known as God’s people for two thousand years before Jesus’ time.

This means that they would need to go through the rite of circumcision and follow the laws of Moses as well.

But Paul says that that’s not right.

You don’t need to be a Jew in order to belong to God.

Look with me at verse three, ‘For we are the circumcision, the ones who worship by the Spirit of God, boast in Christ Jesus, and do not put confidence in the flesh— ‘

Paul says that it’s the Christians who are the special ones, chosen especially to belong to God.

It’s the Christians who worship and serve God by the Spirit of God.

Christians are special to God, not because we are in ourselves better than anyone else.

No, the Christian’s boast, the Christian’s glory, is in Christ Jesus and in Christ Jesus alone.

Our confidence is in Jesus.

We don’t put confidence in ourselves, we don’t put confidence in our flesh.

Now Paul is not just saying this as someone who is jealous of others who have confidence in their flesh.

He’s not one of those have nots who complain about those who’s got it all.

No, Paul himself is actually one of those who can easily boast in himself.

Religiously speaking, he’s got it all!

Paul was a Jew; he was from a good Jewish family who circumcised him; he was an Israelite, in fact a Benjaminite.

His was a Hebrew of Hebrews.

Not only was he a Jew by birth; he was also a Jew in practice.

He was a Pharisee, the strictest and most widely respected religious people of his time.

He also persecuted the church.

Out of his zeal to follow God, he hunted Christians down.

He went above and beyond what was required of him.

He was utterly spotless in obeying God’s law.

According to the law, he was righteous and faultless.

If being Jewish is all that it takes to be righteous, if being Jewish is all that it takes to be confident before God,

then Paul would be righteous.

It’s a little like the privileges that come with being, say, an Australian.

As an Australian citizen, you have many privileges.

As an Australian citizen, you’re eligible for an Australian passport; it’s your privilege; it’s your right.

As an Australian Passport holder, you can use your passport at the SmartGates when you come back from overseas.

You just simply show your passport and you can walk right through.

While people from other nationalities wait at the immigration line, you can skip the queue and walk through the SmartGate

Some of the Jews thought along the same line.

They think that if you’re a Jew, you have the special privilege of being God’s special people.

They think that if you’re as a Jew, then it’s as if you have a passport that lets you walk through a spiritual SmartGate.

While Paul is as Jewish as you can get, he doesn’t rest his confidence in being a Jew.

To be a Jew doesn’t give you a spiritual passport that allows you free access to God.

Paul’s standing before God is not based on whether he’s a Jew or not.

The only thing that matters to Paul is whether he has Jesus Christ or not.

To come before God, you don’t need to become a Jew; you need Jesus.

In fact, Paul considers everything a loss compared with having Christ.

2. Everything a loss compared with Christ (3:7-8)

Recalibrating our values

Look with me at verse 7, ‘But everything that was a gain to me, I have considered to be a loss because of Christ.’

Paul is saying that it doesn’t matter whether you’re born as a Jew or not.

In fact, there’s nothing that we can do which can make us say that we deserve to come into God’s presence.

Whatever it is that we achieve, whatever accomplishment that we can ever boast in, contributes to nothing when it comes to where we stand before God.

In fact, Paul calls these losses.

If Paul had spend his time reading the Bible, praying in churches, following the strictest rules, thinking that God will accept him because he did theses things, then he would have been wasting his time.

There’s only one way, one person who can secure our standing before God; only Jesus can lead us safely to God.

No one else, nothing else can lead us to God.

No one else, nothing else, compares with Jesus.

Here, in this verse Paul tells us that we need to recalibrate our values.

To recalibrate something means to reset the value of a machine to a reference.

One machine that we recalibrate all the time is the scale.

Does anyone here have scales at home?

We use scales to measure the weight of something.

If you’re going away on the plane, the person at the counter will ask you to put your luggage on the scale to make sure you’re not adding too much weight to the plane.

Every now and then, for a variety of reasons, the scales can become inaccurate.

And so, every now and then, the scales are tested and recalibrated, or adjusted, to make sure it’s giving the accurate reading.

In our passage for today, Paul wants us to recalibrate our value.

He wants to reset our values, in order that we might recalibrate what we have to the reference point of Christ.

We are to value the things of our lives, the activities, the accomplishments, the goals of our lives, according to Jesus.

Paul could list a lot of things in his lives as achievements.

From the world’s point of view, there were many things in his life that Paul could be proud of.

But none of these things are worth comparing to what Paul has in Jesus Christ.

Jesus is of supreme worth.

There is nothing better than knowing Jesus.

To Paul, Jesus is the treasure that’s worth more than everything that he has.

He has no confidence in what he could achieve.

Rather, he has complete confidence in what Jesus and in what he has done.

Look with me at verse eight, ‘More than that, I also consider everything to be a loss in view of the surpassing value of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord. Because of him I have suffered the loss of all things and consider them as dung, so that I may gain Christ’

He considers everything a loss compared with the awesomeness of knowing Christ.

He has Christ, what else does he need?

For Jesus’s sake, he’s lost everything.

He would rather have Jesus than all these things.

It’s as if Paul is saying, ‘Give me Jesus, please, give me Jesus and that will be enough.

If being popular, secure and influential means losing Jesus, then please, take these things away and give me Jesus instead.’

Why does Paul want Jesus so much?

Why is Jesus so important to Paul?

It’s because when we have Jesus we have the righteousness from God.

3. Having the righteousness from God in Christ (3:9-11)

Look with me at verse 9, ‘and be found in him, not having a righteousness of my own from the law, but one that is through faith in Christ—the righteousness from God based on faith.’

To be righteous means to be right with God; it means being in a right relationship with God.

To be in Christ means that we’re not coming before God with a righteousness of our own.

For those who are in Christ, we’re coming to God with the righteousness that comes through faith in Christ.

Jesus, the righteous one, died for unrighteous people like you and me.

Because Jesus died in our place, He made us righteous in God’s eyes.

And so, when God looks at people who trust in Jesus from now on, he looks at us through the death of his Son.

We’re made righteous because of what Jesus has done.

Everyone, everyone, who has ever lived will one day face God, the Judge of all men.

On that day, we will be judged on whether we are in a right relationship with him.

On that day, we will be judged according to whether we’re righteous or not.

We can try our best to be righteous; we can try their best to be right with God.

But no one has ever gone into heaven by trying.

Only Jesus can take us to heaven.

It’s a little like the experience of some of my friends when they escaped from Cambodia.

Earlier last century, there were many Chinese living in Cambodia and many were quite well-off.

But then war broke out in the 70’s and they had to leave everything behind.

Some of my friends had big houses with maids serving them, but they had to leave everything behind.

To escape, some of them had to walk through jungles in the middle of night.

It would be pretty useless for them to bring maps, because they wouldn’t be able to use them, since it’s pretty dark.

To avoid getting caught, they had to be really quiet, and walk in the dark, so that they wouldn’t be seen.

And so, they wouldn’t be allowed to use torches.

What would happen if they used their torches?

They would be caught.

They had to rely on somebody who actually knew the jungle so well that they could lead them through the dark.

They had to find someone they could trust, someone they had confidence in to lead them to safety.

Jesus is the only Person who can take us safely to God.

We can try using our methods; we can try to be good, we can try to devise our own way to get to God.

But at the end of the day, they are all rubbish.

They are useless; they cannot get us any closer to God.

Only Jesus, God the Son, can take us to God.

If there’s one thing that all of us here need to get sorted right now, it’s whether we’re ready for our meeting with God.

All of us will meet God one day.

Sadly, this year is one of those years where many sad and unexpected things have happened to millions of people around the world.

Sad things have happened before, but this year, things were worse than usual because of COVID-19.

By now, almost one million around the world that we know of had died because of COVID-19.

One million lives who started this year not knowing that this would be their last.

Some are elderly, some are young.

Some were ready, others weren’t.

Are you ready to meet God?

And if you are to meet God, why are you ready?

Is the basis of your confidence based on rubbish, or is it based on Jesus Christ?

If you’re not ready to meet God yet, then please, please, come and speak with me afterwards.

It’s vital that you’re ready to meet God.

What’s more important than to know that you’re ready to meet God?

If you’re not ready yet, then please make this your priority.

No one has gone into heaven because they are good enough.

Remember, only Jesus Christ is good enough.

It’s only in Jesus Christ, that you can have the righteousness from God.

Don’t place your confidence in anything else.

Get to know Jesus.

For those of us who already know Jesus, don’t stop growing in your knowledge of Jesus.

Look with me at verse 10, ‘My goal is to know him and the power of his resurrection and the fellowship of his sufferings, being conformed to his death, ’

Doesn’t Paul already know Jesus?

Of course he does. But he wants to know Jesus more and more.

That’s what someone who knows Jesus will do – they’ll want to know Jesus more and more.

They also would want to know about the power of his resurrection, because it’s only by the power of his resurrection that we can endure and persevere until the very end.

To know him will mean sharing in his sufferings, just at Jesus has said, "If the world hates you, keep in mind that it hated me first.”

Even so, even if knowing him brings suffering, even if it means death, it’s worth it.

It’s worth it because Jesus is worth so much more.

To know him is to be made right with God, to know him is to know the power of his resurrection.

To share in his suffering is a badge of honour for those who know Jesus, for Jesus considers them his partners, someone that he can share his sufferings with.

That’s Paul’s goal, to know Jesus, to know the power of his resurrection, to be his partner in suffering, to be conformed to his death.

Paul’s ready to meet God, because he looks beyond his death.

Rather he looks forward to his own resurrection from the dead.

Look with me at the final verse, verse 11: 11 assuming that I will somehow reach the resurrection from among the dead.

That’s not to say that he’s doubting his resurrection.

He’s not sure exactly when his resurrection is, because he doesn’t exactly when he’d die.

But he does know that his resurrection will be tied to his knowledge of Jesus.

And so, his goal is to know Jesus, more and more.

What does this mean for us?

Three things

Firstly, what do we boast in? What are we proud of?

While some of us might not explicitly say what it is that we’re proud of, it often doesn’t take long to find out what it is that we’re proud if in our conversations.

What do we talk about all the time?

Do we keep talking about how much we’re making?

Is our wealth what we’re proud of?

Do we keep showing off our knowledge, our talents?

Are our accomplishments what we’re proud of?

Friends, all these will fade away.

In the eyes of eternity, these are all rubbish.

Don’t rely on them; rely on the eternal Christ.

Second, we need to recalibrate our values.

We live in a year where many things have stopped.

We live in a time when many of us have our lives changed dramatically.

In many ways, our Church had to stop and do Church differently.

It’s a time for pause.

Don’t waste this opportunity.

Use this season as a time to recalibrate our values.

What do we value the most?

Again, many of us might say Jesus or the Gospel.

But a more accurate indicator of what we value the most is our budget and our diary.

We might say that Jesus is the most precious person in our lives and that we want people to hear about Jesus.

But does the way we spend our money reflect that?

We might say that we are servants of Jesus and that he is our Lord.

But how many hours in the week are spent in serving him?

This week, be honest with yourself and be honest with God, and spend some time recalibrating your values.

What area in your life needs to be recalibrated?

Finally, make it your goal to know Jesus.

That was Paul’s goal, even though he already knew Jesus quite well by the time he wrote this letter.

How will you grow in your knowledge of Jesus?

You’re never too young or too old to grow in your knowledge of Jesus.

Join the Chinese Bible Study on Saturday, the KOG Bible studies on Sundays.

We hope to restart an English Bible study again soon.

Whatever it is, make it your goal to know Jesus.

No matter how much time it takes, no matter how difficult it might be, it’s worth knowing Jesus.

Make it your goal.

And so, here are the three questions for this week:

What do we boast in?

How would you recalibrate your values in light of Jesus?

How will you get to know Jesus more this week?

If there’s one thing that St Paul’s Kogarah is known for, it is Jesus.

It’s here that you hear and meet Jesus.

Boast in nothing but Jesus, set all your values according to the supreme worth of Jesus, rest in the righteousness that God gives you in Jesus.
