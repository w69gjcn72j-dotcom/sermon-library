From Paradox to Doxology

Luke 5:1–26 · Sunday 1 November 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah · Sermon by Rev David Yung

Big Idea: Glorify God for the incredible Jesus, who recruits the unworthy, cleanses the unclean, honours the faithful friends and forgives the immovable sinner.

Aim: by the end, students will

see that Jesus wants people the world leaves out — the failures, the outsiders and the stuck;

own that they don’t deserve to be near Jesus, and hear his “Don’t be afraid”;

pray one clear prayer for a friend who doesn’t know Jesus, and plan to bring them to him.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • Tell us about a time you practised hard at something you’re good at — sport, music, a game — and it still went badly. How did you feel when someone else told you how to do it?
  • Think about someone left out at school — eating lunch alone, or not added to the group chat. What does it feel like to be on the outside?

Read: Luke 5:1–26 (CSB)

1 As the crowd was pressing in on Jesus to hear God’s word, he was standing by Lake Gennesaret. 2 He saw two boats at the edge of the lake; the fishermen had left them and were washing their nets. 3 He got into one of the boats, which belonged to Simon, and asked him to put out a little from the land. Then he sat down and was teaching the crowds from the boat.

4 When he had finished speaking, he said to Simon, “Put out into deep water and let down your nets for a catch.” 5 “Master,” Simon replied, “we’ve worked hard all night long and caught nothing. But if you say so, I’ll let down the nets.” 6 When they did this, they caught a great number of fish, and their nets began to tear. 7 So they signaled to their partners in the other boat to come and help them; they came and filled both boats so full that they began to sink.

8 When Simon Peter saw this, he fell at Jesus’s knees and said, “Go away from me, because I’m a sinful man, Lord!” 9 For he and all those with him were amazed at the catch of fish they had taken, 10 and so were James and John, Zebedee’s sons, who were Simon’s partners. “Don’t be afraid,” Jesus told Simon. “From now on you will be catching people.” 11 Then they brought the boats to land, left everything, and followed him.

12 While he was in one of the towns, a man was there who had leprosy all over him. He saw Jesus, fell facedown, and begged him: “Lord, if you are willing, you can make me clean.” 13 Reaching out his hand, Jesus touched him, saying, “I am willing; be made clean,” and immediately the leprosy left him. 14 Then he ordered him to tell no one: “But go and show yourself to the priest, and offer what Moses commanded for your cleansing as a testimony to them.” 15 But the news about him spread even more, and large crowds would come together to hear him and to be healed of their sicknesses. 16 Yet he often withdrew to deserted places and prayed.

17 On one of those days while he was teaching, Pharisees and teachers of the law were sitting there who had come from every village of Galilee and Judea, and also from Jerusalem. And the Lord’s power to heal was in him. 18 Just then some men came, carrying on a stretcher a man who was paralyzed. They tried to bring him in and set him down before him. 19 Since they could not find a way to bring him in because of the crowd, they went up on the roof and lowered him on the stretcher through the roof tiles into the middle of the crowd before Jesus.

20 Seeing their faith he said, “Friend, your sins are forgiven.” 21 Then the scribes and the Pharisees began to think to themselves: “Who is this man who speaks blasphemies? Who can forgive sins but God alone?” 22 But perceiving their thoughts, Jesus replied to them, “Why are you thinking this in your hearts? 23 Which is easier: to say, ‘Your sins are forgiven,’ or to say, ‘Get up and walk’? 24 But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins”—he told the paralyzed man, “I tell you: Get up, take your stretcher, and go home.”

25 Immediately he got up before them, picked up what he had been lying on, and went home glorifying God. 26 Then everyone was astounded, and they were giving glory to God. And they were filled with awe and said, “We have seen incredible things today.”

Context

Jesus is getting popular: crowds are pushing in to hear him. In Luke 5 he meets three kinds of people. A fisherman who has failed all night. A man with leprosy, who by law had to live away from everyone. And a man who can’t move, carried by his friends. Each time Jesus does something hard to believe, and each time people end up praising God.

Discuss

  1. vv.4–5 — Simon is the professional fisherman; Jesus is the teacher. Why do you think Simon says, “But if you say so”? Is there an area of your life where you think you know better than Jesus?
  2. v.8 — After the huge catch, Peter says, “Go away from me, because I’m a sinful man, Lord!” Why would a miracle make him feel like that?
  3. vv.10–11 — Jesus doesn’t go away. He recruits Peter to “catch people”. What’s surprising about who Jesus picks? What did Peter leave behind?
  4. vv.12–13 — People with leprosy had to live apart and shout “Unclean!” if anyone came near. What’s amazing about what Jesus does?
  5. v.12 — The leper prays, “Lord, if you are willing, you can make me clean.” He is clear about what he wants, but leaves it up to Jesus. What would it look like for you to pray clear prayers instead of “safe” ones?
  6. vv.18–20 — The friends couldn’t get in the door, so they broke the roof! Jesus “saw their faith”. What did their faith look like? Who is a friend you could bring to Jesus?
  7. vv.20–24 — Why were the religious leaders upset when Jesus said, “Your sins are forgiven”? How does the healing prove Jesus has the right to forgive?
  8. vv.25–26 — Everyone said, “We have seen incredible things today,” and praised God. Which of the three stories surprises you most? How will you praise God for Jesus this week?

Live It Out

KOG 3·2·1·0 — pray for 3, connect with 2, invite 1, 0 left unloved.

  • Pray for 3 — three friends or family members who don’t know Jesus yet. Pray for them by name every day, with a clear prayer.
  • Connect with 2 — be a real friend this week: a lunch, a walk or a proper chat (not just a reaction in the group chat).
  • Invite 1 — invite one of your three to KOGYouth, to church on Sunday, or to our Community Christmas Concert on 13 December.
  • 0 left unloved — spot the person on their own at school or at youth, and sit with them. Jesus touched the person everyone avoided.

This week’s challenge: Break the roof! Pick one friend who doesn’t know Jesus. Every day this week pray, “Lord, if you are willing ...” for them by name, and with a friend from youth, invite them to KOGYouth or the Christmas concert.

Memory verse: “Don’t be afraid,” Jesus told Simon. “From now on you will be catching people.” (Luke 5:10)

Pray

  • Thank God that Jesus wants people like us — unworthy, unclean and stuck — and forgives our sins.
  • Ask God to help us pray clear prayers, and to give us the faith of the friends who broke the roof.
  • Pray for your 3 friends by name — that God would bring them to Jesus to be forgiven.

Leader’s Notes

Framing. Joyfully point to Jesus. Some students may not be Christians yet; never corner anyone. The big surprise of Luke 5 is that Jesus wants the people others leave out. Let students be honest about failing and feeling “unclean” — Jesus isn’t afraid to touch them.

Wrong turn 1: “I need to clean myself up before Jesus will want me.” No — Jesus recruited Peter when Peter knew he was a sinner, and touched the leper while he was still unclean. To come before God, you first realise you’re not worthy.

Wrong turn 2: “If I pray clearly enough, God has to give me what I want.” No — the leper said “if you are willing”. Healing is a gift, not a transaction. We learn God’s will from his word and by praying.

Q1 — Fishing was Simon’s job and he’d just failed all night. “If you say so” is reluctant respect. We often think Jesus doesn’t know our stuff (school, sport, friends) as well as we do.

Q2 — No fish, then fish everywhere: not luck but God at work. Peter realised he wasn’t good enough to be near Jesus, and called him “Lord”.

Q3 — Normal recruiting picks the best. Jesus picked someone both of them knew was unworthy — the paradox of grace. Catching people = telling people the good news so they’re saved. Peter left everything: “burn your boats”.

Q4 — Jesus touched him. Lepers were treated like the living dead. Jesus wasn’t made unclean; his touch made the man clean — reverse contamination.

Q5 — Safe prayers are ones we don’t expect God to answer (e.g. “world peace”, prayed without caring). Pray clearly, and trust God’s will.

Q6 — Giving up isn’t faith; breaking the roof is. “Their” faith — plural — the friends’ and probably the man’s.

Q7 — Only the person sinned against can forgive (if someone stole your phone, I can’t forgive them). Sin is against God, so Jesus is claiming to be God. Forgiveness can’t be seen; healing can — so the healing proves his authority.

Q8 — “Incredible” is paradoxa (paradox); “glorify” is doxazo (doxology). The paradoxes of Jesus aren’t just to marvel at — they lead us to worship.

Church vision. “From faith to faith”: faith is real when each of us comes to Jesus ourselves.
