Young Adults (19–30)

Hope Against Hope

Romans 4:1-25 · Sunday 9 August 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah · For young adults (19-30)

Big Idea: Be fully and joyfully convinced, hoping against hope, in the God who credits righteousness to those who trust in him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead.

Aim: (1) See that righteousness is credited, not earned or performed; (2) name where you are hoping against hope and hand it to the God who raises the dead; (3) commit to one person to pray for and invite.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60-75 minutes.

1. Connect (10 min)

  • You skip the credits at the end of everything you watch. If your life so far had a credits roll, whose names would be on it — and who would be surprised to be there?
  • What is the one thing on your CV, your feed or your group chat reputation that you would least want deleted tomorrow? What does that tell you about where your worth is parked?

2. Read — Romans 4:1-25 (CSB)

1 What then will we say that Abraham, our forefather according to the flesh, has found? 2 If Abraham was justified by works, he has something to boast about — but not before God. 3 For what does the Scripture say? Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him for righteousness. 4 Now to the one who works, pay is not credited as a gift, but as something owed. 5 But to the one who does not work, but believes on him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is credited for righteousness.

6 Likewise, David also speaks of the blessing of the person to whom God credits righteousness apart from works: 7 Blessed are those whose lawless acts are forgiven and whose sins are covered. 8 Blessed is the person the Lord will never charge with sin.

[vv.9-15, read aloud from your Bibles: Abraham was credited righteous while still uncircumcised, so circumcision was a seal on a righteousness already received — making him the father of all who believe, circumcised or not. The promise that he would inherit the world came not through the law but through the righteousness that comes by faith; if law-keepers are the heirs, faith is made empty and the promise nullified, because the law produces wrath.]

16 This is why the promise is by faith, so that it may be according to grace, to guarantee it to all the descendants — not only to the one who is of the law but also to the one who is of Abraham’s faith. He is the father of us all. 17 As it is written: I have made you the father of many nations — in the presence of the God in whom he believed, the one who gives life to the dead and calls things into existence that do not exist.

18 He believed, hoping against hope, so that he became the father of many nations according to what had been spoken: So will your descendants be. 19 He did not weaken in faith when he considered his own body to be already dead (since he was about a hundred years old) and also considered the deadness of Sarah’s womb. 20 He did not waver in unbelief at God’s promise but was strengthened in his faith and gave glory to God, 21 because he was fully convinced that what God had promised, he was also able to do. 22 Therefore, it was credited to him for righteousness.

23 Now it was credited to him was not written for Abraham alone, but also for us. 24 It will be credited to us who believe in him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead. 25 He was delivered up for our trespasses and raised for our justification.

3. Context

Paul has just said everyone has sinned and that God declares sinners righteous freely through Jesus (3:21-31). The natural comeback: surely Abraham was the exception. Paul goes to the receipts and shows Abraham was credited righteous by trusting God, years before circumcision and long before the law. Then he turns it on us: the God Abraham trusted is the God who raised Jesus.

4. Discuss

  1. Read verses 1-3. What did Abraham “find”? Why does it matter that Paul picks the person everyone assumed had earned it?
  2. Verses 4-5 contrast a wage with a gift. What is the difference, and why is “justifies the ungodly” such a shocking phrase?
  3. Verses 6-8 quote Psalm 32. What does David say the blessed life actually is? How is that different from what your feed calls blessed?
  4. Verses 9-12: why does Paul insist so hard on the order — faith first, then the sign? What would change if the order were reversed?
  5. Verse 15 says “the law produces wrath.” What is the law good at, and what can it never give you?
  6. Verses 18-21: Abraham looked squarely at a hundred-year-old body and a dead womb and was still fully convinced. What does that tell you about what faith actually is — and is not?
  7. In verses 23-25 Paul says this was written for us. What are we asked to believe, and what has already been settled before we contribute anything?
  8. Call-out culture says you are your worst post; performance culture says you are your best result; Romans 4 says you are credited righteous in Christ. Which are you living out of this week? Name what you have quietly written off as dead, and say what trusting God there would look like.

5. Live It Out

KOG 3 · 2 · 1 · 0

  • Pray for 3: three names — a housemate, a course mate, a colleague. Daily, by name, this week.
  • Connect with 2: two of them get real contact. Not a like — a meal, a gym session, a proper conversation.
  • Invite 1: invite one of them to church, to this group, or to ALIVE South Sydney on 12 September.
  • 0 left unloved: who has gone quiet in the group chat? Message them tonight, before you go to bed.

This week’s challenge: Pick the person or situation you have quietly written off as dead. Pray for it by name every day this week, and tell one Christian friend that you are doing it so they can ask you on Sunday.

Memory verse: “He did not waver in unbelief at God’s promise but was strengthened in his faith and gave glory to God, because he was fully convinced that what God had promised, he was also able to do.” (Romans 4:20-21)

6. Pray

  • Thank God that he credits righteousness to the ungodly who trust him — that you do not have to perform your way into his approval.
  • Pray by name for your three people, and for the nerve to say something real rather than something safe.
  • Ask God to strengthen your faith where the situation looks dead, so that he gets the glory rather than your own coping.

7. Leader’s Notes

Point joyfully to Jesus. Assume there are guests and not-yet-Christians in the room, and people who feel behind in life. Never put anyone on the spot about their sin or their faith — go first with your own honesty and let the passage do the work.

Brief answers to key questions

  • Q1: Abraham found he had nothing to boast about — idols for most of his first 75 years, and lies to save his own skin. If the poster child is credited rather than paid, nobody is paid.
  • Q2: Wages are a debt owed; a gift is free. God does not wave sin through — Romans 3:25 showed he paid for it himself in Jesus. Justifying the ungodly is costly grace, not a loophole.
  • Q4: Faith came first; the sign followed as a seal. That order makes Abraham father of everyone who believes, not just those with the right background — which is exactly why you belong here.
  • Q6: Faith is not pretending the facts are different. Abraham weighed them honestly and was still fully convinced God was able. Even that faith is God’s gift — trusting God fully is harder, not easier, than relying on ourselves.
  • Q7: The God who gives life to the dead is the God who raised Jesus. Verse 25: delivered up for our trespasses, raised for our justification — finished before you contribute anything.
