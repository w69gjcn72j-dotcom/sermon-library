Home Groups (adults)

Beware the friendly advice of the devil

Luke 4:1–15 · Sunday 18 October 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

For adult home groups and midweek fellowships · Sermon by Rev David Yung

Big Idea: Reject the friendly devil’s temptation to be your own man, and rely on Jesus, who triumphed as the Son of God by trusting his Father’s word.

Aim: by the end of the study, group members will

see why the devil’s offers sound so friendly — be your own provider, your own king, the orchestrator of your own salvation;

name one place where they are tempted to be their “own man or woman”, and learn to pause and pray before acting;

run to Jesus, the Son who triumphed, for mercy and grace in time of need.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

1. Connect (10 min)

  • Think of the most friendly, convincing salesperson or adviser you have ever met — someone who made a bad idea sound like plain common sense. What made them so persuasive?
  • At a family dinner, who is the “head of the table”, and what happens if someone starts on the food before they sit down? Where else in life do we jump the gun?

2. Read: Luke 4:1–15 (CSB)

1 Then Jesus left the Jordan, full of the Holy Spirit, and was led by the Spirit in the wilderness 2 for forty days to be tempted by the devil. He ate nothing during those days, and when they were over, he was hungry. 3 The devil said to him, “If you are the Son of God, tell this stone to become bread.” 4 But Jesus answered him, “It is written: Man must not live on bread alone.”

5 Then he took him up and showed him all the kingdoms of the world in a moment of time. 6 The devil said to him, “I will give you their splendor and all this authority, because it has been given over to me, and I can give it to anyone I want. 7 If you, then, will worship me, all of it will be yours.” 8 Jesus answered him, “It is written: Worship the Lord your God, and serve him only.”

9 Then he took him to Jerusalem, had him stand on the pinnacle of the temple, and said to him, “If you are the Son of God, throw yourself down from here. 10 For it is written: He will give his angels orders concerning you, to protect you, 11 and they will support you with their hands, so that you will not strike your foot against a stone.” 12 Jesus answered him, “It is said: Do not test the Lord your God.”

13 After the devil had finished every temptation, he departed from him for a time. 14 Then Jesus returned in the power of the Spirit to Galilee, and news about him spread throughout the entire vicinity. 15 He was teaching in their synagogues, being praised by everyone.

3. Context

Luke 4 follows straight on from Jesus’ baptism, where the Father called him “my beloved Son”, and from Luke’s genealogy, which ends “son of Adam, son of God”. Led by the Spirit into the wilderness for forty days, Jesus faces three offers from the devil, and each time he answers from Deuteronomy, the book that recalls how God’s people lived — and failed — in the wilderness. Underneath all three is one question: what kind of Son will Jesus be — one who lives for himself, or one who trusts his Father’s word?

4. Discuss

  1. (vv.1–2) Jesus is “full of the Holy Spirit” and “led by the Spirit” into the wilderness to be tempted. What does that tell us about temptation, and about who Jesus is? In what way is Jesus the Son of God differently from us (compare Luke 3:22, 38)?
  2. (v.3) The devil says “If you are the Son of God” — he doesn’t doubt it. Why is his offer to “be your own provider” so friendly and reasonable? Where does the same advice sound like common sense to you — at work, at home, in the budget?
  3. (vv.3–4) Jesus had the power to feed himself, so why didn’t he? Think of the dinner table: why does the little brother have to wait for the head of the table? What does “Man must not live on bread alone” (Deuteronomy 8:3) mean when you are hungry, anxious or vulnerable?
  4. (vv.5–7) What does the devil offer, and what is the catch? Why does it matter that the kingdoms are shown “in a moment of time”? How would a single bow have been easier than the cross?
  5. (v.8) “Worship the Lord your God, and serve him only.” Where do we bow without noticing — to approval, to comfort, to being the boss of our own time and money? How do we rename it: “I’m just being realistic”, “I’ve earned it”, “it’s my time”?
  6. (vv.9–11) The devil quotes Psalm 91. How can the Bible be used to tempt us? What Christian-sounding reasons do we give for doing things our own way?
  7. (v.12) What would it have meant for Jesus to “test” God by jumping? How is testing God (compare Massah, Deuteronomy 6:16) different from trusting him? Where are we tempted to demand that God act on our terms — or to do nothing with what he has already given us?
  8. (vv.13–15; Hebrews 4:15–16) The Son triumphs by trusting his Father’s word. How does knowing that Jesus was “tempted in every way as we are, yet without sin” change what you do when you are tempted? What would it look like to run to Jesus this week?

5. Live It Out

KOG 3·2·1·0 — pray for 3, connect with 2, invite 1, 0 left unloved.

  • Pray for 3 — three people at work, in your street or in your extended family who don’t yet know Jesus. Pray by name daily.
  • Connect with 2 — a coffee, meal or walk with two of them this week.
  • Invite 1 — invite one of your three to the Welcome to the Family course, the Discover Course or the Christmas concert on 13 December (we are praying for 120+ people).
  • 0 left unloved — at morning tea, find the person on the edge: new, alone or drifting.

This week’s challenge: Before one decision this week — a purchase, a commitment, a conversation — “ask the head of the table first”: stop, pray and ask God whether it is his will before you act. Then notice where you have been bowing to something other than the Lord.

Memory verse: “Worship the Lord your God, and serve him only.” (Luke 4:8)

6. Pray

  • Thank God for his word, and for Jesus, who trusted his Father when it cost him everything. Confess where you have tried to be your own provider, your own king, or the orchestrator of your own salvation.
  • Ask the Holy Spirit to protect and guide us, and to make us hungry for God’s kind of hunger — hunger for righteousness.
  • Pray by name for your 3 — that they would meet Jesus, who triumphed for us.

7. Leader’s Notes

Framing. Joyfully point to Jesus. Assume some in the room aren’t yet Christians, or are unsure. The good news is not “try harder to resist the devil” but that the Son has already triumphed for us. Guests may be present; never corner anyone.

Q1 — The Spirit leads Jesus into the wilderness, so temptation is not a sign that God has left us. Jesus is a son of Adam like us, yet he is the Son of God in a way no human being ever was or ever will be: God has never said of us at our baptism, “You are my beloved Son”.

Q2 — The devil does not doubt who Jesus is: “If you are the Son of God — and of course you are…”. The offer sounds like common sense. Choosing to be our own provider, however noble the cause, without trusting God first, is to disrespect God.

Q3 — Jesus was well within his power to make a feast, but until God says so he does not move, not even to feed himself. To do so would have ruptured his relationship with the Father. God sometimes allows us to be hungry and vulnerable; trusting his word, and the God who spoke it, matters more than food.

Q4 — The devil’s rule has an expiry date. One bow would have avoided the suffering, hunger, pain and death of the cross. Taking the shortcut was a lot easier — but it was not the Father’s way.

Q5 — Deuteronomy 6:13: no one is to be above God, full stop. When comfort is our king, or we think no one — not even God — can tell us what to do with our time and resources, we are doing what the devil wanted. Only God is King.

Q6 — The devil knows the Bible and can quote it (Psalm 91:11–12). Scripture twisted to demand that God serve our plan is still temptation. Test every “Christian-sounding” reason by asking: has the head of the table said so?

Q7 — Deuteronomy 6:16: at Massah Israel wanted God to save them on their terms. God would have saved Jesus, but Jesus would have been acting on his own initiative. The issue is whether we wait for the Father’s leading instead of orchestrating our own way of doing his will — and whether we do nothing with what he has given us.

Q8 — We cannot overcome the devil by ourselves. We need our great high priest, tempted in every way yet without sin. When tempted, run to Jesus; at the throne of grace we find mercy and grace in time of need. Jesus triumphed because he is not his own man — he is the Son of God, and if we are adopted children of God, that is our identity too.

Our vision. “From faith to faith”: faith is handed on from one generation to the next, but it is only real when each of us turns to Christ and trusts the Father’s word for ourselves.
