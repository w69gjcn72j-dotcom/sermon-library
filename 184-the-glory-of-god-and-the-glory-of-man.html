What kind of message do our clothes send?

I remember that when I was learning to be a music teacher,

we were told that we’re not to wear t-shirts and jeans,

because that will send the students a message that we’re just like them.

Instead, we’re to wear dark jackets and pants to show that we’re in authority.

Surprisingly, that works really well!

By dressing in a dark suit, you send them a pretty clear message: I’m your teacher, don’t mess with me.

Of course, it’s only an outward sign, and when the class gets out of control, you’ll actually have to discipline the class, your suit can’t do that for you.

But the fact remains that what wear can convey a very clear message.

This doesn’t just happen in classroom settings.

In a court, the way a judge dresses himself or herself sends a very clear message: the judge is in charge.

On a stage, a singer wears the most colourful, the most attention-grabbing costume to send a very clear message: He or she is the star of the show.

In a Church, the way men and women dress themselves can also send a very strong message as well.

It can show whether they’re here to bring glory or shame to God.

How does the way we present ourselves at Church bring glory or shame to God?

In today’s passage, we are going to see that the very way you dress will send a message, whether you like it or not.

It’s a message that even angels will sit up and take notice.

Before we start, let’s pray, “Father, we have come to Mount Zion, to the heavenly Jerusalem, the city of the living God. We have come to thousands upon thousands of angels in joyful assembly. Father, prepare our hearts now as we come into your presence and hear your word. Help me, Father, to explain your word clearly. In Jesus’ name, Amen.”

God’s order

Please open up with me to 1 Cor 11:2 “ Now I praise you because you remember me in everything and hold fast to the traditions just as I delivered them to you.”

In the early church, before they have the New Testament in the way that we have,

church leaders passed on their teachings by teaching it verbally,

passing it down from one generation to another.

These were teachings that were common throughout the churches where they taught.

The passage we’re looking at today is one of those teachings.

Look at how this passage ends, in verse 16, “If anyone wants to argue about this, we have no other custom, nor do the churches of God.”

What Paul is about to tell the Corinthians is not just something for the Corinthians only; it’s for churches everywhere.

What does he want the church to know?

He wants the church to know God’s order of things and what this means for the church.

In Creation

Last week, we read this in 1 Corinthians 10:31, “So, whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do everything for the glory of God.”

Over the next few weeks, we’re going to look at what it means to glorify God,

Especially as a gathering of God’s people at church.

We’re going to see how we should come together in the Lord’s Supper,

How we should use our gifts, how we should love one another,

How we should speak.

But at the beginning and at the end of this section, we see a theme that bookends the way we behave at Church.

At the beginning and at the end of this section, we see how men and women are to relate to one another at Church.

Look with me at verse 3, “But I want you to know that Christ is the head of every man, and the man is the head of the woman,[b] and God is the head of Christ. ”

Paul says that Christ is the head of every man, man is the head of the woman and God is the head of Christ.

What does it mean to be the head of somebody?

That’s our first question for this week.

Talk with the person next to you and send your answer in via WhatsApp, WeChat or Zoom.

What does it mean to be the head of somebody?

You have twenty seconds.

There are various debates about what being ‘the head’ of somebody means, but the most straightforward way to understand it is to take it as having ‘authority’ over somebody.

For example, in Ephesians 1:22, we read that God has appointed Jesus to be ‘head over everything for the church’.

It’s another way of saying that God has given Jesus authority over everything.

Now looking back at today’s passage, we read firstly that Christ is the head of every man.

That is straightforward enough.

Jesus has authority over every man. This is especially in true in the church, where every man who calls themselves Christians calls Jesus Lord.

Jesus is their head.

Next, we read that, “man is the head of the woman.”

What does this mean?

If we go along with what we’ve said before, that Christ has authority over man, then this will mean that man has authority over woman.

This will be the most natural way to understand this.

But aren’t men and women equal in God’s sight? Aren’t we both precious to God? If so, why does God give man authority over woman, and not the way around?

We find it strange and even offensive that someone should follow the lead of another who is their equal.

It’s incompatible with the way we think about headship.

Why should someone who is equal to me be my head?

Why should I submit to the authority of someone who is equal to me?

In himself

But even more shocking is what comes next:

“God is the head of Christ”

Christians believe in the Triune God, that is, we believe in one God who is Three Persons.

We believe in God the Father, God the Son and God the Holy Spirit.

We believe that the Father is God, the Son is God and the Holy Spirit is God, but they are not three gods, but one God.

When we worship God, we worship God the Father, God the Son and God the Holy Spirit.

They are completely equal in terms of who they are.

But even in Triune God himself, there is order.

The head of Christ is God; the head of the Son is the Father.

The Father has authority over the Son.

We see this in other places in the Bible as well. For example, in John 3:17, “For God did not send his Son into the world to condemn the world but to save the world through him.”

The Father sends his Son into the world; the Son comes into the world.

The Father tells his Son to go into the world; the Son obeys the Father and goes into the world.

The Son is equal to the Father, but he subordinates himself to the Father.

The Son submits to the Father.

The Son is equal to the Father, but different

Equal but different

Jesus, as God the Son, is equal to the Father in terms of glory and power and majesty.

But Jesus is willing to submit to the Father.

Jesus is willing to let go of the glory that rightly belongs to him as God and he became like one of us.

In Jesus, we see that the nature of God and the nature of a servant aren’t incompatible.

Jesus, who is truly and fully God, doesn’t find submitting and obeying the Father a problem.

Being equal to God the Father didn’t stop Jesus from serving his Father.

In fact, the nature of God is completely compatible with the nature of a servant.

This has massive implication for how men and women relate to one another.

Yes, men and women are equal in God’s eyes and both men and women are precious to God and loved by God.

But in God’s order, God made man to be the head over woman.

This does not mean that women are in any way inferior to men, just as Jesus as God the Son is in no way inferior to God the Father.

But what it does mean is that both men and women need to recognize that they are equal but different.

They are equal in terms of who they are, but each has a different role to play in God’s order.

The men have the role to be the head, and the women need to recognize and acknowledge that role.

How does this look like?

Paul then shows us what this looks like in a church setting.

He shows us that we’re gathering to reflect God’s order.

Gathering to reflect God’s order

The practice of head covering

Please look with me at verses 4-5, “ Every man who prays or prophesies with something on his head dishonors his head. 5 Every woman who prays or prophesies with her head uncovered dishonors her head, since that is one and the same as having her head shaved. ”

Here, Paul talks about what happens when men and women gather together as the church.

Now notice that the men and women are both doing the same thing.

Paul talks about what happens when a man prays and prophesies and he talks about what happens when a woman prays and prophesies.

And so the first thing we see here is that they are doing the same sort of things at church.

Praying here is publicly leading the congregation in prayer.

Prophesying refers to the times when somebody has a brilliant insight into a passage or how a passage can be applied.

He or she has this brilliant insight because of the Holy Spirit.

Now, we have to be careful about prophecies.

The Bible says that we must test them to see whether they are in accordance with the rest of what the Holy Spirit says, namely, what is written in the Bible.

The closest thing to prophesy that we have is probably is what happens when we meet together in Growth Groups.

There will be times when some of us come up with some brilliant insights in our groups.

And it’ll be the Growth group, usually under the leadership of the Growth Group leader, to discern and find out whether they are insights from the Holy Spirit or not.

In this passage, both men and women pray and prophesy in the church;

they both pray to God and they both share any insights from the Holy Spirit that they might have.

It’s not what they do here that’s in question; they both pray and prophesy.

I’d love to see more men and women in our Church both pray and prophesy, especially when we come together in Growth groups!

Paul is very happy to see both men and women pray and prophesy in Church.

However, when they do pray and prophesy in Church, they must do it in a way that honours and glorifies God.

The Bible says that if a man covers his head while he’s praying, he dishonours his head.

If a woman doesn’t cover her head while she’s praying, she’s dishonouring her head.

Now we don’t know exactly what head covering was used; it was probably some kind of shawl.

But what we do know is that it was something that the women wore.

And so, if you were in that society, and you wore a head covering, you would be saying that you’re a woman.

Now in the Corinthian church, it would be absolutely ridiculous and inappropriate if a man got up, wore a head covering and began to pray to God or to prophesy.

People would be just thinking about this cross-dresser was making a statement.

Similarly, if a woman got up without head covering and started to pray or prophesy, people would’ve been equally shocked.

Because she would be making a statement too; she would be saying, “hey, look at me, I’m just like a man.”

She might as well cut her hair and shave her head; that would’ve been the kind of statement that she was making.

It would’ve been a disgraceful thing to do in a church.

It’s something like this.

It’s not allowed at in Sydney at the moment because of COVID lockdown, but imagine going to a church for a wedding.

Here’s a picture of the last wedding that I went to.

Now imagine the music begins and you stand up for the bridal procession.

You look into the back of the church, you see the bride from a distance.

“Ok, she’s put on a few kilo’s. But that’s ok. It’s her big day, we should be happy for her.”

But as she comes, you realize that it’s the groom.

He’s wearing the white wedding gown, with a full set of beard around his face.

Just before you rush out to vomit, you look at the front to see who was at the front.

And you’ll see the bride, with hair all gelled back, slick in a tux, waiting to meet her ‘husband.’

It’s utterly inappropriate because it confuses the man and the woman.

Yes the man and the woman are equal, but they are different and they have different roles to play at the wedding.

The man is the groom and the woman is the bride.

That’s how God made us, the man as the man and the woman as the woman.

We ought to respect what God has made.

When we gather together, we ought to respect our differences as well.

Yes, God has saved both men and women and both men and women are equally loved by God.

But we are different.

Men are still men and women are still women.

God made us differently and we need to respect that.

Not respecting that, not respecting our differences is an act of rebellion against God’s created order.

Not respecting our differences will bring shame to our heads.

For the women, it will mean bringing shame to the men.

For the men, it will mean bringing shame to Christ.

When we gather, we ought to respect God’s order and not bring shame to ourselves or to Christ.

Instead when we gather together, we ought to bring glory to God.

The glory of God and the glory of man.

Look at verse 7 with me, “7  A man should not cover his head, because he is the image and glory of God. So too, woman is the glory of man.”

Paul here gives a second reason why men shouldn’t cover their heads when the church comes together; man is the image and glory of God.

Man is formed first and he is the first being in the whole creation to reflect God’s glory.

When God created man in his own image, he set his glory on man.

When God created man, man reflected God’s glory like no other.

But as we know, man by himself was no complete.

And so God created woman to be his companion.

God created woman from man and for man.

She’s like him, but she’s different.

And in that sense, the woman reflects man’s glory like no other.

She is his glory.

She reflects his glory like no other in God’s creation.

Women are glorious, right?

In the Bible, they are rightly described as glorious!

And it’s because she’s the glory of man that she should have a symbol of authority on her head in the Church,

So that people of the Church won’t focus on her, the glory of man, rather than the glory of God.

While we should focus on God’s glory all the time, no matter where we are,

This is especially the case at Church.

The way God’s set his order in creation reveals God’s glory.

And so, the way that men and women present themselves must also reflect God’s order, God’s glory.

If a woman dresses in a way that is considered inappropriate in the Church setting,

Then it would draw attention to the glory of man rather than focusing on the glory of God.

It’s with this in mind that the woman should have a symbol of authority on her head.

In the Corinthians’ case, the sign of authority is the head covering.

The head covering is a symbol to show that the woman is like the man but she is not him; she is equal to him, but she does not replace him.

Now a word about angels; how do angels enter into the equation?

The Bible doesn’t expand any more.

What we can say is that when we gather together in the presence of God and of Jesus, angels are also part of our gathering.

According to Jewish traditions, angels were the guardians of the creation order.

They protect the purity of the divine worship and would be offended by any suggestion of rebellion against God’s created order.

The reference to angels here shows the seriousness of what we do when we gather together as a church, even if we do it online.

We are in the presence of God, who sits on his throne, who commands mighty angels to do his will.

We need to remember how significant this gathering is and not treat it with disrespect by deliberately going against God’s created order.

You’re in the presence of God; show God proper respect.

And so, when we gather, we should respect how God has made us, how he has made man to be the image and glory of God and woman to be the glory of man.

Now this doesn’t mean that the men can start looking down on the women.

Paul makes it quite clear that there is mutual dependency between men and women.

The mutual dependency

Look with me at the verses 11-12, “ In the Lord, however, woman is not independent of man, and man is not independent of woman. 12 For just as woman came from man, so man comes through woman, and all things come from God.”

The men are not to exaggerate about how important they are.

Men need women.

Yes, women came from man, but every man since the first birth came about because of a woman, his mother.

Men literally can’t live or even be born without women.

So men and boys, you must respect women and you must treat them with honour.

And so, when we gather, we must reflect God’s order; we must reflect the fact that men and women are equal and different.

Reflecting God’s order in today’s church.

In the Corinthian church, they were told to apply this truth by getting the women to wear a head covering when they pray and prophesy,

and to tell the men not to wear a head covering when they pray and prophesy.

What does this mean in the 21st century church?

Do we need to ask the women to come up with a head covering when they lead us in prayer?

Do we need to ask all the girls to wear a head covering in Growth groups?

Not necessarily.

While there are cultures today where wearing head covering in public is the right and honourable thing to do,

In our culture, not many women actually wear a head covering any more.

Wearing a head covering in our culture today does not convey the same thing that it did in Paul’s time.

In fact, wearing a head covering today will send a confusing message to those in the church, the very thing that Paul was trying to avoid.

What should we do?

Well, the principle remains.

Gather to glorify God by respecting God’s order for us.

When we gather together as the church, we still need to respect and reflect God’s order for us.

We still need to acknowledge that men and women are equal but different.

For the women of our Church, this will mean not deliberately wearing clothes to make people think that you’re a man.

And vice versa, this will mean that the men at our Church will not deliberately wear clothes to make people think that you’re a woman.

In other words, dress sensibly, dress respectfully; don’t be a cross-dresser.

Don’t confuse the sexual distinction that God has ordered in creation.

Respect and celebrate the fact that God has made us male and female.

Second, while we might not necessarily ask our sisters to wear head coverings, the principle still applies.

The sisters in our Church must not deliberately set out to undermine male leadership.

They must not brazenly override God’s order in creation and in the Church.

Now I need to emphasise that men in leadership are sinners as well and they will get it wrong sometimes.

But it will be wrong for the women to undermine the leadership in order to take over the leadership.

If we don’t agree with the leadership, we’ll need to pray and approach the leadership respectfully and lovingly,

as Christ would have us.

We won’t belittle the men God’s placed above us, we won’t spread gossips and we won’t talk behind the leaders’ back.

If we do gossip and deliberately try to undermine the church leadership, we will be deliberately going against God’s order for the church.

Gather to glorify God by respecting God’s order for us.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How do I honour those whom God has put as my head?

Do I come to Church with a reverence for God and a respect of how he orders the Church?

How am I praying and prophesying at Church (how am I praying for the people at Church and contributing to Growth Group discussions)?

Our God loves his church.

God appointed Jesus to be head over everything for the church.

And so, when we gather together as God’s church, let’s give glory to God in all that we do.

Let’s give glory to God as we must reflect God’s order.

Gather to glorify God by respecting God’s order for us.

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you so much for your love for us in Christ. Thank you that in him, both men and women can come to a living knowledge of you. Father, help us to obey you in faith in all that we do. Help us to glorify you in the way that we relate to one another. Help us to respect you and let our relationship reflect the order that you have ordained. May we, with all the angels, bless you and glorify you forevermore. In Christ’s name we pray, Amen.”

“Father, we have come to Mount Zion, to the heavenly Jerusalem, the city of the living God. We have come to thousands upon thousands of angels in joyful assembly. Father, prepare our hearts now as we come into your presence and hear your word. Help me, Father, to explain your word clearly. In Jesus’ name, Amen.”
