The Holiness of God: Jesus’ sacrifice perfects saints for worship

Passage: Hebrews 10.1-10

Additional passages: Psalm 40:6-8, Hebrews 9:28, Romans 12:1-2, Hebrews 10:19-22

Big Idea: Jesus’ perfect obedience to his Father’s will sanctifies us for perfect worship of the holy God.

1. The shadow of imperfect worship

2. The reality of the Perfect Worshipper

3. Jesus the Perfect Worshipper sanctifies perfect worshippers

Last week, someone sent me a Youtube video about how Chinese Churches in Australia are responding to the influx of Hong Kong expats (澳洲華人教會如何應對移民潮?).

Since 2019, there’s been a lot of Hong Kong expats who moved to Sydney, many of whom are Christians.

You would expect that there will be an influx of Hong Kong Christians going to Chinese Churches. Of course, there had been an increase in some Cantonese speaking Churches, but it’s not a simple, straightforward increase. There are some Hong Kong expats who would only go to the same denomination they had in Hong Kong, which may not be in Australia. There are others who prefer to go to English only Churches, because of what they went through back in Hong Kong.

Others simply like to shop around until they find the Church that fits their needs. The podcasters call these ‘Church-shoppers’. Now of course, they are not the only ones; people from all different race groups and all different backgrounds shop around for Churches as well. They may like a Church for its music, or for the kind of people there, or for the children programme. Sometimes, they would travel together as a big group until they find a Church that suits them.

I’ve spoken with other ministers about this before; though it might look like a good thing to have many people coming, we’re always not sure what they want to do next. What if they find another Church that has better music, or a better kids’ programme? They might keep looking until they find the perfect Church.What does perfect worship look like anyway? What makes a Sunday service a perfect worship experience?

You might want to drop down a few ideas even before we begin.

That’s what we’re going to look at in today’s passage.

Please open up with me to Hebrews, chapter ten, beginning from verse one.

We’re going to see three things:

1. The shadow of imperfect worship

2. The reality of the Perfect Worshipper

3. Jesus the Perfect Worshipper sanctifies perfect worshippers

Before we look into it, let’s pray.

“Father, as we come before your glorious throne, by your Holy Spirit, please take away every distractions, every evil thought, every sinful thought, and turn our eyes to your word, that we may see the glorious Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

1. The shadow of imperfect worship

Hebrews chapter 10, verse 1: “Since the law has only a shadow of the good things to come, and not the reality itself of those things, it can never perfect the worshipers by the same sacrifices they continually offer year after year.”

In the Old Testament of the Bible, especially in the first five books of the Bible, there’s a lot of instructions about sacrifices.

There are records of how people sacrificed, the laws about different types of sacrifices, the number of sacrifices made on various occasions.

Why did the people in Old Testament make sacrifices to God?

Because God is a holy God, and we’re unholy sinners.

Last week, we read that God’s holiness is dangerous for unholy sinners like us.

God’s holiness is dangerous not because it’s bad, but because we’re bad.

Unholy sinners cannot stand in his presence; the very holiness of God’s presence will kill us.

And so, God demanded that sacrifices are made on our behalf when we come before him.

These are animals that are killed and offered on behalf of us sinners, so that we can come to worship God.

Animals like bulls, goats and sheep had to be sacrificed so that we can come before God’s presence.

For example, in verse 6 of the passage, we read about whole burnt offerings and sin offerings.

Burnt offerings are whole animals burnt to God for answering someone’s prayer, or to cleanse someone’s sin or to just simply give thanks to God.

Sin offerings are what you offer when you sin against God.

These sacrifices used to happen all the time when God’s people were travelling in the desert, and eventually as they worshipped God in the temple.

One of the ministries I really appreciate but not many of us get to see is the sisters and brothers who clean up the Church before and after Sundays.

These brothers and sisters don’t get paid, but faithfully clean up the Church every week so that we can have a clean Church to come to.

But when God’s people come to worship God in the Old Testament, it wasn’t exactly a clean experience.

On the day of Atonement, God’s priest had to kill bulls and goats.

The blood of these animals would be sprinkled in different places.

There will be blood all over the place, to remind God’s people that they’re sinners and these animals had to die in our place so that we can worship God.

In Heb 9:22, the Bible tells us that ‘According to the law almost everything is purified with blood, and without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness’.

To be purified with blood doesn’t mean that a person is somehow disinfected by the blood of the animals.

To be purified means that the problem of sin has been addressed.

Unholy people can come to God only when blood has been shed, that is, only when a life is given.

That’s the price that must be paid to worship before the holy God, for until the cost of life has been paid, there is no forgiveness of sins.

However, even with the sacrifice of animal, it’s still not enough.

The Bible says that it’s only a shadow, not the reality of the good things to come.

The sacrifices are like shadows, a faint shape of what the real forgiveness looks like.

The sacrifices in and of themselves are not good enough to permanently deal with the problem of sin.

If sacrifices are good enough, then there wouldn’t be any need to offer sacrifices again.

The Bible says that if sin is completely and permanently dealt with through sacrifices, then there would be no consciousness of sins; sin would have been completely irradicated.

But the fact that they have to come back year after year shows that sin is still there.

For as verse 4 tells us, “it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins.”

It’s like this shampoo prank I’ve seen many years ago.

Someone was washing their hair next to the open shower on the beach and someone would secretly pour shampoo on the person washing their hair.

It doesn’t matter how hard the person tries, as long as the shampoo is being poured onto their head, they couldn’t wash away the bubbles.

That’s what our sin is like.

No matter how hard we try to get rid of our sins, no matter how many sacrifices we make, sin is still there, in our hearts, in our minds and in our will.

What is sin?

Sin is our unwillingness to obey God.

Or to put it another way, sin is our will to disobey God.

Sin is our heart refusing to listen to God.

Sin is not just bad things we’ve said and done.

For if that’s the case, we don’t sin if we don’t do anything.

No, even if we don’t do anything, we still sin, because we don’t have the will, the desire to actively obey God. That’s sin!

Sin can’t be irradicated even if we followed all the laws about sacrifices in the Old Testament!

It’s inside each one of us.

We can’t get rid of it by ourselves; we can’t wash it away.

If we’re not loving God with all your mind, with all of our will, then we are sinning against God.

As sinners, we have no right and no power to stand before God.

That’s what the laws do; they remind us of our sins before God.

No one is saved by following the laws’ demands for sacrifices, not even in the Old Testament.

While we don’t make sacrifices to God like God’s people did in the Old Testament, some people still think that what they offer to God can save them.

They might not say it explicitly, but they certainly imply it.

They might have sacrificed a lot of time by serving at Church for many years or by giving a lot to the Church.

They might think that all their sacrifices to God must surely count for something.

But no, not one of our sacrifices are good enough to make us worthy to come to worship before God.

Not one of us in and of ourselves is worthy to come before his presence; not one of us is worthy to come to the Lord’s supper.

If we still have even one sin in our hearts, one evil thought in our will, we’re not good enough to worship God.

It doesn’t matter whether you’ve been to church all your life, baptized even, you’re not good enough, I’m not good enough to worship God.

Let that sink in! You’re not doing God a favour when you come to worship God.

God doesn’t need us to worship him. We need him; he doesn’t need us.

We need to be somehow cleansed otherwise, we’ll die in his presence.

As sinners, with sin ingrained into every fabric of our souls, we will perish in God’s pure, holy presence.

But there’s good news: we’re not qualified to come to God, but God has provided a way to qualify us.

2. The reality of the Perfect Worshipper

Read verse 5 with me, “Therefore, as he was coming into the world, he said:

You did not desire sacrifice and offering, but you prepared a body for me.”

This last line is quoted from Psalm 40, verse 6.

It was written by King David, but points to Jesus Christ, the Son of God, who would come into the world one thousand years after David wrote this psalm.

The Holy Son of God, who is not of this world, who is without sin, who is utterly holy and sinless, came into the world.

All the Old Testament sacrifices point to this reality, the reality of the Perfect Worshipper.

The Psalmist described the quote as something that God the Son said to God the Father as he was coming into the world.

It’s as if we’re overhearing a conversation between God the Father and God the Son.

You did not desire sacrifice and offering, but you prepared a body for me. You did not delight in whole burnt offerings and sin offerings.

Here, the Old Testament laws, along with all its commands and instructions about sacrifices, are completely dismissed.

Why? Because they are not what God wants, these are not what he wills.

What God wants is a body he’s prepared for God the Son.

God the Son had always shared perfect holiness and brilliant glory with God the Father.

God the Son had no sin and was in the perfect and holy place; he was qualified to be in the presence the holy God.

His relationship with God was already perfect.

There was no need for God the Father or God the Son to bother with us.

They could’ve left us to rightly die in our sin.

But God prepared a body for Jesus in order for Jesus to do His will.

And Jesus responds with perfect obedience.

Verse 7, “Then I said, “See— it is written about me in the scroll— I have come to do your will, God.

For true worship to happen, God wants a Worshipper who will perfectly submit to God’s will.

For true worship to happen, we need Jesus, the Perfect Worshipper.

It’s a little like this, when Jenny and I go out to have food, we often ask whether the food is made with love. You know what I mean when I say that some food is made with love, right?

There’s food that’s cooked quickly and effectively, and they might even taste ok, but there’s no love.

Then there’s food where attention is being paid to the details and it is served with pride.

They had put the effort and the love in to make this meal. One meal was prepared, manufactured, to make money; the other was made, created with love.

There’s worship that’s manufactured, and there’s worship that’s given in love. Worship that is manufactured will go through the motions and even say the right words.

But it won’t recognize the seriousness of sin, it won’t take into account seriously that God wants us to obey him. God doesn’t want manufactured worship.

We can give God all the sacrifices in the world, but he doesn’t want it, because a) we don’t have the will to obey God, and b) not even all the sacrifices in the world are worthy of our God.

But Jesus has come into our world to do God’s will.

It’s written about him in the scroll, that is, the law.

Every sacrifice is the shadow of his true and real sacrifice. What makes Jesus’ sacrifice real?

Because Jesus offered himself willingly to obey the Father’s his will.

Jesus aligns his will perfectly with God’s will.

What does God want? What brings God delight? Jesus’ will.

He wants Jesus’ will to align with his will.

And God’s will is for Jesus, the immortal, everlasting, sinless God to have a mortal, frail body for his Son.

Why? Because God’s will is that His Son should die as the Perfect Sacrifice.

For it’s only the perfect sacrifice of Jesus, who perfectly obeys Gods will, can satisfy God’s will.

And Jesus obeyed God the Father perfectly: He came into the world, he took on our mortal body, he suffered and died on the cross, all according to God’s will.

Jesus is the Perfect Worshipper because he perfectly obeys his Father’s will.

That’s what true Worship of God looks like; the true worship of God depends on whether you obey God’s will.

And there’s only one person who obeyed God’s will perfectly; he’s the Perfect Worshipper, Jesus Christ.

God can’t worshipped in any other way, for God doesn’t want to be worshipped in any other way.

No prophet, no monk, no motivational speaker, no holy men, no holy women, can obey God as perfectly as Jesus can.

If you want to see what a true Worshipper of God looks like, look to Jesus.

There’s no true worship of the Holy God apart from Jesus.

In English, what we do here on a Sunday morning is called a Sunday service.

It’s a time when we come to serve God through Jesus.

It’s a reminder that we are not simply here for a gathering, or a lecture, or to hang out, or to eat a delicious meal.

We come to a service; we come to do God’s will.

To worship God is to serve him and obey his will.

Jesus has come not to be served but to serve; we too, when we come together, must come to serve, rather than feeling entitled to be served.

But here’s the problem: we can’t do it by ourselves.

In and of ourselves, our attempt to obey and serve God is so weak, and often so futile.

We are not good at obeying God!

However, we thank God that our worship doesn’t depend on our obedience; rather we depend on Jesus’ obedience.

Jesus perfectly obeyed the will of God by taking on the human body, lived a perfectly obedient life, was obedient even to death on a cross.

Jesus’ worship is the perfect worship because it’s not just obedience for an hour a week, but obedience in all of his life. Jesus’ perfect worship is shown in his perfect obedience.

We must obey and serve God through Christ, the Perfect Worshipper.

3. Jesus the Perfect Worshipper sanctifies perfect worshippers

Look with me at verse 10, “By this will, we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all time.”

We’re not good enough to worship God, but by this will, both God’s and Jesus Christ’s, we are sanctified or made holy though the blood of Jesus Christ, once and for all time.

Last Wednesday, when Jenny and I were having our day off, I received a message on the phone saying that there was someone at the door.I could see him on my phone and I didn’t recognise him. Caitlyn was home alone at that time, and we rang her to tell her not to open the door. After a while, I activated the siren on the front door and the stranger then quickly left.

The man was a stranger; he wasn’t allowed to come into our home.

If we don’t have a relationship with God, then we’re like strangers to Him.

He won’t allow us into his home, not because he doesn’t know us nor because we’re a danger.

He won’t allow us in because we’re not worthy; we’re sinful.

However, those who trust in Jesus are made holy in God’s eyes.

When Israel saw blood and sacrifices, they were reminded them of their sins.

When God sees us, he remembers the blood and sacrifice of Jesus.

He remembers Jesus’ perfect obedience, and His perfect sacrifice.

When God the Father see us in Jesus, He remembers that it is He who sent Jesus God the Son to save us and make us holy,

so we could enter His house, to be with Him, and to worship Him.

Through the blood of Jesus, God makes us perfect for worship.

Through the blood of Jesus, God perfects us as his worshippers.

That doesn’t mean that we’re perfect or sinless.

Rather, it’s the sacrifice of Jesus that qualifies all those who believe in Him to be able to truly worship God in a way that all the sacrifices of the Old Testament never could.

Christ’s sacrifice is one and for all; there are no more sacrifices needed to qualify us.

There are no more sacrifices that could make us more perfect.

The sacrifice of Jesus Christ is enough.

Those who trusts in Jesus are already sanctified and made perfect to worship God in Christ.

This is who we are but do our actions align with who we are?

If we’re Christians, sanctified to be perfect worshippers, are we worshipping God as perfectly as we should?

If we are truly sanctified in Jesus, then we will live our lives as living sacrifices for Jesus.

Look with me at Romans chapter 12 verses 1 to 2, “Therefore, brothers and sisters, in view of the mercies of God, I urge you  to present your bodies as a living sacrifice,  holy and pleasing to God; this is your true worship.  2 Do not be conformed  to this age,  but be transformed by the renewing of your mind,  so that you may discern what is the good, pleasing, and perfect will  of God.”

Since you’ve already been sanctified to worship God, live your lives as a sacrifice.

That’s how you worship God: it takes all your life to worship him.

As you worship him, you will know his good, pleasing and perfect will.

And when you know his will, you will be even more inclined to worship him.

In a little while, we’ll be celebrating the Lord’s Supper.

Unlike our Roman Catholic friends, we don’t believe that this is a sacrifice.

Jesus has already sacrificed once and for all time.

What we do is a remembrance of what he’s done.

We remember that Christ perfects sinners by dying for their sins.

It’s a serious moment in the life of the Church and so it’s not for those who don’t believe what Jesus has done.

For to come to the Lord’s Supper without believing Jesus is to commit false and unacceptable worship.

To worship God truly, you must come humbly before Jesus, the Perfect Worshipper.

He is the only One through whom we can worship God truly.

It is Jesus who sanctifies, who perfects us for worship.

We’re not morally perfect, but by faith we know that when God see us who follows Jesus, he sees us as perfect and holy, because he sees the blood of Jesus.

What does perfect worship look like?

Perfect worship is coming to Jesus, the Perfect Worshipper.

If we step away from Jesus, we won’t be able to worship God.

If you are already a Christians, don’t worship like the world.

Don’t look for the perfect Church; you will ruin it as soon as you step in.

A perfect Church is ruined as soon as any sinners step in.

Instead see the Church, the people, as a people bought with the blood of Jesus – we’re broken, poor and sad. Love them as Jesus would; know that Jesus has perfected them for worship.

How can you say that you love God if you can’t love God’s people in front of you? And if you can’t love God’s people whom God’s placed around you, why should God send any more people to you? May we repent of our sin of not loving God’s people in our Church as much as we should.

If you’re not a Christian yet, then let me invite you to believe in Jesus. I’m so glad that you came!

But please, don’t just go through the motions. We want you to worship God truly.

This can only be done through Jesus.

Trust in what He has done for people like you and me at the cross, trust in his perfect obedience.

Jesus came to do God’s will, to save people like you and me.

Would you trust in him?

Jesus’ perfect obedience to his Father’s will sanctifies us for perfect worship of the holy God.

When you see two or three people gathered in Jesus’ name, who trusts in Jesus, who wants to find out how to obey God’s will as shown in the Bible,

There you will find a more perfect Church than a stadium filled with crowds, who has no desire to obey God’s will.

Jesus’ perfect obedience makes him the Perfect Worshipper, whose perfect sacrifice perfects sinners like us.

Here are three questions for us to think about:

1. Why aren’t the sacrifices in the Old Testament good enough for God?

2. How does Jesus’ perfect obedience mean that his sacrifice is good enough for God?

3. Is my worship of the holy God holy? If so, what does it look like? If it’s not holy, what does it look like?

Jesus’ perfect obedience to his Father’s will sanctifies us for perfect worship of the holy God.

Let’s pray.

If you haven’t put your trust in Jesus and you want to put your trust in Jesus, the perfect worshipper, then you can do so by praying this prayer with me.

I’ll say the prayer slowly, one line at a time.

If you believe what I say, they pray to God by following what I say.

Let’s come before God in prayer,

“Our heavenly Father, thank you for your love for us. We don’t deserve it but you gave your love freely to us through Jesus. Please forgive us when we disobey you, in thought, word and deed. Please forgive us when we refuse to obey you. Turn our hearts toward Jesus, the Perfect Worshipper. Thank you that it’s by his perfect sacrifice on the cross that you forgive us our sins. Please forgive me my sins and help me trust in Jesus. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”
