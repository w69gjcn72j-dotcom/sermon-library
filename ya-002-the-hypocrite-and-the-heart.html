Young Adults (19–30)

The Hypocrite and the Heart

Romans 2:17-3:8 · Sunday 19 July 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah · For young adults (19-30)

Big Idea: Don’t be hypocritical teachers whose falsehood breeds blasphemy; be people with Spirit-circumcised hearts, living out God’s word for his praise and glory.

Aim: That each member (1) spots the gap between their outward self and their real self before God, (2) trusts the Spirit’s heart-surgery over Christian credentials, and (3) confesses one real sin and prays by name for 3 friends.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, this sheet. Allow 60-75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • Someone gets called out in the group chat for something you’ve quietly done yourself. Why is calling out so much easier — and more fun — than owning up?
  • Your feed versus your actual Tuesday night: when have you curated a version of yourself that looked better than the contents?

Read — Romans 2:17-3:8 (CSB)

17 Now if you call yourself a Jew, and rely on the law, and boast in God, 18 and know his will, and approve the things that are superior, being instructed from the law, 19 and if you are convinced that you are a guide for the blind, a light to those in darkness, 20 an instructor of the ignorant, a teacher of the immature, having the embodiment of knowledge and truth in the law — 21 you then, who teach another, don’t you teach yourself? You who preach, “You must not steal” — do you steal? 22 You who say, “You must not commit adultery” — do you commit adultery? You who detest idols, do you rob temples? 23 You who boast in the law, do you dishonor God by breaking the law? 24 For, as it is written: The name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of you.

25 Circumcision benefits you if you observe the law, but if you are a lawbreaker, your circumcision has become uncircumcision. 26 So if an uncircumcised man keeps the law’s requirements, will not his uncircumcision be counted as circumcision? 27 A man who is physically uncircumcised, but who fulfills the law, will judge you who are a lawbreaker in spite of having the letter of the law and circumcision. 28 For a person is not a Jew who is one outwardly, and true circumcision is not something visible in the flesh. 29 On the contrary, a person is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart — by the Spirit, not the letter. That person’s praise is not from people but from God.

3:1 So what advantage does the Jew have? Or what is the benefit of circumcision? 2 Considerable in every way. First, they were entrusted with the very words of God. 3 What then? If some were unfaithful, will their unfaithfulness nullify God’s faithfulness? 4 Absolutely not! Let God be true, even though everyone is a liar, as it is written: That you may be justified in your words and triumph when you judge. 5 But if our unrighteousness highlights God’s righteousness, what are we to say? I am using a human argument: Is God unrighteous to inflict wrath? 6 Absolutely not! Otherwise, how will God judge the world? 7 But if by my lie God’s truth abounds to his glory, why am I also still being judged as a sinner? 8 And why not say, just as some people slanderously claim we say, “Let us do what is evil so that good may come”? Their condemnation is deserved!

Context

Paul has shown God’s anger against open rebellion (1:18-32) and against the moralising judge (2:1-16). Now he turns to the religious insider: the person with the Bible knowledge, the heritage, the badge (circumcision). The privileges are real — but privilege without practice dishonours God’s name, and only a heart changed by the Spirit truly belongs to him.

Discuss

  1. Read 2:17-20. What’s on this person’s ’profile’ — the privileges and titles they claim? Which are actually good things?
  2. In vv. 21-24, what is Paul’s charge? How does hypocrisy from God’s people end with outsiders trashing God’s name (v. 24)?
  3. In vv. 25-27, what happens to circumcision — the covenant ’badge’ — when the law is broken? What are our versions of the badge (church-kid CV, ministry roles, the right opinions online)?
  4. From vv. 28-29: what is true circumcision, who performs it, and whose praise does the real Jew seek? Why can’t we do this to ourselves?
  5. 3:1-2 — if hypocrisy is so deadly, was growing up around the Bible worthless? What advantage still stands?
  6. 3:3-4 — does our unfaithfulness cancel God’s faithfulness? How does David’s confession in Psalm 51 (quoted in v. 4) model the right response when your sin is exposed?
  7. 3:5-8 — what twisted logic does Paul reject? Where do we hear “grace means it doesn’t matter how I live” among Christians our age?
  8. Whose praise are you actually living for — the feed, the group chat, your parents, your church image — or God (v. 29)? What would change if God’s praise were enough?

Live It Out

This term we are practising the KOG 3·2·1·0 rhythm together:

  • Pray for 3 — keep praying daily for three friends (uni, work, share house) who don’t yet know Jesus.
  • Connect with 2 — real conversations with two of them this week; ask genuine questions, not a setup.
  • Invite 1 — invite one friend to church or to Young Adults Camp (31 July - 2 August). Book your own spot this week if you haven’t.
  • 0 left unloved — notice the person nobody talks to on Sunday (or in your group chat) and love them practically.

Weekly challenge: Confess one specific sin to God this week — named, not renamed. Then tell one trusted Christian friend and ask them to pray for you. Posted authenticity is cheap; confession is the real thing.

Memory verse — “A person is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart — by the Spirit, not the letter. That person’s praise is not from people but from God.” (Romans 2:29)

Pray

  • Praise God that he stays true and faithful even though “everyone is a liar” — thank him that Jesus kept the law perfectly for us.
  • Confess honestly the gap between the self we present and the self God sees, asking the Spirit to keep working on our hearts.
  • Pray by name for each person’s 3 friends, and for Young Adults Camp — that friends who come would meet Jesus.

Leader’s Notes

Keep it warm and joyful — we’re pointing to Jesus, not running a call-out session. Guests and not-yet-Christians may be there; many have been burned by church hypocrisy, so let them hear that God condemns it more strongly than they do. Never corner anyone.

Q2: v. 24 quotes Isaiah 52:5 — God’s people caused his name to be mocked. The world reads God through his people; our lives back up or undermine our message.

Q4: true circumcision is by the Spirit (Deut 30:6; Ezek 36:26-27). We can’t operate on our own hearts — the answer isn’t self-improvement or better image; it’s grace. Jesus kept the law for us.

Q6-7: God’s faithfulness survives our failure; David confessed instead of excusing. And grace never licenses sin — it transforms (Titus 2:11-12). Watch for both errors: performing for approval and ’it’s fine, grace covers it’.

Vision tie-in: Romans is taking us “from faith to faith” (1:17) — real faith shows in practised lives, not curated ones. That’s how we grow towards 75 households serving together (currently 52).
