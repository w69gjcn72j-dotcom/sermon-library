When Grace Breaks into the Impossible

Luke 1:26–45 · Sunday 4 October 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

Big Idea: When grace breaks into the impossible, God uses those the world considers “unqualified”: his grace comes first, faith rests on his word, and faith is strengthened in fellowship.

Aim: By the end of group, each student should be able to (1) say why it’s surprising — and good news — that God chose a teenage girl from Nazareth; (2) see that Mary could ask God honest questions and still say yes to him; (3) name one Christian friend to be an “Elizabeth” with this week.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • Have you ever been picked for something you felt you weren’t good enough for — a team, a solo, a speech, a leadership role? How did it feel?
  • You get huge news at 11pm — really good or really bad. Who do you text first, and why them?

Read

26 In the sixth month, the angel Gabriel was sent by God to a town in Galilee called Nazareth,

27 to a virgin engaged to a man named Joseph, of the house of David. The virgin’s name was Mary.

28 And the angel came to her and said, “Greetings, favored woman! The Lord is with you.”

29 But she was deeply troubled by this statement, wondering what kind of greeting this could be.

30 Then the angel told her, “Do not be afraid, Mary, for you have found favor with God.

31 Now listen: You will conceive and give birth to a son, and you will name him Jesus.

32 He will be great and will be called the Son of the Most High, and the Lord God will give him the throne of his father David.

33 He will reign over the house of Jacob forever, and his kingdom will have no end.”

34 Mary asked the angel, “How can this be, since I have not had sexual relations with a man?”

35 The angel replied to her, “The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you. Therefore, the holy one to be born will be called the Son of God.

36 And consider your relative Elizabeth — even she has conceived a son in her old age, and this is the sixth month for her who was called childless.

37 For nothing will be impossible with God.”

38 “See, I am the Lord’s servant,” said Mary. “May it happen to me as you have said.” Then the angel left her.

39 In those days Mary set out and hurried to a town in the hill country of Judah

40 where she entered Zechariah’s house and greeted Elizabeth.

41 When Elizabeth heard Mary’s greeting, the baby leaped inside her, and Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Spirit.

42 Then she exclaimed with a loud cry, “Blessed are you among women, and your child will be blessed!

43 How could this happen to me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?

44 For you see, when the sound of your greeting reached my ears, the baby leaped for joy inside me.

45 Blessed is she who has believed that the Lord would fulfill what he has spoken to her!”

Context

Six months earlier, the angel Gabriel had visited an old priest, Zechariah, in the temple in Jerusalem, to announce that his wife Elizabeth would have a baby — John the Baptist. Now Gabriel visits Mary, a teenage girl from Nazareth, a small village people looked down on. She is engaged to Joseph. Getting pregnant before the wedding could bring shame, or worse.

Discuss

  1. vv.26–27 — Where does God send the angel, and to whom? Why is it surprising that God chose Nazareth and a teenage girl from a poor family (see John 1:46)?
  2. vv.28–30 — What are the angel’s first words to Mary? She was “favored” before she had done anything. How is that different from how school, sport or social media work?
  3. vv.31–33 — What does the angel say Jesus will be? “Jesus” means “the LORD saves”. Why is that the perfect name for him?
  4. v.34 — Mary asks, “How can this be?” Is it OK to ask God hard questions? What’s the difference between “I don’t understand” and “I don’t believe”?
  5. vv.35–37 — How will this impossible thing happen? What’s something in your life that feels impossible right now?
  6. v.38 — What could Mary lose by saying yes? What would it look like for you to say, “I am the Lord’s servant,” at school or at home this week?
  7. vv.39–45 — Why did Mary hurry to Elizabeth? What does verse 45 say made Mary blessed?
  8. vv.39–45 — Mary needed an Elizabeth. When you’re struggling late at night, do you go to your phone (or an AI chat), or to a real person? Who could be your “Elizabeth” — and whose “Elizabeth” could you be?

Live It Out

KOG 3·2·1·0 — pray for 3, connect with 2, invite 1, 0 left out.

  • Pray for 3 — three friends who aren’t Christians yet. Pray for them by name every day.
  • Connect with 2 — two people at KOGYouth or church you don’t normally talk to.
  • Invite 1 — invite one of your three to youth or to church on Sunday.
  • 0 left out — who sits alone at lunch, or never gets tagged in the group chat?

This week’s challenge: text one Christian friend, “How can I pray for you this week?” — and actually pray for them. Be someone’s Elizabeth.

Memory verse: “For nothing will be impossible with God.” (Luke 1:37, CSB)

Pray

  • Thank God that he chooses ordinary people, and that his grace comes before anything we do.
  • Bring God the things that feel impossible right now, and ask for Mary’s trust: “I am the Lord’s servant.”
  • Pray for your 3 friends by name — that God would lead them to Jesus, and give you one chance this week to point one of them to him.

Leader’s Notes

Joyfully point to Jesus. Some students may not be Christians yet: this passage shows God looks for willing people, not impressive ones, and that honest questions are welcome. Never corner anyone. Question 5 and Question 8 may surface real struggles (family, loneliness, anxiety) — follow up privately and let a parent or the youth minister know if needed.

Two likely wrong turns. First, making Mary the hero: she was chosen by grace, not because she was better than other girls. Second, hearing verse 37 as “God will give me whatever I want”: Mary got a promise, not an easy life — saying yes was risky, and she had to wait.

Question helps

Q1 — Nazareth was tiny, far from the temple, never mentioned in the Old Testament, and people looked down on it. Mary was young, poor and unknown. God picks the people the world overlooks (1 Corinthians 1:27–28).

Q2 — “Greetings, favored woman!” Grace came first. School, sport and socials say you have to earn your place; God says you are loved first, and obedience is our response (1 John 4:19).

Q3 — Great; Son of the Most High; King on David’s throne; a kingdom that never ends. His name is his mission: he came to save.

Q4 — Zechariah didn’t believe and was made silent; Mary didn’t understand, asked, and God told her more. Questions asked in order to trust God are fine.

Q5 — The Holy Spirit and the power of God: God himself will do it. Jesus is the holy Son of God — fully God and fully human. Nothing is impossible with God.

Q6 — Joseph might leave her; she could be shamed, or even stoned under the law. She said yes because she trusted God’s word, not because she was fearless. Jesus later prayed the same way in Gethsemane (Luke 22:42).

Q7 — Elizabeth was the one person who understood — she was having an “impossible” baby too. Mary was blessed because she believed what God said (v.45).

Q8 — The sermon said faith needs real friends, not algorithm company. Small groups like this one aren’t optional extras.
