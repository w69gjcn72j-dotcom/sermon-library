The Church is God’s wisdom and God’s sign

Ephesians 3:10-12

Behold God’s wisdom in the Christian Church, who boldly comes to him in Christ

Creation: God’s wisdom in creation; man enjoys unlimited access to God

Fall: Man’s foolishness in thinking they’re wiser than God;

Judgement: Man’s shamefully cut off from God’s presence

Salvation: Jesus comes to die to grant us access to God

Resurrection: Jesus is raised and fulfils the wise purposes of God

Regeneration: Those who trust in Jesus are have the Spirit of the Son

Last Things: God shows his wisdom through the Church, a witness to the wisdom of God and a sign to Christ the Way

So…

Behold God’s wisdom in the Church

Be seen as a witness to Jesus the Way

Be bold and confidently come before God

COVID-19 has caused a lot of changes in the way we do Church over the past few months.

For a long time, we couldn’t meet in person, we couldn’t teach the Bible in various places and we couldn’t have the Lord’s Supper.

But praise God, we can do many of these activities now.

Many of us are back in person at Church, we’ll be celebrating the Lord’s Supper later on and I’m happy to say that all of the classes that were stopped before have resumed.

One of this is the KOGWORX Bible time that I have with the children at the Children Centre.

We started it again on Thursday.

Peter and Naomi were there as well.

I began with a simple verse, a verse that we have already mentioned in today’s service: John 3:16,

“ For God loved the world in this way: He gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who believes in him will not perish but have eternal life.”

It wasn’t the main part of the lesson.

It was a well-known verse and a verse that I’ve done with the children earlier this year.

I didn’t think I’ll spend a lot of time on it

But it must be a new group of children as they had many questions about the verse.

“What does perish mean?”

“Who is the Son?”

“Why does he have to die?”

They were great questions!

I’m so glad that they were genuinely interested in John 3:16.

And it’s not just children who ask me all sorts of questions.

Over the past few months, there were great questions raised in different Bible studies that we have here at Church.

“What does God want me to do?”

“How do I keep trusting God?”

“How can I pray?”

To help us know how to answer these questions, I gave a sermon series earlier this year on the Seven Stars, seven teachings to help us get a better grasp of what the Bible teaches.

Do you remember what they were?

Here are some pictures to help jog our memory.

If you have your paper and pen there, see if you can jot them down as we go through these.

Creation

God is the Creator, we are the Creatures

Fall

God is the Holy One, we are the sinners

Judgement

God is the Judge, we are the judged

Salvation

God is the Saviour, in Christ we are the saved.

Resurrection

Christ is risen, those in Christ will be raised with him.

Regeneration

God sent His Spirit; His Spirit lives in us.

The Last things

God’s focus on the Last Day is Christ; our hope on the Last Day must also be Christ

These seven teachings are like seven stars to help us get a better grasp of what the Bible teaches us.

To answer questions we have on the Bible, it’ll be very helpful to approach with these seven stars as a guide.

Over the next two weeks, we’ll be looking at two questions from these seven stars.

What is the Church?

What is our Mission?

Today, we’ll be looking at the question, What is the Church?

As we look through the Bible, we see that the Church is both the wisdom of God and the Sign of God.

Let’s begin with the first star, creation

Creation

We see God’s wisdom on full display from the very beginning, when he created the heavens and the earth.

God’s creation is so intricate.

Each cell in our body is incredibly effective and efficient.

When I was studying Biochemistry, one of the things that we have to memorise is the Krebs Cycle.

It’s a diagram, a pathway, that describes how our cells uses different fragments from food that we eat and convert them into energy.

That doesn’t just happen in humans but in all oxygen-breathing organisms.

This happens millions of times every second in humans, in animals, in birds to keep us alive.

There’s a lot of God’s wisdom in there.

And God’s wisdom is not just found in us, it’s also found all around us.

As well as making things, God had also made natural laws to keep these things in order.

It’s because God’s order is so precise that scientists can study nature and make reliable predictions.

For example, a few days ago, I found out that today’s sunrise happened at 5:55am.

I can also tell you that in 100 years’ time, on 1/11/2120, the sun will also rise again at 5:55am.

That’s not because I’m clever; it’s because God has so set everything perfectly in place that scientists can predict with confidence when sunrise is going to happen.

Not only is God wise, he’s also accessible.

When God made human beings, he originally gave us free access to him.

The first humans could speak directly to God.

And God spoke directly to them.

In fact, God would walk in the garden that he had made for the first humans and he would seek the humans’ audience.

God and the humans were in a close relationship, like a family.

Fall

However, we human beings think that we’re wiser than God, smarter than God.

God told the first humans, Adam and Eve, not to eat from the fruit of the knowledge of good and evil.

But Adam and Eve had done the very thing that God told them not to do.

Rather than living in God’s world according to his terms, we want to do it on our own terms.

Even though God had warned them that they would definitely die, they went ahead anyway.

That’s utter stupidity!

But Adam and Eve had done it anyway.

They wanted to be like God; they wanted to be able to be in the same place as God.

They wanted “to play God.”

“To play God” is a phrase that’s often used to describe people who act as if they are in total control of something.

It’s to play God’s part even though you’re not God.

It’s sometimes used of scientists, for example, who conduct experiments that involve cloning or alternating human babies are said to play God.

For Adam and Eve, they decided to play God when they deliberately disobeyed God.

And all of us, as sons and daughters of Adam and Eve, play God to a lesser extent.

When we decide that we’re wiser than God, we follow what our first parents did and played God.

When we disobey God, we foolishly play God’s part even though we’re not God.

Judgement

The result? We are shamefully cut off from God’s presence.

As a result of what they’ve done, Adam and Eve were kicked out of God’s place.

They were banished from their home.

And that’s been the way for us their children ever since.

Ever since they were banished from God’s presence, we, too have been banished from God’s presence.

We hadn’t known what it’s like to be in a perfect world.

We hadn’t known what it’s like to come freely into God’s presence.

That’s why even though this world is all that we know, we know that this world is not perfect.

There are natural disasters, there are man-made evils, there is the pandemic, among many other illnesses, that all seem so wrong, so out of place.

That’s because they are.

They don’t belong to God’s perfect world.

This world as we know it is not how the world should be.

It’s a world that’s dying and we’re dying with it.

It’s a world that’s dying because it all started with us trying to play God.

Not only is the world dying; our relationship with God has turned from one of family into one of enmity.

Instead of relating to God as his children, we are relating to God as his enemies.

It’s like families who have broken up.

There are parents who have been absent physically and emotionally from their children.

We have children who have abandoned their parents.

We have brothers who have been feuding with brothers, sisters who have been at war with their sisters.

All these broken relationships are painful.

But at the heart of all these broken relationships is our broken relationship with God.

Like a family that’s been torn apart, humanity has been torn apart from God, because we wanted to play God.

Salvation

But God our Creator doesn’t want to leave us dying in a dying world.

He’s a loving Father who doesn’t want to see his children perish.

And so, He’s sent His one and only Son to die for the punishment that we deserve.

God the Father sent His Only Son, Jesus Christ, who is fully God, to become fully man.

Jesus, fully God, fully Man, came to die our death on the cross.

He died the death that we deserve for playing God.

Can you see how incredible that is?

We played God and were destined for death.

Jesus is God and chose to die for us.

We chose foolishly and disobeyed God but Jesus chose wisely and trusted God.

As a result of Jesus’ choice, those who trust in what Jesus has done can now come into God’s presence.

Because of what Jesus’ done on the cross, our broken relationship with God the Father can now been restored, if we trust in Jesus.

The story is told of a young boy called Pedro in Mexico City who had a fight with his father and had left his home.

Heartbroken, Pedro’s father decided to put posters up to try to reconnect with his son.

On the poster it says, “Pedro, all’s forgiven. Come and meet me at the post office at midday tomorrow. Your Father.”

At midday the next day, Pedro’s father came to look for Pedro at the post office.

When he got there, there were hundreds of men waiting for their father.

All these men were called Pedro too and they also wanted to come home to their father.

When God the Father’s sent Jesus, he’s saying to us, “My Son Jesus has paid for all that you’ve done. All’s forgiven. Come home.”

Resurrection

Not only has Jesus died for us; he’s also risen from the dead.

Jesus’ death is part of God’s plan and wise purposes.

God has planned that Jesus would die for our sins.

More than that, God has planned that Jesus would rise from the dead.

And that’s what happened! Jesus rose from the dead to show that God has been planning this all along.

Jesus has died and has risen just as God has foretold it in the Old Testament part of the Bible.

Not only is the God wise Creator; God is also the wise Saviour, the wise Rescuer.

Jesus has come and accomplished what God the Father had planned.

Regeneration

When a person believes in Jesus, they have a new beginning.

When a person believes in Jesus, the Spirit of Jesus, the Holy Spirit, enters the person.

They become a new person; he becomes a new creature, a new creation.

That doesn’t mean that have supernatural powers.

But it does mean that they can now understand God’s purposes whereas they couldn’t before.

They can understand God’s purposes because they now have a new relationship with God in Jesus.

They’ve been made right with God; they’ve been reconciled to God.

In Jesus, they can come into God’s presence in full confidence, not because of what they’ve done but because of what Jesus has done

Last Things

This takes us to what the Church is.

The Church is a community of people that God has gathered in the last days, the days between Jesus resurrection and the day when he comes back.

This is a community of people who trusts in Jesus, who hopes in Jesus.

It’s a people who have been reconciled to God, filled with the Spirit of Jesus, God’s Son.

God has chosen to use the gathering of people, the Church, to show his wisdom and to show people how to come confidently to him.

In the Church we see both the wisdom of God and the sign of God.

That’s what we see in today’s passage.

In the passage, we’ll see three things that we’re to do in response to what the Church is.

We’re to behold, we’re to be seen and we’re to be bold.

Please open up your Bible with me, beginning from Ephesians 3:10,

“ This is so that God’s multi-faceted wisdom may now be made known through the church to the rulers and authorities in the heavens. ”

When you see the Church, you see the wisdom of God.

Here the word translated as multi-faceted was used to describe a cloak that’s multi-coloured.

It’s one cloak but it has a diversity of colours.

That’s what God’s wisdom is like.

There is one God and his wisdom is multi-faceted, there are many aspects to it.

We see a glimpse of God’s wisdom in his creation.

But now, God has chosen to reveal his multi-faceted wisdom in the Church.

God wants to show the many aspects of his wisdom through the people whom he rescued and reconciled in His Son.

He wants to show his wisdom through the Church.

The Bible here mentions rulers and authorities in heavens.

The rulers and authorities here refer to spiritual beings, beings that are invisible to us but whom God sees.

When God wants to display his wisdom to them, he doesn’t refer to the intricacies of life or the majestic wonders of the galaxies.

No, when God wants to display his wisdom, he points them to the Church.

When these heavenly powers see us here, gathered Sunday by Sunday here at Kogarah, they see the wisdom of God.

When they see other Churches who trust in Jesus, gathering in other parts of the world, gathered online, they will fall down before in God and praise God for his wisdom.

For when they see us, weak and powerless as we are, they see a people whom God in his wisdom has reconciled and gathered in Jesus.

They’ll see a people brought back from death to life, by God himself.

In the Church, they will see the wisdom of God.

Second, we are to be seen as witnesses of God.

Jesus has said to his disciples that they will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come on them, and they will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth. (Acts 1:8)

Filled with the Holy Spirit, the Church is to be a witness of Jesus.

They are like a city on a hill, not to be hidden.

The Church is to be seen not only by the rulers and authorities in the heavens but also by the local people living here on earth.

When people see us, they should be able to see a glimpse of God’s wisdom.

When people see us, they should be able to see what being reconciled to God looks like.

When people see us, they should be able to see the gospel, the good news of Jesus Christ.

To use the story of Pedro earlier on, the Church is like the poster that the father put up all around the place.

Just like the posters that Pedro’s put up carries a message of hope to all the Pedros in Mexico City,

We carry our Father’s message of reconciliation to everyone around us.

We tell the people in our suburbs, on our streets, in our work, in our schools, that because God’s sent Jesus, all’s forgiven. Come back to him!

God chose to use us here, this Church, weak as we are, to rescue people back to himself.

What an incredible honour! What an incredible responsibility!

Which takes us to the third point, Be bold.

Look with me at verses 11-12, “ This is according to his eternal purpose accomplished in Christ Jesus our Lord. 12 In him we have boldness and confident access through faith in him.”

Whereas Adam and Eve were shamefully banished from God’s presence, in Jesus, we can now enter God’s presence with boldness and confidence.

When we come into God’s presence, we’re not coming in based on how good we’ve been.

If we were trying to come before God’s presence based on how good we’ve been, none of us can come before God.

But if we come before God through faith in him, by trusting in him, then we can have full confidence that we can come into God’s presence.

We can have full confidence that when we come before God in Christ, he will hear us.

What do we do when we come into God’s presence?

We praise God and we pray to God.

We praise God for what he has done for us in Jesus and we ask him for the strength to follow Jesus.

And God will hear us.

And we can be confident that God will hear us.

And so, behold God’s wisdom, be seen as God’s witnesses and be bold as you pray to him.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What do you see when you look at the Church?

Take a look around.

God displays his multi-faceted wisdom in the Church.

Can you see it?

Far too often, we look at the Church the way the world sees the Church.

The world is not impressed with the Church and neither are we.

But take a look around again.

See the Church from God’s point of view.

This is the Church that He himself has gathered through his Son.

This is the Church that He’s sent His son to die for.

This is the Church that he chose to show his multi-faceted wisdom.

Look at the Church from God’s point of view, rather than the world’s point of view.

Second, when people see you, what will they see?

If you are a member of the Church, you are what God shows as his wisdom.

What will people see?

When we talk about the Church, we often talk about the failures in other people.

But don’t worry about the other people in the church.

I’m talking about you: when people see you, what will they see?

Will people see you living out the gospel of Jesus Christ?

Will they see you loving your brothers and sisters in Christ, just as Christ loves you?

Will people hear you talking about the message of forgiveness?

Our Church motto begins with these words, See the Gospel.

That’s what people should see in us as they come into the Church.

They should see the Gospel in us.

Thirdly, are you bold in your prayer?

Jesus has died for you to give you access to God the Father.

Be bold in your prayer.

Of course, don’t ask for things for your own glory.

If you have been saved by Jesus, you will ask for things for his glory.

Ask him for wisdom to understand his will.

Ask him for the love to love him and other people.

Ask God to give you the strength to do what he’s asked you to do.

Be confident that he will give you strength equal to your task.

Friends, God has gathered us in Jesus Christ as a Church and it’s through us, his Church that he shows his wisdom.

God has gathered us in Jesus Christ as a Church to be his witness, his sign, to draw people to his son.

Let’s be the Church that shows God’s wisdom, let’s be the Church that points people to Jesus.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for the coming of Jesus Christ. We thank you that through him, you have saved us and have reconciled us to yourself. Father, please give us the love to love you with all of our hearts, all our souls, all our minds and all our strengths, give us a love like Jesus’, so that we can love one another as he has loved us, and give us strength to be faithful witnesses, until the day Jesus comes back, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
