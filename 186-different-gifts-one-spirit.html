Who are the spirituals? PPT

Who are the spirituals?

Look with me at 1 Corinthians 12:1

“Now concerning spiritual gifts: brothers and sisters, I do not want you to be unaware.”

In our Bibles, the phrase translated as “spiritual gifts” is originally just “spiritual”.

In the context of the passage, it most probably refers to people who are “spiritual”.

What do we mean when we say someone is spiritual?

In the West, to be spiritual is to be someone who thinks about otherworldly matter.

It’s someone who talks about their spiritual experiences.

They walk into a so-called sacred building (like a Church) or visit a so-called sacred place (like the rainforest) and describe the experience as being spiritual.

In other countries, to be spiritual means going to the temple, or to have regular prayer times throughout the day.

For many people around the world, to be spiritual is to be devout and to follow religious rituals.

For those in the West, they might find their spiritual experience in the quietness of a Church.

For those in the Middle East or the East, they might find their spiritual experience in the busyness of a religious festival.

When the Christians in Corinth believed, they were still tied to what their society thought about spirituality.

They thought that if they displayed similar traits as those who were ‘spiritual’ around them, then they, too would be spiritual, except that they’ll have a Christian version of spirituality.

But Paul doesn’t want them to be unaware about what it means to be truly spiritual.

Look at what he says in verse 2, “2 You know that when you were pagans, you used to be enticed and led astray by mute idols.”

What they had previously thought of as spiritual were teachings taught by people who followed mute idols.

The pagans’ version of spirituality were based on people who followed a mute piece of clay.

The so-called experiences promoted by these religions were not true spiritual experiences at all.

If the followers of pagan religions aren’t truly spiritual, then what makes a person truly spiritual?

Look with me at verse 3, ‘ Therefore I want you to know that no one speaking by the Spirit of God says, “Jesus is cursed,” and no one can say, “Jesus is Lord,” except by the Holy Spirit.’

How do you know whether a person is truly spiritual?

A person is truly spiritual, if they confess that Jesus is Lord.

That’s the true test to see whether someone is spiritual or not.

No one can say, Jesus is Lord except by the Holy Spirit.

Now of course, the Bible is not talking about lip-service.

The phrase, “Jesus is Lord” is not like a magic formula that you say to become spiritual.

The person who says it, must mean it from the bottom of his or her heart.

It’s not simply an intellectual statement; it’s a phrase of total surrender.

To say Jesus is Lord is to completely surrender our heart, soul, our minds, to Him.

It’s to say, “Lord Jesus, here’s my life, take it, it’s yours!”

It’s a total surrender.

It’s a surrender that’s humanly impossible without the Holy Spirit. PPT

The Bible commentator, Leon Morris, says this about this verse, “The Lordship of Christ is not a human discovery, but one made only with the Spirit working in the heart.”

It’s only as the Holy Spirit vivifies a person, makes the person spiritually alive, that a person can say honestly and sincerely, “Jesus is Lord”. PPT

If a person says, “Jesus is Lord” without believing it, then the person is a liar.

If a person doesn’t publicly say “Jesus is Lord” even though they believe it, then the person is a coward and they need to repent.

But the spiritual person, filled by the Holy Spirit, can say both publicly and privately, in all honestly and sincerity, that “Jesus is Lord”.

In a Church like ours, it’s easy to say the words, “Jesus is Lord”.

But outside the Church, it can be a very dangerous thing to say.

In an empire where the emperor portrays himself as god, Christians in the first century risked their lives when they say, “Jesus is Lord”.

And there are places in the present twenty-first century, where a political leader might portray himself as someone even more important than gods and religions,

Where it’s still dangerous to confess, “Jesus is Lord”.

There are countries, ideologies, interest groups, pop stars and other religions that want to silent the Christians.

But, friends, we mustn’t remain silent.

Whether in season or out of season, we must confess “Jesus is Lord.”

That’s what the Holy Spirit inside of us enables to believe.

We must be honest and confess the truth that will stand, even if the whole world should fall, namely, that “Jesus is Lord.”

That’s what the spirituals will confess.

That’s who the spirituals are; people who confess that Jesus is Lord.

As spirituals, people filled with the Holy Spirit, we have spiritual gifts

Where are the gifts from? PPT

Where are these gifts from? They are from the triune God.

Look with me at verses 4-6, “ 4 Now there are different gifts, but the same Spirit. 5 There are different ministries, but the same Lord. 6 And there are different activities, but the same God produces each gift in each person.”

God has given spiritual gifts to those who confess that Jesus is Lord.

These spiritual gifts are from the Triune God – God the Father, God the Son and God the Holy Spirit.

God the Spirit is the one who distribute the gifts; he is the One who give those gifts to the believers.

God the Son is the one who ministers, the one who came to serve.

He is the one who tells his followers to serve, to be a slave, just as he, the Son of Man, did not come to be served by to serve.

The gifts that the Spirit gives are also ministries or services, by which believers are to serve one another.

Different gifts, one Spirit; Different ministries, one Lord.

Finally there are different activities, but the one God who produces each gift in each person.

God the Father is the one who enables, empowers, each person with their unique gift.

All three of the Persons of the Triune God work to bring his gifts to believers of Jesus Christ.

The Spirit distributes, the Father empowers so that the believers of Jesus Christ may minister or serve.

What the believers of Jesus Christ have received are from God; there’s no room for boasting or for putting others down.

Though there are different gifts, there’s a unity that binds all these gifts together.

It’s the Triune God who gives these gifts.

These are not merely natural talents, but God-given gifts.

What are we supposed to with these gifts? What are these gifts for?

What are the gifts for?

Look with me at verse 7, “7 A manifestation of the Spirit is given to each person for the common good”

God’s given his gifts to each person who believes in him for the common good.

God’s given everyone who confesses that “Jesus is Lord” his spiritual gifts for the good of others who also confess that Jesus is Lord.

That’s what a spiritual person has.

Every spiritual person who confesses that ‘Jesus is Lord’, has spiritual gifts from God.

Some may have more than one, others will have different spiritual gifts at different times.

But every spiritual person has at least one spiritual gift from God.

These spiritual gifts aren’t meant for our own individual benefit.

They’re meant to be used for the good of others.

Our God given gifts are meant to be used for the good of Christians.

In Ephesians 4:12-13, Paul mentions another three purposes for God-given gifts. PPT

Just prior to this passage, the Apostle Paul was mentioning a number of gifts that Jesus has given to the Church.

Why did he these gifts to them? He gave these for:

“12 equipping the saints for the work of ministry, to build up the body of Christ,13 until we all reach unity in the faith and in the knowledge of God’s Son, growing into maturity with a stature measured by Christ’s fullness.”

The gifts that God gives us is vital for the growth of the Church.

God gives his gifts to the Spirituals, people who confess Jesus’ name, so that together, they will grow into maturity.

We need the gifts that God’s given to one another so that we can grow in Christ, together.

As well as equipping each other, building each other and helping each other grow, we also manifest the Spirit when we use our God-given gifts in the Church.

We can’t see the Spirit and we don’t know what the Spirit looks like.

But when we use our God-given gifts to serve others, we see the Spirit at work.

If you want to see evidence for the work of the Holy Spirit, take a look at what God has done through people using their gifts among the public schools in NSW.

If you want to see evidence of the work of the Holy Spirit, take a look at the Church and see how people have used their God-given gifts for the good of others.

How do we know our gifts? PPT

How about us? How do we know what our gifts are?

God has given each person who confesses Jesus Christ a gift that’s particularly suited for the Church at a particular time.

Paul gives a list here in Corinthians of some of the gifts that God gives to Corinthian Church; it’s not an exhaustive list.

For the sake of time, I’ll go through this list fairly quickly.

Look with me at verse 8, “to one is given a message of wisdom through the Spirit, to another, a message of knowledge by the same Spirit,”

The one who has a message of wisdom is the one who talks about what God would want us to do in a given situation.

How does God’s word apply here?

The one who has a message of knowledge is the one who readily looks at what God has already said in his word about similar situations.

What passages in God’s word speak about our situation here?

Verses 9 to 10, “ 9 to another, faith by the same Spirit, to another, gifts of healing by the one Spirit, 10 to another, the performing of miracles, to another, prophecy, to another, distinguishing between spirits, to another, different kinds of tongue to another, interpretation of tongues.

In certain situations, especially in very difficult situations, God might give a special gift of grace to help people trust him during very trying times.

It might be faith to trust that God will deliver someone through a difficult, even life-threatening situation.

It might also be the faith to trust God even if he doesn’t deliver someone through a difficult situation.

To someone else, God might give the gift of healing.

There will be times when God heals someone miraculously.

To yet someone else, God might give the gift of performing miracles.

While it might mean a miraculous healing, it seems to imply a wider category of miracles.

Do Christians believe in gifts of healing and gifts of performing miracles?

Of course we do! We’ve seen many in the New Testament and we might have heard the testimonies of people who have been miraculously healed as a result of prayer.

However, we must remember that these are gifts of the Holy Spirit.

They are gifts freely given by him; they aren’t gifts that we can demand.

And so, while they might be wonderful gifts to have, we shouldn’t be demanding these gifts as if we’re entitled to them.

To some, God’s given the gift of prophecy, that is, the gift to speak with conviction about God’s word and the future consequence of our obedience or disobedience to him.

To other people, God’s given the gift to distinguish the spirits, to distinguish those who speak the truth from those who speak lies.

And finally, to some people he gives the gift to speak in tongues and others to interpretation of tongues.

There are those who speak a language that the congregation doesn’t understand and others who interpret for them.

These are some of the gifts that God’s given to the spirituals, people who confess that Jesus is Lord by the power of the Holy Spirit.

While the gifts are different, they are all from the same Spirit, who distributes to each person according to his will.

To help us get a better idea where the different gifts fit in our Church, I’ve put together a 5G mind map.PPT

I’ve shown this map a while ago but there’s been an update.

The 5G’s stand for: Grow, Gather, Governance, Go and Giftedness.

Grow refers to gifts and ministries that help us grow in the Lord Christ.

This includes ministries like teaching, Bible studies, personal Bible reading and teaching children and youth.PPT

One of the Bible studies that I’ve written recently is the Partnership course.

This is a course that I would like all our Bible studies to do so that we can think about what it means for us to grow together at St Paul’s.PPT

Gather refers to gifts and ministries that help us gather people together, particularly on Sundays.

This includes ministries like music, public reading of the Bible, translation, setting up the tech for audio and video.

Governance refers to gifts and ministries that help the administration and the maintenance of the Church.

This includes ministries about understanding our finance, making sure we’re complying with legal and diocesan guidelines.

Go refers to gifts and ministries that help us get out there in the community to tell people about Jesus.

This includes ministries like SRE, St Patrick’s Green, door-knocking, designing and printing promotional materials, and hosting various events.

Finally, Giftedness refers to gifts and ministries that help us equip people so that they will be able to develop their gifts at Church.

This is a new ministry for us and I’m very excited that we got this started.

This includes ministries where we identify and develop gifts of various people, welcoming people, keeping an eye out on who we can train, coaching and mentor.

Our hope is that under God, we can help one another use the gifts that God’s given to each one of us for the common good.

God has given to each person who believes in him a gift for the benefit of the Church.

What’s yours? How have you used your gift in God’s Church?

When I was thinking about how we can do this last year, I originally thought about using a questionnaire of some sort.

For example, I might have a list of gifts down the page and I would ask you to tick the lists you think you have.

However, after thinking about it, I don’t think that will work.

We don’t determine what gifts God’s given us; God does.

In my experience, we often get our gifts wrong.

We might think we’re good at something, but then we might realise that that thing is not our gift.
