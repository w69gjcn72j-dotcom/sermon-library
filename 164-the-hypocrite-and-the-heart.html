The Hypocrite and the Heart

Big Idea: Don’t be hypocritical teachers whose falsehood breeds blasphemy; be people with Spirit-circumcised hearts, living out God’s word for his praise and glory.

1. Privilege without practice dishonours God's name (2:17–24)

2. God praises the changed heart, not the outward mark (2:25–29)

3. Our falseness cannot cancel God's faithfulness (3:1–8)

On Wednesday, we took KOGYouth out for an excursion to bowling.

It was a fun day and I’m so glad that they enjoyed themselves.

I was in a team with a group of brothers.

They were great and they looked after each other.

Even when they were in a bowling competition with one another, they cheered on each other’s success and showed great sportsmanship.

However, there were times when one of the brothers got ahead by a lot and the other brothers started chanting, ‘for shame, for shame’, in order to distract their older brother.

It was all for fun, of course, and they all laughed whether the older brother struck down all the pins or missed them all.

It was great to see the brothers having fun with one another.

However, the phrase ‘for shame, for shame’, has been used for over seven hundred years to describe someone who ought to be ashamed of their behaviour.

It’s a judgement that a community gives to someone who’s done something bad.

For example, a villager may say, “He stole Farmer John’s sheep.”

And the community responds by saying, “For shame, for shame.”

Over the past few weeks, as we study the letter to the Romans, we’ve seen God’s judgement on the people of the world.

We’ve seen that God’s fury is on those who have been outright behaving badly.

We might echo God’s judgement on them by saying, “For shame, for shame”.

Last week, we saw that God’s fury is not just on those who behave badly, but even on those who call out others who behave badly.

Usually, it’s the good people of the community who call out the behaviour of the bad.

However, God’s fury is also on those people as well.

Why is God furious even on those people whom our community thinks are good, people who appear to be morally superior?

Please open your Bible to Romans chapter two, beginning from verse seventeen.

We are going to see why it is that God is furious at those who are seen by our community as morally superior people.

We’re going to see three things:

1. Privilege without practice dishonours God's name (2:17–24)

2. God praises the changed heart, not the outward mark (2:25–29)

3. Our falseness cannot cancel God's faithfulness (3:1–8)

Let’s pray before we go deeper, “Father, by your Holy Spirit, please open our eyes that we might see even more of your righteousness, for Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

Privilege without practice dishonours God's name (2:17–24)

In Romans chapter one, we read about God’s fury against those who had intentionally turned away from him, who deliberately chose to live a life in outright defiance of God.

Last week, as we began Romans chapter two, we saw that those who judged others and thought of themselves as “good” were no better than the first group.

They looked down on the faults and sins of others, and yet they did the same thing.

They would not escape God’s fury either.

Whereas last week, it was implicit, that is, you kind of get the vibe that the Apostle Paul had the Jews in mind, he makes it explicitly clear in today’s passage.

Open up with me to Romans chapter two beginning from verse 17: “Now if you call yourself a Jew, and rely on the law, and boast in God,” He was referring specifically to the Jews of his day.

Now while they are racially related to the Jews of today, Paul was speaking to Jews who were far more religious than today’s Jews.

While Paul’s original audience were these ancient Jews, there’s a strong warning for all of us here today, especially those of us who have been in Churches for a long time, that privilege without practice dishonours God’s name.

Paul began by saying that these Jews relied on the law and boasted in God.

These Jews knew the Bible very well – they relied on God’s laws.

They would say that they read the Bible, probably every day.

They also boasted in God.

They were not ashamed of God but spoke about him boldly.

They would tell others about how good God is!

And more than that, they knew God’s will; they knew what God wanted because God had shown his will to them in the Bible.

Here the Bible’s not talking about God revealing every detail of his will to them.

Rather, God has revealed what he wanted from his people, generally speaking.

They knew what God wanted, and they also knew that what God wanted was good.

Since they knew God’s laws, they knew what was actually good, not just what the average person felt was good.

They were experts at what’s superior.

It’s like this painting by Caravaggio.

It’s called Cardsharps.

If you just look at it briefly, it just looks like a few people dressed up to play a card game.

However, if you look at it more closely, you’ll see that while the young, rich boy on the left was looking at his card, the guy next to him was giving hints to his partner on the right, telling him what card he had.

The partner on the right is ready to swap his card.

And even though these two partners in crime looked like they were fairly well-to-do, their mismatched clothes showed that they only pretended to be rich.

In contrast, their victim actually wore expensive clothes, duped by these two well-dressed thieves.

The Jews believed that they had superior insight and knowledge, that they were not like the young man in the painting.

Rather they were like us, the observers, who saw the truth.

The Jews were convinced that they were guides to the blind, light to those in the darkness, instructor to the ignorant and teacher of the immature.

They were convinced that they knew God’s truth so well that they could lead others, who were not as enlightened, not as gifted by God, not as mature, to see the truth for themselves.

They believed that they’ve got the law of God, the very embodiment of knowledge and truth.

Can you imagine someone saying that they’ve got the very embodiment of knowledge and truth in their hands, that God’s handed them superior knowledge and that they had the ability to help the rest of us see the truth about reality?

We would probably think that they thought too highly of themselves and that they needed a reality check.

But you know what?

Paul didn’t dispute any of these.

That’s because to an extent what they said is true.

It was a tremendous privilege to be a Jew, to have the law of the living God, given to you in your native language.

It’s a tremendous honour to say that the Creator God spoke to one of your people thousands of years ago and had personally shown you and your people what it meant to live.

Can you imagine the indigenous people living in the middle of Australia, claiming to say that God had appeared to them, came down to Uluru, in the middle of Australia, and told them how to live, in their own native language?

Can you imagine the Japanese claiming that God’s written down his law in Kanji and had passed his will through the Japanese people over many generations?

Neither the indigenous people of Australia nor the Japanese people could claim that, but the Jews can, for that’s what God’s done.

The Jews have received the precious law of God from God himself.

It is a tremendous privilege.

But, knowing the law of God is not the same as practising the law of God.

If you have the privilege of knowing the law of God, you have the responsibility of practising the law of God.

Look with me at verse 21 “21 you then, who teach another, don’t you teach yourself? You who preach, “You must not steal”—do you steal?”

You might know the law of God so well that you can teach other people God’s laws, but do you teach others without teaching yourself?

Look at the Caravaggio painting again.

The two thieves were obviously stealing from the rich young man, and both the painter and us as an audience could clearly see them in the act.

Now imagine the young man absent-mindedly left all the coins behind.

The two thieves look at you and offer you some of the coins.

Would you be an opportunistic petty thief?

Would you make excuses and keep the money for yourself?

Or would you practise what you preach?

In Australia, the number of petty thefts, that is, stealing when an opportunity presents itself, outnumber planned robberies by a ratio of 100 to 1.

Though we might say that stealing is wrong, there are many people in our society, including some of us here today, who would steal, when given the opportunity.

And so, Paul’s challenge to us still stands today, “You who preach, “You must not steal”—do you steal?”

Look with me at verse 22, ‘22 You who say, “You must not commit adultery”—do you commit adultery? You who detest idols, do you rob temples?’

Many of us know that God says it’s wrong to commit adultery, to be unfaithful to our spouse.

And yet, there are still many people who choose to be unfaithful and abandon their spouse and their families.

Finally, there are those who detest idols, false gods whom the real God told us not to worship, and yet, many of us still rob God’s temple.

Robbing God’s temple was a charge that God laid against his people in Malachi 3:8, when God said to his people through Malachi, ‘“Will a man rob God? Yet you are robbing me!” “How do we rob you?” you ask. “By not making the payments of the tenth and the contributions.”’

God’s requirement from his people was to give a tenth of all that they had, plus other contributions, as part of their worship.

However, they refused to give God a tenth of all that God gave them.

They weren’t giving God what they were supposed to.

They were robbing God.

While Christians are not told to give a tenth of all we had, many faithful Christians have done it as part of their worship of God.

Someone once asked how many people it would take to hire a minister.

The answer?

Ten.

If each person gave a tenth of their income, there would be enough to hire a minister.

In fact, the answer would be nine, if the minister also gives a tenth of all he has as part of his worship.

As Christians we detest idol worship.

But aren’t we committing the same sin we accuse others of, when we ourselves detest genuine worship of the real God by robbing God?

Verse 23, “You who boast in the law, do you dishonour God by breaking the law?”

Having the privilege of knowing God’s law carries the responsibility of practising God’s law.

When we don’t practise God’s law, God’s name is blasphemed.

Verse 24, “For, as it is written: The name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of you.”

For God’s name to be blasphemed means that someone had spoken something false about God.

For example, if a Jew steals even though he told other people God’s law says that they shouldn’t steal, that Jew would be seen as a hypocrite and so others would give God a bad name because of him.

That Jew’s disobedience leads to God’s name being dishonoured.

Friends, like the Jews, we have been given a tremendous privilege.

God has given us both the Old and the New Testament.

God has given us the whole Bible and has shown us even more clearly his will.

We have even more reason than the Jews to boast in our God, for we know that God has sent his own Son Jesus Christ to rescue us from the dominion of darkness into the Kingdom of His Son.

God has revealed himself to us in the Person of our Lord Jesus Christ.

There is no higher privilege than to be called a child of God, to be able to call God Abba, Father.

However, with that great privilege comes great responsibility.

It’s no good for us to teach people about the good news of Jesus if our lives don’t show it.

In fact, just like the Jews who taught about God and yet failed to practise what they taught, we Christians can do the very same thing.

And when we do that, God’s name is blasphemed.

When we say, “You must love God with all your heart”, do we love God with all of our hearts?

When we say, “You should show more honour and respect to God”, do we show respect and honour to God?

And here’s one that I hear so often, “I wish I had more friends and more fellowship at Church”, are you a friend to others at Church?

We know what the Bible teaches, we teach what the Bible teaches, we judge others according to what the Bible teaches, but do we teach ourselves the same thing?

Listen to verse 23 again, “You who boast in the law, do you dishonour God by breaking the law?”

Often on Sunday mornings, you will see me running from one place to another, talking to different people along the way, getting ready for the next thing on the programme.

Thinking through the years of ministry, I realise that I often do run around on Sunday because I want to serve the Church in different ways.

And so, I would often miss out on morning tea, not because I wanted to, but because by the time I got there, all the food and all the drinks are gone.

And that’s ok, I usually wouldn’t have time to have morning tea anyway.

However, I remember one time, must have been over thirty years ago, an older sister gave me a cup of water during morning tea.

She didn’t want to ask me anything, she didn’t need anything from me, she just wanted to give me a cup of water.

I still appreciate that cup of drink to this day.

She was a sister who practised what the Bible taught.

She saw me thirsty and she gave me a drink.

Friends, we are a Church that has the wonderful privilege of teaching and preaching the Word of God every Sunday here at Church, as well as throughout the week in our Home Groups and KOG Bible studies, as well as to over 250 students in our local schools.

We get to share the Word of God with the people of Kogarah and beyond.

How good is that!

However, please, as well as knowing the word, live the word.

Start with small steps, like saying Hi to someone at Church and genuinely be interested in them.

Honour God by the way you worship, whether it’s by turning up, not only on time but earlier, to serve and to speak with new people who are already here.

Don’t be a hypocrite who tells people what it means to live as God’s people but fails to do so.

Such hypocrisy breeds blasphemy against God’s name.

God praises the changed heart, not the outward mark (2:25–29)

Paul then turns his attention to a physical mark that every true Jewish boy has.

Look with me at verse 25, “Circumcision benefits you if you observe the law, but if you are a lawbreaker, your circumcision has become uncircumcision.”

In the book of Genesis, God tells Abraham that as a sign of the covenant that he had made with Abraham, every male in Abraham’s household had to be circumcised.

And so for the past four thousand years, every Jewish male had to be circumcised.

This was God’s command and it was a physical mark that showed that the Jewish man belonged to God’s family.

However, while circumcision was a good thing that God gave as a sign for the Jews, it was a sign pointing to something even more important.

Circumcision was the sign that showed that they were God’s people, that they would follow and obey God.

Paul’s saying that if you have the sign of a person who belongs to God and obeys God but in reality you live as if you don’t belong to God and disobey God, then the sign is not good for you.

I don’t know about you, but when I buy electronic items, I often keep the boxes just in case something happens, and I need to return it.

Some boxes are so badly made that they’re not worth keeping.

But some boxes look great and I don’t want to throw them away.

Take this box here for instance.

This is the box of a computer that I bought about twenty years ago.

I mean, look at it.

It’s a nice box, right?

Now, I was on Facebook Marketplace a few nights ago and I saw someone selling just the boxes of computers.

Just boxes!

While the boxes are nice, it’s what’s inside the boxes that counts, isn’t it?

The same goes for the Jew who’s been circumcised.

The mark of circumcision is like the box, but it’s what’s inside them that matters.

It’s good that they can be physically identified as a Jew, the people who received God’s word, but do their lives identify them as a Jew?

Do their lives reflect that they are obeying God’s word?

If they claim to be a Jew and yet live as if they’re not one, then the very sign of circumcision will become the sign of uncircumcision.

That is, the very sign that ought to show who they are on the inside shows that they’re a sham, a fake.

They’re like the box of a computer that has rubbish inside.

Paul takes this idea even further.

What if there’s someone who’s not a Jew but keeps the law’s requirements?

What if there’s someone who doesn’t have the mark of circumcision on the outside, but who keeps what God’s law requires?

It would be someone who might look like a garbage bag on the outside, but in fact, is the real deal on the inside.

To use our example here, the box was supposed to show where the computer was, but it was a lie.

The garbage bag wasn’t a very promising cover, but it’s actually the one that contained the computer.

Does such a person exist?

Can a person who is not a Jew keep the commands that God’s given to a Jew?

Paul says that if this person does exist, then he would judge the Jew, for this person, a Gentile, has kept the commands of God but the Jew, who points his finger to others who don’t have the commands of God, didn’t keep the commands of God.

How the tables have turned!

The outwardly Jewish person thought that he could lead the non-Jewish person to God by teaching them God’s laws, but it’s the outwardly non-Jewish person who was in a better place to judge the Jewish person because he actually kept God’s laws.

Look with me at verses 28-29, “For a person is not a Jew who is one outwardly, and true circumcision is not something visible in the flesh. 29 On the contrary, a person is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart—by the Spirit, not the letter. That person’s praise is not from people but from God.”

It’s not the outward sign of circumcision that counts but the inward circumcision of the heart that counts.

Now notice that the Bible is not talking about someone who’s a diamond in the rough.

The Bible is not talking about someone who has rough edges but is basically a good person on the inside.

Neither is the Bible talking about someone who looks good on the outside, not in terms of their beauty or their charisma or rizz but people who are nice, generous and friendly.

These are the people who contribute to the society and are good people to hang around.

If people could vote on who ought to go to heaven, they would probably get the votes.

In fact, these people probably have more in common with the Jews who prided themselves on being recognised by the outward sign, for their focus is on what could be seen.

Rather than focussing on what could be seen on the outside, the Bible goes far deeper.

The Bible talks about the circumcision of the heart.

It talks about a heart that’s circumcised, a heart that has been transformed, to follow God’s laws.

A nice person may do and say nice things, but without the circumcision of the heart, they cannot follow God’s laws and so they remain outside of God’s kingdom.

A religious person, a Jewish person, may try their best to follow God’s laws, but without the circumcision of the heart, it’s impossible to follow God’s laws.

Without the circumcision of the heart, we can’t follow God’s laws willingly.

We may follow God’s laws begrudgingly, we might even follow God’s laws in full view of the public, but we fall back into sin quickly in private because our hearts have not been circumcised.

How do we circumcise our hearts?

Well, we don’t since we can’t.

Our hearts can only be circumcised by the Spirit.

Only God the Holy Spirit can circumcise our hearts.

Only God the Holy Spirit can spiritually operate on our hearts by taking out our hearts of stone and giving us a heart of flesh.

Only God the Holy Spirit can give us a heart that genuinely longs for God alone.

This is how we Gentiles, we who don’t have a prestigious Jewish heritage can keep the law’s requirements even when the religious Jew couldn’t.

We can keep the law’s requirements not because we’re clever, not because we’re good, not because we’re superior, but because Jesus has kept the law’s requirements on our behalf.

We could not keep God’s law even if we had tried our hardest, but Jesus, the Perfect Son of God, kept God’s laws perfectly.

And from now on, those who had put their trust in Jesus, are reckoned by God to have kept his law.

Left to ourselves, we will continue to break God’s laws.

But as people saved by Jesus, God the Son, and as a people whose hearts have been transformed and are constantly transformed by the Holy Spirit, we will live our lives as those whose sins have been paid for, who live with love and thanks for our God who has wonderfully saved us by his power and grace.

As Titus 2:11-12 reminds us, “For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation for all people, 12 instructing us to deny godlessness and worldly lusts and to live in a sensible, righteous, and godly way in the present age,”

We are a people saved by grace and we will live as a people saved by grace, saying “no” to the godlessness and worldly lusts and saying “yes” to sensible, righteous and godly living.

We live as a people who long for God’s praise, not men’s.

One of the saddest assessments I often hear from people who have been going to Church for many years is that they say that the younger Christians aren’t growing, that they are not mature.

However, as people who have been coming to Church for many years, they should’ve been the ones teaching and discipling the younger Christians, helping them grow instead of criticising them.

In fact, I’ve often seen more growth in younger Christians than some others who have been coming to Church for a while, because they often take their faith more seriously, humbly acknowledging that they still have much to learn.

Ironically, this desire to learn and grow is a sign of maturity.

It’s a sign that their hearts are slowly being transformed by the Holy Spirit.

Friends, the length of our stay at Church is no guarantee that our hearts have been transformed by the Holy Spirit.

A heart that hasn’t been circumcised by the Holy Spirit will long for praise from men and will be more concerned about the faults of others than their own faults.

And so, friends, let’s examine our hearts, and ask the Holy Spirit to continue to shape and transform us, every day.

Every day, think of new ways to trust and obey Jesus, as you read more about Him in God’s word, the Bible.

Our falseness cannot cancel God's faithfulness (3:1–8)

If it’s all a matter of the heart, then what’s the point of having any external signs?

What’s the point of being a Jew?

Paul anticipates this and affirms that our falseness cannot cancel God’s faithfulness.

Look with me at chapter 3, verses 1-2, “So what advantage does the Jew have? Or what is the benefit of circumcision? 2 Considerable in every way. First, they were entrusted with the very words of God.”

What’s so good about being a Jew?

What’s the benefit of the sign of circumcision?

Paul says there are so many good things about being a Jew.

First and foremost, they’ve been entrusted with the very words of God.

God gave his very words to their care.

It’s a privilege of the highest order.

However, as Paul had been arguing in the last chapter, having God’s word but not practising it is bad.

Having the privilege without the practice dishonours God’s name, living with the cover of circumcision without the circumcision of the heart won’t get any praise from God.

And so, what if the Jews were unfaithful?

Paul anticipates two types of objections about our falseness and God’s faithfulness.

The first one has to do with our unbelief and God’s faithfulness.

Look with me at verse 3, “What then? If some were unfaithful, will their unfaithfulness nullify God’s faithfulness?”

Yes, God may be faithful and loving and just, but what if the people weren’t, as has been proven time and time again.

Yes, God had promised to give his people the Promised Land but because the people were so rebellious, so bad, that God punished them by allowing a foreign nation to take over them and exile them away from the Promised Land?

Didn’t God’s promise fail to come true because of the people’s failures?

Wouldn’t God’s faithfulness fail because of the people’s unbelief?

It’s a little like this.

Do we have anyone here who uses Telstra?

As some of you know, earlier this week, there was a national outage for Telstra.

Telstra is responsible for many, many connections around Australia.

However, when maintenance workers were working on an old server in Melbourne, it reset the date to 2006 and it triggered a national outage affecting up to 8.8 million customers and businesses.

God has a masterful, beautiful plan to save his people.

God had thought of everything and proceeded with a plan to save the world.

But what if there were some who were not faithful, what if there were some who did not believe?

Wouldn’t their unbelief mess up God’s plan of salvation?

Throughout history, there have been so many Jewish leaders, Christian leaders and Christian Churches, people who should’ve been faithful but weren’t, who have caused so much damage to God’s people and to God’s Church.

From a human point of view, it seems like though God might have been faithful, his effort, his faithfulness appears to have been nullified by man’s unfaithfulness.

Just as human error had caused millions of Telstra customers to be disconnected, wouldn’t the act of unfaithful men and unfaithful women cancel God’s faithfulness?

Paul replies with an emphatic, “Absolutely Not! Let God be true, even though everyone is a liar,” God’s faithfulness cannot be cancelled by man’s unfaithfulness.

Paul will look at this in more detail later on in Romans chapters 9-11, but for now, Paul says that though everyone is a liar, let God be true.

God’s faithfulness remains even when we’re utterly unfaithful.

God’s faithfulness stands despite our faithlessness, that is, our failure to be faithful.

God’s faithfulness stands even despite our unbelief.

For God’s faithfulness is seen not only in his salvation, but also in his righteous judgement.

Paul uses a quote from Psalm 51 to make a point, “as it is written:

That you may be justified in your words

and triumph when you judge.”

Psalm 51 was written by King David as a confession to God after David was told by God’s prophet that God knew that King David had committed adultery with Bathsheba, the wife of Uriah.

David didn’t make any excuse for himself.

He didn’t blame Bathsheba and he didn’t blame God.

David knew that he was guilty and so he confessed.

When God said that David had sinned, David knew that God was right.

God was justified in passing his judgement on King David.

God was faithful in delivering his judgement against the unfaithful David.

Despite our unfaithfulness, God’s faithfulness remains.

Paul then anticipates another objection that may be levelled against God.

Look with me in verse 5, “But if our unrighteousness highlights God’s righteousness, what are we to say? I am using a human argument: Is God unrighteous to inflict wrath?”

Paul was very careful to say that this argument was not his idea.

He was using a human argument.

The argument goes like this: if our unrighteousness highlights God’s righteousness, then it makes God look good, right?

And if it makes God look good, then why are we being punished for it?

As we’ve been seeing here in Romans and throughout the rest of the Bible, there’s just no way we can live up to God’s righteousness.

And so, standing next to him, of course we’ll be seen as unrighteous.

When we come before the mighty and holy God, we will always be weak and unholy.

And so, why do we get punished for being unrighteous when it’s impossible for us to be holy and righteous like God, ever?

To this, Paul replies, again, very emphatically in verse 6, “Absolutely not! Otherwise, how will God judge the world?”

God will judge the world one day.

Throughout the Bible, we see that one day, God will judge the living and the dead.

There will be some who go to heaven and there will be some who go to hell.

There isn’t a third option; it’s either heaven or hell for everyone who has ever lived.

There also isn’t a universal option where God will just let everyone into heaven because all of them, including those who were unrighteous, had made God more righteous.

No.

Those who remained unrighteous will be sentenced and those who had been made righteous by Jesus will be welcomed home.

On that day, all of us have to give an account of our lives to God and God’s judgement will be recognised by all as right.

Though our unrighteousness does highlight God’s righteousness, it does not excuse us from being accountable for our own unrighteousness.

In verse 8, Paul anticipates that someone might make a similar objection about the truth of God and the lie of men, “But if by my lie God’s truth abounds to his glory, why am I also still being judged as a sinner?”

This is probably the most personal of all the objections.

If my lie makes God’s truth glorious, why does God still judge me?

Whereas in the first objection, the objection was whether our unfaithfulness will cancel God’s faithfulness, both the second and the third objections go in the opposite direction.

If even our unrighteousness and our lies can be used to highlight God’s righteousness and truth, then should God really judge us at all?

If not, then does it really matter how we live?

That was what some people have been saying that Paul’s been saying.

Look with me at verse 8, “And why not say, just as some people slanderously claim we say, “Let us do what is evil so that good may come”? Their condemnation is deserved!”

Some people have so twisted Paul’s teaching that they are saying that Paul says that people should actually do evil so that good may come.

That’s not what Paul’s been saying and that’s not what the Bible’s teaching.

The actions we make matter, the decisions we make matter.

That’s what Paul’s been saying so far.

Don’t fall for a twisted version of God’s grace that excuses your sinful actions.

We thank God that he has saved us by his grace, that we didn’t save ourselves by works.

However, as part of God’s grace to us, he transforms our hearts so that we will trust and obey him.

Being saved by God doesn’t give you permission to sin against God.

In fact, it’s the opposite.

Being saved by God means that you’re thankful for what God has done and that you will not jump back into sin, even as you’re tempted to do so every hour of every day.

On Tuesday night, we’re beginning to try a new structure for our prayer meetings.

Often at prayer meetings, we share our personal needs, the church’s needs and for people’s health.

Recently, we started using the ACTS method.

ACTS stands for Adoration, Confession, Thanksgiving and Supplication.

I was taught many years ago to use this format in private devotionals but we are now adapting this to our prayer meetings.

It’s been very helpful.

Last Tuesday, a few of us spent time talking about sins that we would like to confess.

It is good to confess our sins to one another and ask God to forgive.

As a prayer meeting, we confessed our unfaithfulness where we have failed and also asked God to help us repent from our sins.

The confession of sin, as well as finding our forgiveness in Christ, is vital to help us come to trust in God’s faithfulness despite our unfaithfulness and to ask God to keep us from going back into sin.

Friends, don’t make excuses for your sins.

Confess them before the Lord.

Where possible, confess them to a mature brother or sister and ask them to pray for you.

The act of confessing and forgiving is a beautiful way for Christians to express how in Christ, the demand of the law has been fulfilled and how we, as people whose hearts the Spirit has transformed, can live for the praise of God’s glory.

Why is God furious at what we as a community think of as ‘good’ people?

It’s because if these good people are like the Jews described in here, who saw themselves as a light to the blind, teaching others good things, even the good things of God, but they themselves have not been circumcised in the heart, then they are still outside of God’s kingdom.

If they do lead those whom they think are blind, it would be a case of the blind leading the blind.

What God wants from us instead is to be a people with a heart circumcised by the Holy Spirit.

God wants us to be a people who have been transformed by God the Holy Spirit when we accept the message about God the Son.

He longs to see a people who long to keep the requirements of the law, not by their power but by the Spirit’s power.

He longs to see a people who would confess their sins rather than make excuses for them.

For these are the very people who themselves long to see God, who live out God’s word for his glory and praise.

Don’t be hypocritical teachers whose falsehood breeds blasphemy; be people with Spirit-circumcised hearts, living out God’s word for his praise and glory.

Here are three questions for us to think about this week:

Three Questions

Where do I find my comfort and assurance — Bible knowledge, years of Church service, Christian achievements — rather than trusting in and obeying Christ himself?

Does the watching world see in our life together as a Church a reason to honour God's name, or to disparage it?

When my sin is exposed, do I argue like the objector or confess like King David — trusting the God who stays faithful?

Friends, Don’t be hypocritical teachers whose falsehood breeds blasphemy; be people with Spirit-circumcised hearts, living out God’s word for his praise and glory.

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you for your faithfulness. Thank you that you are true and your words remain true, even though every man and every woman is a liar. Father, please forgive us for our unbelief. Please forgive us for not putting our trust in you. Please forgive us for our hypocrisy, teaching love when we don’t love, teaching the dangers of sin when we practise those very same sins. Father, be merciful and by your Holy Spirit, transform our hearts. Break our hearts if it means you will reform them to long and love Jesus, and his people. We pray this for his sake, Amen.”
