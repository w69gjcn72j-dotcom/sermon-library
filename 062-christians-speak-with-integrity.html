Christians Speak with Integrity

Matthew 5:33-37 · 2021
Big IdeaBe people of perfect integrity, as our heavenly Father is perfect.

Christians speak with integrity

Passage: Matthew 5:33-37

Big Idea: Be people of perfect integrity, as our heavenly Father is perfect.

God is one but we are duplicitous

Jesus is the One who saves even the deceivers

The Spirit-filled ones walk with integrity

It’s good to see everyone back after the lockdown.

I hope and pray that we don’t have to go back to a lockdown anytime soon.

Do you still remember when we went through the first lockdown last year?

It was such a strange experience, wasn’t it?

We couldn’t go to many places, we couldn’t go to school, we couldn’t go to work.

So, for many of us, we just stayed at home and for many of us, we watched a lot of TV.

A show that some of our family members got into was Crash Landing on you.

Has anyone seen it?

It’s a Korean drama about a South Korean woman (Siri) who accidentally found herself trapped in North Korea.

There she met Captain Ri who eventually helped her escape.

Captain Ri had never met Siri before, but he made a promise to protect her, no matter what.

And throughout the series, Captain Ri kept to his word and saved Siri again and again.

People all around the world loved the series.

It’s good to see someone keeping their promises, even when things get tough.

Not only is it a good thing to see someone keep their word.

It’s rare to see someone keep their word when things get tough.

While it’s great to see someone like Captain Ri keeping his word in Crash Landing, it’s rare to see someone in the real world doing the same.

It’s rare to see men and women of integrity who would keep their promises when things are hard.

Instead, it’s far more common for us to hear people making excuses for breaking the promises they’ve made.

It’s far more common because often it’s we ourselves who make those excuses.

We often are the very ones make excuses for the promises that we’ve broken.

It’s so common that most of us have just accepted that integrity is the rare thing and broken promises and excuses are the norm.

But it shouldn’t be acceptable to us because it’s not acceptable to God.

God wants to see integrity in our speech and in our action.

God wants us to keep our promises.

And so, the question we must ask ourselves is this: Do I live with God approved integrity?

That’s what we’re going to explore today.

We’re going to look at what the Bible says about integrity, especially integrity in our speech, using the seven stars as our anchors.

The seven stars, as you might remember, are: Creation, The fall, Judgement, redemption, resurrection, Spirit and Hope.

We are going to see three things:

God is one but we are duplicitous

Jesus is the One who saves even the deceivers

The Spirit-filled ones walk with integrity

Before we look into God’s word, let’s pray,

“Father, as we hear your word, please transform us by your Spirit into the likeness of your Son our Lord Jesus Christ, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

God is one but we are duplicitous

Let’s start from the beginning.

In the beginning, there is our Creator; in the beginning there is God.

If you have your Bible, please open up with me to Deuteronomy 6:4.

This is how the Bible describes God. ““Listen, Israel: The Lord our God, the Lord is one.”

By saying that the Lord is one can mean that there is one God.

This is certainly true.

But it also points to the character of God.

God is One.

There is no conflict within God; there aren’t two opposing sides of God.

What God said back at the beginning of time is true of God then and it’s true of God now and will be true of God forever.

God is perfect in his integrity.

As the apostle John tells us in 1 John 1:5, “God is light, and there is absolutely no darkness in him.”

In contrast, we are not perfect in our integrity.

We were originally to reflect God’s perfect character, but when we’ve rejected God’s way, we descended into darkness.

The Bible calls this sin.

We should’ve been walking in integrity; we should’ve been walking in God’s light.

But now we live in shadows; we walk in darkness.

Unlike God, who is one, we’re duplicitous ( 口是心非).

The word duplicitous comes from a Latin word meaning “double”.

We say one thing, but we mean another.

We put on a show to hide the truth.

In Chinese, the word duplicitous is translated by the phrase is “The mouth is yes but the heart is no.”

We see this in various passages in the Bible:

“For there is nothing reliable in what they say; destruction is within them; their throat is an open grave; they flatter with their tongues.” Psalm 5:9

“Each one betrays his friend; no one tells the truth. They have taught their tongues to speak lies; they wear themselves out doing wrong.” Jeremiah 9:5

It’s as if lying is our mother tongue.

Of course, some people are prone to lying more than others.

Medically there is a condition known as pathological lying.

The medical terms for pathological lying are mythomania and pseudologia fantastica.

A pathological liar is someone who keeps lying.

They might lie to appear as a hero, or to gain acceptance or to gain sympathy.

They lie so much that studies have shown that they’ve become hard-wired to lying.

It gets easier to lie as they keep lying.

They lie so much that they sometimes even believe the lies that they tell.

That is, they become delusional and lose sight of reality.

While most of us aren’t pathological liars and hopefully haven’t met many, we are all prone to lying

We lie to make ourselves look good; we lie to make ourselves look like a hero.

But that’s not what God’s made us to be.

Lying is a sin and there are consequences to lying.

In the Old Testament, God has destroyed whole families because someone in the family lied.

In the New Testament, a husband and his wife dropped dead because they’ve lied to the Holy Spirit about what they gave to the Church.

You can read about them in Acts 5.

Even Churchgoers were tempted to lie to make themselves look good.

But that’s who we are.

God is one but we are duplicitous.

As well as displeasing God, what other consequences can lying or acting duplicitously lead to?

Talk with the person next to you.

What other consequences can lying or acting duplicitously lead to?

You can message via whatsapp, wechat or zoom.

What other consequences can lying or acting duplicitously lead to?

You have 30 seconds.

Even without God doing anything miraculous, left to ourselves, our lies has and will continue to destroy families and friendships, nations and individuals, people like you and me.

Jesus is the One who saves even the deceivers

But the great thing about the message of the Bible is that God has not left us in our lies.

God’s sent Jesus to save sinners, including liars.

One of the most famous liars in the Bible is the Apostle Peter.

When Jesus was taken unjustly to be trialled by the high priests, Peter stayed outside.

When someone asked him whether he was with Jesus, Peter lied, not once but three times.

Here was Peter, the leader of the disciples, denied Jesus, lying about knowing him, to avoid being in trouble.

He lied about Jesus!

But after Jesus died and rose from the dead, he restored Peter.

He asked Peter three times, “Do you love me?”

To which Peter replied, “Yes, Lord.”

Peter denied Jesus three times and Jesus gave Peter three opportunities to renew his relationship with him.

Jesus has come to save even compulsive liars like Peter!

Jesus has come to save people like us!

That’s the good news about Jesus.

Though liars rightly deserved to be condemned, in Jesus they can be saved.

In Jesus they have a second chance.

Jesus has taken the punishment that sinners like liars deserve and Jesus has risen again to show that he has a new life prepared for us.

For people who believe who Jesus is and what he’s done, they are now to live as sons of the living God.

They no longer belong to the shadows, but they are to live in God’s light.

To live in God’s light is not something that we can do ourselves.

We need God’s help.

That’s why God’s given us his Spirit.

When we believe in Jesus, God gives us the Spirit of Truth (John 14:7)

The Spirit of Truth reveals God’s truth to us, and he gives us the power to live by God’s truth.

More than that, the Spirit of Truth keeps teaching and reminding us the truth that Jesus has taught.

One of the truths that Jesus has taught, and the Spirit, reminds us, is what Jesus has taught about speech in Matthew 5:33.

If you have your Bible there, please open up with me to Matthew chapter 5 verse 33,

““Again, you have heard that it was said to our ancestors, You must not break your oath, but you must keep your oaths to the Lord.”

That sounds reasonable enough.

You should keep the oaths you make to God.

But Jesus goes one step further,

“But I tell you, don’t take an oath at all: either by heaven, because it is God’s throne;”

To take an oath is to call down a curse on yourself if you are to break an oath.

You probably heard it before in movies when people say, “If I’m not telling the truth, then may I die a horrible death.” Or something to that extent.

In Jesus’ time, the Jews had built an elaborate system around the law so that even if you take an oath, there are ways to go around it.

For them, there are some oaths that are binding and some aren’t.

For example, if you made an oath by saying that you swear by Jerusalem, then your oath is not binding.

But if you say you swear to Jerusalem, then your oath is binding.

Depending on the words that you use, you can technically say that what you’ve said is not binding.

They’ve made a system that inherently allow liars to lie and to be not responsible for what they’ve said.

But Jesus says, “Don’t take an oath at all.”

Jesus doesn’t want a system that allows you to lie, as long as you do it with the right words.

Some people might have thought that as long as you don’t invoke God’s name directly, then your vow is not as valid.

Perhaps if you refer to heaven, or earth or even Jerusalem, and you’re not directly invoking God’s name, then you’re safe to lie.

But Jesus says there’s no place for lies to, well, lie.

Don’t swear by heaven, for it is God’s throne, don’t swear by earth for it’s his footstool.

Don’t swear by Jerusalem, whether you’re swearing to it or by it, for it’s the city of the great King, that is God himself.

Don’t even swear by your head, offering to give up your head if you break your promise, for you can’t even make a hair white or black.

Only God can make your hair white or black.

You head is God’s property, not yours.

There is no way that you can justify yourself if you decide to break an oath.

Rather than looking for loopholes, Jesus says, “But let your ‘yes’ mean ‘yes,’ and your ‘no’ mean ‘no.’ Anything more than this is from the evil one.”

Don’t lie.

When you say Yes, mean Yes. When you say No, mean No.

That’s what integrity looks like.

You say what you mean.

If you don’t say what you mean, you’re doing what the evil one wants you to do.

When we lie, when we break our oath, it’s not simply a failure to be truthful; it’s actively doing the work of the evil one.

Jesus himself has said that when the devil tells a lie, he speaks from his own nature, because he is a liar, and the father of lies.

When the devil lies, he’s speaking his mother tongue.

Don’t speak the devil’s mother tongue; don’t lie.

The Spirit-filled ones walk with integrity

Instead, as people filled with the Spirit, walk with integrity.

In a Bible study earlier this week I shared this verse and we agreed in the Bible study that speaking with integrity is easy to say but hard to do.

It’s very difficult to always speak with integrity.

God’s standard is not just speaking the truth some of the time;

It’s speaking with perfect integrity all of the time.

As opposed to the devil, the truth is to be the mother tongue of Christians.

We don’t just speak the truth some time, we speak the truth the whole time, so that we don’t need to take an oath to tell people we’re telling the truth.

That’s difficult to do!

But we thank God that he has given us his Spirit.

Not only is the Spirit the Spirit of Truth; He’s also the Spirit of power, love and sound judgement (2 Tim 1:7)

It’s by the Holy Spirit of God that we speak the truth with integrity.

It’s by the Holy Spirit of God that truth becomes our mother tongue.

A few years ago, a young Australian Ben McMahon was involved in a horrific car crash that left him in a coma for a week.

When Ben woke up from his coma, he saw a nurse that looked like an Asian.

And he said to her in Mandarin, “Excuse me nurse, I feel really sore here”.

Now Ben has learnt Mandarin at school but it wasn’t his mother tongue.

However, as a result of his accident, his brain was wired so that he could only speak in Mandarin and not English.

In fact, he had to ask a nurse for a pen and paper because the nurse couldn’t speak Mandarin.

And he wrote down these words, “I love my Mum, I love my Dad, I will recover.”

But he wrote the words in Chinese because he couldn’t write them in English.

Even when his parents came, he could only speak to them in Mandarin, and not his mother tongue English.

Ben went into coma with one mother tongue and woke up with another.

For those of us who believe in Jesus, we also have died in him with one mother tongue and raised in him with another.

We used to live according to darkness and our mother tongue used to be lies.

But now we are to live in the light and our mother tongue is to be the truth.

Before Christ we couldn’t help ourselves but lie to make ourselves look good.

But now, in Christ, by His Spirit of power, we can’t help but speak the truth and make Jesus look good.

Now that we believe in Christ, we’re to live with God’s standard of integrity.

It will be difficult; there’ll be times when we’ll be tempted to lie.

In fact, there will be times when we will fail and lie.

But we thank God that we’re not alone for God’s given us his Spirit.

He’ll be there to provide us with whatever help we need so that we don’t need to lie to save our faces.

And even when we fail, he’ll provide a way for us to repent and to turn back to him, and to keep trusting in him.

Even when we fall, our God will look for a way to restore us, just as he did with Peter.

Even so, we will no longer be known as promise breakers.

Instead, we will be known as promise keepers.

We will be reliable.

We will no longer be known as those who lie to save our faces.

Instead, we will be known to tell the truth, even when it hurts.

We will be truthful.

And we will no longer be Sunday morning Christians.

We will no longer speak and behave one way on Sunday mornings and become a completely different person when we leave the Church building.

No, we will be known as a 24/7 Christian.

We will speak and act with integrity as a Christian always.

We will be true, not duplicitous.

We will remember what Jesus has taught us that every word that we say matter.

Listen to what he says in Matthew 12:36-37,

“ I tell you that on the day of judgment people will have to account for every careless word they speak. 37 For by your words you will be acquitted, and by your words you will be condemned.”

Every word that we say matters.

How will our words be judged on the last day when Jesus comes back as judge?

And so, friends, remember who God is.

Our God is one.

And so, don’t be duplicitous.

Jesus has come to save liars like you and me.

Don’t go back to a life of speaking lies.

Instead, by the Spirit of God, speak with integrity.

Speak according to the Spirit of Truth.

What does this mean for us?

Speak your Heavenly Father’s language.

Every now and then, my children would say the same things as me, in the same way as me.

A few years ago, my children did a video for me as a Father’s Day present, with Ezekiel pretending to be me.

He would pretend to do what I do and say what I would say.

And he was very good at it.

It was like looking at myself in the mirror.

As children of God, we’re to speak in a way that mirrors what God has said.

When you speak, think about what you’re about to say.

What does God want me to say?

How would God want me to say it?

How does God want me to speak the truth?

How does he want me to speak truthfully?

If we’re going to make a promise, know that God wants us to keep it, whether we make an oath or not.

Truth is the mother tongue of God’s family.

Speak with integrity.

Do what you’ll say you’ll do or else don’t say it at all.

Speak your Heavenly Father’s language.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What were some ways my lies had hurt others in the past?

Have I accepted what Jesus has done to save liars like me?

How have I been speaking my Heavenly Father’s language?

Friends, speak the truth, speak with integrity, speak your heavenly Father’s language.

Let’s pray,

“The Lord our God, the Lord is one. Father, you are perfect in your integrity. Please forgive us for our duplicity. Thank you for Jesus, who has come to save liars like me. By your Spirit, please transform us so that we’ll speak the truth always, so that people will glorify you and Jesus and the Holy Spirit. We pray this in Jesus’ name, Amen,”
