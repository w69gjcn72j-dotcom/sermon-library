Young Adults (19–30)

When Grace Breaks into the Impossible

Luke 1:26–45 · Sunday 4 October 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

For young adults, 19–30 · from Sunday’s sermon, preached by Jerry Zhu

Big Idea: When grace breaks into the impossible, God uses those the world considers “unqualified”: his grace comes first, faith rests on his word, and faith is strengthened in fellowship.

Aim: By the end of tonight, each person should be able to (1) explain why God choosing a teenage girl from Nazareth shows that grace comes before performance; (2) see from Mary how to bring honest questions to God and still say yes to him when the cost is real; (3) name one Christian friend to be an “Elizabeth” with this week — and one step towards serving.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • Have you ever been picked for something you felt totally unqualified for — leading a uni group project, a new role at work, MC-ing a friend’s wedding? How did it feel?
  • When big news lands — good or terrible — who do you message first: a group chat, one friend, family, or nobody? Why?

Read

26 In the sixth month, the angel Gabriel was sent by God to a town in Galilee called Nazareth,

27 to a virgin engaged to a man named Joseph, of the house of David. The virgin’s name was Mary.

28 And the angel came to her and said, “Greetings, favored woman! The Lord is with you.”

29 But she was deeply troubled by this statement, wondering what kind of greeting this could be.

30 Then the angel told her, “Do not be afraid, Mary, for you have found favor with God.

31 Now listen: You will conceive and give birth to a son, and you will name him Jesus.

32 He will be great and will be called the Son of the Most High, and the Lord God will give him the throne of his father David.

33 He will reign over the house of Jacob forever, and his kingdom will have no end.”

34 Mary asked the angel, “How can this be, since I have not had sexual relations with a man?”

35 The angel replied to her, “The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you. Therefore, the holy one to be born will be called the Son of God.

36 And consider your relative Elizabeth — even she has conceived a son in her old age, and this is the sixth month for her who was called childless.

37 For nothing will be impossible with God.”

38 “See, I am the Lord’s servant,” said Mary. “May it happen to me as you have said.” Then the angel left her.

39 In those days Mary set out and hurried to a town in the hill country of Judah

40 where she entered Zechariah’s house and greeted Elizabeth.

41 When Elizabeth heard Mary’s greeting, the baby leaped inside her, and Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Spirit.

42 Then she exclaimed with a loud cry, “Blessed are you among women, and your child will be blessed!

43 How could this happen to me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?

44 For you see, when the sound of your greeting reached my ears, the baby leaped for joy inside me.

45 Blessed is she who has believed that the Lord would fulfill what he has spoken to her!”

Context

Luke sets two angel visits side by side. Six months earlier, Gabriel announced John the Baptist’s birth to the priest Zechariah in the temple in Jerusalem (Luke 1:5–25). Now he is sent to Nazareth — a nowhere village in Galilee — to Mary, a teenage girl engaged to Joseph. Engagement was legally binding, and a pregnancy before marriage could bring disgrace, or worse (Deuteronomy 22:23–24).

Discuss

  1. Verses 26–27: Where does God send Gabriel, and to whom? Why is it surprising (see John 1:46)? In a world of curated feeds and polished LinkedIn profiles, what kind of people usually get noticed?
  2. Verses 28–30: What are the angel’s first words? Mary is “favored” before she has done anything. Our culture says identity comes from performance — grades, job titles, followers. How does “grace before response, identity before performance” change the way you live this week?
  3. Verses 31–33: What does the angel say about Jesus? How does this fulfil God’s promises to David (2 Samuel 7:12–16; Isaiah 9:6–7)? Why is “Jesus” — “Yahweh is salvation” — exactly the right name?
  4. Verse 34 (compare 1:18): Mary asks, “How can this be?” The sermon said Zechariah’s question came from “I do not believe”, Mary’s from “I do not understand”. Are there questions you have been afraid to ask out loud at church? How can we ask them in a way that seeks to trust?
  5. Verses 35–37: How does the angel answer? What does “nothing will be impossible with God” mean, and what doesn’t it mean? Notice that Mary gets a promise, not a five-year plan.
  6. Verse 38: What could Mary lose by saying yes? The sermon told of a final-year Bible college student choosing between a comfortable city job and mission in a remote area — and praying Mary’s words. Where are your big decisions (career, relationships, where to live, how to spend money) asking you to trust God’s word over your own control?
  7. Verses 39–45: Why does Mary hurry to Elizabeth? What does Elizabeth see and say? According to verse 45, what made Mary blessed?
  8. The sermon said: when you’re lonely at 1am you can open an AI chat window, but what you need is an “Elizabeth” who can hug you, cry with you and rejoice with you. Who are your Elizabeths? Whose Elizabeth could you be? What gets in the way of that kind of friendship for us?

  • Connect with 2 — two people from church, face to face or on the phone, not just a DM. Ask how you can pray for them.

  • 0 left unloved — notice who sits on the edge of the group chat, or stands alone after church.

And ask God whether he is calling you to serve — as an assistant leader for KOGKids, KOGMini or KOGYouth, or as an accompanist for our Chinese Parallel congregation.

Memory verse: “Blessed is she who has believed that the Lord would fulfill what he has spoken to her!” (Luke 1:45, CSB)

Pray

  • Thank God that his favour comes before our performance, and that he delights to use ordinary, willing people.
  • Ask for Mary’s trust in the decisions in front of you: “I am the Lord’s servant. May it happen to me as you have said.”
  • Pray by name for your three — that God’s kindness would lead them to repentance, and for an opportunity to point one of them to Jesus.

Leader’s Notes

Joyfully point to Jesus. Assume some in the room aren’t yet Christians, or are unsure: this passage tells them God looks for willing people, not polished ones, and that honest questions are welcome. Never corner anyone, especially on Questions 6 and 8, where people may be carrying loneliness or hard decisions.

Two likely wrong turns. First, making Mary the hero: she was chosen by grace, not merit, and Elizabeth’s praise is praise for God’s grace. Second, treating verse 37 as “God will make my plans work”: Mary received a promise, not a guarantee of comfort — her yes brought risk, misunderstanding and waiting.

Question helps

Q1 — Nazareth: no temple, prophets or priests, never mentioned in the Old Testament, looked down on (John 1:46). Mary: young, poor, no status. God chose the most impossible place and person — the logic of the gospel (1 Corinthians 1:27–28).

Q2 — “Favored” means she has already received grace, and it remains in effect. Obedience is a response to grace, not a way to earn it (1 John 4:19). Our worth is settled before our performance.

Q3 — Great in God’s eyes; Son of the Most High; David’s throne; an everlasting kingdom. His name is his mission: he came to save.

Q4 — Zechariah’s doubt led to silence; Mary’s question led to deeper revelation. God deals with each of us personally. Questions asked in order to trust are welcome.

Q5 — The Spirit who brought life at creation (Genesis 1:2); the power of the Most High overshadowing. The child is the holy Son of God — fully God, fully human. Verse 37 is the certainty that God can do all he has promised, not a promise that our plans will succeed.

Q6 — Divorce, stoning (Deuteronomy 22:23–24), shame. Her yes was a commitment made after counting the cost, and it points forward to Jesus in Gethsemane (Luke 22:42).

Q7 — Elizabeth had also experienced an “impossible” pregnancy, and the angel had pointed Mary to her (v.36). Spirit-filled, she calls the unborn Jesus “my Lord”. Mary is blessed because she believed God’s word.

Q8 — Faith needs real fellowship, not algorithmic companionship. God often works through small, devoted groups — two believers encouraging each other.
