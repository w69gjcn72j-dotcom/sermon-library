Home Groups (adults)

Jesus Is the Light

John 8:12–20 · 27 September 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

For adult home groups · Preacher: Rev David Yung

Big Idea: Follow Jesus, the Light of the World, and shine as his lower lights, with his light of life.

Aim: By the end of tonight, each member should be able to (1) explain why “I am the light of the world” was such a daring claim, and why it is true; (2) say why we trust Jesus’ testimony about himself — not because we have judged him by our standards, but because the Father confirms it; (3) leave with three names to pray for and one concrete way to burn bright as a “lower light” this week.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • Think of a blackout, a camping trip, or a long walk in the dark. What did you reach for first — and what was it like when the lights came back on?
  • The Mid-Autumn Festival fell this week: full moon above, lanterns below. What festivals of light do your neighbours or colleagues celebrate? What do you think people are longing for when they light them?

Read

12 Jesus spoke to them again: “I am the light of the world. Anyone who follows me will never walk in the darkness but will have the light of life.”

13 So the Pharisees said to him, “You are testifying about yourself. Your testimony is not valid.”

14 “Even if I testify about myself,” Jesus replied, “my testimony is true, because I know where I came from and where I’m going. But you don’t know where I come from or where I’m going.

15 You judge by human standards. I judge no one.

16 And if I do judge, my judgment is true, because it is not I alone who judge, but I and the Father who sent me.

17 Even in your law it is written that the testimony of two witnesses is true.

18 I am the one who testifies about myself, and the Father who sent me testifies about me.”

19 Then they asked him, “Where is your Father?” “You know neither me nor my Father,” Jesus answered. “If you knew me, you would also know my Father.”

20 He spoke these words by the treasury, while teaching in the temple. But no one seized him, because his hour had not yet come.

Context

Jesus speaks these words in Jerusalem during the Feast of Tabernacles (John 7:2), the week-long festival remembering God leading Israel through the wilderness. The temple courts were lit with great lamps, and men who loved God danced with torches, singing his praise. In the Old Testament, light belongs to God: “The LORD is my light and my salvation” (Psalm 27:1). Standing under those lights, Jesus says, “I am the light of the world.”

Discuss

  1. Read verse 12 again. What does Jesus claim to be? What two things does he promise to “anyone who follows me”?
  2. Look up Psalm 27:1 and Psalm 119:105. Why would it have been so daring — even outrageous — for Jesus to say this at a Jewish festival? What is the only thing that would stop it being outrageous?
  3. Hebrews 1:3 calls the Son “the radiance of God’s glory.” The sermon pictured the Father as the sun and Jesus as its sunbeams. What does that picture teach us about knowing God? And how does John 1:4 add that Jesus is also “the light of men”?
  4. The sermon described us as spiritually blind people walking in a dark world — like walking Hezekiah’s tunnel, 550 metres of total darkness, without a headlamp. Where do you see that darkness around you — in the news, at work, in your street? Where do you see it in yourself?
  5. Verses 13–15: what is the Pharisees’ objection? Jesus says they “judge by human standards.” What human standards do people around us use to size Jesus up — no research, what their community says, how they feel?
  6. Verses 16–18: who is Jesus’ second witness? Why is God’s own testimony the only one that can finally settle who Jesus is? How does that humble us, as well as the sceptic?
  7. Verses 19–20: “You know neither me nor my Father.” According to Jesus, how does anyone come to know the Father? What would you say to a colleague who says, “I believe in God, just not all that Jesus stuff”?
  8. Read Matthew 5:14–16. The sermon urged us to burn bright as Jesus’ “lower lights” in three ways: making Sunday church a top priority, loving one another warmly, and doing good works so others glorify the Father. Which of the three is dimmest in your life right now? What would one step look like this week?

Live It Out

KOG 3·2·1·0 — pray for 3, connect with 2, invite 1, 0 left unloved.

Pray for 3 — three people in your street, workplace or family who are still walking in darkness. Picture their faces, as the sermon asked, and pray for them by name each day.

Connect with 2 — give two of them real contact this week: a coffee, a phone call, a hand with something.

Invite 1 — invite one of them to church on a Sunday morning. That is where we burn brightest together.

0 left unloved — next Sunday, look for the person at morning tea nobody is talking to. Loving one another is how the world knows we are Jesus’ disciples (John 13:35).

This week’s challenge: do one good work at church or at home without being asked and without telling anyone. When you see a need, don’t just report it — think how you can help.

Memory verse: “I am the light of the world. Anyone who follows me will never walk in the darkness but will have the light of life.” (John 8:12, CSB)

Pray

  • Thank God for Jesus, the Light of the World — that his testimony is true, confirmed by the Father, and that whoever follows him will never walk in darkness.
  • Pray by name for your three — that God, who said “let light shine out of darkness,” would shine in their hearts, and give you an opportunity to point one of them to Jesus.
  • Pray for St Paul’s to shine together as a city on a hill — especially for the three new mid-week fellowships, and for our growth towards 75 households.

Leader’s Notes

Joyfully point to Jesus. Assume guests and not-yet-Christians are present — this passage is a warm invitation to them: “Come to him, follow him and have the light of life.” The sermon closed by suggesting that one of the faces we pray for might be our own. Leave room for that gently, and never corner anyone.

Question 4 can drift into a gloomy discussion of the news. Keep it moving towards verse 12: no matter how dark the world is, those who follow Jesus do not need to be afraid, because he leads the way.

Question helps

Q1 — He is the light of the world (not just of Jerusalem). Promise: never walk in darkness. Gift: the light of life. Both are for those who follow him.

Q2 — In the Bible the LORD himself is light (Psalm 27:1), and his word is a lamp (Psalm 119:105). Jesus takes what belongs to God and applies it to himself. It is an outrageous thing to say — unless, of course, it is true.

Q3 — You cannot separate the sun from its radiance; you cannot separate the glory of the Father from the glory of the Son. See the Son and you see the Father’s radiance. And he is “man’s light” — God’s light leading men and women back to God.

Q4 — Wars, tragedies, a divided world despite science and wealth. The deeper problem: from God’s point of view we are blind, and there is a spiritual darkness we do not even recognise. We need a headlamp; Jesus is far more than that. Where there is light, shadows flee.

Q5 — “You are testifying about yourself.” Anyone can claim to be special. Jesus answers that he knows his origin and destination — from God, returning to God. Human standards: dismissing Jesus without research (even John Dickson notes that his existence and crucifixion are “doubtless facts of ancient history”), repeating what a community says, or going by feelings. The sermon called these like blind men describing the sun.

Q6 — The Father who sent him. Only God can testify about God. God’s existence does not depend on what we think of him, so we don’t judge God by our standards; God verifies himself. That humbles the believer as much as the sceptic.

Q7 — By knowing Jesus. “If you knew me, you would also know my Father.” There is no route to the Father that bypasses the Son. The religious leaders were blind to who Jesus was.

Q8 — We don’t have the light of life in ourselves; we have it as we follow Jesus. “You can’t burn bright by yourself, not for long. You’ll only burn out.” Moody: “the Master will take care of the great lighthouse ... it is our job to keep the lower lights burning.” Our motto begins “See the Gospel” — people often see it in our deeds before they hear it in our words.

Our church vision

This is our “from faith to faith” vision in a sentence: a city on a hill in Kogarah, growing towards 75 households serving densely at St Paul’s (we are at 50), on the way to 500 households within ten minutes of us by 2035. Home groups are the engine, which is why three new mid-week fellowships is a live 90-day goal — ask whether your group should multiply. Keep following up the dads who came to “A Father’s Invitation”. And as the sermon said, God answered our cry for more room through the school: pray for the move to the new school hall in 2027.
