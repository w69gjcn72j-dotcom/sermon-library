The Magi seeks to worship the new king of the Jews

After Jesus’ birth in Bethlehem, in the days of Herod the King, Matthew tells us that wise men from the east arrived in Jerusalem.

By the time the wise men visited, Jesus was a child and not a new-born baby.

Who are these wise men from the East? Ppt

They had originally been called the Magi in the Bible.

We don’t know exactly who they are but what we do know is that these Magi have known about the Jewish Scripture.

They have learnt that the Jewish Scripture talks about the coming of the Christ, the King of the Jews.

In ancient Near East, there were Jewish settlements in places like Persia, Arabia and Babylon.

These Magi from the East might have learnt about the Jewish prophecies from the Jews who lived among them.

This could very well have happened when Daniel and many other Jews were exiles in Babylon from 597BC to 538 BC.

According to Daniel 2, the Babylonian King, King Nebuchadnezzar made Daniel the chief governor over all the wise men of Babylon, including the Magi.

And so, there’s a very high chance I think that these Magi from the East could’ve been students who read about Daniel’s teaching on the Old Testament and travelled from Babylon to Jerusalem to worship the King of the Jews.

If they were from Babylon, they would’ve travelled over 1000 kilometres to get to Jerusalem, a very long journey. Ppt

When the Magi from the East saw the Star that signaled the arrival of the King, they travelled to Judea to worship him.

We don’t know exactly what the star is. Ppt

Some say that it’s a comet, other say that it’s the alignment of certain stars.

Still others say that it was an angel.

I personally am inclined to think that the Magi have identified a pattern in the stars, perhaps the movement of the planet Jupiter around certain stars,

And they interpret it to mean that a new King has arrived. Ppt

Whatever it was, it pointed the Magi towards Jesus.

As they enter into Judea, it’s natural that they enter into Jerusalem to look for the king.

After all, Jerusalem is the capital of Judea (at least it was back in those days).

And they start asking the people, “Where is He who has been born King of the Jews? For we saw His star in the East and have come to worship Him”.

Surely people in Jerusalem would know where their new king is born!

Surely they would’ve had a great celebration for the arrival of the king!

But the people of Jerusalem had no idea; they didn’t know that a new King was born.

Even though the Jews had the Old Testament, even though it happened in Bethlehem, just over 8 kilometres away from them, they had no idea.

It was the Gentile Magi from Babylon who told them that their new king was born.

This reminds me of a story that I heard about a missionary called Daniel Morris.

Daniel and his wife Kate work among the University students in Lille, France.

As you would expect, there are many French students in the University.

However, the majority of the people who come to the Christian groups are people from different countries in Africa, Asia and other parts of Europe.

And often, these overseas students would be amazed that there aren’t many French Christians on campus.

They would say, “You brought us the gospel. And so, what happened? Where are the French Christians?”

That’s like what’s happened in Jerusalem when the Magi came.

It’s the Gentiles who are announcing to the Jews that the King of the Jews is here!

The news from these Gentile Magi spread throughout Jerusalem.

Eventually, the news got to the royal court of the sitting King of the Jews.

And he’s not impressed. In fact, this false King of the Jews is greatly disturbed.

The false king of the Jews is disturbed

Look with me at verse 3, ‘When King Herod heard this, he was deeply disturbed, and all Jerusalem with him.’

The Herod in this verse is different to the Herod that we see later in the Gospel.

The Herod mentioned at the beginning of the gospel is Herod the Great.

The Herod mentioned later on in the gospel is Herod Antipas, the son of Herod the Great.

Even though he was known as the King of the Jews, Herod the Great was actually an Idumean eeduMEan (an Edomite).

He was appointed King of the Jews by the Romans and invaded Palestine successfully in 36BC.

He was called Herod the Great because he accomplished many projects.

In fact, Jenny and I went to visit some of these while we were in Jerusalem a few years ago.

He built the beautiful city of Caesarea Maritima ppt; he built the almost impregnable fortress of Masada. Ppt

And of course, he started rebuilding of the Temple in Jerusalem. Ppt

The temple took over 40 years to build and still wasn’t finished by the time Jesus died on the cross.

Herod the Great achieved many great things.

However, he was not a good king; in fact he was terrible.

He was very insecure; he always feared that he would lose his power.

He was so scared that people would take his power that he would kill even members of his own family if he suspected that they were a threat.

In fact, he killed his wife Mariamne, his two sons Alexander and Aristobulus, because he thought they were going to overthrow him.

He was insecure and ruthless and he got rid of anyone who was a threat to him.

Why was he so insecure?

Maybe because he knew he wasn’t the rightful king.

He wasn’t a Jew and so he wasn’t the rightful heir to David’s throne; Herod got to be king because Rome appointed him.

The throne didn’t belong to him.

And so, when these Magi from the East came marching into the capital of Judea, asking for the where-about of the newly born King of Jews, Herod was deeply disturbed.

That’s a threat that Herod won’t tolerate.

Not only is Herod disturbed; all Jerusalem is disturbed with him.

Perhaps they had already known that Herod is cruel and ruthless and they are afraid of what he might do if there is a rival king.

How far would Herod go to destroy his rival?

Look with me at verse 4, ‘So he assembled all the chief priests and scribes of the people and asked them where the Messiah would be born.’

Herod starts by gathering the chief priests and the scribes together to see what the Old Testament Scripture had to say about the Messiah, the Christ, the Anointed One.

Herod has heard about the prophecies about the Messiah.

He knows that God had promised that He would one day send His Chosen King to rule Israel and the world.

He knows that this king would bring blessings to many people.

And he associates the long awaited Chosen King with this King that the Magi are seeking.

It’s Herod himself who made the connection

Herod knows how important this King is; Herod knows that this is the rightful King.

And yet, as we’ll see later on in this chapter, he still wants to kill the Chosen King.

That’s how wicked and evil King Herod was.

He would rather kill the rightful King, the Saviour of mankind, than to lose his grip on the throne.

He would rather the world be damned than to lose his throne, a throne that never truly belonged to him in the first place.

That, right there, is sin on full display.

He didn’t care what God thought, he didn’t care what happens to the people of the world, as long as he remains in power as the phony king.

When this phony king asks where the Messiah was to be born, the chief priests and the scribes replies, ‘in Bethlehem of Judea, because this is what was written by the prophet:

6 And you, Bethlehem, in the land of Judah, are by no means least among the leaders of Judah: because out of you will come a leader who will shepherd My people Israel.”

God has promised that the leader and shepherd of his people would come from the little town of Bethlehem.

It was the City of David, the Great King.

It’s now the City of Jesus, the Greatest King.

Unlike Herod, this King will look after God’s people, like a shepherd.

In John 3:19, Jesus is described as the light who has come into the world, and the people loved darkness rather than the light because their deeds were evil.ppt

That’s what happened when Jesus was born.ppt

As the rightful heir to David’s throne, Jesus was a threat to Herod’s darkness.

Herod, whose heart was dark and whose deeds were dark, was disturbed by the good news of Jesus.

This happens whenever the gospel of Jesus confronts our darkened hearts.

Just like Herod, we might know that what we’re doing is wrong, we might even know that what Jesus says is true, and we’re disturbed by him.

We don’t like what Jesus says because he challenges our darkness.

But that’s what the gospel of Jesus does.

It’s not designed to make you comfortable about your sin.

The gospel makes you uncomfortable about your sin, agitated even.

It does that so that you’ll be convicted of your sin and turn to Jesus for forgiveness.

The gospel makes us see the darkness inside us and invites us to come into God’s light.

There is love and comfort and hope in God’s light.

But before we experience the light, we must confront the darkness inside us.

We must confess our sins and ask God for forgiveness.

If Herod chooses to confront his darkness and walk in the light, then he will share the Magi’s excitement about Jesus.

However, he chooses to remain in his darkness.

He will hold on to his power and worship the true king dishonestly.

The false king of the Jews worships dishonestly ppt

Look with me at verse 7, ‘Then Herod secretly summoned the wise men and asked them the exact time the star appeared.’

Herod asks to see the Magi secretly to keep the news from being spread.

He doesn’t want the Magi to draw even more attention and for people to start hearing about the possibility of a rival king.

He asks for the exact time the star appeared; he wants as much intel from the Magi as possible.

He then sends them off on their journey and asks them to report back to him, so that he, too, may worship the king.

Herod uses pious language to show that he’s on the same team as the Magi.

He wants to worship this king too.

However, we know that that’s an outright lie.

Herod has no intention of worshipping the newly born King of the Jews.

He wants to kill him.

He’s only using the Magi to get to him.

And so, in Herod, we see a man who worships dishonestly.

This is the second time the word ‘worship’ appears in the New Testament.

The first time of course, appears in verse two when the Magi said that they’ve come all the way from the East in order to worship the King of the Jews.

The word for worship refers to paying homage.

Often it’s used to describe a physical bowing action.

In the Ancient Near East, that’s what you do when you meet someone important like the king.

You worship them; you bow down to them.

In the person of Herod, we see a dishonest worship.

He says that he’ll worship the King but he has no intention of doing it.

Now notice that he is someone who reads the Bible, who understands that the Messiah is important, who even uses the biblical word ‘worship’ in his conversations,

But it’s all pretense.

He’s a liar who worships dishonestly.

And sadly, there are dishonest worshippers today, even in Churches.

There are people who will go to Bible studies, who tell you that it’s important to trust in Jesus, who might even often use biblical language in their conversations.

But it’s all pretense.

They might say that they trust in Jesus but then they go ahead and do their own thing.

Like Herod, they might do it for power; other might do it for money.

But in the end, it’s all make-believe.

Theirs is a Herodian worship; theirs is a dishonest worship.

Theirs contrast the genuine worship of the Magi.

The Magi truly believed that the new King of the Jews has been born.

They crossed deserts, travelled for months, they entered into foreign territories in order to pay homage to the King of the Jews.

No obstacles can stand in their way!

They thought that they had come to pay homage to a great King.

That’s true! But we know that Jesus is even greater than all that they imagined him to be.

They have come to bow before the King of Kings and Lord of Lords.

And thankfully, there are genuine worshippers of Jesus like the Magi in our churches today.

If you’ve come, genuinely believing that Jesus is the King of God’s Kingdom, then yours is a Magi-like worship.

(I was going to say Magical worship but that doesn’t sound quite right!)

If you have a Magi-like worship, you’ll pay due respect to the King.

While we’re not called to physically worship or bow in the same way, we’re called to worship him by serving him.

We’ll come in humility and we’ll listen to what he says.

If we have a Magi-like worship, we will place Jesus where He belongs; we will worship Him as the King of our lives.

If we do worship him as King of our lives, we will confess our sins, admit our darkness and ask God to bring us into his light.

If we do worship him as King of our lives, we will not let any obstacles get in the way.

Like the Magi, we’ll travel the distance and we’ll cross cultural barriers if that’s what it means to worship him.

We’ll make worshipping King Jesus our top priority.ppt

When we do worship Jesus, genuinely, we, too, will be like that star of Bethlehem.ppt

Like stars shining in the world, when we have genuine worship and serve Jesus from our hearts, we’ll be pointing other people to come and worship the King, the Lord Jesus Christ.

Friends, be a star. In your words and deeds, Point people to the Jesus.

Let’s pray,

‘Father, we thank you for sending Jesus into our world. We thank you that you’ve prepared him long ago to be the King and Saviour of the world. He is the light that gives light to everyone. And yet, we know that our hearts have been darkened by sin. We are often disturbed when the light of your gospel confronts our hearts. Father, please forgive us for the sins we’ve committed against you. Forgive us for the dark thoughts, the dark words and the dark deeds that we commit. Turn our hearts to Jesus. May your Spirit move us so that we’ll come in worship before him, whether we’re here at Church or at home or at work, so that he may be glorified. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.’
