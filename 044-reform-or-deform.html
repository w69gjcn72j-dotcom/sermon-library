Reform or deform

Passage: Nehemiah 13

When God’s people stop reforming, they deform.

When reformation stops

The redeemer remembers

The redeemed don’t stop reforming

The slap that was heard around the world!

That’s how many people described the slap that the famous movie star Will Smith gave the comedian Chris Rock at the Academy Award on 27th March just two weeks ago.

Chris Rock made a bad joke about Will Smith’s wife.

Will Smith then went up on stage and slapped Chris Rock in front of millions of people around the world.

Yes, the joke was unkind, but Will Smith’s reaction was not way over the top!

I like Will Smith’s movies and TV shows but sadly, from now on, most people will remember his slap.

All his years of hard work, all the good things he’s done on and off camera, are all at risk of being forgotten.

Instead, he will be remembered for his slap.

Sadly, for many people, that’s how their lives will be remembered.

They might have done many wonderful things, many great things, even many godly things,

However, due to various reasons, they will be remembered for their failures, their wrongs.

In today’s passage, we meet someone who had accomplished incredible things for God and country.

But towards the later part of his life, he realised that all his achievements might have been for nothing.

All the good things he’s done for God and country seemed to have come undone, not through his own failures but the failures of the very people he was hoping to restore before God.

The passage we’ve just heard today was Nehemiah 13, the very last chapter.

It sounds very much like an anti-climax to Nehemiah’s story; it sounds like a very sad ending to his story.

You have probably noticed that he’s kept asking God to remember him in this last chapter.

He ends his book with these words, “Remember me, my God, with favour”.

It’s almost as if he’s afraid that God will forget him because everyone else seems to have forgotten about his already.

And so, today, I want us to think about our legacy.

What we will be remembered for? How will I be remembered?

If you have your Bible there, please open to Nehemiah 13.

We’ll see three things in this chapter:

When reformation stops

The redeemed don’t stop reforming

Let’s pray before we begin, “Our heavenly father, please by your Spirit help us see your word clearly, so that we may follow Christ more closely, day by day. Amen.”

When reformation stops

Over the book of Nehemiah, we’ve seen that Nehemiah had been a very effective and successful governor.

He’s transformed the city of Jerusalem from a pile or ruins to an active and God-honouring city.

Over the past few chapters, from chapters seven to twelve, we’ve seen how he’s led the people to hear from God’s word as well as to lead them to repent together as a people of God.

The people made vows to obey and follow God and they worshipped God together with joyful songs.

Their songs and praises were heard far away.

The celebration was great!

It would’ve been a great way to end the book.

In the final chapter, we read that after governing Jerusalem for twelve years, Nehemiah went back to serve King Artaxerses of Babylon.

After serving him for a while, he asked for a leave of absence to return to Jerusalem.

When he got back to Jerusalem, he was utterly disappointed.

His aim during his time in Jerusalem was to help the people remember and to treat Jerusalem as the Holy City of God.

When he returned, it was a broken city of unfaithfulness.

It’s a little like this.

Over the next week, we will celebrate one of the most important, if not the most important, Christian feast in the calendar year.

It’s Easter!

Every year in Easter, we’ll read about how Jesus was crucified at a place called Golgotha, which means Place of the Skull.

When I was reading it, I always imagine it to be something like this…

When I got to Jerusalem, I searched for Golgotha and was disappointed that in reality, it looks like this…

I’d imagine that people would at least treat something that might look like where Jesus was crucified with respect, but it looks a little more like a rubbish dump.

When Nehemiah went back to Jerusalem, he expected to see a Holy City.

Being a realist, he knew that it wouldn’t be a perfect city full of sinless people.

However, it would at least be a city that has repented and has learnt from their mistakes.

There would’ve been signs that they’ve repented, reformed and have grown.

But when he got there, it wasn’t a holy city to be revered but a spiritual rubbish dump.

What rubbish did Nehemiah find in this spiritual dump?

Nehemiah sees broken vows.

If you’ve been here a few weeks ago when we did Nehemiah chapter 10, you’d remember that the Jews made a very solemn vow to follow God’s laws.

They’ve made a vow to not marry people from the surrounding nations.

They’ve made a vow to keep the Sabbath Day holy.

They’ve made a vow to support the people who serve at the Temple so that they can continue with Temple worship.

And what did they do?

They’ve broken all their vows.

All the solemn promises that’ve made to God, they broke them all!

One of the most obvious and most devastating vows that they’ve broken is their promise to support those who serve in the Temple.

Over the last three chapters, there was a lot of focus about the importance of people giving to support the work of the temple.

It’s vital that God’s people support the work of the temple to ensure that they have a functional temple worship, and to worship God properly, according to God’s word.

Serving and worshipping God should be at the heart of the lives of God’s people.

But they stopped giving to the house of God!

It might not sound like much, but when they stopped giving to the House of God, there were devastating effects.

Look with me at verses 10-11a, ‘I also found out that because the portions for the Levites had not been given, each of the Levites and the singers performing the service had gone back to his own field. Therefore, I rebuked the officials, asking, “Why has the house of God been neglected?”’

Because they stopped giving to the temple, the Levites and the singers didn’t have food to eat.

As a result, they left the temple to work in the fields.

When they work in the fields, there were no one to look after the temple.

And so, the temple, the house of God, is neglected.

All the hard work that Nehemiah and others have put into reforming and re-establishing the temple seems to have been undone.

It was as if the reformation at the temple never happened.

It was as if all the repentance they had before God never happened.

Since the temple has become emptier, the priest Eliashib found another use.

Look with me at verses 4-5, “Now before this, the priest Eliashib had been put in charge of the storerooms of the house of our God. He was a relative of Tobiah 5 and had prepared a large room for him where they had previously stored the grain offerings, the frankincense, the articles, and the tenths of grain, new wine, and fresh oil prescribed for the Levites, singers, and gatekeepers, along with the contributions for the priests.”

Eliashib was doing his relative a favour and prepared a room for him that would’ve been otherwise be used to store supplies for those who serve at the temple.

It’s bad enough that the people didn’t give to the Levites, it’s worse that now they don’t have a storeroom to keep the supplies, even if people wanted to give to the Levites.

But it gets worse still.

The person living in the storage room is none other than Tobiah the Ammonite.

This was the same Tobiah who taunted Nehemiah and the Jews when they were building the city wall.

Tobiah said, “Even if a fox climbed up what they are building, he would break down their stone wall!”

Tobiah mocked Nehemiah and God’s people; he was an enemy of God’s people.

Now that the wall has been rebuilt, he now lives safely within the city; and not just the city, but the temple, the very heart of the place of worship.

And he’s not there because he wants to worship God.

He’s taking up precious space that belonged to God’s people; his very presence was an obstruction to worship.

He was a known enemy of God, using up the resources of God, in the temple of God.

We read at the beginning of Nehemiah 13 that he wasn’t even qualified to enter the assembly of God, since he was an Ammonite.

It’s like letting an evil, unrepentant cult leader living inside the Church.

It’s a disgrace!

Now Eliashib the priest might be thinking he was only doing his relative a favour.

But it was a favour that was evil in God’s eyes.

To let Tobiah live in God’s temple was a direct act of disobedience to God and an insult to God’s people.

Eliashib foolishly placed favouring his relative above honouring God.

That’s what makes his act evil.

In response, Nehemiah threw all of Tobiah’s belongings outside to make the room a storage space for those who serve at the temple again.

Eventually, he got the Levites to come back and to restore rightful worship in the temple, again.

Nehemiah then addressed the issue of the people profaning the Sabbath.

The Sabbath was a Holy Day in the week where God’s people stop working to remember and to celebrate what God’s done.

It was a Holy Day, a special Day, and God told them to stop working to celebrate it.

But the people profaned it, that is, made it just like any other day of the week.

They bought and sold things on the Sabbath day, just like they would any other day.

Nehemiah addressed this issue, rightly calling it evil, and stopped trading on Sunday.

Finally, Nehemiah also addressed the issue of the Jews marrying people from other nations.

Nehemiah’s main concern wasn’t racial; rather it was spiritual.

The people from other nations didn’t know God and could only lead God’s people away from God.

They were not able to lead them close to God.

As a result of these marriages, the children were not able to learn from God’s word because they couldn’t understand Hebrew.

Nehemiah then got them to vow again to not give their son or daughter to marry the daughters and sons of other nations.

To marry someone who was not one of God’s people was strictly forbidden.

Nehemiah even expelled the son of the high priest because he had married the daughter of a foreigner, and the foreigner happened to be Sanballat, another well-known enemy of God’s people.

God’s people seemed to have forgotten all the lessons that they’ve learnt and all the vows that they’ve made.

It’s as if they’ve forgotten that God’s saved them, time and time again.

When they forget salvation, God’s people start to deform.

There was a time when God’s people weren’t a people, but God gathered them together and formed a nation.

Though they have failed, time and time again, God would come and rescue them, whenever they repent and call out to him.

When they have repented, they would be reformed as a nation, that is they would go back to God’s word and follow him.

They would be transformed to be a God-honouring nation.

However, as we’ve seen time and time again, they would often fall again and forgot how God had rescued them.

And the whole process starts again.

As soon as they forget that they’re sinners, who need to repent of their sins, they stop reforming as a nation.

When they stop reforming, they stop their transformation into a God-honouring nation.

They go back to being a spiritual dump.

The spiritual life of God’s people is a little bit like that of salmons.

Salmons are famous for swimming upstream as part of their life cycle.

Salmons are born in fresh water rivers but they spend much of their time in the ocean.

When the right time comes, they swim back upstream to the place where they were born to lay their own eggs.

It takes hard work to swim upstream; life would’ve been a lot easier if they took it easy and stay in the ocean.

But if they did that, their life cycle would stop, and they would become extinct.

Christians are like salmons; we swim against the current of our world.

The world teaches tells us to live life one way, but God tells us to live His way, the opposite way.

It would be easy for a Christian to stop swimming against the current and just go with the flow.

However, if a Christian does that, he would stop growing as a Christian.

In fact, he would wither and decay.

If a church stops growing, it would stop reforming and start deforming.

The deformation may not be obvious at first; it might be missing a few Sundays here and there.

But the decay will slowly but surely come.

For a Christian, to not grow is to die.

For the Christian church, to not reform is to deform.

Just like Tobias finding a room in the temple, to not reform is to give the enemy a room in your life to start deforming your spiritually before God.

If you stop reforming as a Christian or a Church, you will deform.

If you have been redeemed, you won’t stop reforming.

The redeemed don’t stop reforming

If you have been truly saved by Jesus, don’t stop reforming.

Always come back to what God says.

Always come with a heart ready to repent of our sins, ready to reform your life and ready to be transformed by God himself.

Christians come to hear what God says when we read from the Bible.

The Bible is like a mirror and a pair of glasses.

It’s like a mirror because when we read the Bible, we get a glimpse of who we really look like.

The Bible gives us a true picture of ourselves because it shows us what we look like in God’s eyes.

If we’re honest with ourselves, we’ll see our sins and our flaws every time we read the Bible.

We’ll see how unworthy we are in God’s presence.

We’ll also see in God’s word how incredible God is, that he would want to save people like us.

We’ll see what it means to amend our lives and to walk in his way.

The Bible is also like a pair of glasses.

It helps us to see our world as God sees it.

It’ll help us to what’s good and evil, according to God.

It’s only as the Bible helps us see ourselves and our world clearly that we can repent, reform and be transformed.

That’s why it’s important for each of us to read the Bible daily or as regularly as you can.

I’m so glad that Mei Hua posts Bible verses almost every day to help the Chinese congregation remember God’s words.

I’m also glad that a number of our members read the Bible themselves every day using aids like Our daily bread.

Keep it up!

That’s why it’s also important for us to attend Growth Groups where we can share and discuss God’s word.

That’s why it’s important for us to come on Sundays and hear God’s word explained at Church.

God is speaking to us but are we listening?

This process of repenting, reforming and transforming as a Christian never stops.

Just as we don’t stop eating our physical food, we don’t stop eating our spiritual food, that is, God’s word.

Don’t believe the enemy’s lies when he says that you know enough about God, that you’ve grown enough.

It’s a lie because as soon as your hunger for God’s word stops, you’ll stop growing as a Christian.

If you stop growing as a Christian, you will decay.

Instead of reformation, you’ll deform.

And so, avoid deformation at all costs.

There are so many reasons to not want to grow as a Christian.

There are so many excuses to not follow God.

There are so many temptations for us to break the promises, the vows, we’ve made before God.

Avoid them at all costs.

They are deadly and they are evil.

They’re often dressed up as concerns for others, like Eliashib preparing a room for his relative Tobias.

But they are disguises and excuses for evil acts done in disobedience against God.

The Bible tells us this in Galatians 6:7-8,

”Don’t be deceived: God is not mocked. For whatever a person sows he will also reap, 8 because the one who sows to his flesh will reap destruction from the flesh, but the one who sows to the Spirit will reap eternal life from the Spirit.”

Some people think they can outsmart God, but they can’t.

You can’t fool God.

If you live for your own pleasure, don’t disguise it with some moral or even godly excuse.

You will reap what you sow.

If you don’t reform according to God’s word, you will deform.

Even the great King David in the Old Testament wasn’t immune.

He was a king chosen by God, a man after God’s own heart.

When he was at the top of his game, after he had won many battles in God’s name, he fell into temptation and committed adultery and murder.

His family was never the same again.

His kingdom was never the same again.

In a moment of weakness, he stopped trusting in God and almost lost everything.

Yes, David eventually repented and God did forgive him, but there were terrible consequences, for him, for his family and for his kingdom.

And so, avoid deformation at all costs.

If you’re a Christian, don’t go back to what you once were.

You were saved, you were bought at a price.

Don’t go back to being spiritually dead.

Rather swim against the current and live according to God’s word.

Read from his word and repent, reform and be transformed.

When God’s people stop reforming, we will deform.

What does this mean for us?

Don’t deform, be reformed.

Be honest and keep asking yourself, is my Christian life reforming?

How am I repenting of my sins, reforming of my ways and being transformed by the Holy Spirit of God?

If I’m not reforming as a Christian, then how am I deforming?

It’s one or the other.

Take some time alone this week if you can and reflect on whether you’re reforming or deforming.

It doesn’t have to take a whole day.

Take 30 minutes sometime this week and hear from God’s word.

What is God’s word saying about my life?

How does God want my life to reform?

Don’t deform, be reformed.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How am I tempted to go back to my old way of life?

How am I tempted to swim with the rest of the world?

How do I want to be remembered by God?

How will you life be remembered?

As much as we want our life to be remembered fondly by our families and our friends,

Only one person’s remembrance matters.

And that person is God.

How will God remember your life?

How will God remember my life?

Will he remember the hard choices we’ve made for him?

Will he remember our repentance, our reformation and our transformation?

Or won’t he be able to remember any of these because we didn’t do any of them?

How will God remember us?

How we God remember me?

How will God remember you?

Indeed, may our prayers be like Nehemiah’s prayer, “Remember me, my God, with favour.”

But now we add, “Remember me, my God, with favour. Remember me because I’ve turned to Jesus. Remember my repentance and my reformation. Remember my transformation by the Holy Spirit.”

Let’s pray,

“Father, indeed, remember us with favour. Thank you that in Jesus we can come to you with repentance and receive your Holy Spirit. Please by your Spirit, lead us to repentance, reform our lives and transform us from glory to glory. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”
