Access to God

Ephesians 2:16-22

Big Idea: Jesus reconciled us to God by his death, proclaimed peace to us by his risen life, grants us access to the Father by the Spirit, and grows us into the holy temple in the Lord. (16-22)

1. Jesus makes and proclaims peace

2. Jesus gives us access to the Father in one Spirit

3. Jesus grows us into God’s holy temple

Last week, after Church, my family took me out for a Father’s Day lunch by the bay.

It was in Monterey, about 6 minutes’ drive from our Church, along General Holmes Drive.

There was this Fish and Chips shop I’ve driven passed many times but had never tried.

And so, we ordered Fish and Chips there and walked to the park by the bay.

As we were looking for a place to sit, we saw a lady who sat on the chair all by herself.

There were no seats nearby and so I was going to ask the lady whether we can share the table with her.

It might sound strange to some of us, but back in Hong Kong, that’s what we do.

When you don’t have enough seats at a restaurant, you ask strangers whether you can share the table with someone (搭枱 / 搭檯).

Anyone else tried it?

Anyway, my family was a bit embarrassed and so we ended up seating on the grass.

It turned out to be the right thing to do because firstly, we had a great time on the grass and secondly, the lady was reserving the table for a friend, possibly even a date.

Imagine how awkward it would be for a family of complete strangers to share the table with a couple on a date!

The table was off-limits to us, we don’t have access to the table, and rightly so!

One of the incredible blessings we have as Christians is that we can pray.

When we pray, we talk to God.

That is, we say that we have access to God!

That’s a very big claim!

Many religions require their followers to offer sacrifices to their gods, especially during festivals.

We as a Church send missionaries to countries where there are many temples to various gods and spirits.

Sometimes their gods and spirits listen; sometimes they don’t.

Christians, on the other hand, say that we have access to the throne room of the one true God.

How do we know whether we have really accessed the throne room of God?

How do we know whether we have access to God?

And what do we do once we have access to God?

That’s what we’ll be looking at today in our Bible passage.

And so, please open to Ephesians 2, beginning from verse 16.

We’ll see three things:

1. Jesus makes and proclaims peace

2. Jesus gives us access to the Father in one Spirit

3. Jesus grows us into God’s holy temple

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Father, please open the eyes of our hearts by your Holy Spirit, that we may see the brilliance of Jesus even more clearly. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”

1. Jesus makes and proclaims peace

Last week, we looked at the peace that the death of Jesus has accomplished at a person-to-person level, along the horizontal axis.

Look with me at verse 14, “For he is our peace, who made both groups one and tore down the dividing wall of hostility.”

Jesus brings peace to people who were once hostile enemies of one another.

By the death of Jesus, he tore down the wall of hostility and brought them together.

The passage then continues by looking at the peace that Jesus’ death has accomplished at a God-to-person level, along the vertical axis.

Look with me at Ephesians 2:16, “He did this so that he might reconcile both to God in one body through the cross by which he put the hostility to death.”

Last week, we learnt that Jews and Gentiles (that is people who weren’t Jews, which would’ve been most of us), were hostile towards one another.

Jesus died so that he might reconcile both the Jews and the Gentiles to God.

Jesus died so that he might reconcile the two warring groups back to God.

While the Jew and Gentile division applies specifically between those from Jewish and those from non-Jewish backgrounds,

What Jesus did on the cross also reconciles other warring groups back to God.

At the heart of our hostility to one another is our hostility towards God.

We’re hostile to one another because we are hostile towards God.

To have peace along the horizontal axis, we must have peace along the vertical axis first.

To try to have peace along the horizontal axis without dealing with the vertical axis will ultimately be futile.

It’s a little like this.

In August 2021, Lili Vachon, 27, thought something was wrong with her contact lenses because she got really dizzy of a sudden.

She took it them out, but things didn’t improve, so she called an ambulance.

After waiting in the emergency for hours, they sent her home with treatment for a migraine.

Lili still didn’t improve and got an MRI scan done.

The result showed that she had a stroke.

The stroke itself showed she had a heart issue and she had to have an operation done on her heart.

Lili’s had a serious and life-threatening condition, which could not have been fixed by two tablets of painkillers.

That’s what we do when we try to make peace with one another without making peace with God.

The hostility and the horror of our world are only the symptoms of a far deeper problem.

We are at war with God, and unless we’re reconciled to God, we won’t be able to reconcile with one another.

Anything else is just painkillers.

And yet, God did reconcile us in Jesus.

By his death, Jesus has provided a way to destroy the hostility between God and man.

The Bible uses a strong word here; Jesus puts the hostility to death.

By his death, Jesus killed the hostility.

As one writer puts it, “'Christ in his death was slain, but the slain was a slayer too.”

When Jesus slain the hostility between God and man, he brought peace between God and man, which itself brought peace between man and man.

How do we know that Jesus’ death was effective in slaying the hostility?

Look with me at verse 17, “He came and proclaimed the good news of peace to you who were far away and peace to those who were near.”

Jesus rose again and preached peace to those who were far and peace to those who were near.

Jesus has sent his followers to keep proclaiming peace to people everywhere.

Whenever you hear the gospel, it’s because Jesus has sent them to preach peace to you.

Last Thursday, I led another funeral here at Church.

It’s the funeral of someone I visited at St George Hospital about an hour before Yimin went home to the Lord.

This lady died a few days after Yimin.

Only her family and a few close friends came to the funeral.

By God’s providence, Gordon knew her and he invited several of their mutual friends to come.

Most of the family members were not Christians; they were far away from God.

But by God’s grace, they heard about the peace that the death and resurrection of Jesus brings.

My prayer is that they will know the peace that they heard about at the funeral.

This is the gospel, the good news that we share.

The death of Jesus brought peace to us; the risen Christ proclaimed peace to us.

2. Jesus gives us access to the Father in one Spirit

Jesus’ death and resurrection brings us peace because through Jesus, we have access to the Father in One Spirit.

Look with me at verse 18, “For through him we both have access in one Spirit to the Father.”

We have peace and satisfaction as we come before God through Jesus in one Spirit.

We have not come before the presence of an earthly king or ruler but before God himself.

We have not come before one god of many gods; through Jesus, we have come before the throne room of the one true God.

Jesus has reconciled us both to God.

Now notice it’s us both; we don’t come to God merely as individuals.

Christianity was never an individual, private religion.

Christianity is personal but it’s always presented as a gathering, a community, coming before God.

Together, though we might be from different backgrounds, from opposing backgrounds, we approach God’s throne.

Together, as one group, as one Church, we have access to God.

Together, as Chinese and Indians, as Indians and English, as Russians and Ukrainians, as North and South Koreans, as indigenous and non-indigenous people, we enter the throne room of God together.

We come to God’s throne room together because we believe who Jesus is and what he has done.

Only God himself can reconcile us to one other and bring true peace because only God can reconcile us to himself through Jesus.

Only God can reconcile enemies together because only he can end the hostility between him and us through Jesus.

After Jesus died and rose from the dead, we now have access to God the Father through God the Son in God the Spirit.

God sent his own Spirit to his people in order to work in us so that we can come before God, and have peace with God.

God sent His own Spirit to his people so that we will have with one another.

It’s powerful and it’s beautiful.

Anything else is like giving pain-killers to a stroke patient.

Elizabeth was a young mother married to a young man called Jim Eliot.

Jim had a passion to bring the gospel of peace to the lost tribes of Ecuador.

He travelled by helicopter to them, gave gifts to them and spoke to them in their native language.

Things were looking good! However, very soon after, members of the Acuas tribe speared Jim and his team to death.

Elizabeth wasn’t there at that time as she was looking after then 10 month old daughter.

However, rather than habouring hatred in her heart, Elizabeth went back to that very same tribe to share the gospel with the very same tribe who killed her husband.

Elizabeth wanted them to be reconciled to God and be saved.

She wanted to enter the throne room of God together with those who killed her husband, through Jesus in one Spirit.

That’s what Jim would’ve wanted.

That’s what Jesus has sent her to do, to proclaim peace, even to her husband’s killers.

To us, it’s humanly impossible but not to God.

And all things are possible with God.

That’s what true peace and reconciliation looks like.

How do you know whether you have access to God?

You know you have access to God when you have reconciled with God the Father in Jesus,

when you’re at peace in his presence, asking for God to help you proclaim His peace to others,

even those who are impossible to reconcile with, from a human point of view.

You ask God for what’s impossible with the flesh, but possible with the Spirit.

3. Jesus grows us into God’s holy temple

There was one time when we were merely Gentiles in the flesh, without Christ, without hope and without God in the world.

But now, in Christ Jesus, we’ve been reconciled to God, no longer strangers but fellow citizens with the saints.

Look with me at verses 19-20, “So, then, you are no longer foreigners and strangers, but fellow citizens with the saints, and members of God’s household, built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Jesus himself as the cornerstone.”

If we’re in Christ, we’re fellow citizens with the saints.

Saints are people who have been made holy by Jesus, which includes everyone who believe in Jesus.

We belong with the saints, who are seated with Christ in the heavenlies, in front of God’s throne.

That’s where we belong! That’s where our true citizenship is.

As residents in a world that doesn’t believe in Jesus, we might feel like outsiders, foreigners, people who don’t belong.

That might be true, but remember we are citizens of heaven with access to the very throne of God.

But more than that, we’re members of God’s household.

We’re members of his family; we call God Father!

We are in God’s household!

And so, we’re no longer foreigners but citizens with the saints.

We’re no longer enemies of God but members of his own household.

And there’s still more!

We also a building, built on the foundation of the apostles and the prophets.

The apostles were the disciples who followed Jesus, as well as Paul.

They were especially sent by Jesus to tell his message.

The prophets were people who taught God’s message in the Churches, telling people what it means to live their lives as followers of Jesus.

And most importantly, Christ Jesus is the cornerstone of this building.

It’s Jesus himself who sets the direction of the building, and how this building will be built.

We are the building, the apostles and the prophets are the foundation and Christ Jesus is the Cornerstone.

On Wednesday, Jenny and I built Ikea cabinets on our day off.

Here’s the set of instructions of what we needed to do.

One of the instructions is to nail a board on the back of the cabinet.,

However, before we do that, we had to mark out the back column of the board so that we know where the column is before we nail the board.

We had to make sure we get the markings right and we must nail according to the line.

Otherwise, you will make holes at the back of the cabinet.

Trust me, I know!

Jesus is the cornerstone of the Church.

We measure our lives according to his life; we measure our teachings according to what he taught.

Jesus taught it to the apostles and they in turn taught it to the Church.

The Church recorded their teachings and they’ve been passed down to us.

As the Church, we live out the gospel and we proclaim the gospel; we live and breathe the gospel.

And as we live and breathe the gospel, we show that we are citizens with the saints, members of the family of God, the building built on foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Jesus being the cornerstone.

What kind of building is this?

It is nothing less than the living, growing, temple of the Lord.

Look with me at verses 21-22, “In him the whole building, being put together, grows into a holy temple in the Lord.  In him you are also being built together for God’s dwelling in the Spirit.”

Here, the Bible pushes the building imager even further.

Yes, it’s a building, but it’s not like any building you’ve ever seen.

It’s a living building.

It’s a building that grows into a holy temple of God.

Together, as God’s people, as God’s Church, we are a living, growing, holy temple of God.

The Bible is not talking about the physical Church building.

This church building is not the temple of God.

However, when believers gather together in Christ’s name, when we access the throne of God as we come before him in prayer, when we come, ready to obey the voice of God as he speaks to us, Jesus makes us into the living, growing and holy temple of God because in Jesus, we now access to God himself.

This is what we do here on Sundays.

We come together as God’s holy temple.

We come before God’s presence, come to access God because Jesus has given us access to God!

And as a people who are given access to God through his Son and by his Spirit, we will grow.

Jesus hasn’t saved us so that we remain static as we were.

Jesus saved us from death to life! He saved us to have life, and to have life is to grow.

He saved us to grow in our love for God and for one another.

He saved us to grow in our knowledge of God and to grow in our service of one another.

He saved us to grow in our yearning for the lost just as God himself yearns for the lost.

That’s what Church is all about.

This place, this people, this Church is where we come together to access God.

It’s also where our community can come to worship God.

For we are the temple of the living and holy God.

As those who are in Christ, God lives in us by his Spirit.

And as God’s Spirit lives in us, we are now his Temple.

What’s the most beautiful part of our Church?

As nice as they are, it’s not the stained glass window, or the beautiful sandstone.

The most beautiful part of our Church is when people from different age groups, different cultural groups, different language groups, come together and pray, “Our Father, thank you that that because of Jesus, we can all come before you.”

And it’s not just prayers that are prayed because the words are on the screen, but genuine prayers said out of our trust in Jesus.

And after we pray, we genuinely love and share our concern for one another, the old speaking with the young, the English speakers actively trying to get to know the non-English speakers, the Chinese speakers actively trying to get to know the non-Chinese speakers. And every combination in between.

That’s beautiful to see.

We’re not there yet, but pray that we will be the beautiful Church that God had paid for with the price of His Son.

Be reconciled to God, be reconciled to one another, through Jesus, through the Spirit.

How do we know we have access to God?

We have access to God if we believe who Jesus is, the one died and rose from the dead to reconcile us to God and to one another.

We have access to God if we gather together and grow together as the living, holy temple of God.

Friends, since we have access to the Father in Jesus, be the living, growing temple of God.

Let me take you back to the picnic I mentioned at the beginning of the talk.

Imagine that there was a picnic table, where a family was having a picnic.

They too, were celebrating Father’s Day and they were having a great time.

Now imagine the son, seeing us seating on the grass, invites us to join his table.

And having invited to the table, we meet with the rest of the family.

We wish the Father a happy Father’s Day, and we share in the fish and chips.

Friends, this is what Jesus, God the Son has done.

He has brought us to the table, as it were.

He invited us to 搭枱 at his Father’s table.

He invited us to be his family.

We’re now meeting with God as a family member of God.

More than that, if people are to come to God, this is the place to be.

The temple is where people meet with God and in Christ, by the Spirit, we are now the temple of God, where people come to meet with God.

Be the living, growing, temple of God, be the place that people come to meet with God.

There will be visitors who come who don’t know God yet.

Let’s speak God’s word to them, let’s live according to God’s word in our lives so that they will see that God really does live in us.

We do that when we come together on Sundays but we also do that throughout the week.

Over the past week, I’ve been really encouraged by our Men’s Growth Group and our Women’s Growth Group.

It was so good to hear how we can help one another live our lives as Christians and what it means to be fruitful in our Church.

In a little while, as we have morning tea, see if you can talk with someone that you haven’t had a chance to talk to yet.

Speak with someone who’s not like you.

Share your table with them. Invited them to 搭枱.

More importantly, invite them to God’s table.

For if we do have access to the Father in Jesus, we will be the living, growing temple of God, pointing people to find peace with God in Jesus.

Here are the three questions for this week:

1. Do you have peace with God in Jesus?

2. What is the state of your heart when you access God in prayer? Do you rush to give him a wish list? Or are you at peace in his presence, asking for his will to be done?

3. How am I helping someone else to find peace in Jesus and to grow in him?

Friends, since we have access to the Father in Jesus, be the living, growing temple of God.

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you for giving us access to your very presence through our Lord Jesus. Thank you that by his death and resurrection, Jesus has reconciled us, both Jews and Gentiles, to you and to one another. Please forgive us when we don’t treat you with the respect that we ought when we come into your presence. By your Spirit, may our Lord Jesus grow us into a living, growing, holy temple for you, so that those around us will find peace in Jesus also, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
