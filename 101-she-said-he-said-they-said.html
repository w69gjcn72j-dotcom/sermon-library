She Said, He Said, They Said

John 4:39-45 · 2020
Big IdeaBelieve Jesus for yourself: hear his word and know that he is truly the Saviour of the world.

Sermon Title: She said, He said, they said

Passage: John 4.39_45

Big Idea: True believers testify to honour the Saviour Jesus; Jesus testifies against false believers who don’t honour him.

The woman’s testimony about Jesus: “He told me…”

The Samaritans’ testimony about Jesus: “We have heard…”

Jesus’ testimony about his own people: “A prophet has no honour…”

Our testimony about Jesus and his testimony about us: “Truly I tell you…”

Intro: Welcome once again to St Paul’s Kogarah.

It’s great to have you with us this morning.

One of the things that we used to do as we welcome people to Church is to shake people’s hands.

We can’t physically shake hands now because we need to restrict the possibilities of COVID-19 from spreading.

But it’s actually quite hard not to shake hands when you see someone.

I had to literally pull my arm away at times because I was so used to welcoming someone by shaking their hand.

Now, we just do the elbow tap or use the Wuhan shake with our feet

If Jesus comes into your living room now, how will we welcome him?

I think we can safely shake Jesus’ hands because we know that we can’t transmit COVID-19 to Jesus, and he won’t pass it to us, but will we still try?

How would we welcome Jesus?

How would he receive our welcome?

Today, we’ll see two different groups of people welcoming Jesus and we’ll see how Jesus responds.

We’ll see that not all welcomes are equal, especially when it comes to welcoming Jesus.

The woman’s testimony about Jesus: “He told me…”

If you have your Bible there, open up with me to John 4 beginning from verse 39, ’Now many Samaritans from that town believed in him because of what the woman said when she testified, “He told me everything I ever did.”’

We know from earlier on in this passage that this person is Jesus.

In their conversations, Jesus knows and tells her that she’s had five husbands and that the man she’s living with now is not her husband.

She’s not particularly proud of the fact that she’s living in sin.

But even though Jesus knows about this woman’s condition, he still wants to speak to the woman and to teach this woman about God.

This leads the woman to ask, “could this be the Messiah? The chosen one?”

The Messiah was the one God has promised in the Old Testament part of the Bible, who would bring in a new era of God’s rule.

She doesn’t keep this question to herself; she goes back into town and tells the other Samaritans about Jesus.

She simply told the other Samaritans about her encounter with Jesus.

It’s her story about Jesus; it’s her testimony, her witness of Jesus.

Many Samaritans believed in Jesus because of what the woman’s said about him.

What did these Samaritans actually believe about Jesus?

We don’t know exactly at this stage.

Whatever it is that they had believed about Jesus, it’s not enough.

And so, they ask Jesus to stay with them; they’re keen to find out more about Jesus.

It’s a great story, isn’t it?

We have a woman who’s so excited about Jesus that she goes into town and tells many people about him.

The people in town then goes and meet him for themselves.

They don’t just want to meet and greet Jesus; they don’t want to give him a phoney and superficial welcome.

They’re keen to find out more about him and ask him to stay.

And so, Jesus obliges and stays with them another two days.

The Samaritans’ testimony about Jesus: “We have heard…”

After spending two days with Jesus, the emphasis shifts from the woman’s testimony to the Samaritans’ testimony about Jesus.

Look with me at verse 41-42, “Many more believed because of what he said. 42 And they told the woman, “We no longer believe because of what you said, since we have heard for ourselves and know that this really is the Saviour of the world.”

Many more Samaritans have now believed in Jesus because of what Jesus’ said to them during his stay with them.

They’re no longer believing Jesus just because of what the Samaritan woman’s said about him.

Though the woman’s testimony about Jesus had played an important part to them meeting Jesus, it’s no longer the main reason why they’re believing in Jesus.

The Samaritans have now heard the words of Jesus for themselves.

They have heard the words of Jesus for themselves and they now believe in Jesus.

What do they believe about Jesus?

That he’s really the Saviour of the world.

That’s a massive call to make about Jesus!

In the Old Testament, God was seen as the Saviour, time and time again.

God is the one who saves his people.

And now, the Samaritans see Jesus as the Saviour of the world.

Here in the Gospel of John, the Samaritans testify that Jesus is the Saviour of the world.

They genuinely believe that Jesus is going to save the world.

That’s their testimony of Jesus!

It all started from a conversation by the well between Jesus and the Samaritan woman.

It then led to the woman telling some people in her town.

This then led to the Samaritans inviting Jesus to stay, which led to many more people believing in Jesus.

By the end of the two days, many Samaritans who heard Jesus believes that Jesus is the Saviour of the world.

That’s the harvest that Jesus was telling the disciples while the woman was away telling her friends about Jesus.

We read about this last week, in verse 35, “Listen to what I’m telling you: Open your eyes and look at the fields, because they are ready for harvest.”

Little did the disciples know that in two days, they would see a harvest among the Samaritans.

And it all started with a quiet conversation by the well.

That’s how the message of Jesus spreads.

It often starts small and grows into a harvest.

A few years ago, a 74 year-old grandmother by the name of Josie wanted to share the Bible with her family members.

In particular, she wanted to share the Bible with her grandson, who wasn’t living near her and her son-in-law, who doesn’t have a computer.

And so, this 74 years-old grandmother goes through online Bible lessons with her grandson

And she prints off the Bible lessons for her son-in-law.

Over time, as the son-in-law grows in his faith in Jesus, his wife, Josie’s daughter, wanted to find out more about Jesus too.

Josie’s daughter was raised in a Christian family but had walked away from the faith.

But seeing her mother bringing her husband to Jesus, Josie’s daughter wants to read the Bible with him too.

The simple gospel work that the 74 year-old Josie was doing led to a harvest for eternal life for her family members.

Wouldn’t it be great if we can see more stories like this here at our Church as well?

If the 74 year-old Josie can share Bible stories online with her family members, I think that under God, many of us can do it as well.

You might just start by attending the online Bible studies and online Church services.

We’re now working on new ways to share the story about Jesus in this season of social isolation.

Keep praying, keep looking for opportunities.

Who knows, it might be just a simple conversation over Facetime, over Zoom, which may lead to a harvest in the not too distant future.

Jesus’ testimony about his own people: “A prophet has no honour…”

At the beginning of the chapter, Jesus was down south in Judea.

He wanted to go north to Galilee and on the way through, he had to go through Samaria.

And so, after spending two days with the Samaritans, Jesus leaves them and continue his journey to Galilee.

But as he does, we find an interesting description in verse 44, “(Jesus himself had testified that a prophet has no honour in his own country.)”

That’s Jesus’ testimony about his own people: a prophet has no honour in his own country.

Why is there suddenly this strange sentence in the passage?

For most of this chapter, Jesus has been spending time with a group of foreigners, the Samaritans.

Remember, the Jews and the Samaritans at Jesus’ time didn’t get along.

But in this chapter, the Samaritans were shown to be very receptive to Jesus.

The Samaritans testified positively about Jesus, they wanted Jesus to stay and they honoured him by calling him the Saviour of the world.

How would his own people, the Jews, respond to Jesus?

Jesus doesn’t have very high hopes for his own people.

He sees himself as a prophet who won’t be honoured in his own country.

Will it really be as bad as Jesus anticipated?

It doesn’t look that way if we look at the last verse of today’s passage, in verse 45, “When they entered Galilee, the Galileans welcomed him because they had seen everything he did in Jerusalem during the festival. For they also had gone to the festival.”

When Jesus came to Galilee, his home country, among fellow Jews, they welcomed him in.

They welcomed him in because they’ve seen everything that he did in Jerusalem during the Passover feast.

In Jerusalem, Jesus was doing various signs and some of these Jews must have witnessed what Jesus did.

They were excited to see Jesus in Galilee!

Why? It’s because they probably want to see more signs, more miracles.

Unlike the Samaritans, who believe and testify that Jesus is the Saviour of the world,

The Jews in his own country see Jesus as an Entertainer, a celebrity.

They welcomed Jesus but they didn’t honour him.

We see this even back in John’s account of what happened back in Jerusalem in John 2:23-24, “23 While he was in Jerusalem during the Passover Festival, many believed in his name when they saw the signs he was doing. 24 Jesus, however, would not entrust himself to them, since he knew them all.

These people believed in Jesus but it’s a superficial belief.

Their belief in Jesus is one that might superficially welcome Jesus but it’s not a belief that honours Jesus.

It’s like what happened at a hockey game that was played in 2013 Men’s Hockey World League Semi-finals at Rotterdam, Holland.

Belgium was playing against Australia.

They were welcomed to the field by the various officials and there was a camera crew in front of them.

The two teams stood together as they prepared for the game to start.

The first anthem to be played was Australia’s national anthem.

And so, the music starts playing and out comes these words: “E Ihowā Atua, O ngā iwi mātou rā”

Hold on, they don’t sound like English, or Australian!

They broadcasted the New Zealand national anthem instead.

They welcomed the Australian team but they weren’t being honoured as the Australian team, by mistake, of course.

The Jews in Jesus’ home country welcomed him but they didn’t give him the honour that’s due to him.

They should’ve been honouring him as the Saviour of the world, just like the Samaritans did.

But they weren’t honouring him as the Saviour; they were just treating him as an Entertainer, someone who puts on a good show.

That can happen today, can’t it?

There are many people who read about, sing about and even teach about Jesus.

But rather than honouring him as the Saviour of the world, as we should, there are many who treats Jesus as a teacher, a miracle-worker or just someone who puts on a good show.

What’s your view of Jesus? What do you say about him?

What’s your testimony of him?

Our testimony about Jesus and his testimony about us: “Truly I tell you…”

The Samaritan woman in today’s passage did a great job with her testimony about Jesus.

There’s a lot we can learn from her.

She points out that Jesus knows all about her and asks whether he’s the Messiah.

Our testimony about Jesus can begin the same way.

Jesus knows everything about us.

He knows our deepest, darkest secrets, secrets that we’ve never told anybody.

But he still loves us the same.

He still loves us despite of who we are, enough to die for our sins.

That’s how he’s saved us.

And now this is how our lives have changed.

And now this is how we obey him.

And now this is how we honour him.

If you haven’t done so yet, write down your story, your testimony of how you’ve become a Christian.

Here are three steps to writing a testimony:

Step 1: Write down what your life was like.

Step 2: Write down how you met Jesus

Step 3: Write down how your life has changed since meeting Jesus.

Write down your testimony and share it with someone!

We’ll all have different stories of how we come to meet Jesus.

But one thing will be the same; it’s Jesus who’s saved us.

It’s Jesus who died to save us; it’s Jesus who rose from the dead.

And so our testimony must be ultimately about honouring Jesus.

Our testimony must honour Jesus as the Saviour of the world.

Like the Samaritan woman and the Samaritan crowd, the goal of our testimonies must be to honour Jesus.

Because at the end of the day, as important as our testimonies may be about Jesus, it’s not about us, it’s about him and what he thinks about us.

What will be Jesus’ testimony about us?

One of the most common sayings that Jesus had was, “I tell you the truth”

Jesus knows the truth about us.

He knows about the true state of our relationship with him.

Even though we can lie to others about our relationship with him, we can’t lie to him.

He knows the truth.

Often in Jesus’ teaching, he has a stern warning against those who thinks that they’re in a relationship with Jesus but aren’t.

To some of those who had been performing miracles in Jesus name, Jesus will one day say, “Then I will announce to them, ‘I never knew you. Depart from me, you lawbreakers!’

That would be a horrifying thing to hear Jesus say about anyone who thought that they knew Jesus.

That would be a terrifying testimony to hear Jesus say: “I never knew you”

But that would be the testimony Jesus have of anyone who might welcome him but does not honour him as the Saviour of the world.

Whether you’re new to our Church or whether you’ve been to Church for many years, don’t be someone who welcomes Jesus but doesn’t honour him.

Don’t live with the lie that says that you’re a Christian if you’re not honouring Jesus in your life.

Will you honour Jesus with what he’s given you?

He’s given you your health, your family, your wealth.

Will you honour him with all that he’s given you?

Will people testify that Jesus is the Saviour of the world by what you say and do?

Or will people fail to see Jesus in your life because you have failed to honour him?

Here are the three questions for this week:

What’s my testimony about Jesus? How my story about how I’ve met with Jesus?

How does my life honour Jesus?

How might I fall into the danger of welcoming but not honouring Jesus?

One of my favourite hymns is May the Mind of Christ my Saviour

The last verse goes like this:

“May His beauty rest upon me

As I seek the lost to win,

And may they forget the channel,

Seeing only Him.”

May the lost only see Jesus in our lives.

To Jesus be all glory.

Let’s pray, “Father, we thank you for sending Jesus as the Saviour of the world. We thank you that it’s through him that we are saved from the certainty of death. It’s through him that we can come to know you. Father, forgive us for our lack of faith. Please help us to trust in you and give honour you by honouring Jesus in our words and deeds. By your Spirit, give us the right words to say so that others, too, will testify that Jesus is the Saviour of the world, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
