Eating and drinking for God’s glory

Passage: 1 Cor 10:14-11:1

Big Idea: Don’t provoke God; instead glorify God in everything by trying to please everyone, that they may be saved.

Don’t provoke God with your meals

Eat your meals for the other person’s good

Glorify God whenever you eat or drink or whatever you do

When I was growing up, one of the most challenging movies I’ve seen was the Joy Luck Club.

It’s a movie that follows the life of Chinese women who immigrated to American and their girls who grew up in America.

In one scene, in modern America, a young Chinese woman brought home a white American boyfriend to dinner.

It’s the first time that the parents met this American boyfriend and so it was an important dinner.

However, the young Chinese woman had forgotten to tell her American boyfriend a few important rules when you come to a Chinese home for dinner.

Rule one: Don’t refill your wine when no one else has finished theirs yet.

Rule two: Don’t take multiple spoonfuls of your favourite dish and pile them on your plate. Share!

Rule three: Probably the most important one. When your potential future mother in law says that her best dish is not good, don’t agree with her.

It’s a trap; she’s lying

Don’t agree with her and improve the dish by pouring soya sauce over it.

Unsurprisingly, even though the American boyfriend thought little of it, his actions were disastrous and provoked a lot of tension in the girl’s household!

Have you been in situations like that?

Have you been in dinners where how somebody ate had caused a lot of damage?

In today’s passage, we’ll see a situation in the Corinthian Church where a person’s choice of how they eat can have significant effects on the lives of others.

Today we’re going to see that there are two ways to eat.

One type of eating insults God and provokes him, the other type of eating pleases God and glorifies him.

We’re going to see that the way we eat reveals what’s important in our hearts.

Does my life glorify God, as it should, or does it provoke God?

If you have your Bible with you, please open up to 1 Corinthians 10 beginning from verse 14.

We’re going to see three things:

Don’t provoke God with your meals

Eat your meals for the other person’s good

Glorify God whenever you eat or drink or whatever you do

Before we do, let’s pray, “The earth is the Lord’s and everything in it. Father, we thank you for all your provisions for us. We thank you for food and we thank you for your word. Father, help us now to listen to what your word says about food. Amen.”

Don’t provoke God with your meals

Firstly, don’t provoke God with your meals.

How do you provoke God with your meals?

You provoke God with your meals when you celebrate idols with your meals.

Look with me at 1 Corinthians 10:14, “So then, my dear friends, flee from idolatry.”

We’re told to flee from idolatry.

It’s like a video that I saw from a documentary video by David Attenborough.

It shows a young iguana walking carefully across the rocks when suddenly, a snake appeared next to it, wanting to eat it.

The iguana starts running very quickly and then out of nowhere, dozens of snakes appeared to get the young iguana.

And then all of a sudden, several snakes wrapped themselves around it.

That’s it; it’s gone!

But no, it escaped and continued to run even as the snakes kept chasing it.

And it escaped to safety; it fled from danger!

That’s what Paul tells his dear friends to do.

Just like the young iguana, flee from the danger of idolatry.

That’s a sensible thing to do, to flee from the danger of idolatry and as sensible people, Paul expects the Corinthian Christians to understand it.

He then uses the Lord’s Supper as an example.

Look with me at verses 16-17, “The cup of blessing that we bless, is it not a sharing in the blood of Christ? The bread that we break, is it not a sharing in the body of Christ? 17 Because there is one bread, we who are many are one body, since all of us share the one bread.”

Just before he died on the cross, Jesus instituted the Lord’s Supper for his followers in remembrance of what he’s done for us.

Since the New Testament times, Christians have come together to share the meal we commonly called the Lord’s Supper or the Holy Communion to remember what Jesus has done for us.

It’s a meal that we share with the Lord Jesus, in the presence of the Lord Jesus.

When we bless the cup of blessing, we’re not blessing to make it more blessed.

When we bless the cup, we bless God and give thanks to God for what he’s done for us in Jesus.

We give thanks that we share in the blood of Christ, that is, we identify as a people who had been saved by the blood of Christ.

When the Israelites have a meal and break bread, they literally break one bread into pieces.

When Christians break bread as we have this meal, it shows that we are one body, namely, the body of Christ.

It shows that we’re united in Christ.

At the head of this meal is God himself.

God himself is the host of the meal; he’s the one who paid for this meal with the death of his Son, Jesus.

It’s a meal that symbolises what God has done for us in Jesus.

And so, we take this meal seriously.

This is a meal that we eat, not only in front of each other’s presence but in the presence of God.

It’s a meal that believers of Jesus participate in to show that we belong to Jesus.

And so, when we have it at Church, we want people to take it seriously as well.

I look forward to the day when we can come back in person to have this meal together again.

It’s an important meal that we share in God’s presence to remember Jesus.

That’s what the Lord’s supper signifies.

If that’s how significant the Lord’s Supper is, how can a believer of the Lord Jesus have this meal in God’s presence at Church,

Then go off and have another meal in an idol’s presence in the temple?

Paul’s saying that if you go and have a meal that celebrates another god, an idol,

You’re participating in that religion.

You’re involved with worship of another god.

When you have a meal at the temple, you’re having a meal where the other god is the host.

Is Paul saying then that the food sacrificed to idol is anything, or that an idol is anything?

Look with me at Paul’s answer in verse 20, “No, but I do say that what they sacrifice, they sacrifice to demons and not to God. I do not want you to be participants with demons!”

Paul is not saying that there are idols who are alternative gods to the real God.

However, Paul is saying that when someone worship idols, they are not merely bowing down before woods and stones,

That they are bowing to nothing.

Behind the wooden idols, behind the intricate designs on stones, behind the meticulously detailed drawings, are dark, invisible, spiritual forces.

There are demons behind the worship of idols and Paul doesn’t want Christians to be anywhere near worship inspired by demons.

Look with me at verse 21, “21 You cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons. You cannot share in the Lord’s table and the table of demons.”

The worship of the one true God is mutually exclusive of the worship of demons.

Don’t have a meal where demons are celebrated.

If you do, you will only provoke God.

Verse 22, “Or are we provoking the Lord to jealousy? Are we stronger than he?”

I like multiculturalism; I love it!

I enjoy meeting people from other nations, trying their food (though I confess I do try some more than others!)

I love seeing how people from all around the world celebrate different cultures.

However, as Christians, we need to be discerning about joining any of the festivals of other religions.

While it might be a long time before we have massive religious celebrations due to our current COVID restrictions,

It’s worth us thinking about it before we come back together again.

While there are some great things about seeing different cultures, there may be aspects of religious festivals that we should avoid.

For example, Christians should avoid joining explicitly religious festivities in Buddhist temples or Hindu temples, for example.

There might be certain religious rituals that people have around meals that we should avoid, especially if we don’t understand its significance.

We need to take care not to share in a meal that celebrates another god rather than the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ.

Don’t provoke God to jealousy. Are we stronger than he?

Besides eating food sacrificed to an idol, what else can we do that might provoke God to jealousy?

Talk with the person next to you and text your answer in by whatsapp, wechat or Zoom.

Besides eating food sacrificed to an idol, what else can we do that might provoke God to jealousy?

You have twenty seconds.

Someone once said, ‘They must not try how far they can go, but how far they can fly.’

Don’t see how far you can go near the temptation; like the young iguana fleeing from the snakes, see how far you can fly away from temptation.

Don’t provoke the Lord to jealousy.

Stay as far away from worshipping idols as possible, because you love God and not idols.

Don’t provoke God with your meals.

But when you do eat, eat your meals for the other person’s good.

Eat your meals for the other person’s good

Look with me at verses 23-24, ““Everything is permissible,”[k] but not everything is beneficial. “Everything is permissible,”[l] but not everything builds up. 24 No one is to seek his own good, but the good of the other person.

Eat your meals for the other person’s sake, for the other person’s good.

On Thursday, in the final event of the men’s decathlon, Ashley Moloney was running for a medal.

His fellow team mate, Cedric Dubler was also in the decathlon.

However, knowing that he won’t make it, Dubler decided to put all his energy to encouraging Moloney to win the race.

At the end, Moloney got the bronze medal, which was also Australia’s first ever medal in an Olympic Decathlon.

Dubler could’ve finished his race earlier but spent his last lap on the decathlon for Moloney’s good.

Now I don’t know whether Dubler was a Christian or not, but what he did was an example of what it means for us to not to see our own good but the good of others.

Everything is permissible – there’s freedom in what we can do.

But not everything builds up – is what I’m about to do a good thing for others? Is what I’m about to do helpful?

That’s the mindset that Paul wants the Corinthians to have as they think about the food that they eat.

In ancient Corinth, there’s a very real possibility that the meat people buy in meat markets would’ve been offered to idols.

It’ll be impossible for Christians to know whether the meat has been offered to idols or not.

However, a Christian is free to have all types of meat.

They don’t need to raise questions about where the meat has come from.

Since “the earth is the Lord’s and all that is in it.”

The food doesn’t belong to idols but to God.

The food, in and of itself, is made by God.

When Christians eat their food, they’re thankful to God for it for God is one who ultimately provided for it.

The same freedom applies when the Christian goes to the home of an unbeliever.

When you’re invited to an unbeliever’s home, don’t ask questions about the origin of the food.

You don’t need to know whether it’s been offered to an idol or not.

Just eat what they give you.

To ask questions about origin of your dinner might be a little strange, and even a little rude.

While there might be a possibility that it could’ve been offered to idols, you don’t need to interrogate your host to find out.

You’re not doing anything wrong by not asking questions about your food.

However, if your host or someone at the dinner says to you, “This is food from a sacrifice”,

Then don’t eat it.

You do it for their sake and for their conscience’s sake.

As an unbeliever, they might assume that your Christian God is no different to any of the other gods,

And they’ll think that the Christian is inconsistent when he says that their God is the only God.

That is, they might assume that the Christian is not serious about what he believes.

On the other hand, if there’s a weak Christian standing by, they might think that since a strong Christian doesn’t take the threat of idolatry seriously,

Neither should they.

Even though in your mind, you’re not worshipping an idol,

Your act can unintentionally lead someone back into idol worship.

And so, rather than causing unnecessary offence, if someone tells you that the food has been sacrificed to idols, then you shouldn’t eat it.

Don’t lead anyone unintentionally to idol worship, whether it’s an unbeliever or a weak believer.

Don’t eat the food before you out of your concern for the other person.

Give up your rights to have good food.

Who know? They might ask you about it and you can explain to them why you’re different,

You can explain to them why your faith is important enough for you to limit your freedom for the sake of the other person.

As Paul says in verse 30, “If I partake with thanksgiving, why am I criticized because of something for which I give thanks?”

While many of those watching now may be from Christian homes, there might be times when we have dinner with friends and family members who do offer sacrifices to idols.

There might be some of us who would go to dinner where the food has been offered to idols.

If they give you the food without saying anything, then give thanks to God and eat the roast pork.

However, if they had a full ceremony involving the roast pork and they had offered it to an idol, along with incense.

Then they chop the pork into pieces and offered it to you, saying, “this has just been offered to the ancestors”,

Then you should politely decline and eat other food on the table.

You might eat the vegetables instead.

In my experience, most of the people won’t tell you about the origin of the food.

And it’s right to eat the food because God’s made it, not the idols, and not the ancestors.

However, for those times when they do, you must make a stand and choose to give up your freedom to eat the food,

Not for your sake but for the other person’s sake.

Glorify and honour God with what you eat.

If it doesn’t glorify God and it if can even remotely lead people to idol worship, then don’t eat.

Which takes us to our last point.

Glorify God whenever you eat or drink or whatever you do

Glorify God whenever you eat or drink or in whatever it is that you do!

Look with me at verses 31-32, “So, whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do everything for the glory of God. 32 Give no offense to Jews or Greeks or the church of God,”

Whatever you have decided to do, do it for the glory of God.

Do it so that God gets the honour and the praise.

Even though you have the freedom to do many things, don’t be offensive to the Jews or Greeks or the church of God.

Jews, Greeks or the church of God – that covers everybody!

Don’t offend anybody. Why not?

Verse 33, “33 just as I also try to please everyone in everything, not seeking my own benefit, but the benefit of many, so that they may be saved.”

You will limit your freedom, you will restrict your diet, you will give no offence to anybody and try to please everyone in everything, so that others might benefit.

So that others might be saved.

Go out of your way to please others, so that they might be saved.

Prior to the lockdown, we were planning to invite our new neighbours to our Church.

We were going to have a BBQ; we even had the brochures printed.

In our planning, we thought about the importance of not offending different cultural groups in our area.

We were aware that there might be some people from Hindi background and others from a Muslim background.

And so, we planned our food so that it’s acceptable to the widest spectrum of people as possible.

We want to go out of our way to welcome our neighbours so that God will be glorified.

We do everything for the glory of God.

That’s the goal of the past few chapters in 1 Corinthians.

Over the past few chapters, we’ve seen sexual immorality and idolatry, both of which God hate,

Both of which God hates.

Both sexual immorality and idolatry are the opposites of what it means to glorify God.

However, as believers of the Lord Jesus, we do everything for the glory of God.

And so, we will limit our freedom so that others won’t stumble, so that others won’t fall.

We want to please everyone in everything, not because we’re people pleasers but because we want to see others saved.

We want to please everyone in everything so that we can see the other people come to know Jesus Christ and grow in Him.

We want to please everyone in everything so that they in turn will glorify God.

Where do we learn to have that sort of selflessness?

How do we cultivate a desire to glorify God?

Look with me at the last verse, 11:1 “Imitate me, as I also imitate Christ.”

The Apostle Paul is a follower of Christ and so he follows Christ’s teaching; he follows in Christ’s footsteps.

He models his life after Christ; he imitates Christ.

And so, imitate Paul as he imitates Christ.

Study his life, learn from his words as he follows Christ.

Follow his example, as he follows Christ’s example.

You do that best as you read his account in Acts and as you read his letters in the New Testament.

Also, follow Christians leaders as they imitate Christ.

A good Christian leader doesn’t just tell people what they should do.

They practice what they preach, and they invite people to imitate their lives.

Whether you like it or not, if you’re a Christian leader, you will be an example to others to what it means to imitate Christ.

If you are a hypocrite, they will imitate your hypocrisy.

If you don’t practice what you preach, they won’t follow what you preach.

However, those who are faithful to God will look for leaders who do faithfully imitate Christ.

They will imitate the leader’s grace as the leader imitates Jesus’ grace.

They will imitate the leader’s selflessness as the leader imitates Jesus’ selflessness.

They will imitate the leader’s desire for God’s glory as the leader imitates Jesus’ desire for God’s glory.

On a Friday afternoon, there was a knock on the cathedral door.

The Archbishop elect, Kanishka Raffel was getting ready for his inauguration at the Archbishop.

The Archbishop opened the door and welcomed the stranger in.

The stranger was someone who was going through a difficult time and was living around the Cathedral.

The Archbishop elect asked, “Would you like a cup of coffee? Do you take sugar?”

And the Archbishop made a cup of coffee for this stranger to drink.

Four hours afterwards, the newly appointed Archbishop, the one with the highest office in the Diocese of Sydney, would give his first sermon as Archbishop on the topic of humility.

The Archbishop practised what he preached.

He rightly imitated Christ’s humility.

I think our diocese will do well to imitate our Archbishop as he imitates Christ.

Friends, that’s the secret to how we glory God.

We glorify God as we imitate Christ.

Be willing to give up your rights and your comforts for the good of others.

You do it not because you’re a nice person but because Jesus has saved you.

You do it because he’s saved you to be his and to follow his example.

You do it because Jesus wants them to be saved and so you want them to be saved.

Jesus gave up his rights so that you might be saved.

If you are his, you will have the same attitude.

You will give up your rights so that others might be saved by Jesus.

You will imitate Christ.

Friends, Don’t provoke God by being identified with idols and demons.

Flee from idolatry like the young iguana fled from the snakes.

Glorify God whenever you eat or drink and in whatever you do.

What does this mean for us?

Don’t provoke God but glorify God by imitating Jesus.

Fathers and mothers, be imitators of Christ at home.

Like Christ, give thanks to God in everything.

Start by giving God thanks every time you and your family have food.

Your children will imitate your thankfulness as you imitate Christ.

Workers, be imitators of Christ at work.

I hope your colleagues already know by now that you’re a Christian.

They’ll be watching you at work.

Show them what a genuine Christian looks like at work.

I’ve heard many wonderful testimonies of unbelievers being saved because of a believer’s testimony at work.

When they become believers too, they’ll imitate you at work.

Students, be imitators of Christ at school.

Just as Christ longs to see your friends saved, long to see your friends saved too.

Just this past week, I’ve heard a wonderful story about a young student at St George Girls who insisted her friend to read the Bible.

The friend obliged and became a Christian over time.

This friend eventually went to Bible college and has encouraged many people to go into all the world to tell people about Jesus.

This friend imitated her friend at school, as she imitated Christ.

How will you imitate Christ in your life?

How will your life be imitated as someone who imitates Christ?

Friends, here are the three questions for this week:

Are there areas in my life where I’m in danger of provoking God?

Are there areas in my life where I must lessen my offence to others, lest they stumble and fall?

How am I imitating Christ?

Friends, Don’t provoke God but glorify God by imitating Jesus.

Let’s pray,

“Father, thank you for sending Jesus to save an unworthy people like us. Forgive us when we’re saved but our lives still provoke you to jealousy, when our insistence on our rights have become stumbling blocks for other people. Please help us to do everything for your glory. Help us to seek to please everyone in every way, that they might saved. Father, by your Spirit, transform us into Christ’s likeness. Please incline our hearts to imitate Christ, that others might see how wonderful, how marvellous our Lord Jesus is, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
