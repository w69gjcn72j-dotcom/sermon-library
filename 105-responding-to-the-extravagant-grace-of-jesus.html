Intro

In England, as with many countries around the world, people have a football club that they belong to.

It might be the big clubs Chelsea or Manchester United or it might be the little-known ones like Queens Park or Clapham Rovers.

Often the supporters are fiercely loyal to their club, willing to support them through the thick and thin.

Many of these supporters live in the same area as their club and tie their identity closely to the club.

When their team is honoured, they’re honoured; when their team is shames, they’re shamed.

However, there are some supporters that switch their alliance depending on how successful their teams are.

There are often called Glory Supporters.

These glory supporters will support whichever team is winning.

However, when another team wins, they switch their allegiance to the new team.

They support whichever team is popular; they support whichever team gets the glory.

And so, they’re not particularly loyal to any one team; they’re just loyal to the winning team.

It’s not just in football that we see glory supporters.

We see them in other sports, in music, in politics and even in religions.

Some people will see which belief is the most popular among their friends, the one with the most glory, and they’ll simply follow along.

That’s what we’ll see in today’s passage.

In today’s passage, we’ll see that Jesus gets a large following.

However, throughout this passage, just like many other passages in the Bible and throughout the history of the Church, we have to ask: are these people really following Jesus?

Or are they just following the crowd?

Are these people loyal followers of Jesus or are they glory supporters?

If you have your Bible there, please open up to John 6:1-15.

We’ll see three things in this passage:

A large crowd attracts to Jesus

Extravagant response of Jesus

Don’t cheapen Jesus’ grace

As we read through the passage today, ask yourself the question:

Am I following Jesus or am I following the crowd?

Am I following the glorious Jesus or am I a glory supporter?

Before we look into it, let’s pray,

“Father, by your Spirit, please open our eyes and help us understand your word, that we may see the glorious Jesus. Amen.”

A large crowd attracts to Jesus

Please open your Bible with me to John 6, beginning from verse 1, “After this, Jesus crossed the Sea of Galilee (or Tiberias). 2 A huge crowd was following him because they saw the signs that he was performing by healing the sick.

There was a big crowd following Jesus because they’ve seen Jesus’ miracles on the sick.

There were people who were dead but whom Jesus raised to life.

There was a man who could not walk for 38 years but Jesus has raised him up to walk.

The crowd was fascinated by this young miracle worker and followed him, most likely to see what else he could do.

We’re told that there were about five thousand men, not including women and children.

It’s highly likely that there were up to four times the number of people in the crowd if we include the women and children.

It’ll be like filling up the Kogarah Oval full of people, or, it’ll be multiplying the number of people we have at Church today by four-hundred fold.

Imagine that, a gathering four-hundred times bigger than what we have at Church today.

That’s a lot of people!

Many people would call that a success!

They’ve done it! Thousands of people were following Jesus.

That’s what they wanted, wasn’t it? Jesus got the approval of thousands upon thousands of people.

That’s quite an accomplishment!

Isn’t that what we want to see in our Churches today?

Wouldn’t it be great to have people, thousands of people, coming to Church?

Wouldn’t it be incredible if our Church could fill the Kogarah stadium one day?

I’ve seen churches that are that big! It’s possible to have a Mega Church! They do exist!

And that’s often how we gauge the success of a Church; we gauge the success of a Church by the crowd that comes along.

However, Jesus’ focus wasn’t on the crowd.

Rather, Jesus’ focus was on the disciples.

Jesus went up on a mountain and sat down with his disciples.

Jesus’ focus was on the disciples; the 20,000 strong crowd just tagged along.

The Apostle John tells us that this happened around the Passover time.

The Passover was a festival the Jews celebrated to remember that God rescued the people from the land of Egypt, the land of slavery, over three thousand years ago.

Back then, God used his servant Moses to lead the Israelites out of Egypt, through the wilderness and into the Promised Land.

Jews have been celebrating God’s rescue at the Passover even to this very day.

In fact, this year, they celebrated Passover when we were celebrating Easter.

As the crowd was following Jesus around the time of the Passover, were they expecting God to do something extraordinary again through Jesus?

After all, Moses himself said in Deuteronomy 18:15, “The Lord your God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among you, from your brothers—it is to him you shall listen—”

Is Jesus the Prophet they’ve been waiting for?

I love how understated the passage describes Jesus, in verse 5, “So when Jesus looked up and noticed a huge crowd coming toward him, he asked Philip, “Where will we buy bread so that these people can eat?”

It sounded so casual! Jesus looked up and he noticed the 20,000 people coming toward him!

He calmly asked Philip, who came from the local town of Bethsaida, where they could buy bread.

Philip wasn’t concerned about where he could get the bread but how will he get the bread.

How can you find enough bread for 20,000 people?

Philip did a quick calculation in his head.

1 denarius was about one day’s wage back in Jesus’ days.

200 denarii is about two hundred days’ worth, which is approximately $57,000.

Philip said that even with 200 denarii, there wouldn’t be enough for everyone.

If this happened today, Philip would say, “Even if we can find $57,000 between us, that’s not enough to feed 20,000 people!”

A little nibble, maybe, but definitely not enough for a satisfying meal!

The problem was too big for them to handle! They simply couldn’t afford it.

Another disciple, Andrew, approached it from another angle.

Rather than looking at what they needed, he started by looking at what they have.

And all he had was the lunch that a boy or the boy’s mother packed: Five barley loaves and two fish.

Here’s what it looks like.

That’s not going to be enough to feed 20,000 people!

Jesus attracted a large crowd, but having a large crowd is not without its problems.

The first one they needed to tackle was to know how to feed them!

Even so, generally speaking, a lot of people enjoy being part of the crowd.

They enjoy the safety that comes from being with other people.

However, just because there was a large crowd following Jesus doesn’t mean that they were all disciples of Jesus.

They were following Jesus, not as disciples, but as the crowd.

And Jesus himself differentiated between the two.

His focus was on the disciple; the crowd was almost an after-thought.

Even today, we see the differentiation between the two, don’t we?

During special events, like Christmas and Easter, we often see a jump in visitors to Church, which looks great!

These special events often attract a crowd.

But how many come back the following week?

Usually, we experience a big drop in numbers the week after the big events.

The crowd can come and go but the disciple comes even when there’s no big events on.

And Jesus has always been more focussed on his disciples than the crowd that comes and goes..

Extravagant response of Jesus

Even so, Jesus has an extravagant response to the problem of how to feed this massive crowd.

After getting the people to sit on the grass, Jesus took the loaves, gave thanks, then distributed to all those who were seated.

He also did the same thing with the fish.

And the bread and the fish kept coming.

Jesus gave them as much fish and bread as they wanted.

He didn’t break the bread and the fish in microscopic pieces for the thousands of people share.

No, Jesus miraculously gave enough food for 5000 men as well as possibly 15,000 women and children.

It was an incredible miracle! Jesus created food for the crowd!

I still remember going to an all-you-can-eat-restaurant when I was child.

I couldn’t believe it! How can they possibly make so much food!

It was great! We would always come back full, usually too full, afterwards.

Jesus gave the crowd all they could eat and more!

After they had finished, they collected twelve basketfuls of leftovers.

What they had was far more than what 200 denarii or the modern equivalent of $57,000 could buy them.

That’s what God’s grace looks like!

When we say God’s grace, we refer to God’s extravagant and faithful kindness to unworthy people like us.

In Jesus, God has shown us his extravagant grace! He gives us what we are unworthy to receive.

As unworthy people, we have a great need and in Jesus, we have a great Saviour!

Whatever trouble we might think we’re in, no matter how far we’ve fallen, Jesus has more than enough to take care of us.

He’s shown his grace in the feeding of the 5000 men, possibly up to 20,000 people all together.

His grace is more than enough!

His grace is more than the gifts that he gives.

Later in this chapter, we see Jesus refers to himself as the Bread of Life.

The greatest gift that he gives us is himself.

Jesus says in verse 35, “I am the bread of life,” Jesus told them. “No one who comes to me will ever be hungry, and no one who believes in me will ever be thirsty again.”

Yes, it’s great that the crowd had the bread and the fish but sooner or later, they would grow hungry again.

However, those who trust in Jesus, those who goes to Jesus, those who believes in Jesus, will never be hungry or thirsty again.

It’s not that they won’t be physically hungry or thirsty again.

But they won’t be spiritually hungry or thirsty again; they will have true peace.

They will be truly satisfied.

That’s what we’re going to explore in the coming weeks.

But it all begins from this sign of Jesus feeding the 5000 men.

This miraculous sign will point to Jesus as the Bread of Life.

It points to Jesus as the One who is full of God’s grace.

He’s the one who gives extravagant kindness and faithfulness to the unworthy.

One such person who had experienced God’s grace is John Newton.

John Newton was a slave trader in the 1700’s who would capture slaves and sold them for a profit.

On one journey, he was in the middle of a massive storm and lost the lives of a few men who fell overboard.

He held on the wheel of the boat and prayed to God, “Lord have mercy on us.”

And The Lord did have mercy on him and calmed the storm.

But even better than that, the Lord had mercy on his soul.

After coming home, he began to study the Bible and spoke to various churches about what led him to become a Christian.

He even turned from being a slave trader to someone who actively worked towards abolishing it.

Towards the end of his life, he began to lose his memory but he could always remember two things,

“That I am a great sinner, and that Christ is a great Saviour.”

Jesus has lavished his grace on John Newton and he was a thoroughly changed man.

Jesus’ grace transforms us to be recipients of his grace.

It’s life-changing and it’s a marvel to behold!

Don’t cheapen Jesus’ grace

However, as wonderful as Jesus’ grace is, we, as fallen, woeful sinners, often cheapen his grace.

That’s what’s happened at the end of our passage.

Look with me at verse 14, “When the people saw the sign he had done, they said, “This truly is the Prophet who is to come into the world.”

The people probably had seen the parallel between Moses and Jesus and decided the best thing for Jesus was to be their king.

After all, Jesus was powerful since he could do all kinds of miracles.

He was also one of them, a Jew.

With his power, he could help the Jews fight against the Roman invaders.

In fact, the crowd was about to take Jesus by force to make him a king.

They wanted to make Jesus a king, even it was against his will!

Rather than being transformed into grateful recipients of Jesus’ grace,

The crowd wanted to manipulate Jesus and make him their king according to their will.

They understood Jesus, but only at a very superficial level.

That’s what makes them dangerous.

Billy Graham, the famous evangelist, once said that the problem with many of us is that we’re inoculated against Christianity.

What he meant was that many of us had a little dose of Christianity.

We understood Christianity at a very superficial level only and think that we know enough.

When we stop growing deeper in our knowledge of Christianity, we get all kinds of false impressions about Christianity.

It can lead us teaching a flawed understanding of Christianity; it can even lead us teaching a false gospel that’s completely different to the one the Bible teaches.

The crowd in Jesus’ days might have thought of Jesus as a prophet, a liberator, even a genuine miracle worker.

These might be true but it’s not the full picture.

Yes, Jesus is the Prophet like Moses but he’s so much more.

If they had listened to Moses more carefully, they would’ve listened to and obeyed Jesus.

But as we read on, we’d find out that they didn’t want to listen to what Jesus said about himself.

Even though they had followed Jesus and experienced Jesus’ grace and miracle for themselves, they rejected what Jesus has said about himself.

They would rather have Jesus the king according to their terms rather than following Jesus on his terms.

They’ve made a version of Jesus in their own image and rejected the real Jesus as a result.

Today, people have different opinions about Jesus as well.

They think of him as a religious figure, a miracle worker, a great moral teacher.

However, if that’s all they think Jesus is, then they’ve cheapened his grace.

Yes, he’s all these, and he’s so much more!

Jesus is the Bread of Life.

Jesus is the Son of God; Jesus is the Way to God.

Jesus is the Creator and Jesus is the Judge.

That’s what Jesus says about himself!

Do you believe that?

That’s the difference between someone who follows the crowd and someone who follows Jesus.

Someone who follows the crowd follows what the crowd thinks about Jesus.

Someone who follows Jesus obey what Jesus says about himself.

Those who follow Jesus receives Jesus’ grace and finds satisfaction in him.

These are the true followers, these are the true disciples of Jesus.

And Jesus focuses on his disciples, not the crowd.

In fact, Jesus actively has turned his back on the crowd.

Look with me at verse 15, “Therefore, when Jesus realized that they were about to come and take him by force to make him king, he withdrew again to the mountain by himself.”

When Jesus found out about their plan to forcefully make him king, Jesus went away from them.

He left the crowd behind because they didn’t have in their mind the things of God but the things of man.

Jesus intentionally chose to leave the 20,000 plus crowd who followed him behind.

Today, if we follow the footsteps of the crowd, satisfied with only a superficial understanding of Jesus, we too, would be in the danger of making a Jesus in our own image.

If we are to make a Jesus in our own image, then we’ve cheapened his grace for us, we would’ve insulted his grace, we would’ve insulted his extravagant kindness and faithfulness towards unworthy people like us.

In the end, Jesus would leave us behind.

Over the past week, the Archbishop of Sydney diocese, Archbishop Kanishka, along with over 70 clergy and lay people,

Represented us in the General Synod of Australia.

This is like a parliament of all the Anglican Churches in Australia.

Archbishop Kanishka spoke about the need for our Church to hold the historic view of marriage between a man and a woman.

This is what the Bible teaches; this is what Jesus teaches.

Here’s a quote from Archbishop Kanishka, “I also said in my speech that the historic Anglican view may be a minority position in our community already, or in the future. But across time and place, it has often been the case that the biblical view of a matter has been different to the majority culture.”

Even though it’s not a view that the crowd have, we still have to follow Jesus’ view.

We’re followers of Jesus and we follow his teaching, even if the crowd doesn’t.

Most of the laity and the clergy agreed with Kanishka.

However, very sadly, most of the bishops in Australia didn’t agree.

Our prayer is that these bishops will repent and turn back to what Jesus teaches.

That’ because this is the difference between a someone who is a follower of the crowd and a true disciple of Jesus.

Disciples of Jesus will follow Jesus and his teaching even if the crowd doesn’t.

It doesn’t matter how big the crowd is, at the end of the day, it’s only Jesus who matters.

And so, please pray for our Archbishop Kanishka along with the other bishops as they help us to focus on Jesus and what He teaches.

Pray that we won’t cheapen Jesus’ grace by following the crowd rather than following Jesus.

Pray that all of us will go deeper and find out what Jesus says about himself.

For Jesus’ concern is for the disciple who follows him rather than the size of the crowd.

What does this mean for us?

Do not cheapen the extravagant grace of Jesus.

When I was younger, I was a member of a Christian band and we wrote our songs.

We even recorded our own album; it’s called ONE.

It’s our first and our last album.

It took a lot of hard work to write the songs and to produce the album.

Since then, I understood why it’s so bad to illegally copy other people’s music.

Not only is it illegal; it shows that you don’t have respect for the work they’ve put in to produce the music.

People cheapen the musician’s music by distributing their music without paying for them.

Friends, don’t cheapen the extravagant grace of Jesus by only having a superficial knowledge of Jesus.

Don’t dishonour Jesus’ grace by treating it as nothing.

Go deep in your understanding of Jesus.

Join one of the Growth groups we have around Church.

If you’re not a Christian yet, join the Christianity Explored Group after Church today.

Don’t follow the crowd’s opinion of who Jesus is.

Discover the real Jesus by reading what he’s said about himself.

Pray to him; Tell him your needs

His grace is extravagant; he has more than enough grace for you.

Don’t make a Jesus in your own image.

Don’t cheapen the extravagant grace of Jesus.

Don’t be a glory supporter, following the wherever the crowd goes.

Rather, follow the glorious Jesus, wherever he might lead you.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How are you seeking to grow in your understanding of Jesus?

How has Jesus shown you his extravagant grace recently?

In what way are you tempted to cheapen his grace?

Friends, Jesus indeed is the great Saviour.

May we never cheapen the extravagant grace of Jesus.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we are great sinners, but Jesus is the great Saviour. Please forgive us when we haven’t come before Jesus with grateful hearts. Please open our eyes, that we might see the riches of your grace for us in Jesus Christ. Please keep us from cheapening the extravagant grace of Jesus Christ. Give us courage and strength, that we may follow Jesus, even if’s against what the crowd wishes. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
