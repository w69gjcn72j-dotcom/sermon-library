Inexcusable in God’s Righteous Judgement

Romans 2:1-16 · Sunday 12 July 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah · Adults Home Group

Big Idea: God’s judgment is perfectly fair — He judges by truth, by works, and by light — so no one can plead “I’m a good person.” Our only escape is Jesus.

Aim: by the end, members can (1) explain why respectable morality cannot survive God’s judgment; (2) name where they judge others while excusing themselves; (3) rest in Christ alone — and long for friends and family to meet Him as Saviour, not Judge.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, this sheet · about 60-75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • 1. Share a time you criticised someone for something you also do — complaining about people glued to their phones... while glued to yours. Why are other people’s faults so much easier to see?
  • 2. Suppose every private verdict you’ve passed on others this year were played aloud to the people concerned. How would you fare when measured by your own standards?

Read — Romans 2:1-16 (CSB)

1 Therefore, every one of you who judges is without excuse. For when you judge another, you condemn yourself, since you, the judge, do the same things. 2 Now we know that God’s judgment on those who do such things is based on the truth. 3 Do you think—anyone of you who judges those who do such things yet do the same—that you will escape God’s judgment? 4 Or do you despise the riches of his kindness, restraint, and patience, not recognizing that God’s kindness is intended to lead you to repentance? 5 Because of your hardened and unrepentant heart you are storing up wrath for yourself in the day of wrath, when God’s righteous judgment is revealed.

6 He will repay each one according to his works: 7 eternal life to those who by persistence in doing good seek glory, honor, and immortality; 8 but wrath and anger to those who are self-seeking and disobey the truth while obeying unrighteousness. 9 There will be affliction and distress for every human being who does evil, first to the Jew, and also to the Greek; 10 but glory, honor, and peace for everyone who does what is good, first to the Jew, and also to the Greek. 11 For there is no favoritism with God.

12 For all who sin without the law will also perish without the law, and all who sin under the law will be judged by the law. 13 For the hearers of the law are not righteous before God, but the doers of the law will be justified. 14 So, when Gentiles, who do not by nature have the law, do what the law demands, they are a law to themselves even though they do not have the law. 15 They show that the work of the law is written on their hearts. Their consciences confirm this. Their competing thoughts either accuse or even excuse them 16 on the day when God judges what people have kept secret, according to my gospel through Christ Jesus.

Context

Paul has just shown a world running from God (Romans 1:18-32) — and it’s ugly. Now he turns to the person clapping along: the respectable, moral person who thinks, “Well, I’m not like that.” In Paul’s day that was especially the religious Jew. Paul’s point: moral and immoral alike stand guilty before God, so everyone needs the gospel.

Discuss

  1. Read verses 1-3 again. What does Paul say happens every time we pass judgment on someone else — and why does it leave us “without excuse”?
  2. Verse 2: God’s judgment is “based on the truth” — on reality, motives included, not appearances. Why is that bad news for someone whose life looks impressive from the outside?
  3. Verse 4: what is God’s kindness meant to lead us to? How do people quietly reason, “God hasn’t punished me, so He must be satisfied with me”?
  4. Verses 6-11: God “will repay each one according to his works.” Is Paul contradicting salvation by faith? How do faith and works relate? (How do you know a tree is an apple tree?)
  5. Verses 12-15: how can God justly judge those who never received the law or heard the gospel? What has He given every person?
  6. Verse 16: God will judge “what people have kept secret.” Why is the hidden life the true measure of who we are?
  7. We “rename” our sins: “I’m just being honest”, “righteous anger”, “sharing a prayer point”. Which renamings are you most prone to?
  8. If church attendance and decency cannot save, what is our only hope? How does Romans 2 make Romans 1:16-17 — the gospel as God’s power to save everyone who believes — even sweeter?

Live It Out — KOG 3·2·1·0

God’s kindness is meant to move hearts — starting with ours. Keep the rhythm going this week:

  • PRAY for 3 people — colleagues, neighbours, family members — especially those who’d say “I’m a good person; I’ll be fine.” Write their names down tonight.
  • CONNECT with 2 of them this week — a coffee, a meal, a phone call — genuine interest, no agenda.
  • INVITE 1 of them to church or to your home group — God’s kindness often reaches people through an ordinary invitation.
  • 0 left unloved — no newcomer stands alone at morning tea; no neighbour in your street is invisible.

Weekly challenge: each time you catch yourself passing judgment this week — aloud or in your head — stop and pray for that person instead. One prayer per almost-judgment.

Memory verse: “God’s kindness is intended to lead you to repentance.” — Romans 2:4 (CSB)

Pray

  • Thank Jesus that He faced the judgment we deserve, so we can meet Him now as Saviour and not later as Judge.
  • Pray by name for your 3 people — that God’s kindness would lead them to repentance and faith.
  • Ask God to make us honest about our own hearts — quick to repent, slow to judge, glad to point people to Jesus.

Leader’s Notes

Tone: judgment is a weighty theme, so keep pointing joyfully to Jesus — Romans 2 makes the gospel land as good news. Guests or not-yet-Christians may be present: never corner anyone. The invitation is “meet Jesus as Saviour now”, not “try harder.”

  • Q2: “based on the truth” = God judges reality — motives and secrets included — not the curated exterior. That dismantles the “I look fine” defence.
  • Q4: we are saved by faith and judged by works — works are the fruit that reveal the tree. We change because we’re saved, not to get saved.
  • Q5: everyone has a conscience and a God-given moral code (vv.14-15), and nobody keeps even their own standards — so God’s judgment is fair.
  • Q8: the only hope is Jesus. Sunday’s big line: “Either we face Him as Saviour now, or we face Him as Judge later.”
