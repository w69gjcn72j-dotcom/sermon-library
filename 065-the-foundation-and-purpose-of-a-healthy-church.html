The Foundation and Purpose of a Healthy Church

Matthew 16:13-20 · 2022
Big IdeaThe church is built by Christ on the confession that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of the living God.

St Paul's Kogarah Anglican Church Sermon 22-5-2022

Topic: The foundation and purpose for a healthy church

Passage: Matthew 16:13-20

Introduction

Today I want to share with you a message on the topic of "church".

What exactly is a "church"?

Over the course of its two thousand years of history,

there’s been many misunderstandings about the church.

Some have thought that the church was a building,

Others have thought of it as a cultural organisation,

a traditional organisation or a religious organization.

Some have thought of the Church as what the Roman Catholic Church had established in the Middle Ages, namely, the Christendom.

A Pope met Thomas Aquinas, a famous theologian at that time.

The Pope proudly said, "Look at how rich our church is now!

Who dares to say that we have neither gold or silver?"

Aquinas answered, somewhat sadly: “Nor can we now say, “In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, I tell you, get up and walk!”

If any denomination or church deviates from the foundation and goal established by God, it will lose God’s presence and will be of no use to God.

Indeed, it will even lose the meaning of its existence.

To be precise and concise: the church is not a church building nor any man-made organisation.

Rather, the Church is God's will from time immemorial, his people whom he has both chosen and called to belong to him.

Here’s a brief overview:

As recorded in Genesis, the beginning of the Bible, God created the heavens and the earth, and all things in them.

He even created human beings in His image.

He wanted humanity to be His people.

However, the first ancestor sinned and was expelled from the Garden of Eden.

God prepared His plan of redemption, called Abraham to follow God by faith, promised his descendants the Israelites to become God’s chosen people.

God even prepared one of the descendants of David to sit on the throne forever, as God’s anointed Lord and Saviour, namely the Messiah.

When the time was fulfilled, God sent his one and only Son into the world, namely Jesus.

It’s Jesus who will be “the Messiah”, which in Greek is translated “the Christ”.

Through the death and resurrection of Jesus, God has redeemed the people of the world by paying for their sins, so that whoever believes in Jesus the Lord and Saviour will not suffer perish, but will have eternal life.

Not only are they saved, but they also become a member of the church.

Now, let’s consider the foundations of the church and her goals.

The foundation of the church is the community that confesses Jesus as the Saviour, Lord and Christ (Matt. 16:15-18)

Jesus asked his disciples a question about his identity at Caesarea Philippi, to test their knowledge of him.

The disciples may have identified Jesus as the Messiah in the past, but their expectation of the Messiah was not very different from the rest of the Jews.

They expected the Messiah to be merely a prophet who rescues the Israelites and help them escape from the oppression of the enemy.

But by now, Peter's conviction that Jesus was the Messiah has grown significantly, and he called Jesus the ‘Christ’ (the Greek translation of the word ‘Messiah’)

He is God’s anointed Saviour and Lord, and furthermore, Peter called him the one and only Son of God.

Jesus went on to praise Peter's answer, affirming that it was a revelation from the heavenly Father.

Then Jesus declared that he would build "His Church" on the foundation of this confession of the Christ.

Therefore, we must understand the foundation and essence of the church.

She is not only a social organization, nor a religion, nor a cultural tradition.

Nor is she a building.

The church is the community that confesses Jesus as Christ the Lord and Saviour; she is the people whom God has established by the new covenant.

The church is a group driven by the Gospel to achieve its goals

When Christ said in Matthew 16:18 that he was going to build his church, he clearly had a special purpose in mind,

Our job is not to create goals for the church, but to understand the goals set by Christ and to achieve them.

Although the method of achieving the goal has changed from time to time, the essence of the goal will never change.

From a theological point of view, especially with regard to the doctrine of salvation and the doctrine of mission, the faith professed and preached by the Church is the "gospel” of Jesus Christ.

The Gospel also defines the goals of the church.

To put it simply, the goals of the Church are directed by the Gospel.

This "gospel" contains five elements, which are also the goals that every believer in the church needs to accomplish:

(1) A God must be honoured and worshiped

(2) A message must be believed

(3) A saviour must be received

(4) A life must be lived out

(5) A commission must be obeyed

There are two places in Jesus' teaching that summarize what the church "is" and what the church "does”, namely, the Great Commandment (Mt.22:37-40) and the Great Commission (Matt 28:19).

You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your mind...you shall love your neighbour as yourself. All the Law and the Prophets hang on these two commandments (Matt 22:37-40).

Go, therefore, and make disciples of all nations, baptising them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, 20 teaching them to observe everything I have commanded you... (Matt. 28:19-20).

Twenty-five years ago, I was led by God to serve in Hurstville Anglican Church, and I read Rick Warren’s Book: The Purpose Driven Church.

In the book, Rick Warren summarises the five goals for a healthy church using the Great Commandment and the Great Commission:

Goal 1: Love God with all your heart.

The way to accomplish this goal is to read the Bible daily, worship on Sundays, and sing hymns of praise.

Goal 2: Love your neighbour as yourself.

The way to accomplish this is through the care and fellowship of believers and their participation in small groups.

Goal 3: To make disciples.

The way to accomplish this is to preach the Gospel.

Goal 4: To baptise people.

The method to accomplish this is the nurture of new believers and to have baptism classes, so that people may belong to and become faithful members of the Church.

Goal 5: Teach obedience.

This is done through Bible studies, discipleship training, and actively engaging in serving.

As you follow these five goals faithfully, you will experience both the presence and the leadership of the Lord Jesus.

You will see many people who have not believed become those who have newly believed.

From there, you will see the newly believed mature and grow, as they devote themselves to serving in the church, and then lead others to become disciples of the Lord.

In this way, the church can grow healthily, and the glory of God can be manifested through the church community.

Obviously, it is the work of the Holy Spirit which adds to the number of those people who are being saved.

May God grant grace and strength to this congregation, so that you will be a lampstand for witnessing the Gospel in this area and to accomplish the mission and goals of the church.
