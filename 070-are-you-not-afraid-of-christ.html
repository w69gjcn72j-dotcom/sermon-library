Are You Not Afraid of Christ?

Matthew 28:1-10 · Easter 2023
Big IdeaDon't be frightened by the glorious risen Jesus; behold him and be gone to tell His gospel.

Don’t you even fear Jesus Christ?

Big Idea: Don’t be frightened by the glorious risen Jesus; behold him and be gone to tell His gospel.

Why aren’t we afraid of Jesus

Why we will be afraid of Jesus

Jesus tells his own, “Don’t be afraid”

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you for bringing us together as a Church on Easter Sunday. Please help us see Jesus even more clearly today. Take away any distractions and let us hear what you have to say to us about Jesus as we hear from your word. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

When Jesus was dying on the cross, there were two criminals who were also dying with him.

One on his right, and the other on his left.

One of the criminals yelled insults at Jesus saying “Aren’t you the Christ? Save yourself and us!”

But the other criminal answered, rebuking him back, “Don’t you even fear God, since we are undergoing the same punishment?”

He then says that the criminals got what they deserved but Jesus did nothing wrong.

“Don’t you even fear God?”

What a great question!

The criminal who said this knew that God is just and powerful and that God is to be feared.

He might not have understood perfectly who Jesus was in his relation to God, but he knew that Jesus was from God and that Jesus was going to his kingdom.

This man knew what it was to fear God and he told his fellow criminal off for insulting Jesus, for not fearing God.

“Don’t you even fear God?”

That’s a great reflective question we can ask ourselves when we’re tempted to do things contrary to God’s will.

“Don’t you even fear God?”

The criminal’s question on the cross still speaks to us today in the 21st century.

But can we ask the same question of Jesus?

“Don’t you even fear Jesus Christ?”

Since Jesus is God the Son, can we ask, “Don’t you even fear Jesus Christ?”

Is it right to be afraid of Jesus?

1. Why aren’t we afraid of Jesus?

We don’t usually think of Jesus as someone we’re afraid of, don’t we?

In Christmas, we see Jesus presented as a helpless baby, born in a manger.

At Easter, in some places, we see Jesus presented as someone helplessly dying on the cross.

In the Bible, we hear Jesus’ teaching people to love their enemies and to turn the other cheek when someone strikes you.

We see Jesus as someone gentle and loving.

He’s not going to hurt anybody; he’s not a threat to people.

Why would we be afraid of him?

Not many people I know are afraid of Jesus.

There are at least two reasons why people aren’t afraid of him:

1. They don’t know Jesus

They haven’t heard about Jesus; they don’t know that he exists.

And so, they’re not afraid of Jesus because they don’t even know he’s there.

2. They are overconfident about how much they know about Jesus.

They’ve heard the children’s stories; they’ve seen Jesus in the movies.

The Jesus presented there are always kind and harmless- that’s the Jesus they know.

They’re sure that they know all that they need about Jesus.

They will tell you that they know more about Jesus than the Christians.

They might see him as a good teacher and a historical person.

They see him as only one of many thousands of religious figures in the world.

To them, Jesus is not significant in the grand scheme of things.

Why would anyone be afraid of Jesus?

They’re not afraid of him because they are ignorant of his existence, or they are ignorant of his significance.

They don’t fear Jesus because they don’t really know him.

While there are many in the world who are ignorant of the existence of Jesus, many people around us are ignorant of his significance.

Many of those living around us, perhaps even many of us here, are in danger of being overconfident when it comes to knowing who Jesus is.

It’s a little bit like this.

A group of young men drove a powerful car into a garage.

They were laughing and making jokes when the owner of the garage, Aurelio, asked, “Where did you get that?”

They told the owner that they bashed up a guy and stole the car from him.

The garage owner punched the leader of the young man in the face and told him to get the car out of his garage.

Later on, as he was drinking a glass of whisky to calm his nerves, the father of the young man gave the garage owner a call.

The father, Viggo, was a powerful, underworld crime lord.

Viggo says, “I heard you struck my son”

Aurelio replies, “Yes, sir. I did.”

“And may I ask why?”

“Yeah, well, because he stole John Wick’s car, sir, and, uh, they killed his dog.”

“Oh” Viggo hangs up.

Viggo’s son insulted and attacked someone whom he thought was a nobody.

He was ignorant of who John Wick was.

But both Viggo and Aurelio knew who John Wick was and they were afraid, very afraid.

The very mention of John Wick’s name was enough to instill fear on these two hardened criminals.

If we get a glimpse of who Jesus truly is, we too, we be very much afraid.

Like Viggo’s son, we don’t fear Jesus at our own risk.

2. Why we will be afraid of Jesus

After Jesus rose from the dead, we see how people reacted to the news of his resurrection.

And a common reaction that we see over and over again is fear.

Open up your Bible with me to Matthew 28, beginning from verse 1, “After the Sabbath, as the first day of the week was dawning, Mary Magdalene and the other Mary went to view the tomb. 2 There was a violent earthquake, because an angel of the Lord descended from heaven and approached the tomb. He rolled back the stone and was sitting on it.”

In the older, more literal translations of the Bible, the word, “Behold” appears at the beginning of verse 2.

The word behold means “Watch out” “See here”

Matthew wants us to take special note of this.

There are four ‘Beholds’ in this passage.

Here in verse 2, he says, “Look, there was a violent earthquake because an the angel of the Lord descended from heaven.”

On Friday, Hien talked about how the creation was disturbed at the death of Jesus.

Here at the resurrection of Jesus, we see the creation disturbed again, this time by the coming of an angel of the Lord with news about Jesus.

The ground recognised the significance of this messenger from heaven, and it shook in his presence.

He was clearly a powerful figure.

In other accounts of the resurrection, some of the women worried about who would roll the stone away.

This angel however, rolled it back and used it as his seat.

He was like lightning, and his clothes were as white as snow.

He was powerful and he was bright.

The guards were so afraid of him that they shook and became like dead men.

They were there to guard the tomb of a dead man but they had now become like dead men themselves, because they were so afraid of the angel.

They weren’t afraid of the angel because it was something dark and evil.

It’s the other way around.

They were scared because the angel was, bright, good and powerful and they weren’t.

They’re like earthworms in the sun.

Earthworms are afraid of the sun because the sun can dry out its skin.

And so, they always try to move away from the sun and crawl their way back into the dark, damp, earth.

The two soldiers were like worms exposed to light.

The shrivelled and shook in the bright and glorious presence of the angel of God.

And it’s not just them.

When an angel appeared to the shepherds on the night Jesus was born, they were afraid too.

In fact, when angels appear to people, they almost always begin by saying, “Don’t be afraid”.

Instead of saying, “Hi, how are you?”, the angels often greet people by saying, “Do not be afraid”.

Their presence usually instils fear on people, and that’s on a good day, when they bring good news!

And these angels are but messengers and servants of Jesus.

When he was about to be captured, Jesus said he could ask God and he would send twelve legions of angels to save him.

There are different opinions, with some scholars saying that twelve legions would equate with 72,000 angels.

These obey their Lord, their Master, without question.

If you’re afraid of the angels, you will be afraid of Jesus.

Jesus says that on day, as the Son of Man, he will come back to earth along with the angels to judge the people of the world.

This is what he says in Matthew 13:41-43,

“41 The Son of Man will send out his angels, and they will gather from his kingdom all who cause sin and those guilty of lawlessness. 42 They will throw them into the blazing furnace where there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth. 43 Then the righteous will shine like the sun in their Father’s kingdom. Let anyone who has ears listen.”

On that day, Jesus will send his angels to throw all those who cause sin and guilty of lawlessness into the blazing furnace.

The angels will obey Jesus; they will make sure that no sinner escapes Jesus’ judgement.

We see a glimpse of the fear of sinners in Revelation 6, beginning from verse 15, “Then the kings of the earth, the nobles, the generals, the rich, the powerful, and every slave and free person hid in the caves and among the rocks of the mountains. 16 And they said to the mountains and to the rocks, “Fall on us and hide us from the face of the one seated on the throne and from the wrath of the Lamb, 17 because the great day of their[k] wrath has come! And who is able to stand?”

The soldiers on the day of Jesus’ resurrection had a glimpse of the glory of one angel and they shook with fear.

On that day, every sinner, whether they’re ignorant of Jesus’ existence or ignorant of Jesus’ significance, will fear Jesus.

Today, many people are afraid of being ‘cancelled’ in our society.

They’re attacked online and sadly, some have even been attacked physically by a mob who doesn’t even know them.

Other times, we might be afraid that if we talk about Jesus with our friends and relatives, we will be cancelled by them,

That we will lose our relationship with them.

I don’t know about you, but I often find it very hard to talk to some of my relatives about Jesus.

As Chinese, we want to keep the peace, and that often means avoiding topics that will upset the other person.

And if that topic is Jesus, we will be very much tempted to avoid it.

Instead of gladly and boldly talking about Jesus, we shrink of these opportunities.

But Jesus himself said, “For whoever is ashamed of me and my words in this adulterous and sinful generation, the Son of Man will also be ashamed of him when he comes in the glory of his Father with the holy angels.” Mark 8:38.

If we’re more afraid of what people think than what Jesus thinks, then Jesus will be ashamed of us in the presence of God the Father and his holy angels.

One of the most frightening words that a person will ever hear are these words of Jesus, “I never knew you”,

Because after he says this, the person will be taken by an angel of Jesus to eternal punishment.

There’s nothing more frightening than to hear that the Lord Jesus, the Saviour of the world, doesn’t know you.

But that’s what we can expect if we have ever sinned against God in our lives.

Whether we like it or not, whether we believe Jesus or not, will at one stage or another, be afraid of Jesus.

The question is whether you fear him now or you fear him when it’s too late.

3. Jesus tells his own, “Don’t be afraid”

Though we ought to be afraid of Jesus, the message from the angel to the women is one of comfort, “Do not be afraid”.

This was his message for them.

At the birth of Jesus, the angels greeted the Shepherds and Mary with the phrase, “Do not be afraid”.

Why? Because they bear good news!

And here, at the resurrection, his angel from the Lord also has Good News for these women at the tomb.

Look with me at verse 5-6, “The angel told the women, “Don’t be afraid, because I know you are looking for Jesus who was crucified. He is not here. For he has risen, just as he said. Come and see the place where he lay.”

Jesus is not at the tomb anymore because he’s not dead anymore.

He’s risen from the dead.

The crucified Jesus is now the risen Lord!

And it shouldn’t be a surprise for the,

He’s said it multiple times.

He’s said that he will die and he will rise again.

And it happened just as he’s said!

It wasn’t a spiritual resurrection where his spirit rose and went to God.

No, he literally and physically rose from the dead.

As the old song goes, “Death could not hold him down; for he is risen!”

Jesus has conquered the grave!

And the angel invites the women to come and see the grave.

Take a look! He’s not here! He’s risen!

Christianity is a faith that we invite people to see the evidence for themselves.

Come and see!

There’s nothing in the tomb! It’s empty!

Many great men have died, and people have built tombs and memorials for them.

But Christians haven’t built a tomb for Jesus because he’s not in the tomb.

He’s not dead, he will never die, because he’s risen!

Christians thank God for the empty tomb.

And Christians also thank God for the empty cross.

The crosses of our Church don’t have Jesus on it because Jesus has already died, and his body was placed in the tomb.

And the tomb is now empty because Jesus is risen!

After the angel told the women about the risen Christ, he told the women to be gone!

Look with me at verse 7, “Then go quickly and tell his disciples, ‘He has risen from the dead and indeed he is going ahead of you to Galilee; you will see him there.’ Listen, I have told you.”

He told the women to be gone, to get out of here and to tell the disciples that

Jesus has risen from dead.

Here, we have the second “Behold” in this passage.

In the older translations, it says, “Behold, he is going ahead of you to Galilee!”

Behold, take note of this, go to Galilee to meet Jesus.

Jesus will be meeting his disciples there.

And the angel finishes off with another “Behold”.

“Behold, I have told you”.

The angel of the Lord had given them a mission from Jesus.

Now, get going!

And these women obeyed! They ran to tell the news!

The place where the disciples were staying was probably not too far away.

It was most probably a sprint rather than a marathon.

However, someone met them while they were running.

It was from none other than Jesus himself!

This is the last “behold” in this passage.

It’s the most significant one too!

Jesus, the risen One, greeted them.

When they saw Jesus, they worshipped him.

They knew Him as their teacher, but they now know him as the One who has risen from the dead.

The right response of meeting Jesus is to worship the Risen Jesus.

Listen to what Jesus says to them in verse 10, “Do not be afraid. Go and tell my brothers to leave for Galilee, and they will see me there.”

Just like his angel, Jesus assured the women to not be afraid and he repeated what the angel had already told the women earlier on.

Now notice that in this passage, Jesus and the angel only gave the command to not be afraid to the women.

They didn’t give the same command to the guards.

This was a message specifically for Jesus’ own.

It was message of comfort for them.

They don’t need to be afraid, for they are with Jesus.

Jesus even calls his disciples his brothers.

They are family!

That’s the Good news of Christianity.

Jesus says to those who are his, “Do not be afraid”.

They don’t need to be afraid because Jesus has died for them.

Though they were stained with sin, Jesus’ death on the cross cleansed them. Jesus has now washed them so clean, so pure, that they can now join his family. Jesus is their Lord, but he is also their brother, and God, Jesus’ Father, is their Father.

If you belong to his family, you don’t need to be afraid.

However, this message of comfort is only for those who are Jesus’ own.

If you are not Jesus’ own, you have no such assurance.

If you don’t put your trust in Jesus, you’re not his brother and God is not your Father.

You remain on your own.

And you will face Jesus, the King of kings and Lord of lords and you will bow before him, whether you like it or not.

It matters little whether you live with little fear or lived in constant fear in this life, it’s nothing compare with the fear that you’ll live with for the rest of eternity.

Are you Jesus’ own? Do you call him brother and God your Father?

If you do call Jesus your brother and God your Father, then keep looking to Jesus, keep beholding him.

There were four beholds in today’s passage.

Matthew wants us to see the significance of who Jesus is and keeps highlighting key facts about Jesus, again and again.

If you do know Jesus, keep beholding his significance by reading the Bible.

Read about how good Jesus is.

Get to know him.

If you haven’t joined a Growth Group yet, join one!

If you want to find out more about Christianity, join one of our Christianity Explored groups.

But don’t stop at beholding Jesus. Be gone!

Get out there and share the gospel.

As a Church, we’ve been sharing the gospel through are SRE teachers in local schools, through our support of missionaries, through the Mobile Pantry, through KOGWorx at the Children centre, and other methods as well.

Don’t just stand there, go away and make disciples of all nations.

Do it quickly and don’t delay!

You might start with small steps first.

Some of us have helped with leafletting in the past and you that might be something that you want to help with first.

Over time, we want to keep working on different projects to go out, and continue to reach different people in our community and beyond.

Be gone, get out of here and tell the good news of Jesus!

Aren’t we even afraid of God? Aren’t we even afraid of Jesus?

If you’re not disturbed after hearing from Gods’ word, then you haven’t been listening to what Jesus’ been saying.

Don’t fall into the danger of being overconfident of ourselves and underestimating Jesus.

We foolishly underestimate Jesus to our own peril.

Jesus’ message of comfort to not be afraid is only for those who are his.

If we don’t know him, if we don’t see his significance, then we have every reason to be afraid.

And so, friends, this Easter, Behold the risen Jesus and be gone!

Don’t turn your eyes away from the glory of the Risen Jesus Christ.

He has conquered the grave, his cross is empty, his tomb is empty and he’s ascended to heaven, where he is seated at the right hand of the Father.

Behold the glorious, Risen Jesus.

And be gone. Go out there to tell the good news of Jesus.

On Friday, after the Good Friday service, I was walking in Kogarah and asked someone for direction.

I ended up praying for the person who had been going through a very difficult time.

Go out there! People need to hear the good news of Jesus!

Be gone!

If you haven’t been beholding Jesus, if you don’t know who Jesus is, but want to turn to him, now is a good time.

Make today, Easter Sunday 2023, the day when you turn to Jesus.

The way we turn to Jesus is to come to him in prayer.

I’m going to say a short prayer slowly.

If you want to turn to Jesus, then say the prayer with me in your heart.

“Dear Lord Jesus, thank you for coming down from heaven to save people like me. You see all the darkness in me and yet you still love me. Please forgive me for my sins against you. Please forgive me when I don’t give you the respect and the honour that belongs to you. Thank you for dying on the cross for my sins. Thank you for rising from the grave to show us you have conquered death. Please help me now to trust in you, to serve you and to love you, in all that I say and do, Amen.”
