It’s been a hot week, hasn’t it?

What do you do to cool off from the heat?

Who goes to the beach? The swimming pool? The shopping centre?

Who gets into water fights?

I remember a long time ago, in my other Church, the youth were throwing water balloons at each other because it was a hot day.

One of the youths (he’s a good boy and a good friend) came up from behind me threw one at me.

He started running, and someone gave me a water balloon and I started chasing him around Church, at full speed.

I can’t remember whether I got him or not, but I do remember that it was fun, and I think not many youths got home without getting hit by a water balloon that day.

Living the Christian life can be like trying to get through a water balloon fight without getting wet, can’t it?

As Christians, we know that there are things we should do and there are things that we shouldn’t do.

The Bible teaches us to follow Jesus in the way that we live, but the world screams at us to take a different path.

God wants us to be holy but the world wants us to be unholy.

How do we stay holy in an unholy world?

That’s what we’ll be looking at in today’s passage.

If you have your Bible with you, please turn to John chapter seventeen beginning from verse 9.

We’ll see three things in today’s passage:

Jesus prays that the Holy Father’s protects His own

Jesus prays that the Holy Father makes His own holy

Jesus sends His holy ones into to an unholy world

Before we begin, let’s pray, “Holy Father, thank you for rescuing us out of the world into the kingdom of your Son, our Lord Jesus Christ. Please take all worldly distractions away from us and incline our hearts to hear and obey your words. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

1. Jesus prays that the Holy Father’s protects His own

Please turn with me to chapter 17 verse 9, “I pray for them. I am not praying for the world but for those you have given me, because they are yours.”

This was the night before Jesus would die on the cross.

He knew that he would rise again from the dead and physically ascend back into heaven.

He also knew that his disciples will be facing trials and persecutions after he leaves.

And so, Jesus prays that his Father, the Holy Father, would protect them.

Now notice that He prays not for the world, but specifically for his disciples—those whom he’s chosen, and who themselves have chosen to follow him.

The term ‘the world’ refers to how we as the community of humanity rejected God’s way.

It’s a word used to describe God’s enemies.

God loves the world, and he’s sent his Own Son to save the world.

He loves the world, even while we were his enemies, but he loves those who have turned to Jesus even more.

Jesus says that these are God’s own, and God shows even more warmth and love.

It’s for God’s own that Jesus prays.

These disciples, these followers of Christ, would one day glorify Jesus.

Through them, the world will get a glimpse of how glorious Jesus is.

These disciples would glorify Jesus, which in turn glorifies God, but they needed protection from the world.

And so Jesus prays for their protection.

Look with me at verses 10-11, “Everything I have is yours, and everything you have is mine, and I am glorified in them. 11 I am no longer in the world, but they are in the world, and I am coming to you. Holy Father, protect them by your name that you have given me, so that they may be one as we are one.”

As parents, Jenny and I would take our children on different trips.

When they were young, we would organise everything for them.

Their clothes, their toiletries, their passports (if we were going overseas) and their transport.

As they grow older, we trusted that their school would look after them on excursions and camps.

And now as older teenagers and adults, they are beginning to take these journeys by themselves.

And we would wonder if they had enough money and whether they’ll get to the airport on time.

Once they get there, we wonder if they know what to do in an emergency.

Will they remember what we taught them? Will they be wise in the way that they behave? Will they do what we expect of them if we’re not there with them in person?

And so, as Christian parents, we would pray for them.

Similarly, Jesus was about to leave his disciples in a world that was hostile to Him, yet he entrusted them to the Father's care.

Jesus prays that God the Holy Father will protect them by his name, the name that God the Father had given to God the Son.

This brings us to a powerful concept: living under the name of God.

Jesus calls God the Holy Father; this is the only time in the gospel where God is referred as the Holy Father.

Throughout the Bible, we see that God is the Holy God, who is awesome in power.

There was a record of God’s appearance in the Old Testament where angels call out to one another, “Holy, holy holy is the Lord of the Armies, his glory fills the whole earth” (Isaiah 6:3)

God’s holiness is also a way to describe his utter uniqueness.

There is only one holy God; there is none like him.

God’s people are called to be holy as well.

They are to live lives according to God’s character and they are to worship God only.

That’s what it means to live under God’s name.

To live under God's name is to reflect His character in our actions.

Just as my children, who carries my surname, represent me and my family when they live their lives,

God’s people, who identifies with God’s name, represents God in the way we live our lives.

To be identified with the Holy God, to be seen as a child of God, is the greatest honour we can receive.

To live under the name of the Holy Father then, means embracing the honour of being part of His family and living in a way that brings glory to Him.

If we do live under God’s name, we will see unity.

We see this at the end of verse 11, “So that they may be one as we are one.”

Those who live under God’s name, lives under the name of Christ.

If a Church lives in the name of Christ, we will live and breathe the gospel of Jesus, we’ll see a profound unity.

We won’t be selfish but selfless; we’ll live for the good of others.

We’ll be one as the Father and the Son are one.

Jesus taught them how to live under God’s name while he was here on earth. Jesus guarded them, protected them by living according to God’s name.

He protected the Twelve apostles, all except one – the one who heard all about what it means to live under Jesus’ name and yet still refused to live under God’s name.

He heard the words of Jesus but failed to believe and live according to the words of Jesus.

He was Judas, the son of destruction.

And so, what does God’s protection look like?

It’ll be God’s people, living under God’s name, displaying a profound unity.

To live under God’s name is protection from the world.

How good it’ll be to be in a church where we live in unity, under God’s protection and under God’s name!

Now please notice that Jesus didn’t say these word to give us an ideal to strive for.

He didn’t even command his followers to live in unity, even though he does so in other passages.

Here, in this passage, Jesus prays that this will happen to us, that God the Father will protect us as we live under his name.

What we see here is what God’s people would look like, if God answers Jesus’s prayer of protection.

We often don’t think we need protection from the world.

Here in the West, we’re generally not in danger because we’re a Christian.

Quite the opposite, we can be Christian and still be quite comfortable.

However, someone once said (and this is not from the Bible),’ The greatest trick the Devil ever pulled was convincing the world he didn’t exist.’

It’s a quote from the movie The usual suspects.

There is some truth in that.

We are so much part of the world, living under the devil’s influence, and yet we don’t even know about it.

We can often talk about what our world (or at least our part of the world) thinks about politics, health, money and relationships.

But What does God think?

What does the Bible say about God’s thoughts on these?

And if we know what God wants, do we do anything about it?

Is it any wonder, then, that many countries in the west where we find comfortable Christians are also where Christianity have dropped in large numbers.

In our comfort, we might unknowingly drift away from the truth.

We need strong men and women who will humbly listen to God’s will, not the world’s.

And so, follow Jesus and pray for protection—not just for safety, but for the strength to live under Gods name and follow His ways.

For that’s how God protects us from the world.

2. Jesus prays that the Holy Father makes His own holy

Please open up to verse 13-14, “Now I am coming to you, and I speak these things in the world so that they may have my joy completed in them. 14 I have given them your word. The world hated them because they are not of the world, just as I am not of the world. “

Jesus spoke these words to His disciples, so that Jesus’ own joy would be complete in them.

Jesus prays for our good because it gives him joy.

You know what, not only does Jesus love us; he likes us.

He looks forward to us living faithfully under God’s name.

He likes us, as we put our trust in him, living holy lives in an unholy world.

The unholy world shuns the light of God’s holiness as darkness shuns light.

We know that the world hates holiness because it hated Jesus.

However, as bad as the world is, Jesus doesn’t ask God take us out of it. Instead, He prays for our protection from the evil one.

The evil one, that is the devil, will lie and try to tempt people away from God, as he had always done.

The devil wants the Christians to go back to his world, but they are no longer part of the devil’s world.

Look with me at verses 16-17, “They are not of the world, just as I am not of the world. Sanctify them by the truth; your word is truth.”

They are Jesus’ own and Jesus prays that God will make them holy by the truth.

Now you may wonder, how do we know a holy person when we see one?

I know some churches who focus on external symbols and rituals have people literally putting elaborate robes on the priest every Sunday.

Their priest stretches out their arms and people would put massive robes on them,

I also know of other Churches where the minister dresses just like everybody else, and sometimes, they don’t even wear shoes.

Here, we find ourselves somewhere in between.

But true holiness doesn’t come from what we wear; true holiness comes from God himself, who’s giving us his truth.

It’s by God’s truth that we live holy lives.

God has already shown us what it looks like in his word, the Bible.

God’s word is our compass, pointing towards a holy life.

By living according to His truth, we become set apart from the world, we become holy to God in an unholy world filled with lies.

How does God make us holy?

By revealing Himself through Jesus' words and by the guidance of the Holy Spirit, who led the disciples into all truth, remembering what Jesus had taught us and recording Jesus’ teachings in the words of the Bible

Friends, holiness doesn’t come by accident.

You can’t be holy if you don’t think, meditate and live out God’s word.

If we want to be holy, we have to immerse ourselves in God’s word, we have to breathe it, pray it, and live it out daily.

When we plan our lives or spend our resources, God’s word as our foundation, for that’s how God makes us holy.

Do you want to see God’s holiness in action?

You’ll see it in people who live according to His word.

Thats holiness lived out; that’s a community sanctified by God.

This isn’t just a strategy or a goal.

It’s Jesus’ prayer for us, that God will sanctify us by his word.

One of the joys we have in our weekly prayer meetings is seeing our brothers and sisters asking for prayer as they sought to live holy lives according to God’s word.

We had a brother who faced an uncertain future but chose to trust in God. Throughout his challenges, God answered his prayers in unexpected and wonderful ways—beyond what he could have imagined.

We thank God for him and others who have shared similar answers to prayer.

They weren’t praying for everything to be better; they were praying to be holy in an unholy world.

Remember, we can’t sanctify ourselves.

We need God himself to make us holy through His word.

We need to pray!

3. Jesus sends His holy ones into to an unholy world.

God didn’t make us holy to remain within the walls of our sanctuaries, but to be sent by Jesus into an unholy world carrying the message of God’s holiness.

Look with me at verse 18, “As you sent me into the world, I also have sent them into the world.”

Just as God sent His Son, Jesus now sends His disciples, and all the disciples after them, to continue this mission.

It’s a little bit like this, in moments of crisis, we instinctively seek those equipped to respond.

We look for Doctors or other first responders.

Imagine how bad it would be for a doctor to just idly watch while a patient is suffering from horrific injuries.

As believers, we are the spiritual first responders, entrusted with the truth of God.

We cannot stand idly by as the world suffers in sin.

We must act, speak, and spread the light of the Gospel, for it is our calling.

God forbid that we should ever be silent while the world rots away.

Jesus did not save us to remain comfortable in our spiritual enclaves.

For Jesus himself didn’t stay in his home in heaven but came down to save a people like us.

Verse 19, “I sanctify myself for them, so that they also may be sanctified by the truth.”

He sanctified Himself, stepping from the holiness of heaven into our broken world to offer salvation.

Jesus saved the disciples who were the first witnesses of what Jesus’ said and done.

They were sanctified by the truth.

They taught and wrote the New Testament of the Bible for us to come to know Jesus.

If you have believed in Jesus, you, too are sanctified by the truth to tell the gospel that the first apostles proclaimed.

As His followers, we are sanctified by this truth, called to continue the apostolic mission.

When we go into the unholy world to tell about Jesus, we show ourselves to be the spiritual descendant of the first apostles.

That’s what we mean when we say that we believe in the One, Holy, Catholic, and Apostolic Church, united by God’s word, as passed down to us by the first apostles.

We are not Roman Catholic, but we are the universal church, the true catholic church, committed to proclaiming God’s truth across time and space.

That’s what the word Catholic means: universal.

As we enter 2025 and beyond, let’s remind ourselves again our identity and mission.

To help us remember, I made this little hand action to help us.

It’s the Three, Two, One, zero.

Three stands for three questions:

Why do we gather as Church?

God gathered us to build and to nurture a nation which lives and breathes the Gospel of Jesus Christ.

How does God grow us as a Church?

God grows us as one body through servants of his word, who equips us to thrive in him, to love his own and to save the lost.

Where are we going as a Church?

God leads us home as we follow Jesus on the path of the cross, and he has commissioned us to be witnesses who lives to point people to Jesus. Here are six checkpoints along the way:

We want to grow to 500 households who live within a 10-minute radius from us in the next 10 years.

4/5 of us serving 5 hours each week.

One new fellowship / congregation once a year, in harmony with the current 9am service.

To meet in 3 meeting spaces for hosting multiple congregations at the same time (church, parish hall, school hall).

Meeting new people within two minutes of coming to Church and meeting the minister for a meal within three weeks of coming to Church.

My hope is that Jenny and I, Peter and Hanna, will get to meet up with you either in our homes or yours sometime in 2025.

Have three classrooms filled with KOGkids and one small hall filled with KOGYouth

Two: Two checkpoints to pray for.

One: The next one to invite to Church

Zero: We owe our God and his people our love.

Pleaser pray that God will move us forward as a church, just a little at a time, but always moving us forward towards where he wants to take us.

God’s made us holy by his word; let’s pray that God will protect us as Jesus send us into the unholy world with his message of holiness.

How do you stay holy in an unholy world? Only as God answers Jesus’ prayer.

Has God answered Jesus’ prayer in your lives?

Friends, Jesus, the Holy Son, prays that by his Word, the Holy Father will protect his disciples, make them holy and send them into an unholy world.

Let’s pray that God will answer Jesus’ prayer in our lives.

Let’s echo Jesus’ prayer in our own prayers.

One of the most beautiful prayers I’ve heard this year was Grace’s prayer.

Grace has been living at St Patrick’s Green for the past few years and members of our Church drive Grace to our Church every Sunday.

Grace often has trouble remembering our names, but she remembers how to pray.

Here’s a prayer that she prayed a few months ago:

1. She might love God and grow in her faith in Him

2. She might continue to obey Jesus’ commands in his word

3. She will help others to know God

Isn’t that great?

That’s a prayer that echoes Jesus’ prayer.

We’ll finish with that prayer later on.

But before then, here are the three questions for this week:

Why would Jesus pray for protection for his own people?

When was the last time you prayed for protection for being faithful?

What would a church whose prayer aligns with Jesus’ prayer look like?

Jesus, the Holy Son, prays that by his Word, the Holy Father will protect his disciples, make them holy and send them into an unholy world.

Let’s pray,

“Our Holy, heavenly Father,

We pray that we might love God and grow in our faith in Him

We pray that might continue to obey Jesus’ commands in his word

We pray that we will help others to know God

Hear our prayer for we pray In Jesus’ name, Amen.”
