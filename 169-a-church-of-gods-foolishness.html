A Church of God’s foolishness

Big Idea: Saints are called to boast not in a doomed wisdom but in Christ Crucified, the wisdom of God.

The Church of God

The Church divided

The Church’s boast

Intro:

Has anyone seen graffiti before?

They are writings or drawings that people spray paint across trains, cars or buildings, usually without the owner’s consent.

Thankfully, we don’t have graffiti on the Church building, though we do have some on the side fence.

One of the most famous graffiti is this one here.

In fact it is said to be the earliest and only known image of the Crucifixion.

It shows a human-like figure with the head of a donkey nailed to a cross.

It’s an anti-Christian graffiti etched into a plaster wall of slave school between 50AD and 250AD.

Beneath the cross is ancient Greek text that reads, 'Alexamenos worships God,'

It’s a way to mock a Christian called Alexamenos who worship a donkey crucified on the cross as a God.

I wonder how Alexamenos would’ve felt.

Would he be embarrassed by the graffiti that shows him worshipping a donkey?

This can happen today, too, can’t it, in different forms.

People often mock those who worship Jesus Christ as foolish, even evil members of the society who need to be re-educated or worse, gotten rid off?

How do we respond?

Are we sometimes embarrassed to talk about a God sent his Son to die on a cross some two thousand years ago?

Are we tempted to not talk about Jesus because it makes us look like a fool?

This isn’t a modern problem.

This was something that the first Churches had to face.

It was embarrassing to talk about Jesus in places where the first Churches gathered.

The message the first Christians shared sounded crazy to the ears of ancient people.

In today’s passage, we’re going to see how a Church has struggled with a gospel that appeared to be foolishness to the world around them.

And we’re going to see how the Apostle Paul deals with their struggles.

We’re going to spend some time over the coming months looking into the Corinthian Church by looking into the first letter to the Corinthians.

It’s a Church loved by God by it’s also a Church with many, many issues.

We’re going to cover around one chapter a week and so I highly recommend you join a Bible study where you can discuss on issues not covered by the talks.

Of course, I would love you to read the chapters at home, preferably ahead, so that you can think through the various issues before coming together on Sundays.

Today, we’re going to go through 1 Corinthians chapter 1.

We’ll see three things:

The Church of God

The Church divided

The Church’s boast

Before we look into chapter 1, let’s pray.

“Father, our Lord and our God, please by your Spirit open hearts so that we may receive your word by faith, and that we might be transformed into the likeness of your Son our Lord Jesus Christ, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

The Church of God

If you have your Bible with you, please open up to 1 Corinthians chapter 1.

The ancient city of Corinth was one of the most important cities in the Roman Empire.

It’s situated on a narrow neck of land connecting the Peloponnese to the south with the rest of Greece to the north.

It also separated two gulfs of the Aegean Sea and the Ionian Sea.

There was only a short distance of nine kilometres between the two seas.

Today, there’s a canal that’s built between the two seas to allow ships to sail through.

A smaller version of the Suez canal that was blocked a few weeks earlier.

Being at the centre of two such important trade routes had really benefitted Corinth economically.

It was a prosperous city, a cosmopolitan city, with many different religions and many skilled speakers giving talks and sharing ideas.

It was also a city known for worshipping idols, sexually immorality and greed.

The Apostle Paul started a Church in this city and wrote to this Church after he went to other places to start new Churches.

Look with me at 1 Corinthians chapter one, beginning from verse 1,

“Paul, called as an apostle of Christ Jesus by God’s will, and Sosthenes our brother:

2 To the church of God at Corinth, to those sanctified in Christ Jesus, called as saints, with all those in every place who call on the name of Jesus Christ our Lord—both their Lord and ours.”

As Christians in this sophisticated city, Paul reminds them that they are the Church of God at Corinth and they are sanctified, made holy, in Christ Jesus.

He calls the Corinthian Christians saints, that is, the holy ones.

To be holy means to be someone set apart for a purpose.

In the case of the Corinthian Christians, they’re holy to God, they’re set apart for God.

It’s like this.

In our household, we wear very similar clothes.

Sometimes, we have T-shirts, hoodies that look the same.

And so, we often get it mixed up.

In fact, on Friday, I wore Ezekiel’s new T-shirt because it was in my pile.

Before that, Caitlyn wore it.

I don’t think Ezekiel even had a chance to wear it yet!

And so, Ezekiel has asked me to write his name on it.

It’s his T-shirt; it’s set apart for him; it’s holy for Ezekiel.

When the Bible says that Christians are saints or holy ones, it doesn’t mean that we’re perfect or sinless.

Rather, it means that we’re set apart for God, we’re holy for God, we belong to God.

One of the first thing that Paul says to the Corinthian Church is that they’re holy, they’re saints.

Now if you’ve ever had a chance to read the rest of 1 Corinthians, or even the rest of this chapter, you know that Church at Corinth is not a perfect Church.

Far from it!

It’s a messy Church with scandals and divisions.

It’s not an exemplary, model Church and yet, at the same time, it’s what church is like.

The Bible is here not describing an ideal Church but it’s giving a very honest, realistic account of a messy Church.

As we read on, the Apostle Paul will address the different challenges that the Church will face.

But here at the beginning, he wants to remind the Corinthians that they are a holy church, that they’re saints.

And they’re saints because of the Lord Jesus.

It’s only because of the Lord Jesus that they’re holy.

In fact, Jesus is mentioned many times in the introduction to 1 Corinthians.

Here’s the first question for today:

How many times was Jesus mentioned in the first nine verses of 1 Corinthians?

Go through the first nine verses and text me your answer via Wechat or whatsapp.

If you’re watching from home, you can send me your answers too.

How many times is Jesus mentioned in the first nine verses of 1 Corinthians?

You have 20 seconds.

In these nine verses, Jesus is mentioned 10 times.

The fact that the Corinthian Church is called the Church of God and the Corinthian Christians are called saints is all because of Jesus.

Jesus is the reason why they’re the Holy Church of God.

It’s Jesus who made them holy.

Jesus made them holy when they believed in the testimony of Jesus, the good news about Jesus.

But more than that, as believers of the good news about Jesus, God gave them spiritual gifts as they eagerly wait for the revelation of our Lord Jesus Christ.

They haven’t seen Jesus yet, but they will and God’s given them spiritual gifts to help them live as Christians as they wait for Jesus to come.

But wait, there’s more!

God himself will strengthen them to the end, so that they will be blameless in the day of our Lord Jesus Christ.

God will give them the strength to last until that final day, to keep them blameless for that day.

That’s the sure hope that Christians everywhere have, all those in every place who call on the name of Jesus Christ our Lord.

We have the hope that God will keep his people, his saints, holy and blameless until the day of Jesus Christ.

We have the hope of being holy and in fellowship with Jesus because God is faithful.

He’s said it; he’ll make it happen.

A few weeks ago, a friend told me that if there’s ever a perfect Church, he won’t go there.

Because as soon as he goes there, it won’t be perfect anymore.

It won’t be perfect anymore because my friend acknowledges that he’s not perfect and has no place in a perfect Church.

The truth is on this side of heaven, there is no perfect Church.

All of us are fallen sinners; all of us are imperfect.

And you’ll see this in every Church.

In every Church, you’ll see highs and you’ll see lows.

And we see it in this church here at St Paul’s Kogarah.

But we’re not just St Paul’s Kogarah, are we?

We’re the Church of God, the saints of God, that meet here at Kogarah.

And God’s been faithful.

Despite our failures, God calls us into having a relationship, a fellowship, with him.

I thank God that one of the hallmarks of our Church is our resilience.

Despite of our weaknesses, God kept us here.

It’s by God’s grace and power that we’ll continue to be here.

As long as God keeps us here, we’ll continue to proclaim Jesus Christ

When you come to St Paul’s Kogarah, you will keep hearing about Jesus Christ

We keep talking about Jesus Christ.

The Church divided

That’s what the Church of God should be focussed on.

Sadly, there are times when the Church of God loses its focus on Jesus Christ.

When that happens, the Church is divided.

Look with me at verse 10-11,

“Now I urge you, brothers and sisters, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that all of you agree in what you say, that there be no divisions among you, and that you be united with the same understanding and the same conviction. 11 For it has been reported to me about you, my brothers and sisters, by members of Chloe’s people, that there is rivalry among you.”

The Bible is saying that Churches should not be divided but united.

But sadly, that’s the experience of many Churches.

A while ago, I met a young man, who have gone to a few different Churches and in all of them, he experienced divisions in churches.

That is, in every Church that he’s been to, he’s experienced Church divisions.

And he assured me that he wasn’t the one causing it!

There can be many reasons for divisions in a Church but here in Corinth, we see a common one.

The Church in Corinth is divided because people had formed different groups and they identified themselves with different leaders.

One of the leaders that some Corinthians identified with was Paul.

Paul had previously spent 18 months in Corinth and had planted the Church there.

He describes himself as a father to them.

Another leader that the Corinthians identified with was Apollos.

Apollos was a learned Alexandrian Jew who might have taught in a way that appealed to certain Corinthians.

A third person was Cephas, also known as Peter.

We don’t know much about Peter’s ministry at Corinth, except he might have taken his wife there with him.

But what we do know is that the three leaders all preached the word truthfully and faithfully.

There’s no evidence that they encouraged division.

However, it’s their followers who have caused the division in the Church.

They are the ones who say, “I follow Paul” or “I follow Apollos” or “I follow Cephas”.

Some may prefer one style over another.

But all along, every one of them should’ve been saying, “I belong to Christ”.

Listen to what Paul tells them in verse 13, “Is Christ divided? Was Paul crucified for you? Or were you baptized in Paul’s name?”

The last two questions are fairly straightforward to answer.

No, Paul wasn’t crucified, and no, they weren’t baptised in Paul’s name.

It’s Christ who was crucified; they were baptised in Christ’s name.

But the first question, “Is Christ divided?” is not as straight-forward as it first appears.

Can Christ be divided? No, there is only one Christ.

But is the Church, the body of Christ, divided?

Sadly, yes, it’s too true; Even though the body of Christ should be United as one, it’s not if the church is divided.

What led to the division among the Corinthian Church?

What made them prefer one leader over another?

It’s the Corinthian’s love for eloquent wisdom that has led to divisions in the Church.

Look with me at verse 17, “ For Christ did not send me to baptize, but to preach the gospel—not with eloquent wisdom, so that the cross of Christ will not be emptied of its effect.”

The Corinthians are drawn to engaging and entertaining speakers.

That’s like many of us today.

We enjoy listening to good speaker and we’re willing to pay for good speakers.

For example, Bill Clinton, the former President of the United States and his wife would often give talks.

If you’ve heard them, you’ll know that they both can speak eloquently.

From 2001 to 2015, both Bill Clinton and his wife Hilary gave 729 speeches.

How much did people pay to hear Bill and Hilary? $153 million US dollars.

I’m not sure what the speaker fee was back in Ancient Corinth, but people were just as attracted to good speakers then as they are now.

However, Paul was not interested in being a speaker with eloquent wisdom.

He’s here to preach the gospel, and he deliberately avoids eloquent wisdom.

In fact, Paul says that the eloquent wisdom of this world is doomed.

Look with me at verse 18-19,

“For the word of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but it is the power of God to us who are being saved. 19 For it is written,

I will destroy the wisdom of the wise,

and I will set aside the intelligence of the intelligent.”

The Gospel message, the Word of the Cross, sounds crazy to some people.

To those who love eloquent human wisdom, the gospel message is foolishness.

It sounds like garbage.

However, God says that it’s human wisdom that’s garbage, or at least will be made garbage.

God will destroy the wisdom of the wise and set aside the intelligence of the intelligent.

God has condemned the eloquent wisdom of the world.

All human wisdom, from those who spoke the first eloquent words to all those who make a living speaking eloquent words, are doomed for destruction.

Why?

Verse 21, “For since, in God’s wisdom, the world did not know God through wisdom, God was pleased to save those who believe through the foolishness of what is preached.”

The world did not come to know God through its wisdom.

The world might have solved many problems using its wisdom but it failed with the most important of problems: how do we know God?

No amount of human wisdom can get you any closer to God.

You can’t solve who God is by human wisdom; indeed, human wisdom can only turn you away from God.

Human wisdom is useless to help you come to know God.

And so God was pleased to save those who believe through the foolishness of what is preached.

And what is preached, the gospel, looks like foolishness in the world’s eyes.

Look with me at verse 22, “22 For the Jews ask for signs and the Greeks seek wisdom, 23 but we preach Christ crucified, a stumbling block to the Jews and foolishness to the Gentiles.”

The Jews ask for a sign to show that Jesus is worthy to believed.

The Greeks ask for wisdom and they want you to persuade that Jesus is worthy to be believed.

But we preach Christ crucified.

To the Jew, to be crucified is to be mocked by men and cursed by God.

It’s not a sign to show that Jesus is the King.

To the Greek, it’s ridiculous to believe that there’s any good reason why they should worship a crucified man as God.

It’s criminals who die on the cross, not God.

It’s utter foolishness to follow an obvious loser.

It’ll be like asking for financial advice from someone who’s bankrupt and living rough on the streets

Or asking help from someone who’s about to be executed on the electric chair.

They’re losers; why listen to them? Why follow them, let alone worship them?

But that’s what the Christians are saying? We preach Christ crucified.

That’s what the message of the cross is; it’s utter foolishness to the world.

And yet, look with me at verse 24-25, “24 Yet to those who are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God, 25 because God’s foolishness is wiser than human wisdom, and God’s weakness is stronger than human strength.”

God uses something as unbelievable and as weak as the crucified Christ to save his people

Sometimes, as Christians, we’re so used to hearing the gospel on Sundays that we don’t find it strange or confronting anymore.

But if you listen to the gospel the first time, it can sound very strange, even very foolish.

Dr Sam Chan, a gifted Evangelist has told this story to tell us how strange the gospel might sound to someone:

Imagine I told you this:

“﻿Last night, while my wife and I were watching TV, a UFO landed in our back yard. A green alien got out of the UFO and asked us to join him. So my wife and ﻿I got into his UFO, and he took us to his home planet, Jupiter. There he showed us around his home city. We had dinner with his family. Afterward, we got into the UFO and returned to earth. But when we got back, because of the space-time continuum, we went through a time portal, and only one second of earth time had passed.”

Do you believe me?

Let’s say I tell you another story:

Two thousand years ago, God sent us his Son, Jesus. He was born from a virgin! While he was on earth, he healed sick people and raised dead people back to life. And then he died on a cross. If you believe this, he will take away all your sins and forgive you. When you die, your soul will leave your dead body to be with Jesus in heaven. And one day, he will return and set up a kingdom on earth. And when he does, your dead body will rise from the grave and be reunited to your soul.

Do you believe me?

It can sound crazy, right?

It’s very hard to believe!

Of course, we probably don’t tell the story exactly like that, we expand on it, we put illustrations in, we use our life’s example to make it easier for people to understand it.

But at the heart of the gospel message is the crucified Christ.

It’ll always be a stumbling block and foolishness to people who cling on to the wisdom of the world.

But for those who are called, for those who believe, for the saints of God, Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God.

Because God was pleased to use the foolishness of the crucified Christ to save people so that no one can boast.

If someone comes to believe in Jesus, it’s not the result of human wisdom or human cleverness.

If someone comes to believe in Christ crucified, it’s because and only because of God’s wisdom and God’s power to save that person.

The Church’s boast

Since it’s God alone who saves, the people in God’s Church don’t boast in themselves.

Rather, Christians boast in God.

It’s God who’s done all the hard work; we just tell people what God’s done.

In fact, if people look at Christians, there isn’t much to boast about.

Listen to what the Bible says in verse 26, “Brothers and sisters, consider your calling: Not many were wise from a human perspective, not many powerful, not many of noble birth.”

When God called the Corinthian Christians to believe in him, not many of them were wise, or powerful or of noble birth.

And that’s true today as well.

While there are a few Christians who may be well educated and from the upper classes of the society,

Many are just ordinary, average people like you and me.

We’re not exceptional according to the world’s standards.

And just like God’s message, we the messengers are not attractive to the world.

But that’s precisely why God choose us.

God chooses what appears to be weak to shame the strong and God uses what appears to be foolish to shame the wise.

God’s chosen those who are insignificant, those who are despised, those viewed as nothing, to destroy those who are viewed as something.

God’s chosen ordinary people to proclaim what appears to be a foolish message to save the world, so that no one can boast.

This foolish message is in fact what true wisdom look like.

Look with me at verse 30, “It is from him that you are in Christ Jesus, who became wisdom from God for us—our righteousness, sanctification, and redemption.”

Christ Jesus is our wisdom from God.

Christ Jesus is what true wisdom is about.

Being our wisdom, he’s also our righteousness; it’s through him that we’re declared right with God.

He’s our sanctification; it’s through him that we’re made holy for God.

He’s also our redemption: he’s the price God paid to bring us back to God.

How do we come to God?

Human wisdom has no answer.

Only God can answer that question.

And that answer is Jesus Christ, him crucified.

And he chose you to tell that answer to the world.

He didn’t choose the wisest, the cleverest, the best in the world to tell his gospel.

He chose us, as imperfect and foolish as we are.

It’s both a humbling and a reassuring thought.

Humbling because we know that we’re not worthy of the task.

But also reassuring because we know that it’s all God’s work and he’ll make it happen.

I often hear that people don’t want to serve God because they don’t feel that they’re good enough or that they’re not educated enough.

But God has chosen those who are insignificant and despised in the world to be his servants.

God has rejected those who rely on their cleverness and chose people who would rather trust in him and the gospel of his Son.

That’s whom God’s chosen and that should be the people that we look for as well.

Over my time in ministry, I’ve seen so many people who are very clever, very persuasive, very wise according to the world’s standards,

Who are loved by the people of the world, and even by the people of the Church,

Who also sadly often end up being faithless and unreliable.

In contrast, often it’s those who aren’t brilliant, who aren’t attractive, who aren’t clever in the world’s eyes,

Who trust in God and have done marvellous things for God.

As a Church, we need to take care not to judge people according to the ways of the world.

Rather, we need to see them according to the wisdom of God.

Indeed, you can usually tell the maturity of a Christian by seeing the kind of activities and people that they praise.

Those who are mature in Christ will praise people for their faithfulness in Christ.

Those who are immature will praise people according to their cleverness, just like the rest of the world.

And so, as the Church of God, as God’s saints in Christ, let’s not look at the world according to the world’s wisdom;

rather look at the world according to God’s wisdom, according to the Crucified Christ, who is foolishness to the world but who is the true wisdom of God.

Where does this leave us?

The very last verse of today: “in order that, as it is written: Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord.”

Boast in the Crucified Lord

Boast in him as often as you can.

Make it a habit in your family.

Make it into a game!

In our family, we would often play a game called Spoto.

For example, sometimes, when we see a yellow car, we’ll shout Spoto.

In our Church, we would say that twice, for our two yellow cars.

Rather than doing spoto with yellow cars, do spoto with boasting in the Crucified Lord.

Praise Jesus in the morning, thank him for the food, thank him for your family, thank him in front of your family.

Start at Church, straight after today’s service.

Try it at home, then maybe at work, or as you begin school tomorrow.

See how many times you can verbally give thanks to Jesus.

As we think about how to give thanks to God for Jesus, we’ll begin to realign our wisdom from the world’s standard to God’s standard.

This week, aim to boast in the crucified Lord.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How does my life reflect my holiness in Jesus?

How do I need to realign my thinking to the wisdom of God rather than the wisdom of the world?

How many times have I boasted in Jesus today?

Friends, saints of the Living God, boast in the Lord Jesus, boast in Christ crucified.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for making us holy in the Lord Jesus Christ. Please forgive us when we still think, speak and act according to the wisdom of the world. By your Holy Spirit, move and transform us so that we’ll have the mind of Christ. Let us think according to your wisdom and your way. Thank you for using insignificant and despised people like us to proclaim your glorious message. Make us faithful and humble servants who boast in the Lord Jesus Christ, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
