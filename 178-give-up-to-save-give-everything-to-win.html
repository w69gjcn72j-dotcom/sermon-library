Give up to save, give everything to win

1 Corinthians 9:1-27

Give up your rights to freely become a slave

Give everything to win the crown so that you won’t be disqualified

On Tuesday we had a great Prayer meeting with our Chinese brothers and sisters.

It was a wonderful time of sharing and praying.

One of the prayer points that came up was the mental health of our community in this new round of lockdowns.

Since the lockdown began, Beyond Blue, a service that helps people with mental health, has reported an increase of 15% in calls in New South Wales.

Beyond Blue’s lead clinical adviser, Grant Blashki, says that it’s quite typical to see this during lockdowns.

He says that “They’ve got the triple whammy of worrying about money and small businesses and jobs, pressure in family situations, and they’re also worried about the actual COVID-19 infection ... so there's a bit of pressure on people at the moment.”

In a Covid restricted world like ours, we worry about our security and freedom.

In a fast-changing society like ours, we worry about our identity, we worry whether people will look down on people like us.

These things are precious to us, and we’re worried that we might lose them.

But rather than holding on tightly to them, what if we let these go.

What if we’re asked to willingly let go of our security, our freedom and our identity?

What would it take for you to give up your security, your freedom, your identity?

And to do it gladly?

Sounds crazy?

Well, that’s what we see in today’s passage in 1 Corinthians chapter 9.

In today’s passage, we’ll see the apostle Paul gladly giving up his security, his freedom, his identity for something bigger and better.

When we have what Paul has, we’ll have joy, exceeding joy, even if we don’t have our security, our freedom and our identity.

We’re going to see two things:

Give up your rights to freely become a slave

Give everything to win the crown so that you won’t be disqualified

Give up your rights to freely become a slave

Look with me at 1 Corinthians 9:1

Paul begins by asking four rhetorical questions.

“Am I not free? Am I not an apostle? Have I not seen Jesus our Lord? Are you not my work in the Lord?”

Of course Paul is an apostle, of course Paul has seen the Lord.

He’s the one who started the Corinthian Church.

He keeps these rhetorical questions coming, we’ll continue from verse 3,

“My defence to those who examine me is this: 4 Don’t we have the right to eat and drink? 5 Don’t we have the right to be accompanied by a believing wife like the other apostles, the Lord’s brothers, and Cephas?”

As an apostle, doesn’t Paul have the right to eat and drink?

Doesn’t he have a right to the necessities of life?

Of course he does!

Doesn’t he have the right to marry a believing wife who accompanies him on his mission?

Other apostles, including the Lord’s brothers have believing wives who accompany them and support them in their mission trips.

Even Cephas, that is the Apostle Peter, have a believing wife who accompanies him.

If they can, surely Paul and Barnabas can as well!

That is, as an apostle, as a missionary, Paul has the right to eat and drink and Paul has the right to marry.

Paul is entitled to both.

Paul then uses a series of imageries to show that it’s right for Paul to be supported by the Corinthians in his work as an apostle.

Look with me at verse 7, “ Who serves as a soldier at his own expense? Who plants a vineyard and does not eat its fruit? Or who shepherds a flock and does not drink the milk from the flock?”

In here, Paul lists three types of positions: the soldier, the vineyard planter and the shepherd.

Firstly, Paul compares himself to a soldier.

Whenever a country prepares for war, they have to make sure that they have enough money to pay for all the soldiers who will fight on their behalf.

Because when the soldiers go to war, they can’t earn a living.

If they just use their own money to go to war, it won’t be long before he’ll run out of supplies.

And so, the soldier doesn’t go into war at their own expense; their country pays for them.

It’s the right thing to do.

Second, the man who’s planted a vineyard ought to eat of its fruit.

The man worked hard to grow these grapes, surely he has the right to try some!

And thirdly, we have a shepherd.

The shepherd may be a servant who looks after the sheep for this boss.

But even he is entitled to some of the milk from the flock.

God’s people, the Church, is like an army, a field and a flock and God’s workers are like soldiers, vineyard planters, and shepherds.

As a soldier, as a vineyard planter and as a shepherd, apostles like Paul should be supported by the Church.

It makes sense!

Even the law of Moses affirms it, in verse 9, “Do not muzzle an ox while it treads out grain.”

While the ox is treading on the grain, don’t put something over the mouth so that it can’t have some of the grain.

Allow it to have some of the grain while it’s working.

It’s the right thing to do.

It’s a compassionate and humane thing to do.

And if that’s how we should treat animals, how much more should we be humane to humans!

Look with me at verse 10,

“Isn’t he really saying it for our sake? Yes, this is written for our sake, because he who plows ought to plow in hope, and he who threshes should thresh in hope of sharing the crop.”

It’s right for those who work to work in the hope of sharing in the crop.

Even Jesus himself has said in Luke 10:7 ,”For the worker is worthy of his wages.”

And so, we read in 1 Corinthians 9:14, “14 In the same way, the Lord has commanded that those who preach the gospel should earn their living by the gospel.”

For someone to preach the gospel, they should earn their living by the gospel.

That is, it’s right for the Church to financially support those who work in ministry.

It makes sense, it’s humane, the principle is outlined in the Old Testament and Jesus himself affirms it in the New Testament.

And I’m thankful that even though we’re not a big church, a number of us are giving sacrificially to support our ministry here.

I’m thankful for those who give regularly electronically.

I’m also thankful who don’t give electronically but save up their money to give whenever they have the opportunity.

I’m thankful that over the past year, we’re giving not only to my work here but also to two other gospel workers in our local area, Nick in the local schools and Jairus in the local university.

We’re also about to start giving to a missionary couple in the coming months.

Finally, we’re looking for someone to help, particularly with the Mandarin speaking ministry.

I praise God for the generosity of our Church in giving support for the ministries here at St Paul’s and beyond.

It’s the right, humane and godly thing to do.

However, Paul has a surprising twist for the Corinthians, in verse 15,

“For my part I have used none of these rights, nor have I written these things that they may be applied in my case. For it would be better for me to die than for anyone to deprive me of my boast!”

After arguing persuasively that it’s right for him to be given support for his work as an apostle, Paul says that he takes pride in not taking any of these rights!

Paul has not taken up money from the Corinthians for his daily expenses for food and drinks.

He has not taken for himself a believing wife.

He boasts that he’s not using his rights as an apostle even as he works among the Corinthians as an apostle.

Paul’s not preaching the gospel in order to get some financial benefits from the Corinthians.

He’s not a preacher for hire.

No, Paul’s compelled to preach! He has to preach!

Look with me at verse 16, “For if I preach the gospel, I have no reason to boast, because I am compelled to preach —and woe to me if I do not preach the gospel!”

Paul must preach the gospel! Indeed, if he doesn’t preach the gospel, he’s cursed!

Because that’s what God’s called him to do!

God called him to preach the gospel!

Ultimately, it’s not the people of the Church who tells Paul to preach the Gospel.

It’s God who tells Paul to preach the Gospel.

This is important for Paul to clarify because you might remember from a few chapters ago that we’ve learnt that the Corinthian Church is a divided Church.

It’s separated into different tribes.

If one tribe decides to give financially to Paul, to become their patron, as it were, then they would have a huge influence on what Paul’s message.

That will cause the division to worsen in the Corinthian Church.

And so, Paul emphatically affirms that he doesn’t take any benefit from them.

He doesn’t work for them.

He works for God.

God is his Ultimate Patron.

There is an important principle for us to see for today’s Church.

Strictly speaking, the Church does not employ the minister.

Yes, the Church pays and supports the minister, but the Church is not the minister’s boss.

The Church is not the employer and the minister is not the employee.

The Church doesn’t employ the minister but does give support for the minister so that he doesn’t have to worry about his daily expenses.

In the Sydney Anglican Church, the minister is appointed by the Archbishop of Sydney and the congregation gives to support the minister.

Now there are some Churches who see themselves as the employer and they see the minister as their employee.

While in most cases, this arrangement can work well, there are times when the Church members have a huge influence on what the minister do.

In some extreme cases, if the minister doesn’t preach what they like to hear or support their agendas, they withdraw their support for the minister.

Of course, that doesn’t mean that the minister is always right.

As a people of God, the Church must examine the minister’s words and actions in light of God’s words.

However, it’s a very important principle to see that the Minister is not just a preacher for hire.

A minister is called by God to preach the gospel; he’s cursed if he doesn’t preach God’s gospel.

As an apostle, Paul would do it without pay, lest his message be compromised to please his patron.

In the same way, a true minister of the gospel must not let money compromise his message to please his audience.

For ultimately, the true minister of the gospel works for God, his Ultimate Patron.

And a true church of God supports the minister to do his work for God’s army, God’s field, God’s flock.

Last week, I’ve heard the news of an Anglican minister by the name of Rev Andrew Pearson.

Rev Pearson was a minister of a large Church over 1000 people in the Advent Cathedral in Birmingham.

He was a faithful minister of the gospel but the Anglican diocese he was in was turning away from God.

He was told by the bishops in charge of him, “Change or leave the denomination”.

After much prayer and thought, he decided to leave.

He lost his job at the Cathedral, he’s lost his security but he’s not compromised as a Minister of Jesus.

He’s now starting a new Church and he’s come under a new Anglican bishop who believes the gospel.

Rev Pearson hasn’t compromised but stayed faithful to God.

For his boss is ultimately not his bishop; his Ultimate Patron is God.

Rather than taking what’s rightfully his, the Apostle Paul gives up his rights to become a slave.

Look with me at verse 19, “Although I am free from all and not anyone’s slave, I have made myself a slave to everyone, in order to win more people.”

Why would anyone in their right mind want to be a slave?

In the ancient, a slave has no rights; a slave is seen merely a possession of the slave-master.

To be a slave is to be vulnerable; it’s to lose your security.

Indeed, it’s to lose your identity.

You’re not free to do what you want; you’re your master’s slave.

And though Paul’s ultimate Patron, his ultimate Boss is God himself, Paul’s made himself a slave to everyone.

Paul, a free man, the Apostle of Jesus, is everyone’s slave.

Why does he do that?

It’s so that he may win people to Christ.

Look with me at verse 20, “To the Jews I became like a Jew, to win Jews; to those under the law, like one under the law—though I myself am not under the law —to win those under the law.”

Of course, Paul’s a Jew, through and through.

But he longer identifies himself firstly as a Jew; he identifies firstly as a Christian.

If Paul was Chinese, he would say, To the Chinese, I became like a Chinese, to win the Chinese.

It’s an incredible thing to say!

Paul would be a Jew to a Jew to win the Jews; he would be a like the one under the law to win those under the law (even though he wasn’t under the law).

Paul would be like those who are without the law (though he’s under the law of Christ) to win those without the law.

Paul would become weak to win the weak.

Paul steps right outside of his comfort zone.

His comfort zone would be where he would be feel comfortable, surrounded by people like him, who think like him and who talks like him.

His comfort zone would be a familiar place where he likes the people there and people like him.

That would be his comfort zone.

But Christians are not called to be comfortable in their comfort zones.

We’re called to serve and to minister to people who aren’t like us.

Indeed, if you come to a Church and expect to be comfortable, then you haven’t come to Paul’s Church.

Indeed, if you expect to church expecting to be comfortable, you haven’t come to Christ’s Church.

Pal has given up his rights to be comfortable, got out of his comfort zone, to bring the gospel to people who aren’t like him.

Jesus has given up heaven, the greatest place of comfort, came to our miserable, sick, sinful world, in order to save us.

Jesus has not come to be a slave in order to save us.

Paul follows his Lord’s footsteps, gave up his rights, got out of his comfort zone and made himself to be slave to win people to Christ.

He would become all things to all men so that by all possible means he might win some.

One of my favorite missionary of all time is someone by the name of Hudson Taylor.

Hudson Taylor was born in England in 1832.

He was originally studying in Medicine and could’ve had a wonderful and stable career in England.

But in time, God called him to be a missionary in China.

It wouldn’t have been easy for Hudson to be a missionary in China.

Even though he brought medical supplies, the Chinese didn’t welcome him and called him the “Black Devil”.

However, he was committed, and he spent 51 years as a missionary in China.

Not only did he learnt Chinese and spoke in Chinese, if you have a look at his pictures, he even dressed like a Chinese.

Hudson Taylor gave up his security, his freedom and his identity to become a missionary in China.

Because of Hudson Taylor gave up his security, his freedom and identity, many thousands and millions of Chinese have come to know Jesus as their Lord and Saviour.

What comfort would we give up, what security would we give up, that some Chinese, some Australians, some Indians may know Christ as their Lord and Saviour?

My prayer is that we’ll be a Church that’s willing to put aside our comfort, put aside our rights, put aside even our identity, so that by all possible means, some may be saved.

My prayer is that we’ll be able to step outside of comfort zone and speak to someone who is not like us and to share the good news about Jesus with them.

Indeed, I pray that we’ll become like those around us so that we might save them.

They are only a heartbeat away from the frightening realities of hell.

May God so move us so that we’ll put up a moment, indeed, even a lifetime of comfort, so that others might be saved.

And so, give up your rights, your comfort, to freely become a slave so that others might be saved.

For many of us to give up our rights and comforts is a big ask.

Where do we begin?

Well, just like you can’t win at the Olympics with no training, we won’t be a faithful Christian without spiritual training.

And so, give everything to win the crown so that you won’t be disqualified.

Give everything to win the crown so that you won’t be disqualified

Look with me at verse 24, “Don’t you know that the runners in a stadium all race, but only one receives the prize? Run in such a way to win the prize.”

On Friday, night, the Tokyo 2020 Olympics game has officially started.

It’s been delayed by a year but it’s finally here.

Many athletes have trained for many years to reach their peak physical capacity in order to participate in these games.

They have all had strict diets of things they can and can’t eat.

They all had exercised specially designed exercise regimes to keep them at their peak.

Paul says that Christians are like athletes who compete in a race.

We’re to be focussed and disciplined like Olympic athletes as we live the Christian life.

We’re to focus our energy and time and live our life in such a way as to win the prize.

Like Olympic athletes, we exercise self-control and give up on our desires that distract us from winning at the end.

We run to win a race, not against other Christians but against all the distractions and obstacles that will stop us from finishing the Christian race.

The athletes in Paul’s days do it for a crown made of leaves.

The athletes in our days do it for a medal.

But we run a race to win an imperishable crown.

And we need to run in a way that doesn’t disqualify us on the final day.

Sadly, there are already reports of some athletes who have cheated by taking some prohibitive drugs which led to their disqualification.

All their years of disciplined training, after all their preparation, they let themselves down and they let their countries down by giving in to their distractions and desires.

As we live our Christian lives, there will be times when we’ll be tempted to take our eyes off Jesus.

There’ll be times when we’ll be distracted and spend our time, even most of our time, on things that will take us a way from Jesus.

But don’t get distracted; rather stay focussed on completing the Christian race.

Listen to what Paul says in verses 26-27, “So I do not run like one who runs aimlessly or box like one beating the air. 27 Instead, I discipline my body and bring it under strict control, so that after preaching to others, I myself will not be disqualified.”

Just as athletes stay focussed on keeping a close eye on their dietary intake and their exercise regimes,

In order to stay in their peak physical condition,

Keep an eye on your spiritual intake and your godly disciplines

In order to stay in your peak spiritual condition.

Be as spiritually fit as you can be.

In a time of lockdown, it’s easy to get spiritually distracted.

It’s easy to spend our time watching our phone and our TV because that’s our main engagement with the outside world.

It’s easy to get distracted spiritually and focus on the immediate as opposed to the eternal.

While it’s true that there are less things that we can do in terms of physically going out and sharing the gospel,

There are still fundamental spiritual disciplines that you can do day to day.

Make a habit of reading the Bible daily.

Make a habit of praying for the world, the Church and our loved ones daily.

Come to Church service online on Sundays, join a Growth Group online during the week.

Get the basics right; make sure you keep up with your spiritual discipline.

What’s one spiritual discipline you can work on now?

Think about it, talk to the person in your home.

Send a message to me via wechat, whatsapp, zoom.

You have twenty seconds.

What spiritual discipline can you work on this week?

Friends, for gospel’s sake, give up your rights and become a servant. Like an athlete, give everything you’ve got to save and to win.

What does this mean for us?

By all possible means, save some; avoid every possible distraction and win.

The American swimmer was the most decorated Olympian of all time.

He won a total of 28 medals, 23 of them being gold medals.

As part of his training, he swam 80kms per week, taking only four days off in four years as part of his preparation for the Olympics.

Phelps was focussed on winning and disciplined himself to win perishable medals.

What will you do to win the imperishable crown?

Friends, focus on running the Christian race.

Make sure you get your spiritual sustenance daily: keep reading the Bible, keep praying!

Make sure you exercise your faith daily: trust in God’s word, obey God’s word, love God’s people.

As part of our training for our members, we have the partnership course and the discovery course.

A number of you have started the course.

Before we come back together, aim to finish them.

We’ll be starting online Partnership Course and Discovery Course soon – make it your priority to join them.

Whatever you do, don’t be a lazy Christian.

Have a plan to grow in your faith; work hard to reach the peak position in your faith.

By all possible means, save some; avoid every possible distraction and win.

Whatever it takes!

Here are the three questions for this week:

What’s one spiritual discipline I can work on while the lockdown is on?

How can I learn to more about someone else in order to be like them, so that I can win them for Christ?

What’s one distraction that I must give up lest it takes my eyes away from Christ?

Friends, by all possible means, save some; avoid every possible distraction and win.
