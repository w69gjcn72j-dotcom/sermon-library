Move to Jesus

Nehemiah 11

Move to where God wants you to follow and worship Jesus

Activating a city to worship

Be followers who move to Jesus

Move yourself and your loved ones to Jesus

Do you have the Great Australian Dream?

The Great Australian Dream is to own a house, with your own backyard, in a nice suburb.

When you have your own a nice house, a nice little place you can call your home, you’ve made it.

You got yourself the Great Australian Dream.

Of course, it’s not just an Australian Dream.

To own a nice house in a nice suburb is the Great Chinese Dream, the Great Indian Dream, the Great Lebanese dream.

Who wouldn’t want to live the dream of living in your own home?

And so, many people work very hard to get there.

They work hard, they save hard and God willing, they get the house that they want.

They get to live their dream.

As people who follow Jesus, though, we don’t want to just live our dream.

We want to live and work where God wants us to be.

We ask ourselves, “Am I living and working where God wants me to be?

Is this where God wants me to serve him?”

That’s what we’ll see in today’s passage.

In today’s passage, we’ll see over three thousand men and their families who left the comfort and security of their dream home for something grittier, something harder.

Rather than living in the leafy suburbs, they chose to live in a rundown city with hardly any houses standing.

Instead of living their dream, they chose to live in a place where life would probably be harder and more life-threatening.

They do it not because they like challenges but because that’s where God wants them to be.

So if you have your Bible there, please open up to Nehemiah 11.

We’ll see three things:

Activating a city to worship

Be followers who move to Jesus

Move yourself and your loved ones to Jesus

Before we look into God’s word, let’s pray,

“Father, please help us see your words more clearly, and move us by Spirit so that we can understand your will and obey what you commanded. We pray this in Jesus’ name, Amen.”

Activating a city to worship

What’s been happening in Nehemiah so far?

At the beginning of Nehemiah 7, we read that the city was large and spacious but only a few people lived there and no houses were built yet.

Nehemiah then organised the people according to their family trees.

The people then asked Ezra the priest to read God’s law to them.

Moved by God’s word, they confessed their sins before God and recommitted their lives once again to God.

Last week in Nehemiah chapter 10, we read that they were serious about obeying God’s law, even calling on a curse and making a sworn oath, that they would keep God’s law or be cursed.

All these happened in a fairly short period of time, probably no more than six months.

They had made incredible progress as a nation in a fairly short period of time.

And now, they will do something that will have an impact for years, even decades, to come.

Their decision will affect the lives of the whole nation, and especially to 3000 families who had chosen or been chosen to move to Jerusalem.

They will move to Jerusalem to activate the city for worship.

Many years ago, our family went to a family’s home for BBQ.

It was BBQ with Uncle Ravik’s family down at Badgery’s creek.

Back then, in the early 80’s, Ravik learnt that there will be an airport built there one day.

And so, he bought a lot of land in the area with the hope of giving his children very valuable land one day.

There was hardly anything happening at Badgerys’s creek for many years.

But now, the airport is indeed coming.

Bradfield, the suburb next to the airport, currently has 160,000 people.

It’s projected that over the next 18 years, it will grow to 1.5 million people.

That’s a lot more people than when we had the BBQ at Uncle Ravik’s place many years ago.

The land was unused for many years but now the government is activating the land.

They are making the land active!

That’s what’s happening here in Nehemiah.

Jerusalem was once a bustling city.

However, due to the disobedience of the people, God gave them over to their enemies.

But now God had brought his people back.

Now’s the time to repopulate and activate the city.

Unlike the Badgerys’s Creek airport, they are not activating to make people richer or happier.

No, they’re activating the city for the glory of God.

Jerusalem is the Holy City, the city where the Temple of God is supposed to be.

This is the city that God is identified with!

But in Nehemiah’s time Jerusalem is a city in ruins.

It’s a city that needs to be activated.

It needs to be repopulated; it needs to be filled with people who will look after the city once more.

Look with me at Nehemiah 11:1,

“Now the leaders of the people stayed in Jerusalem, and the rest of the people cast lots for one out of ten to come and live in Jerusalem, the holy city, while the other nine-tenths remained in their towns.”

The city of Jerusalem needs to be filled with people who obey God, people who are willing to give up their dream, their dream of living in the comfortable and safe Judean countryside, to live within the walls of Jerusalem.

They will have to work hard to build new houses, restore temple worship while defending against the enemies of the city.

Who will step up and move to the city?

The first people mentioned here are the leaders.

That’s what godly leaders do; they’ll follow God’s lead even when things are hard.

You’d expect that some people might be inspired to follow the leaders’ lead.

And there’s a hint in verse two that some volunteered to live in Jerusalem.

But the majority seemed reluctant, even after the confession and their recommitment to God.

The fact that they had to cast lots to see who will live in Jerusalem suggests that most people didn’t want to leave their lifestyle behind.

At the end, the leaders of the people, along with one tenth of the rest of the population, will stay in Jerusalem.

As well as the leaders of God’s people, along with their families,

Priests, Levites and Gatekeepers will stay in Jerusalem.

Of the three thousand men and their families who stay in Jerusalem, over 1600 are the priests, Levites and gatekeepers.

That is, more than half of the people who stay are involved with temple worship.

The focus of the people is on making sure that worship of God will be properly restored.

The chapter then finishes off with a list of places outside of Jerusalem, where nine out of ten people who returned from the exile will live.

Look with me at verse 2, “The people blessed all the men who volunteered to live in Jerusalem.”

Those staying in or moving to the city of Jerusalem have the blessing of the rest of the people.

I mentioned earlier that there will be a lot of people moving into the area around Badgerys’s Creek over the next two decades.

Many Christians are now moving to this area and the areas throughout western Sydney because they are now planting Churches there for the population explosion that will happen in those areas.

Many Christians have moved there to start a new life and to start a new Church.

Many Christian families are going there with one another to begin something new.

It’s going to be difficult, but they’re determined to go and to stay there for many years.

Like the Jewish leaders in Nehemiah’s time, they are willing to lift their families up, give up what comfort they’ve had in the suburbs that they’ve grown to love, to activate within these new suburbs with places of worship.

And we thank God that many great things have been happening in these new suburbs already!

Think about the last time you moved.

What was the main reason for your move?

Talk with the person next to you.

If you’re at home, you can text me your answer by whatsapp or wechat.

What was the main reason for your last move?

You have twenty seconds.

Be followers who move to Jesus

We might have many reasons for our moves.

But the biggest move of all time was made by Jesus himself.

Jesus was in heaven, a place of infinite joy and where he enjoyed infinite and absolute power as God.

We can’t even imagine the kind of bliss and security that Jesus had.

And yet, God’s sent Jesus into our world, in order for Jesus to make his home with us.

It’s a little like sending Prince William and Princess Catherine of England to Ukraine.

Right now, the royal couple is on a royal tour in Jamaica.

They’re surrounded by bodyguards and live in the best accommodation in Jamaica.

But imagine that they were sent to Ukraine instead, not dressed as royalties but as an ordinary citizen, fleeing from the gunfire.

Imagine them living as a local, unrecognised by friend or foe, in the horror that’s fallen on Ukraine.

That’s like what Jesus was going through except it was worse.

Look with me at John 1:9-11, “ The true light that gives light to everyone was coming into the world.

10 He was in the world, and the world was created through him, and yet the world did not recognize him. 11 He came to his own, and his own people did not receive him.”

God created us through Jesus but we did not recognise Jesus, we did not receive him.

That’s because we fell into darkness when we disobeyed God.

When Jesus came, he was loved by some by rejected by most.

He was as Man of Sorrows.

Though he made this world, he couldn’t call this place his home.

Look with me at Luke 9:58, “Jesus told him, “Foxes have dens, and birds of the sky have nests, but the Son of Man has no place to lay his head.”

Jesus didn’t have a house in the Judean countryside; he didn’t even have a house in Jerusalem.

He had no place to lay his head, full stop!

He always stayed in somebody else’s house.

And yet, that’s the life that God’s sent him to live, and Jesus lives it perfectly.

More than that, he died to make a perfect sacrifice to God on our behalf.

He died for our sins to make us holy before God.

He died to exchange our darkness for his glory.

After three days He was raised to life to show that what he’s said was true and that he’s the Only Way to God the Father.

Having been make holy by Jesus, we are now invited to live with God the Father and God the Son.

Look with me at John 14:23, “If anyone loves me, he will keep my word. My Father will love him, and we will come to him and make our home with him.”

Those of us who believe in Jesus will live with God the Father and God the Son.

We can look forward to moving into their home in heaven.

That’s where Christians are moving towards.

Each day is a day close to being home with Jesus.

For now, we get a glimpse of what it means to be at home with Jesus by knowing that both God the Father and God the Son have already made their home with us, when we receive the Holy Spirit.

By the Holy Spirit, God moves into the hearts of his people and live inside of us.

If we believe in Jesus and have received the Holy Spirit, God has moved in with us.

And we also want to move in with God.

We want to be where God is and so we will move to get there.

We will move towards God.

That’s why throughout the Bible, God’s people are described as those who follow God.

When we follow God, we will move towards God, for following implied movement.

One person who has moved towards God was the Apostle Paul.

Paul thought he was doing the right thing by God by cracking down on and putting Christians in jails.

He was a religious teacher and thought that he knew all about God.

It wasn’t until Jesus appeared to him that he believed in Jesus and was saved.

As a result, he moved from being the Hunter to being the hunted.

He used to hunt down people who spread the good news of Jesus.

After becoming a Christian, he was hunted for spreading the good news of Jesus.

He was putting his life at risk, again and again for the sake of following Christ.

Why would Paul abandon the safe position of being a religious teacher to being hunted down as a proclaimer of the good news of Jesus?

Look with me at Philippians 3:12, “Not that I have already reached the goal or am already perfect, but I make every effort to take hold of it because I also have been taken hold of by Christ Jesus.”

Since then, many Christians have followed Paul’s example of taking hold of Christ, no matter the cost, because they recognised that Christ has taken hold of them.

They have followed Christ and moved to wherever Christ has led them.

As Christians, we move by faith to wherever Jesus Christ will lead us, knowing that no matter how hard it is, it’s nothing compare to what Jesus has already done for us.

Move yourself and your loved ones to Jesus

And so, move yourself.

Get on with moving towards Jesus.

Take the next step in your journey if faith.

Where does Jesus want you to be by the end of this year?

Where does Jesus want you to be by next year?

Earlier this year when my family was travelling down to Jervis Bay, we had a few checkpoints along the way.

We stopped by Berry for lunch and then we went down to Nowra for some supplies.

We then finally made it to our destination at Jervis Bay in late afternoon.

What are your checkpoints in your journey of faith?

For their next step in faith for the Jews in Nehemiah’s time was to move to Jerusalem and to start the process of repopulation and restoration of temple worship.

How about you?

Where does God want you to go next?

Maybe God wants you to start a new job where you will serve him more faithfully.

Maybe God wants you to move to a new suburb where there’s a great need for the gospel to be heard and believed.

What’s your next step in your journey of faith?

What’s your next move?

As well as thinking about moving yourself, think about how to move others on their journey of faith as well.

How do you move your family members in their journey of faith toward Jesus?

One of the things that I really enjoy is to see children here at our bilingual service.

Thank you to all the parents who have brought them to Church.

By bringing them here, you are moving them towards Jesus.

It’s an important part of their journey of faith.

What you do here at Church with your children will have a lasting impact on their lives.

Now I know that some parents will mind having children here.

Will they behave? Will they make too much noise? How will they last through the service?

Sure, every now and then, we’ll hear them but it’s ok, we love hearing them.

Of course, we also need to teach them to respect God’s word and to listen while God’s word is being taught.

Wouldn’t you tell them to stop talking if they were in class, to show respect to the teacher?

How much more should they respect God at Church?

But from our experience, our children behave wonderfully at Church, most of the time, almost all of the time.

And I think it’s mostly due to what their parents do.

If the parents listen attentively to God’s word, the children will follow and do the same.

If the parents follow Christ, they will follow the parents in the way they follow Christ.

And so, parents, uncles, aunties, older siblings in Christ, be godly examples to the next generation.

They will follow your example.

Show them, train them on what it means to follow Christ.

Following Christ is not for the faint-hearted.

The Jewish leaders in Nehemiah’s days chose the more difficult path of living in Jerusalem rather than the Judean countryside.

Countless Christians since the time of Christ have chosen to take the more difficult path of hardship and obedience rather than a life of wealth and pleasure.

They have chosen a path of faith.

The true followers of Jesus will choose a path of faith knowing that Jesus has moved from heaven and earth to save us.

He’s moved from heaven to earth to save us and to take us from earth to heaven.

Won’t we move from our place of comfort to his place of everlasting comfort and joy, even if the path ahead is difficult?

And so, friends, move to where God wants you to follow and worship Jesus.

Wherever God leads you, move and follow him there.

By faith, take the next step to Jesus.

In the Bible, there are multiple accounts of Jesus healing the lame, making them able to walk again.

Even though we might feel like we’re spiritually lame, unable to take the next step,

Have faith in Jesus, he can make the lame, even the spiritually lame, rise and walk again.

It might be a big move, like moving from a job or moving homes.

Or it might be a smaller move, like moving back to meeting here at Church on Sunday mornings.

I’m so glad for many who have made it back physically back with us already.

My prayer is that as Easter approaches, we will have more people come back with us on Sundays.

Our aim, our checkpoint, is to have 80% of our members back on the first Sunday of May.

It might seem very difficult to see that happening.

We will do our utmost to welcome people back.

It will be a step of faith from our side but it will also be a step of faith from those who have been mainly joining us online.

My prayer is that as a church we can take this step of faith together.

My prayer is that by faith, we’ll take the next step to Jesus.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What’s the next step that God wants me to take by faith?

What will be my checkpoints of faith this year, next year, and five years from today?

Do I trust that God will give me what I need to take the next step of faith in him?

Friends, God will give what he demands.

He will give you whatever you need to take the next step of faith.

By faith, take the next step to Jesus.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we praise you for being a faithful God, gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love. We thank you that you’ve shown your faithfulness to us in Jesus. We thank you that Jesus has come so that by believing in him, loving him and keeping his word, you and Jesus will make a home with us. Please by your Spirit move us toward Jesus. Give us the faith to take the next step of faith. Keep us near you as we journey home. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
