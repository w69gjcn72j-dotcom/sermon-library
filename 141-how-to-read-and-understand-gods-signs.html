Passage: Acts 2:1-36

Additional passages: Joel 2:28-32, Psalm 16:8-11, Psalm 110:1

Big Idea: Spirit-filled servants of Jesus understand God’s signs and so calls on the world to call on the name of the risen Lord Jesus.

The Sign of the Holy Spirit

Understanding the God’s signs in the last days

All signs point to the risen Lord Jesus

In Australia, one of the things we were taught from a young age is that when you go to the beach, there’s only one place where you’re supposed to swim.

Can anyone tell me where?

Between the flags. Why? Because that’s where the lifesavers are.

The lifesavers are there to stop people from drowning.

However, research done in 2022 showed that 50% of overseas-born beachgoers and 40% of those born in Australia rarely and never used the sign.

What do the flags actually mean: you must always swim between the flags.

But 30% of people born overseas thought that only good swimmers swim between the flags.

If you’re not a good swimmer, don’t swim between the flags. And so, if you don’t think you’re a good swimmer, you swim outside the flags, which is wrong!

As a result, many people born overseas are more vulnerable to drowning.

From 2021-2022, out of the 939 people who drowned by the coast, almost half of these were born overseas.

Reading and understanding the meaning of the flags at the beach is very, very important.

Throughout the ages, God has given human beings signs.

These are very clear signs about who we are and the danger that we’re in.

Have you seen those signs? Have you understood their importance?

In today’s passage, we’re going to see one of the clearest signs that God’s given and the significance it has for all of us.

Please open your Bible with me to Acts chapter two.

We’re going to see three things:

But before we look into God’s word, let’s pray, “Father, please open our eyes to your glorious truth. By your Spirit help us both to read and understand your signs clearly, so that we may be saved by the Saviour, the Lord Jesus Christ, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

The Sign of the Holy Spirit

Firstly, we see the sign of the Holy Spirit.

Please open up your Bible with me to Acts chapter two beginning from verse one and let’s say the verse together,

“When the day of Pentecost had arrived, they were all together in one place.”

The Day of Pentecost is the 50th day since the Passover festival.

In the Jewish calendar, this is also known as the Feast of Weeks, a time when the Israelites celebrate the first fruits of the harvest.

It’s a time of giving God thanks for what he’s done.

From the Christian’s point of view, it also marks the 50th Day since the resurrection of Jesus Christ.

At the first Pentecost after the resurrection of Jesus, the disciples gathered together in one place where suddenly a sound like the violent rushing wind came from heaven and filled the house where they were staying.

It would’ve sounded something like this!

But there was no wind, only the sound of wind.

Also, tongues like flames of fire settled on each one of the disciples of Jesus.

Both the wind and the fire were symbols of God’s power and God’s presence in the Old Testament.

And now, on this Day of Pentecost, God showed that He’s there, in the presence of the disciples.

In fact, he wasn’t just there in their presence; he filled them up with his very own presence, with his very own Spirit.

After being filled with God’s Spirit, the Holy Spirit, the disciples started to speak in other languages, as the Spirit enabled them.

In Jesus’ time, the franca lingua, the common language, of that part of the world, was Greek.

However, people from different regions had their own language, their own dialect.

And so many of them would have been able to speak multiple languages.

For example, they would know Egyptians because they were from Egypt, but to speak with people from Rome, they would speak their common language, their lingua franca: Greek.

It’s a little bit like us here in Australia.

Some of us might speak Mandarin, and other speak Italian, but to communicate with one another, we use our lingua franca, our common language: English.

At the first Pentecost, many devout Jews came to Jerusalem from every nation under heaven, including the Medes on the west and the Romans in the east.

After the Holy Spirit filled the disciples, they started praising God in different languages and different dialects.

They could hear them praising God in their mother tongue!

Have you ever had the experience of going to a foreign country where no one spke your language?

A few months ago, our family went to Japan where most people don’t speak English or Chinese.

However, when we were in a car park at Ōwakudani, we heard people speaking English.

More than that, it was Australian English.

And more than that, we knew them and their parents! True story!

We were so happy to hear them and see them; in fact they took this phot of our family!

On the Day of Pentecost, Jews from different nations, heard people praising God in their own mother tongue.

It would’ve been something like this:

“The Lord is good - 耶和華本是美善的” “His steadfast love endures forever - la sua bontà dura in eterno,” “Jesus is Lord - 耶和華本是美善的 (Shanghainese)”

They would’ve been excited to hear their mother tongue, but they would’ve been surprised to hear their mother tongue spoken by these foreign Galilean disciples.

These Galileans were foreigners; how could they have known their dialect?

What’s happening?

I found it absolutely fascinating that the firs sign that the Holy Spirit chose to make himself known on the Day of Pentecost has to do with languages.

The first thing that the Holy Spirit when he filled these disciples was to enable them to speak in the mother tongue of the people gathered around them.

From the first day of Pentecost until now, the Christian Church, empowered by the Holy Spirit, is one that goes out to proclaim the message of Jesus to as many people as possible, in as many languages as possible.

While we don’t receive the Holy Spirit in the same way, the Holy Spirit still moves us, still enables us to reach across our linguistic barriers, so that as many people as possible can hear the good news of Jesus!

Last week, Range’s family and I went to a fundraiser for Zoe’s University Christian group.

The leader there talked about a film project that they’ve been involved in.

Their organization, Power to Change, was responsible for translating the movie Jesus film, into 2148 different languages.

That’s a lot of languagse!

They’re doing this because they have the same heart as the first church that met on the day of Pentecost, when the Holy Spirit came upon them.

They wanted people everywhere, from every nation, to hear the good news of Jesus in their own language.

However, even as they were praising God in the various languages, they were being teased.

There were some who said that these disciples were drunk!

Without the proper explanation, even such a spectacular sign from the Holy Spirit can be misunderstood.

Understanding the God’s signs in the last days

And so, Peter got up to explain the significance of what’s happening to the people.

He uses three Old Testament passages in his explanation.

Please look with me at verses 15-18, “For these people are not drunk, as you suppose, since it’s only nine in the morning. 16 On the contrary, this is what was spoken through the prophet Joel: 17And it will be in the last days, says God, that I will pour out my Spirit on all people; then your sons and your daughters will prophesy, your young men will see visions, and your old men will dream dreams. 18 I will even pour out my Spirit on my servants in those days, both men and women and they will prophesy.

The first passage tells us that the coming of the Holy Spirit is a sign.

It tells us about the time and the danger that we’re in.

In this prophecy, God says through the prophet Joel that when God pours out His Spirit on all people, it will be the last days.

How do you know that God has poured out his Spirit on his people?

Your sons and daughters, God’s male servants and female servants, will prophesy, young men will see visions, and old men will dream dreams.

God’s Spirit will so fill them that they will speak of things that are beyond their natural understanding.

They will speak about what God has revealed to them by his Holy Spirit.

They will see vision and dream dreams, ways that God had revealed his will to his prophets in the past.

When these things happen, know that we’re in the last days.

It’s like a warning system.

In Australia, we have a warning system that goes like this:

If you’re in a fire and you see a yellow sign, then be aware that an incident has started; your life is not in immediate danger.

If you see an orange sign, then you need be aware to watch and act.

You’ll need to start thinking about taking actions to protect yourself and your family.

When you see the red sign, then you are in danger and you need to do something immediately.

Any delay can put your life at risk.

Peter is saying that the coming of the Holy Spirit is the red sign.

When you see the prophesies of Joel fulfilled in the followers of Jesus being filled with the Holy Spirit, praising God in different languages, it’s the sign of the end.

It’s the last days, you are in danger, and you need to take action immediately.

These are the days of God’s final warning.

In the second part of Peter’s quote of Joel, he mentioned that there will be wonders in the heaven above and signs of the earth below: blood and fire and a cloud of smoke.

The sun will be turned to darkness and the moon to blood.

Did that happen on the Day of Pentecost?

No.

The first part of Joel’s prophecy was fulfilled with the coming of the Holy Spirit.

The second part of Joel’s prophecy was fulfilled in the person of Jesus Christ.

Peter highlights four significant events in the life of Jesus that shows us why he’s at the very centre of how we’re to understand God’s signs.

Firstly, the life of Jesus.

Look with me at verse 22, “Fellow Israelites, listen to these words: This Jesus of Nazareth was a man attested to you by God with miracles, wonders, and signs that God did among you through him, just as you yourselves know.”

God himself had attested him to the Jews by his mighty power.

Jesus performed clear and undeniable signs.

He raised the dead, fed thousands with five loaves of bread and two fish; he calmed the raging seas and even walked on water.

The Jews have seen and can testify to the signs and wonders of Jesus.

Second, the death of Jesus

Look with me at verse 23, “Though he was delivered up according to God’s determined plan and foreknowledge, you used lawless people to nail him to a cross and kill him.”

Jesus lived his life as a powerful man of God, but he was delivered and betrayed according to God’s plan.

The Jews and all those who lived in Jerusalem, along with the Israelites; they all nailed Jesus to the cross and killed him.

They killed this man whom God has approved.

However, they couldn’t do the killing by themselves, and needed the help of the Romans, those lawless men (that is, men without God’s law).

However, not even the mighty Roman Empire was ultimately capable of killing Jesus.

How is anyone capable of killing someone who could raise the dead?

How do you stop someone who can command the winds?

No, it was all within God’s plan and Jesus allowed himself to be killed by men.

Thirdly, the resurrection of Jesus.

Look with me at verse 24, “God raised him up, ending the pains of death, because it was not possible for him to be held by death.”

Men’s verdict of Jesus was overturned.

God raised him up because it was impossible for death to hold him down.

What Jesus went through was horrible and painful.

It was described as the pains of death, more literally, the labour pains of death. However, just as labour pain was the prelude to new life, the labour pains of death was what Jesus went through before being risen to new life.

Death could not hold him down, for he is risen!

And here, Peter refers to his second passage.

It’s taken from Psalm 16.

In the psalm, King David said that the Lord was ever before him and so he’s not afraid.

He’s so confidence in God that he said God will not abandon him in Hades (the place of death) and will not allow his holy one to see decay.

While there would’ve been many times when God saved King David from certain death, David did in fact died.

David did go to the grave, he did see decay.

And so, Peter concludes, that this psalm is not really about King David after all.

Instead, David was speaking as a prophet.

God had told King David that his line will last forever, that someone from the line of David will be on the throne of a Kingdom that God himself would built.

And it will be a kingdom that lasts forever.

One of David’s son will be on this throne, the throne that will last forever.

This psalm wasn’t fulfilled by King David but by his Greater Son, the Lord Jesus.

This Kingdom will always have a king, not because there will always be someone next in line but because Jesus himself will live forever and be king forever.

This prophecy is fulfilled by Jesus when he rose from the dead, ascending to the throne of the Kingdom of God, on which he will reign forever and ever.

The disciples were witnesses of this risen Jesus; they can confirm that it has been fulfilled.

But how do we know that the disciples’ witnesses are reliable?

This takes us to the fourth point: the exaltation of Jesus Christ

Read verse 33-35 with me, “Therefore, since he has been exalted to the right hand of Goda and has received from the Father the promised Holy Spirit,b he has poured outc what you both see and hear. 34 For it was not David who ascended into the heavens, but he himself says: The Lord declared to my Lord, ‘Sit at my right hand

35 until I make your enemies your footstool.’

Jesus has risen and has now ascended, taken up, in bodily form to heaven.

He’s at God’s right hand and from there, he poured out the Holy Spirit on his followers.

We see the Trinity here again:

God the Son received the promised God the Holy Spirit from God the Father and pours out God the Holy Spirit on his followers.

Peter says that’s why the Jews could see the sign of the Holy Spirit, that’s why they could hear the disciples praising God in the mother tongue of their hearers.

It’s because Jesus came, Jesus died, Jesus has risen and Jesus has ascended to the Father’s side.

And being ascended to the Father’s side, he poured out the Holy Spirit on his people.

These people will be his signs in two ways: They will be a people filled with His Holy Spirit and they will point to Jesus.

That’s their prophesy; that’s their visions; that’s their dreams.

They don’t talk about themselves or share visions for a better world.

No, instead, filled with the Holy Spirit, they talk about the Lord Jesus.

The Lord Jesus is their vision; the Lord Jesus is the One whom Spirit-filled people will talk about.

In a recent conference, one of the conference leaders commented that he hasn’t heard anything about the Holy Spirit in the conference.

However, one of the other conference leaders quickly asked (and I paraphrase), “What do you mean? The Holy Spirit has been doing a wonderful work all around the conference for we’ve been talking about Jesus the whole time.

All this time we’ve been talking about how to make him known. That’s what a Spirit-filled people will do – they talk about Jesus because the Spirit wants to talk about Jesus.”

Friends, if you want to know whether a people is filled with the Holy Spirit, listen to what they talk about Jesus.

A Spirit-filled people will talk and sing and praise Jesus, for that’s what the Holy Spirit would want them to do.

see how they talk about Jesus. Do they point to him as the risen King?

It’s a reminder for me and you as well.

Are we filled with the Holy Spirit? Do we talk about Jesus?

Friends, Pentecost is a unique event; it can’t be repeated.

However, the same Spirit still works among us; he still has the same goal.

He wants to get us talking about Jesus to as many people as possible.

He wants us to tell people all around us that he’s powerful, he’s crucified, he’s risen and he’s exalted!

All signs point to the risen Lord Jesus

Friends, every sign that God has placed in the Bible, whether it’s in the Old or New Testament, points us to Jesus.

All signs point to him.

It’s always been about Jesus and in the last days, it’s still all about Jesus.

That’s why Peter ends his talk with this challenge, in verse 36, “Therefore let all the house of Israel know with certainty that God has made this Jesus, whom you crucified, both Lord and Messiah.”

In verse 21, we read that the final call in Joel’s prophecy was to call on the name of the Lord, for everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved.

And what’s the name of the Lord?

The name of the Lord is Jesus.

“God has made this Jesus, whom you crucified, both Lord and Messiah (Christ)”

The last days are here, the world’s days are numbered.

There’s only one name we can call to be saved, and his name is Jesus.

We call on the name of the Lord when we pray to him.

In Australia, if you are to travel more than two nautical miles from the coast, you need to install an Emergency Position Indicating Radio Beacon on your boat.

When you’re in trouble, you can activate the beacon and notify the authorities so that they can send rescue boats or rescue helicopters to come and save you.

No one is automatically saved in the last days.

Those who had been blind to God’s signs won’t be saved; those who have read but ignored God’s signs won’t be saved.

To be saved, you must call on the name of the Lord Jesus.

When we pray, it’s like us sending our emergency signal to Jesus.

We call out to him because we see and understand God’s signs and we recognise the danger that we’re in.

We pray to Jesus so that we may be saved.

If you haven’t called us to Jesus yet, then friends, please call out to Jesus now.

Call on the name of the Lord Jesus and you will be saved.

All those who don’t, won’t.

If you are saved, then don’t just sit there.

Be filled with the Holy Spirt of God – tell as many people as you can!

I’ve mentioned this before and I’ll mention this again: our Church is a lifeboat, not a cruise.

We don’t come to Church expecting to be comfortable and pampered.

No, we come to church to rescue as many people as God would allow us.

Those who are saved will be committed to saving others.

We listen to the Holy Spirit’s lead, and we look for those who are lost.

When we see them, we tell them to call on the name of the only One who could save them.

We tell them to call on the name of the Lord Jesus Christ.

Friends, do you know the signs of God? Can you understand the signs of God?

All of God’s signs, including the coming of the Holy Spirit, points to Jesus.

And so, if you have already been saved by Jesus, be a sign that God uses to point people to Jesus.

Read up on the signs that God has put in the Bible about Jesus.

Teach the signs of God as they point people to Jesus.

Tell people to call on the name of the Lord Jesus and be saved.

Friends, Spirit-filled servants of Jesus understand God’s signs and so calls on the world to call on the name of the risen Lord Jesus.

A long time ago, I went to Seoul for a Pastor’s conference.

There must have been over 3000 pastors from all over Korea and all over the world who came to the conference.

At one point of the conference, the leader asked us to all pray at the same time, in different languages (but since most pastors were Korean, it was mostly in Korean).

I prayed and I heard what must have been the sound of violent wind filling the whole hall.

It was incredible.

We were all calling on the name of Jesus.

The sound I heard wasn’t the same as Pentecost; I’m sure what I heard was the prayers of 3000 pastors.

But it was overwhelming and amazing.

I think it’s the closest I’ve been to what Pentecost was like.

Friends, as you know, at St Paul’s we have translation in our services.

It’s a wonderful blessing to be able to share the good news of Jesus in multiple languages.

My prayer is that we can share in more than two languages.

My prayer is that more and more people from different language backgrounds will be able to share the gospel message.

Wouldn’t it be great if we can gather people whose heart language is Nepali and for them to hear God’s love in Nepali?

Wouldn’t it be great to hear the gospel preached in Shanghainese?

But more than that, my prayer is that more and more of us can learn God’s word together, whether it’s here on Sunday mornings or in Growth groups throughout the week.

Learning God’s will, reading God’s signs in the Bible, is something that we must keep doing, all the days of our lives.

I’ve been reading God’s word for almost 50 years and there’s still so much I want to learn!

God’s people will always want to learn, always want to be trained, so that we can better see how God’s word unfolds in the Lord Jesus Christ.

For we want to be Spirit-filled servants of Jesus understand God’s signs and so calls on the world to call on the name of the risen Lord Jesus.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Do I understand God’s signs?

Do I explain God’s signs about Jesus to other people?

How can we proclaim God’s signs in a multilingual place like ours?

Friends, be filled with the Spirit, understand God’s signs, call on the world to call on the name of the risen Lord Jesus.

We praise you for raising him from the grave and exalting him to the highest place, and gave him the name that is above every name. Lord Jesus, we thank you that you have come, you have died, you have risen and you have exalted on high. May the lives of those who call on your name, live for your glory and praise. Give us a heart like yours, longing for the lost to be saved. Holy Spirit, thank you for coming to fill us with wisdom and understanding from above, so that we can see and understand the signs of God. Please fill us with your presence and lead us to proclaim with joy and confidence the mighty name of Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
