Nursing Home Talks

The Doctor Who Came to Save

Nursing Home Talks · 25 October 2026

Big Idea: God sent Jesus to bring the gospel, and he would not waver before contempt or confusion. Now that we have heard it, we answer with a life given to him.

Friends, it is a joy to be with you this morning.

Many of you have known the Lord Jesus for sixty or seventy years. I come not to teach you something new, but as a fellow servant of the Saviour we share.

Let us open our Bibles, or listen as they are opened, to Luke chapter 4, from verse 16. Before we do, let us pray. Father, we thank you for your word. Soften our hearts and quieten our minds, that we may hear Jesus, and answer him with lives given to you. In Jesus’ name, amen.

1. Jesus did not waver before contempt (vv.16–35)

Jesus returns to Nazareth, the town where he grew up. On the Sabbath he stands in the synagogue, as was his custom, and reads from the prophet Isaiah: “The Spirit of the Lord is on me, because he has anointed me to preach good news to the poor… to proclaim release to the captives and recovery of sight to the blind.”

Then he sits down, and every eye is on him. “Today, as you listen, this Scripture is fulfilled.”

At first they marvel. But then someone says, “Isn’t this Joseph’s son?” They had watched him grow. They had perhaps had their tables mended by his hands. How could he be the One? And Jesus tells them what they are thinking: “Doctor, heal yourself.” Sort yourself out first, before you start telling us who you are.

Many of you know something of this. You have loved a husband or wife, a son or daughter, a dear friend, and prayed for them for many years, and they have shrugged at the gospel. Sometimes it is the people closest to us, who think they know us best, who are slowest to hear. Perhaps you have been gently smiled at, or quietly dismissed, for your faith.

Jesus knows that. He was dismissed by his own neighbours, and they were so angry that they dragged him to the edge of the cliff. Yet he simply walked through the middle of the crowd and went on his way. He did not waver. Not at the cliff in Nazareth, and not later at the cross.

And then, in the synagogue at Capernaum, a man with an unclean spirit cries out, “I know who you are, the Holy One of God!” Notice that. The demons knew exactly who Jesus was. And Jesus silences them with a word, and the man is freed.

So here is our comfort. Jesus is not rattled by contempt, whether it comes from neighbours or from the powers of darkness. If you have prayed for years for someone who has not yet believed, do not lose heart. Their coldness does not make your prayers worthless. Our Lord was refused by his own people, and still his purpose went forward. Your faithful prayers, offered for decades, are not wasted either.

2. Jesus would not stay with those who were confused about him (vv.36–44)

After Nazareth, we see a very different town. In Capernaum, they cannot get enough of Jesus.

Many of you know the anxious wait beside a sickbed, or what it is to be the one who is ill. Jesus is not too busy for this. He bends over one sick woman in one small house, and then the whole town brings its sick to his door.

He goes to Simon Peter’s house, where Peter’s mother-in-law lies with a high fever. Perhaps it was malaria; in those days such a fever could carry a person away. Those who loved her asked Jesus to help. He stood over her, spoke to the fever, and it left her.

But in the morning, Jesus slips away to a quiet place to pray. And the crowds come looking for him, and they try to keep him from leaving. Why wouldn’t they? Here is a Doctor who never fails.

Yet Jesus says, “I must proclaim the good news of the kingdom of God to the other towns also, because I was sent for this purpose.”

Capernaum was right that Jesus could heal. But they were confused about why he had come. He did not come mainly to be their Doctor. He came to be their Saviour. The healings were a sign of something greater: a King whose kingdom will one day have no fevers, no funerals, and no more tears.

This is a gentle word for us in our own frailty. We may not be healed of every ache in this life. Some of us carry pain every day. But we have not been left without help. We have been given the greater gift: forgiveness, and a place in a kingdom where every sickness will one day be undone. We do not love Jesus only for what he gives. We love him because he is our Lord, and because he has given himself for us.

3. Having heard the gospel, we respond with consecrated service (vv.38–39)

Between the contempt of Nazareth and the confusion of Capernaum, we see one right response.

Peter’s mother-in-law is healed, and what does she do? Luke tells us, “She got up immediately and began to serve them.”

She did not serve to earn her healing. She served because she had been healed. Jesus had served her first, and her service was her thank-you.

Notice the difference with the demons. They knew exactly who Jesus was, and they would not serve him. But she knew him, and she served him. Real faith always looks like this. It is grateful, and it gives itself back to the one who gave himself for it.

Now, some of you may be thinking, “I am not able to get out of bed and serve like she did. My legs are weak, my hands tremble, my days are slow.” Dear friends, listen carefully. Service is not measured by what our bodies can do. It is measured by what our hearts give.

When you pray by name for the minister, for the children of the church, for missionaries you have never met, you are serving. When you smile at a nurse who is tired, when you thank a carer, when you tell a visitor about the Lord who has carried you through every season, you are serving. When you hold on to Christ in pain and do not let go, you are preaching a sermon to everyone who watches you. A saint who has walked with the Lord for seventy years and is still trusting him is among the most powerful witnesses a church can have.

We have an old saying in Reformed churches: grace comes first, and service follows. We do not serve in order to be healed. We serve because we have been healed. And you are still serving, in the place where God has put you, in the way that God has given you.

Conclusion

Let us gather up what we have seen.

First, Jesus did not waver before contempt, from people or demons. He walked through the crowd at Nazareth, and he walked all the way to the cross for us.

Second, Jesus would not stay with those who only wanted his gifts. He came to be our Saviour, and he is still, first of all, our Lord.

Third, having heard the gospel, we respond with a life given back to him, in whatever way he has put in our hands today.

Friends, you are not forgotten by the Lord, and you are not forgotten by his church. You are beloved saints. The people of St Paul’s Kogarah pray for you and long to see you, and this nursing home is not a waiting room. It is a place where the King of kings meets his people. “Today, as you listen, this Scripture is fulfilled.” Today you have heard the gospel again. So rise, in heart if not in body, and give him your thanks and your life.

Closing Prayer

Father, we thank you for sending Jesus to be our Lord and Saviour. Forgive us where we have treated him or his word lightly. Forgive us when we have wanted your gifts more than we have wanted you. For those here who are weary, give strength; for those who are in pain, give comfort; for those who are lonely, give the sweet presence of your Son. Bless the families we love and have prayed for over many years, and bring them to know Jesus. Make us servants of Jesus, the Holy One, who loved you perfectly and now loves us. It is in his name we pray, amen.

Questions for Reflection

1. Who have you prayed for over many years that has not yet believed? What encouragement can you take from the fact that Jesus himself was rejected by those closest to him?

2. When you are unwell or in pain, what helps you remember that Jesus is your Saviour and not only your Doctor?

3. Peter’s mother-in-law served because she had been served. In what small ways, perhaps through prayer or kindness, is God giving you opportunities to serve in this season of life?
