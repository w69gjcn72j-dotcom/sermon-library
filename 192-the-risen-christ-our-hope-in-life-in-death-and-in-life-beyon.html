The Risen Christ: Our Hope in Life, in Death and in Life Beyond

1 Corinthians 15:1-34
Big IdeaWe live and die for the risen Christ; we'll rise again because of the risen Christ.

Sermon Title: The Risen Christ – our hope in life, in death and in life beyond

Passage: 1 Corinthians 15.1_34

Big Idea: We live and die for the risen Christ; we’ll rise again because of the risen Christ

We live by faith for the risen Christ

We’ll rise again in Christ the firstfruits

We face death by faith in the risen Christ

Lockdown can be a very lonely time for a lot of people.

To help with the loneliness, some people look for romantic relationships online.

Sadly, there are some con artists online who take advantage of lonely people.

They would begin by sending friendly messages and then they’d try to persuade people to send them money via gift cards.

All in all, in 2020, people over 60 in America lost about $139 million US dollars to romantic scams

There are some people that we should never believe in!

They promised a romantic relationships to earn the victims’ trust.

At the end, the victims put their trust in something that’s utterly worthless.

Sadly, there are some people who identify themselves as Christians whose trust is also utterly worthless.

I’m not talking about Christians who are victims of some devious internet scams.

I’m talking about Christians whose belief is utterly worthless.

The Apostle Paul says that those who believe in this is to be pitied more than anyone.

We should feel most sorry for the Christian who believe this than for the person who fell for the internet scam.

Who is the Christian who has the most pitiable faith?

That’s what we’ll see in today’s passage.

If you have your Bible there, please open up to 1 Corinthians 15, beginning from verse 1.

We’ll see three things in today’s passage:

We live by faith for the risen Christ

We’ll rise again in Christ the firstfruits

We face death by faith in the risen Christ

Before we begin, let’s pray, “Father, please open our eyes so that we may see the wonder and the glory of your Son, the Lord Jesus Christ. Incline our hearts now to fall in worship before our Risen King. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

We live by faith for the risen Christ

Before we look at what the most pitiable faith looks like, we need to look at what real faith is.

Look with me at 1 Corinthians 15:1-2,

“Now I want to make clear for you, brothers and sisters, the gospel I preached to you, which you received, on which you have taken your stand 2 and by which you are being saved, if you hold to the message I preached to you—unless you believed in vain.”

The Apostle Paul wants to remind the Corinthians of the faith that they’ve received from him.

It’s absolutely essential that they hold on to the message for it’s by it that they’ve been saved.

If they don’t hold on to it, they would’ve believed in vain.

If they don’t believe this, they would’ve wasted all their time claiming to be believers.

And Paul’s been building up to this over the past few chapters; indeed, he’s been building this up for most of 1 Corinthians.

Earlier this year, just before we had the lockdown, my family and I started watching this TV series called Loki (洛基)

It’s a show about someone who had to travel through time to solve a mystery.

It was a very exciting show, and our family would get together to watch it once a week.

I remember one night, we were watching an exciting part of the show when suddenly, there was a loud exploding noise.

We were shocked: was that from the show or was it real?

It was real! We could smell smoke coming out of the TV.

And so what did we do?

We turned the TV off and continued watching the show on a smaller screen.

The show was getting really exciting and we didn’t want to miss out!

That’s a little bit like what’s happening at this point in 1 Corinthians.

Ever since chapter five, Paul’s begun to talk about the idea of the body, how we’re to take care of our own body in light of being a Christian.

He’s talked about the Church, the Body of Christ.

He‘s talked about how the Body of Christ is to worship God especially in the last four chapters.

Now in chapter 15, he’s come to the climax of the letter: Christians, the body of Christ, believe in and worship a risen Lord.

The Body of Christ is an important concept throughout the letter of 1 Corinthians and throughout the Bible.

And now, Paul wants us to focus on what happened to the actual physical body of Christ and the implication this has on us, the Church, the spiritual body of Christ.

Paul wants us to focus on the physical resurrection of the body of Jesus Christ.

Look with me at verses 3-5, “For I passed on to you as most important what I also received: that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, 4 that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures,5 and that he appeared to Cephas, then to the Twelve.

This is the summary of the gospel message: Jesus Christ died for our sins and he was raised on the third day, according to the Scriptures.

This is what you’ll find at the heart of Christianity: Jesus died for our sins and rose, according to the Scriptures.

And it wasn’t something that happened in the imagination of people.

Jesus died, rose and appeared to Cephas, that is Peter, and also to the Twelve Apostles.

Jesus then appeared to over five hundred brothers and sisters at one time and to James, the Lord’s brother.

Jesus appeared to many different people and the Corinthians could’ve visited some of these, many of whom were still living by the time Paul wrote to them.

The resurrection of Jesus was a historical event that happened in time and space.

It was real!

What Christians believe is not simply a subjective, individualistic belief.

Christianity is a belief that was based on the historical death and resurrection of Jesus Christ.

It’s the story of Jesus before it’s our story.

The Christian writer Dietrich Bonhoeffer has put it beautifully like this:

It is in fact more important for us to know what God did to Israel and in God’s Son Jesus Christ, than to discover what God intends for us today. The fact that Jesus Christ died is more important than the fact that I will die. And the fact that Jesus Christ was raised from the dead is the sole ground of my hope that I, too, will be raised on the day of judgment. Our salvation is “from outside ourselves.” I find salvation not in my life story, but only in the story of Jesus Christ.

The Christian message is the story of Jesus – his death and his resurrection.

Jesus has also appeared to Paul last of all.

Paul describes himself as one born in the wrong time, not necessarily because he has any issue when he was born but because there was a big issue when he was born spiritually.

When Jesus appeared to Paul, Paul wasn’t a Christian.

In fact, Paul was out chasing after and persecuting Christians.

But Jesus had mercy on Paul and Paul became a follower of Jesus.

Paul didn’t deserve to be saved by God saved him by God’s grace.

As a result, his life changed and he worked hard, very hard, to bring God’s grace to other people as well.

He worked hard to share the gospel with many people, including the Corinthians.

He would travel far and wide to tell them that Jesus died and rose from the dead.

God’s grace has completely transformed Paul.

Isn’t it wonderful to know that God’s grace can completely transform a person, even for someone like Saul who persecuted the Church?

It’s a comforting message for us for we know that God’s grace is so encompassing; By his grace, God can save even the most violent, the vilest of the vile.

No matter how bad we think we might be, God can save us.

No matter how impossible we think it will be for someone to change, God can still transform.

And like Paul, those who are saved and transformed by God’s grace can say this, in verse 10, “But by the grace of God I am what I am, and his grace toward me was not in vain. On the contrary, I worked harder than any of them, yet not I, but the grace of God that was with me.”

Because God’s grace in the risen Lord Jesus, Paul is saved, Paul is transformed.

And so, here’s the first question for this week:

Finish this sentence for me, Because of Jesus is risen, I…..

How have you been transformed because of God’s grace in the risen Lord Jesus?

Message me by whatsapp, wechat or zoom.

Because Jesus is risen, I….

You have twenty seconds.

Apparently, there were some people in the Corinthian church who says that there’s no resurrection of the dead.

Even though the message that Paul’s passed on to them centres on the resurrection of Jesus,

There were still some people who rejected it.

And it’s not surprising.

In Paul’s days, almost no one believed that someone could rise from the dead.

If someone died, they became non-existent.

Only the Jews who believed that the whole Old Testament is God’s word at that time believed in the resurrection.

Even though the concept of resurrection has been preached by Christians, many who aren’t Christians in today’s world don’t believe it either.

The message of resurrection was strange back in the first century; it’s still strange today.

Sadly, even some people who call themselves Christians today don’t believe in the resurrection.

To try to not look strange themselves, some people reject the resurrection.

What would you lose if you reject the resurrection?

Look with me at verses 13-14, “If there is no resurrection of the dead, then not even Christ has been raised; 14 and if Christ has not been raised, then our proclamation is in vain, and so is your faith.”

If you don’t believe in the resurrection, your faith is in vain.

It’s a pointless faith.

To reject resurrection is to walk away from the very heart of Christianity.

If Jesus has not been raised, we have believed in vain; we’ve would’ve wasted our time.

If Jesus has not been raised, then all those who preached about the resurrection were liars, we’re still in our sins, waiting for the judgement of God.

If Jesus has not been raised, those who died would perish.

If Jesus has not been raised and if our hope in Christ is for this life only, then we should be pitied more than anyone.

People should feel sorry for us more than anyone, for we would’ve been duped by the greatest deceit in the history of mankind.

Having a Christian faith without believing the resurrection is the most useless belief someone could ever have.

And yet, there are people who call themselves Christians who don’t believe in the resurrection.

They pick and choose what they believe in the Bible.

They like the part about love but they hate the part about sin.

The love the moral commands but they hate any part that mention anything miraculous, like the resurrection.

Theirs is a pitiful faith.

Some people come to Church only for what they can gain, perhaps financial gain, or even freedom from sickness but they care nothing about the resurrection.

Theirs is a pitiful faith.

Others are here for the intellectual stimulation, still others for friendships.

Their faith, too, is pitiful.

Your faith is pitiful, you believe in vain, even your service at Church is useless, if you don’t believe that Jesus Christ has risen.

And it’s more than just mouth service.

If you do believe in the risen Christ, this belief will shape the way you live.

It’s the risen Christ that gives meaning to Christianity.

I was speaking with Tony about this passage on Friday, and he gave a wonderful illustration.

It’s like taking the wind out of the sail.

Without believing in the resurrection, your faith will be stuck in the ocean, unable to go anywhere.

And certainly unable to go to God.

It’s the risen Christ who gives meaning to Christianity.

Without the risen Christ, Christianity is useless.

Real Christianity is not pitiful but admirable; not useless but precious.

For real Christianity believes in the risen Christ.

Knowing the risen Christ will determine how we’re to live in this life.

Knowing the risen Christ will prepare us for to live again after we come back from the dead.

We’ll rise again in Christ the firstfruits

We’ll rise again in Christ the firstfruits.

Look with me at verse 20, “But as it is, Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep.”

The Bible describes the risen Christ as the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep.

A long time ago, before COVID struck, back in November 2019, there was an auction at Sydney Markets.

It was for the first box of cherries for the season.

The McInerney brothers wholesalers paid $20,000 for the first box of cherries.

This first box of cherries is a celebration and a sign that the season of cherries has begun!

In the Old Testament times, the Israelite would bring the firstfruits of their produce before God and offer them as a sacrifice to God.

The firstfruits they produce was also a celebration and a sign, not just of good fortune, but of what God’s given and a hope for what’s to come.

Jesus, too, is describe as the firstfruits.

He’s the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep.

Just as he’s risen from the dead, those who belong to him will also rise from the dead.

Jesus is the firstfruits of the same event, the same harvest, as it were.

In the death and resurrection of Jesus, the problem of our sin and rebellion against God is resolved.

In Jesus, humanity has a new beginning.

The resurrection of Jesus has implication not only for a small group of people called Christians.

The resurrection of Jesus has implication for the whole human race.

Look with me at verse 21-22, “21 For since deatha came through a man,b the resurrection of the dead also comes through a man.c 22 For just as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive”

The resurrection of Jesus gives us a glimpse of what the new human race will look like.

Jesus, the first person to rise from the dead, never to die again, shows us what the risen life looks like!

Those who have put their trust in Jesus now live a life in light of the new risen life that is to come.

That’s why Paul can say in the next letter to the Corinthians in 2 Corinthians 4:17, “For our momentary light affliction is producing for us an absolutely incomparable eternal weight of glory.”

In light of the eternal glory we have in the risen life, our present troubles are light and momentary.

The risen life that we have in Christ gives meaning and hope in the life that we live today.

Not only is Jesus the firstfruits of the risen life, he is also the King who will rule until the end;

He is the king who will rule until we rise from the dead.

Look with me at verse 24, “Then comes the end, when he hands over the kingdom to God the Father, when he abolishes all rule and all authority and power. 25 For he must reign until he puts all his enemies under his feet. The last enemy to be abolished is death.”

Jesus is the King and he will abolish all rule and all authority and power.

Jesus will triumph over any and every force that opposes God.

The last enemy to be abolished is death and He’s already shown that he’s conquered it.

On that last day, when Jesus comes back, when we rise from the dead, Jesus’ victory will be complete.

On that day, God will put everything under his feet.

Jesus’ victory will be absolute; Jesus’ victory will be complete.

When everything is placed under his feet, Jesus will subject himself to the Father, so that God mat be all in all.

Throughout 1 Corinthians, we’ve seen the importance of God’s order in the world.

On the day of Jesus’ victory, God’s order will be restored.

God’s peace will be experienced completely throughout the new creation, enjoyed by the new humanity.

All rebellion against God will be defeated and God will be glorified.

On that final day, God will be perfectly worshipped and honoured, as the new creation love him with an ever increasing love and praise him in never-ending praise!

That’s what Christians who believe in the death and resurrection of Jesus can look forward to.

Since Jesus is the firstfruit of those who have fallen asleep, we will follow him and model our lives after him.

For after all, if he is the firstfruits, then we should be like him, for we belong to the same harvest.

We will live according to His way and we our life’s goal will according to his life’s goal.

What’s Jesus’ life goal?

To see that God may be all in all, to see that God is glorified.

Johann Sebastian Bach, is one of the most famous musician the world has ever produced.

He’s also a Christian.

Whenever he began a new piece, he bowed his head and prayed. "Jesus, help me show your glory through the music I write. May it bring you joy even as it brings joy to your people."

Before writing even one note, Johann carefully formed the letters J J at the top of the page.

The initials JJ stands for Jesu Juva, which means Jesus help.

When he was finally satisfied, he wrote the letters SDG at the bottom of the page - Soli Deo Gloria - For the Glory of God Alone.

Bach wants to see God may be all in all. He wants God alone to have the glory.

Like Bach, as people who know the risen Christ, who have the hope of the risen life, we want to follow Jesus and we want to see God glorified.

That’s what our life will be about.

It’s a life that reflects our firstfruit, Jesus Christ.

As our firstfruit and as our king, he will lead us triumphantly to rise again.

Since death has now died in Jesus Christ, we now have hope as we face death.

We face death by faith in the risen Christ

Look with me at verse 29, “Otherwise what will they do who are being baptized for the dead? If the dead are not raised at all, then why are people baptized for them?”

Here, Paul is not talking about someone getting baptised on behalf of someone who died.

Rather, it’s someone who’s baptised on account of the dead.

This is possibly someone who believed in Jesus after they’ve seen the death of Christians.

On this side of the final day, Christians still die a physical death.

Sometimes, Christians die as a result of their faith; other times, Christians die of natural causes.

However, when Christians die, they often show a peace not shown by those who don’t have faith.

I remember speaking with a woman who worked as a funeral director.

She noticed the difference between Christians’ death and a non-Christian’s death.

The difference was that she noticed that a Christian often approach death peacefully whereas the non-Christian approach death with fear.

As a result, she wanted to become a Christian because she wanted to know the peace that Christians have at death.

This sister was in a sense baptised because she’s seen the testimony of dying Christians.

And it’s a story that’s repeated in different times in different places.

Since Christians have the assurance of a risen life, we’re not afraid of death.

Paul says that he faces death every day.

As a Christian he would face dangerous situations because of his faith in Jesus.

And countless Christians since Paul’s time have also faced death, sometimes on a daily basis, as a result of their faith in Christ.

Even though it might be dangerous, Christians are willing to put ourselves in dangerous situations for Jesus’ sake.

Of course, we don’t live foolishly, we don’t put ourselves in unnecessary risks.

If anything, too many Christians, myself included, have been too cautious, too afraid to take risks for Jesus’ sake.

May God be merciful and help us to see things in light of eternity, lest we only see things in the here and now.

Those who only see things in the here and now has this motto: Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die.

That’s the cry of someone who has no concept of eternity or the risen life.

The Apostle ends this passage with this warning for the Corinthians:

“Come to your senses, and stop sinning; for some people are ignorant about God. I say this to your shame.”

It’s a sin to not believe in the resurrection.

To not believe in the resurrection is to be ignorant about God, that is, to not know God.

If we’re ignorant of God, we won’t be saved.

However, if we do believe the resurrection, if we believe in the risen Christ, then we do know God and we can look forward to a risen life in Christ.

We can look forward to a life beyond this life.

Friends, Christ is risen!

He is the reason we live; he is the reason we die and he’s the reason we live again.

The Risen Christ is our hope in life, in death and in life beyond death.

Over the past eighteen months, over 733 thousand people in America had died from COVID.

733 thousand people; it’s a tragedy that’s very hard to comprehend.

Many Americans have started to think more seriously about preparing for death.

Many websites that help people think about the last things before death, like the will, had seen a large spike in interest.

Many people are thinking about death, knowing that the possibility of them dying is a lot higher than the days before COVID.

When it comes to planning for your death, the most important step we need to think about is not your will, or what you leave behind.

The most important part is to plan about life beyond death.

Do you know the risen Christ?

If we don’t know him, then everything we do now is in vain.

To know the risen Christ is the most important thing we can do right now as we prepare for death.

Do you live for the risen Christ?

He is the firstfruits of all those who will rise from the dead.

If you belong to him, you will follow him, you will be transformed to be like him.

Your life goal will model after his life goal, which is see that God glorified.

Many of us now are making decisions in light of the COVID situation.

Do they bring glory to God?

Or are we more concerned about bringing glory to ourselves?

Do you live for the risen Christ?

But more than living for the risen Christ, will you die for the risen Christ?

What will you give up for the sake of the risen Christ?

Are you ready to face the ridicule, the hardship, indeed the danger that comes from being a Christian?

Does the death of Christ mean more to you than your own death?

Friends, if you don’t want a pitiful faith, believe and live for the risen Christ.

Put your hope in the risen Christ, who is our hope in life, in death and in the life beyond death.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Do you believe the risen Christ?

Do you live for the risen Christ?

Will you die for the risen Christ?

How do you make sure you don’t have the most pitiful faith?

Trust in the risen Christ.

May the risen Christ be our hope, always.
