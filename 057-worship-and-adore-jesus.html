Sermon Title: Worship and adore Jesus

Passage: Matthew 2:9-15

Big Idea: Worship and adore the Son

Wise men worship Jesus with sacrificial joy

The righteous man protects Jesus with the help of God

A few months ago, my family and I went the Louvre in Paris.

The Louvre, of course, is a museum and is home to many, many incredible artwork from around the world.

The most famous painting, of course, is the Mona Lisa.

Every tourist who has ever visited the Louvre will almost certainly look for the Mona Lisa.

And so our family, got the Louvre map guide, looked for the where-about of the Mona Lisa and headed for it.

At the end, it was pretty easy to find.

It’s the painting where everyone is trying to take a selfie with!

When the wise men came into the city of Jerusalem, they asked for the where-about of the one who has been born King of the Jews.

You can imagine the wise men speaking to one another, ‘are you sure this is the right country?’

‘Yes, this is where the Star has directed us.’

‘But they don’t even know about the new king. Let me see that map again.’

Just like tourists who had to ask locals for directions, these Wise men went and asked the locals where the new King was born.

Herod the Great, the sitting King of the Jews finds out about the wise men.

He consults his own wise men and asks them to search the Bible to see where the Christ, the Chosen One, is to be born.

Herod isn’t doing this to help the Wise men.

Last week, we’ve learnt that he was a phony king; he wasn’t even Jewish!

But as far as he is concerned, he is still the King of the Jews.

And now, these foreign Magi, these wise men, from a distant land comes and asks to worship the newly born King of the Jews.

And so he wants to use the Magi to find out where this new King is.

Once he knows where this new King is, he’s going to kill him.

Herod’s wise men tells him that the Christ, the Chosen One would be born in Bethlehem.

And so Herod sends these foreign Wise Men there.

As we’ll see in today’s passage, these wise men worship Jesus with sacrificial joy

Wise men worship Jesus with sacrificial joy

Look with me at verse 9, ‘9 After hearing the king, they went on their way. And there it was—the star they had seen in the east! It led them until it came and stopped above the place where the child was. 10 When they saw the star, they were overjoyed beyond measure.’

The Star is almost like a heavenly GPS, the heavenly Global Positioning System.

I don’t know about you, but if I need to go somewhere that I haven’t been before, I’ll take out my phone and enter the address on my phone.

My phone, like most phones on the market today, has GPS.

After entering the address in, we just blindly (sometimes too blindly) follow the voice on the phone and it’ll take us there.

That’s all good until the phone is out of battery or if we’ve lost the signal somehow.

And then we’re lost. Has that happened to anyone here?

That’s what happened to the wise men.

They had the ‘signal’ from the Star; the Star pointed them in the right direction.

But once they got to Jerusalem, they seemed to have lost the signal, so to speak.

They didn’t know where to go after they got to Jerusalem.

However, as they start their journey to Bethlehem, the Star reappears!

It’s as if they got their signal again; they now know where they’re going.

And they’re ecstatic about it.

They’re overjoyed beyond measure! Literally, the verse says, ‘they rejoiced exceedingly with a great joy!’

They’re really, really, really, really happy.

There would’ve been a lot of laughter; there would’ve been shouts of joy.

There might have even been tears of joy.

They’re so happy because they are going to find the King of the Jews.

And sure enough, the star leads them to Bethlehem and finally to the house where Jesus and his family stayed.

As they enter the house, they see the Jesus the Child.

It’s possible that Jesus is around two years old by the time the Wise men comes to visit.

When the wise men see Jesus and his mother, they fall on their knees and worship Jesus.

They fall down before Jesus and bow to Jesus.

They don’t worship Mary his mother, just Jesus.

They come to worship Jesus, someone they know who would one day become a great king.

This is the One these wise men have travelled for many months, crossed many deserts to see.

They’ve come to worship and adore Jesus.

However, what they don’t know is that Jesus is more than just a great King.

As the Christmas Carols remind us, He’s the true God of true God, he’s the Godhead veiled in flesh, he’s incarnate Deity. (O Come all ye Faithful, Hark the herald angels sing).

As well as bowing down to Jesus and worshipping him, these wise men also presented Jesus with three gifts.

Traditionally, we associate these three gifts with three wise men but the Bible actually doesn’t say that there were three wise men.

All we’re told is that there are wise men (more than one) with three gifts.

These gifts are gold, frankincense and myrrh.

Gold, of course, is a precious metal.

It’s precious back in New Testament times and it’s precious now.

I don’t know the value of gold back in Ancient Palestine but two days ago gold was being sold at US$40.73 per gram.

Frankincense is the gum derived from the resins of the tree Boswellia.

In the Bible, it’s used as part of the recipe for the incense used on the altar to the God of Israel.

Myrrh is the sap that comes from a small tree found in Arabia, India and a place called Abyssinia.

It’s a mixture of resin, gum and the oil myrrhol.

It’s used for many things, like incense, perfume and even as a strong drink.

When preparing a dead body for burial, the Jews would use myrrh and other spices to hide the smell of the body.

Now I know that in the Christmas Carol ‘We three Kings of Orient’ the three gifts are associated with the Kingship of Jesus, the deity or the Godhood of Jesus and the death of Jesus.

While it’s possible, that’s probably not what Matthew had in mind when he wrote this account.

Gold, frankincense and myrrh were precious back in Jesus’ days and they were appropriate gifts to give to a King.

Now we know gold is precious, but frankincense and myrrh were also very precious back in those days, probably even more precious than gold.

And they wouldn’t have just brought a small amount to Jesus.

I can’t imagine them coming all the way to worship Jesus with just a few specks of gold and a few drops of frankincense and myrrh.

They would’ve brought a substantial amount in order to honour the king.

The gifts would’ve cost them a small fortune but it was worth it.

They’ve come to give Jesus a sacrifice and they’ve done it with an exceeding joy.

It was a joy for them to give sacrificially to Jesus.

These weren’t simple fools who were somehow conned into giving to a religious figure.

These were wise men, men who have great understanding, men who were rich and influential.

These wise men understood the significance of Jesus and gladly gave out of their wealth to worship him.

Today, we have many wise men and women who have read the Bible and who had also understood the significance of Jesus.

For most of his life, Lee Strobel was an atheist.

Strobel was the legal editor for the Chicago Tribune.

He thought that the idea of an all-loving and all-powerful God was stupid.

But then, his wife became a Christian and he thought that he would lose his wife to a religious charity.

In fact, he thought that his marriage was coming to an end.

One day, he went to Church with his wife and heard the gospel of Jesus preached clearly.

He didn’t become a Christian straight away but thought that if Christianity was real, then this would have huge implications for him.

And so he went out and did some serious investigation.

After spending one year and nine months investigating, he decided that it would actually take more faith to be an atheist than to be a Christian.

And so he gave his life to Jesus and trusted him.

Lee Strobel then uses his skill as a journalist to write about and to honour King Jesus.

Lee Strobel might appear wise by the world’s standard, but he became wiser still when he came to know and honour Jesus as his King.

The wise men from the East used the resources available to them to find Jesus.

When they found him, they were filled with joy and were willing to give sacrificial gifts to honour the king.

If you don’t know him yet, use all the resources you have to find out about Jesus.

The best book we have to find out more about Jesus is of course the Bible.

The whole book is ultimately about Him.

If you have already found him, then serve him with a sacrificial joy.

Don’t give him something that costs you nothing.

Give him a gift that honours him as the heaven-born Prince of Peace.

The wise men gave precious gold, frankincense and myrrh.

What will you give him?

As it turns out, it was good for the wise men to give these gifts to Jesus because the life of this peaceful little family is about to change.

The righteous man protects Jesus with the help of God

After the wise men visited Jesus, they were warned in a dream by God to not go back to Herod but to return to their country by another route.

God also sends an angel to Joseph in a dream saying to him, “Get up! Take the child and His mother, flee to Egypt and stay there until I tell you. For Herod is about to search for the child to destroy him.”

The threat to this little family is imminent.

Joseph then got up in the middle of the night, took the Child and His mother and escaped to Egypt.

This little family has become political refugees overnight, trying to escape a phony but tyrannical king.

That’s why it’s good that they have gold, frankincense and myrrh.

They are going to need them to exchange for food and other necessities when they get to Egypt.

Joseph would have taken his family on a 130 km journey from Bethlehem to Egypt, possibly going to a city called Alexandria, which had around one million Jews at that time.

They stayed there until the death of Herod the Great.

This action also fulfils another prophecy from the Old Testament, “Out of Egypt I called My Son.”

This sentence was originally used to describe God saving the Israelites out of Egypt, back in the days of Exodus.

By applying this to Jesus, Matthew was using this to say that the whole nation of Israelites was like a foreshadow of Jesus.

The fact that the nation of Israel, who was symbolically the son of God, came out of Egypt foreshadows the day when Jesus, the true Son of God, will come out of Egypt.

We see this quite often in the Bible.

David, the great king, foreshadows Jesus, the Greatest King.

Jonah’s experience in the fish, which lasted three days, foreshadows Jesus’ death, which lasted three days.

Even the great temple of God, the chosen place where God’s people worships God, foreshadows Jesus, the one true Person in whom we worship God.

The word used to describe these associations between what happened in the Old Testament and the New Testament is called typology.

The nation of Israel was the foreshadow; the person of Jesus is the reality.

When the threat of Herod was over, Joseph took his family to Nazareth.

And that would be the last we’ll hear from Joseph.

Joseph was a righteous man who obeyed God and took the pregnant virgin Mary home as his wife.

He didn’t know Mary intimately until Jesus was born.

Joseph stayed in the background when the wise men came to visit Jesus.

He took care of the family that God’s given him and he protected them with the help of God.

He took the responsibility that God’s given him as the adoptive father of Jesus very seriously.

He must have loved and adored both Mary and Jesus.

It must have given him so much joy to see Jesus growing up.

He wasn’t formally prepared for this. No one told him how to take care of the Messiah.

How do you look after the Son of God?

And yet God chose to use this lowly, humble carpenter, an ordinary man, most probably an ordinary teenager, to look after His only begotten Son.

Jesus, God the Son, would one day be the Greatest King.

But for now, God placed Jesus the Child, small and vulnerable, in the household of righteous Joseph.

For those of us who are fathers, be like righteous Joseph; protect and look after your family.

God’s given you the responsibility; he’s chosen you to look after your family.

Like Joseph, you might not feel adequately prepared.

Like Joseph, you might have to suddenly change your plans in order to look after your family.

But most importantly, be like righteous Joseph, who listens to God and obeys his commands.

God tells Joseph to take his family in one direction and Joseph follows.

This takes us to the parents and grandparents in general.

All of us, whether we’re parents of grandparents, need to follow God’s direction when it comes to leading our families.

Because like Joseph, there’s a sense in which our children don’t belong to us; they ultimately belong to God.

Of course, it’s different in the case of Joseph; Jesus was born in a miraculous way without Joseph and Mary coming together.

Even so, we need to remember that our children are not our properties; they belong ultimately to God.

They are his gifts to us.

God’s chosen to place them in our families and we have the responsibility to teach them God’s ways.p

We’re not our children’s owners; we’re more like their ambassadors.

We don’t take them to where we want them to be; ultimately, we want to take them where God wants them to be.

And so, we’ll look into the Bible for God’s guidance, we’ll pray for God’s guidance and we do our very best to obey God’s guidance.

We won’t always succeed; as sinners, we will fail.

As sinners, our children will fail.

But we thank God for providing Jesus, who came to save and forgive sinners like us and sinners like our children.

As we prepare for the new year ahead, I hope that we’ll be able to help one another do our very best in looking after our children.

I’ve been speaking with the Sunday School teachers, the youth group leaders, the parents in the Mandarin Family Fellowship to see how we can provide a better environment for our children.

And not only for our children, I hope that we can continue to work on our partnership with the Childcare centre so that we can continue to bring many more children and many more families to Christ.

And so, friends, this Christmas, come, let us worship and adore Jesus.

It’s the wise thing to do; it’s the joyful thing to do; it’s the right thing to do.

Let’s pray,

‘Father, we thank you for sending your own Son Jesus into our world. It’s amazing how you would send your one and only Son into the home of an ordinary and humble household like Righteous Joseph’s. Father, we pray that you’ll raise up more households around us like Joseph’s, a household who obeys and follows you. And Father, would you continue to bring wise men into your kingdom, that they may know Jesus and become truly wise, in his name we pray, Amen.’
