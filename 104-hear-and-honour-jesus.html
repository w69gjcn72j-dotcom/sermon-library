Hear and Honour Jesus

John 5.16_30

Big Idea: Hear the voice of the Son and honour him, for He does what the Father does and the Father has made Him Judge.

Jesus equal to God

Jesus does what the Father does

Jesus to be honoured as God is honoured

For many years, one of the most requested songs at funerals was Frank Sinatra’s “My Way”.

Have you heard of the song?

It’s a song about a man who’s about to face the final curtain; he’s near the end of his life.

As he reflects on his life, he congratulates himself at the end of his life by saying,

“To say all the things he truly feels And not the words of one who kneels The record shows, I took the blows But I did it my way”

That was how his judgement on his life.

It was as good life because he did it his way.

And since Frank Sinatra’s time, many people identify with the theme in the song.

If they lived their life their way, then it was a good life.

However, not long after his death, there were many reports of how terrible Sinatra has been to many people, including both men and women.

He had close connections with criminals and the Mafia.

He had broken marriages and countless affairs.

He might have congratulated himself on the life that he lived, but he did it at the expense of many people and burned many relationships.

Many people would condemn what Sinatra did in his lifetime.

When we come to our final curtain, how will we be judged?

Will we be celebrated, or will we be condemned?

How will we be remembered?

That’s what we’ll be exploring in today’s passage.

So please open up to John 5, beginning from verse 16.

We’ll see three things:

Jesus equal to God

Jesus does what the Father does

Jesus to be honoured as God is honoured

Before we do, let’s pray, “Father, please focus our hearts and our minds on your words. By your Spirit, please help us to hear the voice of Jesus, for whose sake we pray, Amen.”

Jesus equal to God

Last week, we read that Jesus had healed the man who had been disable for 38 years.

Jesus healed the man by telling him to get up, pick up his mat and walk.

The man was instantly healed.

However, the Jews began persecuting Jesus because he healed the man on the Sabbath, which was forbidden under their law.

Listen to how Jesus responds to them in verse 17, “My Father is still working, and I am working also.”

Most Jewish Rabbis believed that God, the one who created the universe, must be working all the time.

The universe keeps going because of God’s power.

If God stops working, then the universe would stop because the universe is not self-sustaining.

And since God must work all the time, then God must also work on the Sabbath.

That’s why Jesus’ response to the Jews must have been outrageous response in their ears.

Firstly, Jesus refers to God as his Father.

He’s not referring to God as his Father in the corporate sense, like he’s the Father of everyone.

No, Jesus refers to God as ‘My Father’; God is exclusively Jesus’ Father in a way that’s not shared by any other.

And since God, Jesus’ Father, is working, as you would expect, then Jesus can also legitimately work on the Sabbath as well.

That is, Jesus justifies himself to work on Sabbath because God himself works on the Sabbath.

He’s putting himself in the same league as God!

To say that you’re in the same league as someone means that you belong in the same level as that person.

For example, one of my favourite piano players is someone called Martha Argerich.

She’s been playing piano for many years.

She’s over eighty now but it’s wonderful watching her play.

She has a photographic memory and can reproduce music perfectly after listening to it once.

I can play piano but I’m not in the same league as Martha Argerich!

In the Jewish mind, there is only one God.

Only one God exist and there is only one God who is in the divine league.

However, Jesus has now declared himself to be in the same league as God, the one and only God!

It’s shocking for a Jew to hear it.

That’s why the Jews now want to kill him even more, not only for breaking the Sabbath, but by calling God his own Father, making himself equal to God, putting himself in the same league as God.

However, though Jesus calls God his Father, he’s not putting himself forward as another God or a rival God.

There is only one God.

Christians believe in one God; we don’t believe in two gods or three gods.

We believe that there is one God, and that Jesus is in the same league as God the Father.

In fact, in our statement of faith, we say that we believe one God, the Father Almighty, we believe in one Lord, Jesus Christ and we believe in the Holy Spirit, the Lord, the Giver of Life.

We believe one God in trinity.

We believe that Jesus is God.

Today, there are many people who still object the deity of Jesus.

For example, followers of Islam opposes the deity of Jesus; Jehovah witness opposes the deity of Jesus.

Atheists oppose the deity of Jesus.

That is, they all say that Jesus is not God.

Try saying that Jesus is God in public and see what happens!

You will face oppositions!

And as Christians, we often want to make the Gospel more palatable.

We want to make our message easier to swallow.

And so we look for common grounds and we avoid conflicts.

And of course, there’s a place for that.

However, Jesus here does something extraordinary.

He outright proclaims that he is the Son of God, and knowingly declares his equality with God.

He doesn’t make it more palatable for the Jews and he certainly doesn’t try to correct the Jews’ understanding, because they heard it right!

Jesus is claiming equality with God!

He knows that what he says will be very hard for his Jewish hearers to accept but he says it anyway because it’s true.

It’s shocking, but it’s true.

I still remember many years ago door knocking in a suburb in Sydney.

We knocked on many doors and didn’t get a lot of invitations to talk.

However, when we came to one of the doors, it just so happens to be the home of a Jehovah Witness.

We spent almost an hour at the door talking about the deity of Jesus.

My friend and I affirmed the deity of Jesus, but the Jehovah Witness rejected the deity of Christ.

It was a friendly and yet robust discussion!

Friends, some of our non-believing neighbours and relatives do have an opinion of who Jesus is and whether he’s God or not.

As believers, we must think about it as well.

If someone asks you who Jesus is, how would you answer?

How would you demonstrate that he is God?

To start, let’s listen to how Jesus explains his identity to us.

Jesus does what the Father does

Look with me at verse 19, “Jesus replied, “Truly I tell you, the Son is not able to do anything on his own, but only what he sees the Father doing. For whatever the Father does, the Son likewise does these things.”

Jesus doesn’t do anything on his own; he doesn’t act independently of God the Father.

He only does what he sees the Father does.

Jesus’ actions are completely dependent on the Father’s actions.

That is, Jesus won’t do anything that his Father wouldn’t do and will only do what he sees his Father does.

Whatever the Father does, the Son also does.

Jesus the Perfect Son, reflects the Father perfectly.

His Sonship is perfect.

And yet, look at what he does!

He does what God the Father does!

To be able to do what the Father does must mean that he’s as great and as powerful as the Father himself!

The Father creates, the Son also creates.

The Father saves, the Son also saves.

The Father raises the dead, the Son also raises the dead.

If you want to know who the Father is, look at the Son, look at Jesus!

Jesus reveals who the Father is because Jesus as God the Son does exactly what the Father does.

The New Testament scholar Don Carson puts it succinctly like this:

“The Father initiates, sends, commands, commissions, grants; the Son responds, obeys, performs his Father’s will, receives authority.”

More than that, God the Father loves the Son and shows him everything that he is doing.

And so, in Jesus, we see who God truly is for God has shown Jesus everything.

In Jesus, we see the true revelation of God the Father himself.

In the words and deeds of God the Son, we see God the Father.

That’s the basis of our knowledge of God.

Our knowledge of God is based on the love God the Father has for God the son.

In showing him everything that he does, the Son reveals a true revelation of God to us.

If God the Father doesn’t love God the Son, we would not have a true revelation of God at all.

It’s through Jesus God the Son that we come to know God the Father.

And yet, Jesus is more than a channel through which we know God.

Jesus isn’t just a revealer of God the Father, even though He is the greatest revealer of God the Father.

Jesus is also the life-giver.

Look with me at verse 21, “And just as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so the Son also gives life to whom he wants.”

Just as the Father can give life by raising the dead, the Son can also give life to whom he wants.

See also verse 26, “For just as the Father has life in himself, so also he has granted to the Son to have life in himself.”

Jesus is more than a channel of life; Jesus has life in himself just as the Father has life in himself.

We humans derive our life from God; our life comes from God.

Without God, we won’t exist.

But Jesus is different.

Just as God the Father has life in himself and can give life to others, God the Son is not reliant on God the Father to be alive.

Jesus also has life in himself and can also give life to others.

Even with the most advance technology, humans can only make things that look like life.

We can make robots that act like they’re alive, we can make programmes that sound like they’re alive.

But we can’t make life; we can’t make a dead thing live.

Jesus, however, has life in himself.

He can give life; he can make dead things, even dead people, live.

Since Jesus has life in himself and since he’s the One who gives life, it makes sense that Jesus is also the one who judges.

Look with me at verse 22, “The Father, in fact, judges no one but has given all judgment to the Son”.

Unlike the verses before, where we see the parallel between the Father and the Son, here we see a distinction between the Father and the Son.

The Father judges no one; he lets the Son judge.

It’s the Son who gets to decide who is good and who is wicked, who lives and who dies.

The role of the Life-giver and the role of the Judge is tightly related.

Since Jesus is the Life-giver, it follows that he also has the authority to judge.

That’s because if Jesus has the authority to give life, he also has the authority to withhold life.

And that’s what a Judge does – a judge has to decide whether a person is righteous or wicked.

That’s the authority the Father has given to the Son.

The Son decides who live sand who doesn’t.

Look with me at verses 28-29, “Do not be amazed at this, because a time is coming when all who are in the graves will hear his voice and come out—those who have done good things, to the resurrection of life, but those who have done wicked things, to the resurrection of condemnation.”

One day Jesus will speak and all those in the grave would rise at his voice to face either a resurrection of life or a resurrection of condemnation.

This is what the prophet Daniel prophesied in Daniel 12:2, “Many who sleep in the dust of the earth will awake, some to eternal life, and some to disgrace and eternal contempt.”

One day, on that great final day, we all must give an account to Jesus for what we’ve done with the life he’s given us.

For those who have done good things, it will be a resurrection of life.

For those who have done wicked things, it will be a resurrection of condemnation.

On that day, Jesus will call and Albert Einstein will rise up, Napoleon will rise up, Fa Mulan will rise up, Emperor Qin will rise up, Hitler will rise up, your families will rise up, you will rise up.

We will all rise up to face Jesus.

Pastor John Piper has put this well, “He lets no one go out of existence—there is no such loss for the righteous, and no such hope for the wicked. Jesus raises them all”

Everyone will rise at the voice of Jesus.

What will we rise to? A resurrection of life or a resurrection of condemnation?

It will all depend on Jesus the Judge.

Earlier this year, Novak Djokovic was preparing to come to Australia for the Australian Open Tennis championship.

However, three Australian Federal Court judges ordered that Djokovic be deported on the advice of the Immigration Minister.

The Judges along with the Immigration Minister decided that Djokovic was not eligible to stay and exercised their power to deport the World Number one tennis player out of Australia.

On Judgement Day, it will be Jesus who decides whether you will rise to life or rise to condemnation.

There will be no appeals, there will be no place to wait.

Jesus’ decision will be final; it will be one or the other.

God has made Jesus the Judge and he will decide what will happen to each one of us for all eternity.

How does Jesus decide our fate?

Jesus to be honoured as God is honoured

It all depends on how we respond to his voice.

Look with me at verse 24, ““Truly I tell you, anyone who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life and will not come under judgment but has passed from death to life.”

That’s what takes one person from judgement to life.

The person who hears the word of Jesus and believe the One who sent Him, that is God the Father, has eternal life.

The verdict is made, he has eternal life, he’s passed from death to life.

It’s not just something that might happen in the future if he follow the rules.

He has eternal life and has passed from death to life when he hears Jesus’ word and believes the Father.

Our response to Jesus’ voice now is what makes the difference.

Earlier this week, I had a conversation with a friend who told us about a member of his church who was in hospital.

This church member was working in a factory when an explosion occurred by accident.

Even though he was burning with pain, he ran and shouted to everyone around him, “Get out, close the door behind you! There’s been an explosion!”

My friend said that this church member had probably saved a lot of people because if they stayed behind, they would’ve been badly hurt as well.

By listening to this Church member’s voice, many people left the factory safely.

By listening to Jesus’ voice, we will safely survive the Judgement day.

Look with me at verse 25, ““Truly I tell you, an hour is coming, and is now here, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live.”

Last week, we read that at the voice of Jesus, the legs of the disabled man were healed.

Just as the dead legs were made alive at the voice of Jesus,

those who had been spiritually dead will hear the voice of the Son of God and live.

Jesus is the One who gives life to the lifeless.

Even though our hearts may be hearts lifeless hearts of stones, Jesus can make them live.

We live when we hear and believe the word of Jesus.

At the resurrection, when we hear the voice of Jesus, those of us who have heard and obeyed Jesus will rise to continue to live and obey the word of Jesus.

We will rise to live.

But those who had refused to listen the voice of Jesus in this life will one day rise at the voice of Jesus, against their will.

They will rise to be condemned; they will rise to everlasting destruction.

This doesn’t mean that our salvation will be by our good works, the goods that we’ve achieved by ourselves.

Rather, the good things referred to here is the things that we do in obedience to Jesus.

After all, how do we define a deed as good?

A deed is good if it brings honour to Jesus.

That’s why God made Jesus both the Life-giver and the Judge.

Look with me at verse 23, “so that all people may honor the Son just as they honor the Father. Anyone who does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him.”

God the Father wants the Son to be honoured just as he is honoured.

A deed is good if it brings honour to Jesus and if it brings honour to Jesus, it honours God.

A deed is wicked if it doesn’t bring honour to Jesus for it doesn’t bring honour to the Father who sent Him.

If we live a life that doesn’t bring honour to Jesus, we will one day rise to face Jesus the Judge, and he will condemn us for our wickedness.

However, if we live a life trusting Jesus, seeking to bring him honour, we can look forward to a day when we will rise to a new life, at the sound of his voice,

This doesn’t mean that we will have a perfect life or a sinless life, but it will mean that we will actively seek to honour Jesus,

Asking for forgiveness when we have failed,

Asking for strength when we’re weak,

Seeking to bear good fruit for him, longing to live a life that honours him.

Jesus speaks through his Word the Bible, but how have we been responding to his voice?

A lot of people say that the Bible is hard to understand and don’t know where to start.

Yes, there are parts of the Bible that can be hard to understand but often it’s not those parts that we have problems with.

As the author Mark Twain famously quipped, “It ain’t those parts of the Bible I can’t understand that bother me; it’s the parts I do understand.”

Often, it’s not that the Jesus is hard to understand; often it’s us who refuse to listen.

Start by reading and listening to the part that you do understand but are not obeying in.

Ask Jesus for forgiveness and to help you obey.

Honour Jesus by asking him to help you obey his voice.

That’s how we give true honour to God.

We honour God as we honour Jesus.

And we honour Jesus when we listen and obey his voice.

As we obey his voice, we will live a good life of faith.

And so, Hear the voice of the Son and honour him.

Jesus does what the Father does; he reveals God the Father truly to us.

God the Father has made Him both the Life-giver and the Judge.

Listen to him!

What does this mean for us?

Hear Jesus’ voice, honour Him with your life.

Who gets the greatest honour in your life?

Who gets the final say in your decision?

If your answer is anything or anyone other than Jesus, then you’ll need to think whether you are giving him the honour that is his due.

For on the last day, there’s only one Person’s decision that matters, there’s only one Person’s honour that matters.

That person is the person whom God the Father wants all people to honor.

That Person is Jesus.

Hear Jesus’ voice, honour Him with your life.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Do I know Jesus as God the Son, the One who is equal to God?

Do I honour Jesus as God?

How have I been responding to the voice of Jesus in God’s word the Bible?

Friends, Hear Jesus’ voice, honour Him with your life.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for Jesus. You have granted him life in himself. You have granted him all judgement. You love him and showed him everything that you are doing. In Him, we come to know you truly. Father, please forgive us when we don’t honour Jesus as we should. Please forgive us for our wickedness. By your Spirit, please turn our ears and our hearts toward Jesus. Produce in us fruits of repentance that bring honour and glory to you, the Son and the Holy Spirit. Amen.”
