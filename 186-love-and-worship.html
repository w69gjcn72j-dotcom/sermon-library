Sermon Title: Love and Worship

Passage: 哥前13.1_13

Big Idea: Love gives meaning to worship at Church for it reflects God’s perfection among us

Love gives meaning to our gifts

Love reflects God and rejects wrong

Love is God’s perfection on the last day and beyond

It’s been a long time since I went to the shops to buy cards.

But when I do, I would often see cards that says something like this:

“You’re special”

It might be for a Father’s Day, “You’re a special Dad”

Or it might be a valentine Day’s Card, “You’re special to me”

There are so many cards for special people!

However, I haven’t seen a card for an ordinary person

Imagine getting to for Father’s Day, “You’re an ordinary Dad”.

Or on a Valentine Day’s Card, “You’re just ordinary”!

They probably won’t sell too many because we want to be special.

We all want to be somebody special.

No one wants to be irrelevant.

No one wants to be a nobody.

That’s why it’s so shocking to see this phrase in today’s passage, “I am nothing”.

Paul’s here not talking about being humble.

He’s saying that it’s possible for him, the apostle of Jesus Christ, gifted in so many ways, to have done it all for nothing,

to have gained nothing,

to be a nobody after all that he’s said and done.

It’s possible for him to be nothing.

How is it that the gifted and talented Apostle Paul, after all he’s done for God, to be nothing, to be unimportant?

If it’s possible for Paul, is it possible for us?

Can we be a nobody, someone whose life means nothing?

What must we do to stop ourselves from being a nobody? A nothing?

That’s what we’ll see in today’s passage.

Today, in 1 Corinthians 13, we’ll see three things:

Love gives meaning to our gifts

Love reflects God and rejects wrong

Love is God’s perfection on the last day and beyond

Before we do, let’s pray, “Father, by your Spirit please prepare our hearts to hear from your words, in Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”

Love gives meaning to our gifts

Love gives meaning to our gifts.

Over the last weeks, we’ve been looking at 1 Corinthians 12 where we heard what the Apostle Paul has to say about gifts. 1 Corinthians 14 will also be about gifts.

In the middle of these two chapters, we have a chapter that seems to talk a lot about love.

While that’s true, it’s in the context of Paul talking about gifts.

Just like a hamburger with the bun, meat and bun, we have three sections in these three chapters:

Chapter 12 on gifts, chapter 13 on love and gifts and chapter 14 on gifts.

But if we look at it a bit more closely, we’ll see that chapter 13 has three sections within it as well.

There’s a section on gifts without love from verses 1-3, then a section on love from verses 4-7, finishing off with a section on the superiority of love over gifts.

If we use the hamburger analogy a bit further, it’s like seeing the buns on the top and bottom of the burger,

Then we have two pieces of lettuces, one at the top and one at the bottom,

And then we have the meat in the middle.

And we can go even further, the meat in the middle, the love section in verse 4-7, is itself made of three sections:

What love is in verse 4a, what love isn’t from verse 4b to verse 6a, then followed by what love is from verse 6b to 7.

Again, to push the burger analogy further, the meat patty looks inviting on the outside and tastes juicy on the inside.

1 Cor 12-14 has a beautiful structure to it, much like a well-made burger.

And today, we’ll be looking at the meat of the burger, 1 Corinthians 13.

If you have your Bible with you, please open up with me to 1 Corinthians 13, beginning from verse 1,

“If I speak human or angelic tongues but do not have love, I am a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal.”

For the past chapter, we’ve seen that God’s given many gifts of the Church.

God’s given them for the common good of the Church.

He’s given these gifts to them to show that He’s with them.

The members of the Church are to use God’s gifts together when they come before him as the Church.

They are to use God’s gifts to worship Him.

To use these gifts properly for God, the members of His Church must understand what love is.

We read throughout the Bible that God is a loving God.

In fact, in 1 John 4:16 we read, God is love.

In order to worship God, we must come to understand and have experienced for ourselves what God’s love is.

It’s God’s love that inspires and motivates us to worship him.

There’s not point in worshipping God and glorifying God if we don’t have love.

Paul says that even if we speak human or angelic tongues but don’t have love, we are a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal.

Here, we have a cymbal (Ezekiel bring out cymbal)

It’s an important part of the drum it.

Together with the rest of the drum kit, it makes a beautiful sound.

But by itself, it can’t do much.

It just makes loud noises.

Paul compares a person who has the gift of human and angelic tongues but has no love with the clanging cymbal.

To be able to speak in a human tongue is a wonderful gift; to speak in multiple human tongues is a marvellous gift.

And I’m so glad to have different brothers and sisters here who can speak multiple human tongues or languages.

But to be able to speak in an angelic tongue, sounds like a incredible gift to have.

We don’t know exactly what Paul’s referring to; perhaps it’s a language spoken by angelic beings.

Or it could a hyperbole, an extreme example, that Paul’s using.

Whatever it is, Paul’s saying that even if we have these incredible gifts of tongues, I am as useless as a clanging cymbal if I don’t have love.

The same goes for mysteries and knowledge.

Verse 2, “If I have the gift of prophecy and understand all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I have all faith so that I can move mountains but do not have love, I am nothing.”

This is definitely a hyperbole here, for even the great Apostle Paul can’t claim to understand all mysteries and all knowledge.

And as faithful as Paul is, he can’t claim to have all faith to move mountains.

But, for the sake of argument, let’s say that Paul has the gift of prophecy and can understand all knowledge and understanding and all faith.

Even you have these, if you don’t have love, you are nothing.

Notice that Paul’s not saying that these gifts are nothing.

These gifts of prophecy, understanding, knowledge and faith are wonderful, essential even, for the Christian church.

But even if you have these, even all of these, but not love you are nothing.

Paul goes further. Look with me at verse 3, “And if I give away all my possessions, and if I give over my body in order to boast but do not have love, I gain nothing.”

One of the highest good we can do for others is to sacrifice ourselves for them.

There have been extraordinary men and women who have given themselves for the good of others.

Soldiers, nurses, doctors, rescue workers, charity workers are people who regularly have given themselves for the good of others.

There have been even examples of Christians who have sold themselves as slaves in order to ransom those who were slaves.

They would give themselves up, give up their possessions, their freedom, their bodies for the good of others.

But Paul says that even if they give themselves up sacrificially, but they don’t have love, they gain nothing.

You can have all these wonderful gifts but if you don’t have love, you are nothing, you gain nothing, you’re a clanging cymbal.

Love is essential for all these gifts because it’s love that gives them meaning.

Without love, all these gifts will lose their meaning.

Without love, God won’t call you a good and faithful servant.

Without love, our life will be meaningless, even we’ve spent our lives in the service of God.

It’s like singing a love song to someone without actually loving them.

It might sound lovely but it’s not love.

The same goes for singing a hymn of a Christian song at church.

It might sound lovely, but if you don’t love God and if you don’t love his people, it’s meaningless, it’s nothing more than a clanging cymbal.

Because without love, it’s impossible to worship God.

Love is essential to worship.

We need love to worship God.

What is this love?

It’s a love that reflects God and rejects wrong

Love reflects God and rejects wrong

Here, we have the meat of our burger, so to speak.

To truly worship, we must truly love.

To truly love, we must turn to God, who is love; he is the source of love and the goal of love.

I still remember many years ago, Jenny and I visited this theme park in China called the Window to the World.

It had many replicas of well-known places around the world, like the Eiffel Tower and Mount Fuji.

It was great to see so many replicas in one place.

Many years later, we visited the actual Mount Fuji and the Eiffel Tower.

They were spectacular!

While the replicas were great, the real thing was far more impressive, far more memorable.

There are many examples of love in this world.

We see different examples of love in different cultures and in different religions.

But these examples of love are like the replicas.

Most of the time they’re good but if you want to see the real thing, if you want to know what love truly is, you have to go to the Creator of love and the ultimate goal of love.

You have to come back to God himself.

In his description of what love is, Paul begins by pointing out characteristics that have often been used to describe God.

Love is patient, love is kind.

God is patient with sinners like us, time and time again.

God is kind with those who are injured and hurt, the poor and the depressed.

God is there for us even when no one else is.

God is patient and kind and that’s what love is.

Love is how God deals with his people, patient, and kind.

We then see eight things that love isn’t.

Have a look at the list of what love isn’t and let me know which one stands out to you.

You can send in your answer by whatsapp, wechat or zoom.

What’s one thing about what love isn’t that stands your to you?

You have thirty seconds.

What’s not love?

Love does not envy.

Love doesn’t want and desire something that another person has.

Love is not boastful either.

That is, love doesn’t boast about what you have that another person doesn’t have. You can’t boast and love at the same time.

Love is not arrogant; it doesn’t puff up with pride.

Love doesn’t behave improperly, it’s not rude.

That is, it doesn’t do shameful things.

Love is not self-seeking; love is not self-fulfilment.

Love is not about what makes you happy, what gives you satisfaction.

Love is not someone who says, “I like this Church because of what it does for me.”

That’s self-fulfilment and not love.

Love is not irritable; it doesn’t easily blow up in anger; it’s not always on the edge.

Love doesn’t keep a record of wrongs; it doesn’t hold grudges against those who might have wronged her.

Love will find no joy in unrighteousness.

To allow a child to act wrongly is not love; it’s spoiling the child.

To allow an adult to act wrongly is not love; it’s enabling a wrongdoer to keep doing wrong.

Instead, love rejoices with the truth; love is someone who is delighted to see truth.

That’s what love isn’t.

In contrast to these, love is like God; love is patient and kind.

Love also bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things.

There are two pairs here: Love bears all things and endures all things.

Love doesn’t give up and love doesn’t give in.

Love keeps going, love keeps loving because God himself keeps loving and God tells us to keep loving.

Love believes all things and hopes all things.

This doesn’t mean that love believes everything but, in all things, someone who loves believes and hopes in God always.

Someone who truly loves always believes in God and always hopes in God.

Someone who truly loves know that God will keep his word and that God can always be trusted.

And so they keep on believing and they hoping in God in all things.

Theirs is a love that will never end.

A few years ago, a movie came out by the name of Unbroken.

It’s a story about “Louis "Louie" Zamperini”, who was an Olympic runner who became a pilot in the US army.

In World War 2, his plane was shot down and he was taken as a prisoner of the Japanese army.

He remained resilient throughout his time as a prisoner and was eventually rescued.

How did he stay strong throughout?

I would want to say that it was his faith but he wasn’t a Christian when he was captured by the Japanese.

However, the movie had a scene that suggested a lesson early in life might have something to do with it.

While Zamperini wasn’t a Christian, his family was, and they took him to Church.

There was a scene where the pastor preached about the creation, how God made the greater light over the day and the lesser light over the night.

And the Pastor preached, “You must live through the night. Don’t battle with it…God sent Jesus to forgive sin. Accept darkness. Live through the night. Love thine enemy.”

We don’t know the exact words that the Pastor preached, but it’s a great reminder that God made both the day and the night.

God made the times when we can see things clearly and the times when we can’t.

God made the good times and the bad times.

Even the night, even the bad times, are under God’s control.

So, live through night.

That’s the hope that Christians have.

We believe that God made the day and the night, even the dark nights in our souls.

And when we do meet the darkness that comes, we will keep trusting God, we will keep hoping in God.

Because we know that even the darkness we experience can’t separate us from God’s love.

And so, even in the darkness, we will hope, we will trust and we will love.

Even in our darkness, we will continue to love according to God’s love.

Our love is like the moon, reflecting God’s love in the darkness.

Though we can’t see everything yet, though the full day hasn’t come yet, we who have experienced God’s love in Christ will reflect God’s love to the world.

We will love according to God’s love as we reflect on the glory of the day that is to come.

We love because the Day is coming.

Love is God’s perfection on the last day and beyond

Love is God’s perfection on the last day and beyond.

Look with me at verses 8-10,

“Love never ends.a But as for prophecies, they will come to an end; as for tongues, they will cease; as for knowledge, it will come to an end. 9 For we know in part, and we prophesy in part, 10 but when the perfect comes, the partial will come to an end.”

This is my Digital Piano; I’ve had this for almost 25 years.

I’m still glad that it’s working after all these years.

It wasn’t always the case though.

There was a time when some of the keys weren’t working.

It was frustrating to have a partially working piano.

And so, I took it to get fixed.

After a few weeks at the repair shop, I got it back with all keys working.

It was good to have it back!

It was like having a new piano again!

Prophecies, the gift of tongues and knowledge will come to an end.

As important as these gifts are, these have an expiry date.

There will come a time when we won’t be using them anymore.

The prophet Jeremiah tells us that there will come a day when no one will teach his brother or neighbour, saying “know the Lord” for they will all know me, from the least to the greatest of them.

There will come one day when people who are in Jesus Christ will know the Lord.

God will make himself clear to us.

Our knowledge of God will be like night and day.

What we know of him now, as wonderful and as true as that knowledge is, for we know him through Jesus His Son,

Even this knowledge won’t be comparable with what will know of him on that final day.

It’s like the knowledge of a child and the knowledge of a man.

Look with me at verse 11, “11 When I was a child, I spoke like a child, I thought like a child, I reasoned like a child. When I became a man, I put aside childish things.”

There was a time when what we’ve understood about the world as a child is appropriate but there a time will come in our lives when we put aside a childish approach to our world.

What we know about God now may be true, but a time will come when what we know about God will be far more superior than what we know of God now.

And that day will come when Jesus comes back.

Right now, we see only a reflection as in a mirror, but then face to face.

Our knowledge of God is like our knowledge of each other through Zoom.

I can see some of your faces and have some knowledge of you.

For many of you, I don’t even get to see your faces.

And so I look forward to the day when I see you in person, so that we can talk face to face, so that I can get to know you better.

It’ll be far more superior to zoom, as good as zoom is.

One day, Christians will come to know God face to face.

It will be far superior to any knowledge we have of him now.

This is how Paul describes our knowledge of God on that day: Now I know in part, but then I will know fully, as I am fully known.

I will know God fully as he fully knows me now.

That’s what love on the last day and love from that day on will be like.

God knows me fully and I know God fully.

That’s love.

And so, verse 13, “Now these three remain: faith, hope, and love—but the greatest of these is love.”

Faith, hope and love are known as the pillars of the Christian faith.

And the greatest of these is love for God is love and that’s how those who are his will relate to him for the rest of eternity.

That’s why love is so essential in the Church.

Love in the Church is a glimpse of heaven.

In heaven, we won’t be living individualistic lives but in perfect harmony with one another.

We will live as sinless, perfect people in God’s presence.

We will live in love in the presence of God, who is love.

But we don’t need to wait until heaven to start living in a loving relationship with one another.

We start that loving relationship with one another here, now, in Church.

We won’t be perfect at it on this side of heaven.

Indeed, we should have a holy dissatisfaction because we know that we’re not at loving as we should be.

But we won’t be doing it arrogantly by pointing out at how unloving other people are.

We’ll start from ourselves, our dissatisfaction will be with ourselves first.

We will ask, “How can I love more? How can I be more patient? How can I be kind?”

How do I reflect God’s love more?

I look forward to the day when we will see God face to face.

I look forward to the day when I will know him fully even as I’m fully known.

I look forward to the day when I, together with all those who belongs to Jesus, can love God fully and sinlessly.

But until that day, let us not give up on love.

When we’re tempted to be unloving, think of God’s love for us in Jesus Christ.

A genuine Christian can’t help but love God’s way.

God has given us the hope of loving him for all eternity; we will have a holy yearning for God’s love to be expressed among us in the here and now.

That’s why love is so essential to the Church.

It’s love that gives meaning to all the gifts that God’s given us at Church.

It’s love that gives meaning to our worship of God at Church.

When we love, we reflect among us God’s perfection, what God’s prepared for us in heaven.

What does this mean for us?

Be loving or be nothing.

Just as a torch that doesn’t shine in the darkness is useless,

A Christian who doesn’t love is useless.

And so, don’t be nothing. Don’t be a useless Christian.

God might have given us wonderful gifts but unless we have God’s love, they are all useless.

By all means, use your gifts for God’s glory, use your gifts for the common good, but remember to use your gifts according to God’s love.

There’s nothing quite as repulsive as a loveless singer or a loveless Church member, a loveless Christian,

Who falsely professes to follow the God who loves.

His profession will be false because the one who follow the God who loves will love.

Be loving or be nothing.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Do I have love? That is, a love for God and a love for his people?

How does my love reflect God’s love?

Do I have a holy dissatisfaction with my love?

Friends, don’t be nothing, be loving.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for your love for us. We thank you that in Jesus Christ you’ve demonstrated your love for us, that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. Father, by your Spirit, please impress your love upon us. Please let your love be expressed among us. Forgive us for our lack of love. Make us a loving people, who loves and trusts and hopes in you in a dark and fallen world. Let us keep loving until the day of our Lord Jesus comes, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
