As our kids grow older, there are now more and more nights when it’s just me and Jenny left at home.

And so, that means there are more nights when we can go out on date nights.

Last week, Jenny and I went to a Korean BBQ for dinner.

Anyone else had Korean BBQ before?

The Korean BBQ is already very nice but what made this even better was that the waitress would come and cook for us.

Yes! Instead of us putting the meat on the grill, the Korean waitress put it on for us.

She would go around serving different customers then come back to check on the meat.

She would change the grill every time she put new meat on, and she knew exactly when the meat was ready.

And she did it cheerfully as well!

It was a great experience.

The food was great, and the waitress did an excellent job waiting on our table.

In the passage we had just read from where it talks about church in the first century, we have this curious phrase, “Wait on tables”.

In other words, there were waiters in the first century Church.

Who were these waiters?

Why were these waiters there?

What makes a good waiter in God’s Church?

Cultural tension in a growing Church

Please open to Acts 6 verse 1 with me, “In those days, as the disciples were increasing in number, there arose a complaint by the Hellenistic Jews against the Hebraic Jews that their widows were being overlooked in the daily distribution.”

The Church in Jerusalem was growing, as Jews who live in Jerusalem as well as Jews who had come from far away heard the gospel, believed the gospel and came together as Church.

However, as the Church grew, there were growing cultural tension within the Church.

You see, there were Jews who were Hellenistic, who spoke Greek primarily.

And there were Jews who were Hebraic, who probably could speak Greek, but only as a second language.

These Hebraic Jews would’ve spoken Aramaic as their heart language.

Most of us would have experienced this in our own context.

For example, when I came to Australia, I came as someone who spoke Chinese Cantonese.

For many years, I was like the Hebraic Jew, I spoke English, but my heart language was Chinese Cantonese.

Over time, I met many friends who are Chinese but they grew up mainly with English.

They’re like the Hellenistic Jew: though they look Chinese, English was their heart language.

Last week, I spoke with Steaphen, Mercy and Gordon and found that there’s an extra level for those friends who are from India.

There are many languages in India, including Tamil, Gujarati, Hindi and many more, but they would often use English to communicate even among themselves.

From a distance, the Chinese might look the same and the Indians might look the same but go a little deeper and you realise there are a lot of differences inside of each people group.

In the first century Church, the Greek-speaking Jews felt that they were discriminated against.

They said that their widows were overlooked in the daily provision, that they weren’t provided for.

Widows, of course, were very vulnerable people in the first century because they depended on their husbands to provide for them.

Now notice that nobody was disputing the complaint; nobody said that the Hellenistic Jews were overreacting.

It was very probable that the Hebraic Jews were discriminating against the Hellenistic Jews.

Who did they take the complaint to? The apostles!

In chapter four, we read that the Church was selling what they had and brought the money to the apostles.

They trusted the apostles with their money and now they’re saying that the apostles hadn’t distributed the money evenly.

They were implicitly saying that the apostles were responsible, and they had to do something about it.

More than that, they were implicitly challenging the apostles’ authority, just like the Israelites challenged Moses when they had just left Egypt.

Do you remember what the Israelites said to Moses, just weeks after God led them out of Egypt through Moses?

“The entire Israelite community grumbled against Moses and Aaron in the wilderness. 3 The Israelites said to them, “If only we had died by the LORD’s hand in the land of Egypt, when we sat by pots of meat and ate all the bread we wanted. Instead, you brought us into this wilderness to make this whole assembly die of hunger!” Exodus chapter 16:2-3

Just like the Israelites in the wilderness, the new disciples in the Jerusalem Church was grumbling about food.

Not only were they grumbling against the Church leaders; they were grumbling against God.

Listen to how the Apostles’ responded in verse 2, “The Twelve summoned the whole company of the disciples and said, “It would not be right for us to give up preaching the word of God to wait on tables.”

The apostles didn’t say waiting on tables is a bad thing.

In fact, waiting at tables, making sure people are being fed and looked after is a good thing.

It is good for the apostles to serve as waiters, providing for the physical needs of the Church, BUT not at the cost of the word ministry.

The Word Ministry has priority.

This is what church is about; this is the heart of the apostles’ ministry.

It’s like this: you probably have been to very small restaurants where the chef is also the waiter.

However, this only works for a little while, for one to two customers.

Once you get more than two customers, you can’t be both waiter and chef.

You can’t serve at the table AND cook the food without one or the other suffering.

You can’t be both waiter and chef if you want to grow your restaurant.

This applies to the Church as well.

As we grow, you can’t expect the Bible teachers to also look after the physical needs of the church.

The Church needed to look after both the spiritual and the physical needs of the Church, but as people tasked with teaching God’s word, Bible teachers, like the apostles, mustn’t neglect that responsibility.

In the Church setting, the pastor and the Bible teachers are like the chef and the sou chefs, assistant chefs.

As teachers of God’s word, we prepare by reading God’s word, by praying, by studying, by training ourselves through groups like the School of Christian Living.

We prepare the talk, we prepare the lesson, we prepare the meal.

Of course, we can also serve in other capacities as well, but if we’re so busy doing other good things around the Church and neglect the task of properly preparing the Bible lesson, then the Church will be in huge trouble.

The Church will then be like a restaurant that’s good at serving food, people might even feel welcomed there but the meal is undercooked, or worse, poisonous.

The Church would’ve failed to do what it was supposed to do.

You can go to a social club if you want to hang out with your friends.

You can go to a concert to hear the kind of music you like in a concert.

You can even go to a restaurant to eat the kind of food you like to eat.

But when you come to Church, you have come, as a Church, to hear and obey God’s word.

You have come to pray, in this short hour where you physically gather with God’s people, to worship God together, and to encourage other Christian to love and good deeds.

This must always remain the priority of the Church; pray that we will never ever prioritise anything, anyone, any event, even any ministry above the Word ministry.

The cultural tension in a growing Church is as real today as it was back in the first century.

Today, in many churches that have multiple generations of immigrants, there is almost always a lot of intergenerational tension.

The tension between the first and second generation is the cause of many church splits not only here in Australia but also around the world.

I’ve seen and heard about these in Chinese churches, Indian Churches and Korean Churches.

The clashes could be over cultural identity or the type of music we sing, or the type of food we eat.

These are important things in the life of the Church!

As important as these things are, they mustn’t take priority over the Word ministry.

The Church must always be a creature of God’s word.

For when we let other things take over the priority of the Word ministry, the Church turns into another creature.

I’ve seen Churches that have let their cultural identity become the primary identity and I’ve seen churches that have let community outreach become their identity.

To use the restaurant analogy again, instead of providing a wedding feast that is the proclamation of the Gospel of Jesus Christ,

churches who failed to prioritise the Bible dishes out cheap, expired, fast-food meals.

They may look good for the first second but they will sicken your soul over time.

And so, there’s a cultural tension between the Hellenistic and the Hebraic Jews, and there’s an organizational tension between adequately providing food for the vulnerable and feeding the church with the spiritual food of God’s word.

How did the first Church resolve the tension?

A growing Church chooses Spirit-filled waiters

The growing church chooses Spirit-filled waiters, as it were.

Look with me at verse 3 to 4, “Brothers and sisters, select from among you seven men of good reputation, full of the Spirit and wisdom, whom we can appoint to this duty. 4 But we will devote ourselves to prayer and to the ministry of the word.”

The Church was tasked to choose seven men of good reputation, seven men whom they’ve witnessed to be good men, full of Spirit and wisdom.

These weren’t just people who were good at administrative work; they weren’t chosen because they had worldly wisdom.

They were chosen because they were full of the Spirit and wisdom.

They had wisdom from the Spirit.

They needed wisdom from the Spirit to manage the complexity of working through the needs of both sets of cultural groups.

They were godly, wise and reputable waiters.

I remember going to a nice restaurant once, many years ago, and there were flies circling our table.

The waitress was there and she had a dilemma.

Would she get her fly squatter out and just smash the fly?

Or will she just let the fly, fly?

What would you do?

This waitress looked young but she was wise beyond her years.

She used a piece of really wide sticky tape.

She saw the fly landed in a particular spot and she held the sticky tape over the fly.

As soon as the fly flew, it stuck on the sticky tape.

No mess and no flies.

The other flies saw it and flew away.

The waitress was experienced and considerate.

That’s wisdom!

She wasn’t just concerned about the fly or the food; they were important tasks but nor the most important task.

Her main task was making sure the customer was cared for.

In the Church, our main task is to make sure people get to hear God’s word taught, whether here at Sunday services or in Growth groups afterwards.

We also want to make sure we teach God’s word in a safe environment, so that your focus is on God’s Word.

More than that, we don’t just want to give lessons on God’s word; we want to help one another to continue to walk with God on other days of the week.

We want them to obey God and to love one another not just on Sundays but on other days as well.

In other words, we want our Church to understand God’s word and apply them well.

We will need godly waiters in the Church who are filled with the Holy Spirit and wisdom to help us apply God’s word in the life of the church.

Like good waiters in a restaurant who help distribute the food from the chef effectively from the kitchen to the tables,

We need godly waiters in the Church who will help apply the teaching that we learn from sermons and Growth groups to the lives of the individuals.

We need godly waiters who will welcome people to Church.

We need godly waiters who will serve on the band.

We need godly waiters who can help look after the grounds of the Church.

That’s what the first Church did: they chose seven godly, Spirit-filled and wise leaders so that the apostles can devote themselves to prayer and to the ministry of the Word.

The apostles and the godly waiters worked to make sure both the physical and the spiritual needs of God’s people are addressed.

Now notice the godly waiters aren’t just random people who had put their hands up to volunteer for the job because no one else wanted to do them.

They need to be qualified before they can become waiters in God’s church.

They need to be filled with the Spirit, that is, they needed to be a Christian.

They needed to be wise and have a good reputation.

They needed to be shown that someone had witnessed their lives and their actions and that others have affirmed that they are a good fit.

They needed to be shown that they have applied God’s word in practice.

Where do we find these godly waiters?

At St Paul’s, we are serious about finding and training godly waiters.

We’re so serious that one of our key ministries is called Giftedness.

We don’t want people just to fill the holes in our positions.

We want them to see that if they are a Christian, God has given them as as gift to the Church.

And they have a responsibility to apply their gift to be used for God’s glory in God’s Church.

They need to have wisdom to know how to use their gift well.

And so, we have developed the Discovery Course.

The Discovery Course is to help us to identify each other’s gifts.

It’s no good if you tell me that you want to be the lead singer in the band when no one else have heard you sing, or worse, when you’re not a Christian and you don’t believe in what you sing.

In the Giftedness ministry, we want to help you first of all think through your relationship with God and to work on the gift that God’s given you.

If you do have a gift, and you’ve already been using it for a while, God might want you to use your gift to train others.

We want to help one another to develop God’s gift in us so that the Word of God can continue to spread.

That’s what happened in the first century Church.

They chose seven men. Here are their names, in verse 5, Stephen, a man full of faith and the Holy Spirit, and Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolaus, a convert from Antioch.

These seven men have Greek names; they were Hellenistic Jews.

The Church chose seven Spirit-filled waiters from the Hellenistic group, the group that complained, to look after the need of both the Hellenistic and the Hebraic groups.

These seven men were publicly acknowledged by the apostles and God’s word continued to spread as a result.

Up until this point, the Church had gone through all kinds of difficulties.

Sometimes, like what just happened, they were able to resolve it by wisdom in leadership.

Other times, God miraculously intervened.

They came to danger many times, but they hadn’t been killed.

However, all that’s about to change.

The Bible now focuses on one of the seven Spirit-filled, godly waiters.

The Bible now turns our attention to Stephen.

Slander against a Spirit-filled waiter

Look with me at verse 8, “Now Stephen, full of grace and power, was performing great wonders and signs among the people.”

Not only was Stephen one of the seven godly waiters, he also did great signs and wonders among the people, just like the Twelve apostles.

And like the Twelve apostles, Stephen also faced opposition.

Those who opposed Stephen were members of the Freedmen’s Synagogue.

The synagogue was the place where the Old Testament was taught.

These are different from temples because Jews could only sacrifice in the temple, and not in the synagogues.

The members of this particular synagogue were probably made up of people who were at one stage slaves but are now freed, and so, their synagogue was called Freedmen’s synagogue.

What’s interesting was that these freed men were from different areas, including Cyrene and Alexandria, Cilicia and Asia (modern day Türkiye).

Like Stephen, these people were Hellenistic Jews; they shared a similar cultural background.

And so, Stephen found himself in this strange situation:

As one of the seven godly waiters, he had to deal with the tension between Hellenistic and Hebraic Jews within the church.

He was doing cross-cultural ministry inside his church.

As someone who taught about Jesus and did signs and miracles like the apostles, he had to deal with opposition from Hellenistic Jews.

He had to face opposition from people of his own culture.

These people shared the same cultural background as Stephen but they did not share his belief.

They tried to argue against Stephen, but they were no match for his wisdom.

Jesus himself experienced something similar.

When he preached in Nazareth, his home town, his own neighbours, the very people he grew up with, didn’t believe him.

They rejected his teaching and even tried to kill him.

When the Hellenistic Jews from the Freedmen’s synagogue couldn’t win their argument, they resorted to slander.

They said that Stephen had said blasphemous, false things against Moses and God.

Stephen was chosen because he had a good reputation of being Spirit-filled, wise, full of grace and power.

They tried to smear Stephen’s name and stirred up a crowd against Stephen.

They got false witnesses to say that they heard Stephen speaking against the Holy Place (that is, the temple) and the law (that is the law of Moses).

They said that Jesus, Stephen’s Lord and Master, had said that Jesus of Nazareth would destroy this place (the temple) and change the customs Moses handed down to them.

Stephen had probably taught about these things and would’ve certainly referred to Jesus, but they represented his teaching falsely.

The most persuasive lies are those which have hints of truth in them.

They took some of the truth that Stephen taught and applied it falsely, turning them into lies.

They slandered against Stephen and their slander spread like fire.

Slander is a dangerous and one of the most damaging sins.

Proverbs tells us that slander destroys neighbours (Proverbs 11:9) and separates close friends (Provers 16:28).

All of us will face slander one day, especially if we are to act with integrity as a Christian.

When you are slandered, don’t fall into victimhood thinking.

Don’t get defensive and don’t wallow in self-pity.

As the famous preacher Charles Spurgeon has said, “If any man thinks ill of you, do not be angry with him; for you are worse than he thinks you to be.”

Just remember: God is in charge.

Be more worried about Christ’s honour than the honour of your name.

Another famous Christian preacher, George Whitefield once said, “Let the name of Whitefield perish, but Christ be glorified. Let my name die everywhere, let even my friends forget me, if by that means the cause of the blessed Jesus may be promoted. . . . I am content to wait till the judgement day for the clearing up of my reputation; and after I am dead I desire no other epitaph than this, “Here lies G. W. What sort of man he was the great day will discover.”

How will Stephen respond?

He will respond with the longest speech recorded in Acts.

We’ll spend the next three weeks looking into it.

But even before he responds, we read this in verse 15, “And all who were sitting in the Sanhedrin looked intently at him and saw that his face was like the face of an angel.”

Even those who were openly slandering him saw that his face was like that of an angel.

Stephen, the Spirit-filled, godly, wise waiter with the face of an angel, will respond in the next chapter.

Make sure you come back over the next three weeks to find out.

What makes a good waiter in God’s Church?

It’s the Holy Spirit who makes good waiters in God’s Church.

It’s someone who’s filled with the Holy Spirit, who lives by the wisdom and the faith that comes from the Holy Spirit who can serve as good waiters, good leaders, in God’s Church.

These people will have a good reputation; they have shown that they had applied wisdom from God.

They are also ready to have their reputation tarnished by slander, if it means standing with integrity before Jesus.

May God give us more men and women who will be good waiters in God’s Church.

The first century Church was a growing Church.

And growing Churches choose Spirt-filled waiters who will deal wisely with cultural tension from within, even as they face slanders from without.

Friends, God is growing our Church.

As a Church that is growing, we will see that there are different cultures among us.

We will see differences between the Chinese speakers and the English speakers.

We will see differences between First generation and second generation immigrants.

There will be tensions between us.

But don’t pray that God will take these tensions away; instead, pray that God will give us wisdom to know how to handle these tensions.

The first Church didn’t automatically split even though there were cultural tensions among their members.

Instead, they appointed wise, godly, Spirit-filled waiters to resolve the tension.

And so, there’s no need to shy away from our differences.

Instead, let’s recognise the differences between us and embrace one another as members of one body in Christ.

Here are the three questions for us to think about this week:

Would it have been easier for the early Church to split and focus on their own people (even thought they were all Jews)?

What’s the requirement of the ‘waiter’ who looks after the physical needs / issues of a growing Church?

Does serving as a ‘waiter’ excuse us from being a ‘proclaimer’ of the gospel?

Growing Churches choose Spirt-filled waiters who will deal wisely with cultural tension from within, even as they face slanders from without.

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you for saving us in Christ to be one body, serving one Lord, in the One Spirit. Thank you that Jesus has come to be the Servant of all. Please forgive us when we want to be served rather than to serve. Please fill us with your Spirit so that we can be the good, godly servants you called us to be. Please raise up good, godly and wise waiters in your Church, who will help us apply your word in our lives. Please help those who are teachers of your word to devote themselves to prayer and the Word ministry, so that we can grow in your word together. Please grow our Church so that your word will spread and so that more people will come to know and serve our Saviour, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
