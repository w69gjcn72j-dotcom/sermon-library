Growth Groups (adults)

The Hypocrite and the Heart

Romans 2:17-3:8 · Sunday 19 July 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah · For adult home groups

Big Idea: Don’t be hypocritical teachers whose falsehood breeds blasphemy; be people with Spirit-circumcised hearts, living out God’s word for his praise and glory.

Aim: That each member (1) names one gap between profession and practice, (2) treasures the Spirit’s heart-work over religious credentials, and (3) confesses sin honestly and prays by name for 3 people.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, this sheet. Allow 60-75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • At work you’re known as the honest one — then you catch yourself doing the thing you’d quietly judge in others (a fudged timesheet, a ’convenient’ sickie). Why is it easier to spot others’ faults than our own?
  • Share a time when packaging (a product, a house, a first impression) promised far more than what was inside.

Read — Romans 2:17-3:8 (CSB)

17 Now if you call yourself a Jew, and rely on the law, and boast in God, 18 and know his will, and approve the things that are superior, being instructed from the law, 19 and if you are convinced that you are a guide for the blind, a light to those in darkness, 20 an instructor of the ignorant, a teacher of the immature, having the embodiment of knowledge and truth in the law — 21 you then, who teach another, don’t you teach yourself? You who preach, “You must not steal” — do you steal? 22 You who say, “You must not commit adultery” — do you commit adultery? You who detest idols, do you rob temples? 23 You who boast in the law, do you dishonor God by breaking the law? 24 For, as it is written: The name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of you.

25 Circumcision benefits you if you observe the law, but if you are a lawbreaker, your circumcision has become uncircumcision. 26 So if an uncircumcised man keeps the law’s requirements, will not his uncircumcision be counted as circumcision? 27 A man who is physically uncircumcised, but who fulfills the law, will judge you who are a lawbreaker in spite of having the letter of the law and circumcision. 28 For a person is not a Jew who is one outwardly, and true circumcision is not something visible in the flesh. 29 On the contrary, a person is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart — by the Spirit, not the letter. That person’s praise is not from people but from God.

3:1 So what advantage does the Jew have? Or what is the benefit of circumcision? 2 Considerable in every way. First, they were entrusted with the very words of God. 3 What then? If some were unfaithful, will their unfaithfulness nullify God’s faithfulness? 4 Absolutely not! Let God be true, even though everyone is a liar, as it is written: That you may be justified in your words and triumph when you judge. 5 But if our unrighteousness highlights God’s righteousness, what are we to say? I am using a human argument: Is God unrighteous to inflict wrath? 6 Absolutely not! Otherwise, how will God judge the world? 7 But if by my lie God’s truth abounds to his glory, why am I also still being judged as a sinner? 8 And why not say, just as some people slanderously claim we say, “Let us do what is evil so that good may come”? Their condemnation is deserved!

Context

Paul has shown God’s anger against open rebellion (1:18-32) and against the moralising judge (2:1-16). Now he turns to the religious insider: the Jew who has God’s law and the covenant sign of circumcision. The privileges are real — but privilege without practice dishonours God’s name, and only a heart changed by the Spirit truly belongs to him.

Discuss

  1. Read 2:17-20. List the privileges and roles the Jew claims. Which of these are actually good things?
  2. In vv. 21-24, what is Paul’s charge? How does insider hypocrisy end with God’s name being blasphemed by outsiders (v. 24)?
  3. In vv. 25-27, what happens to circumcision — the covenant ’badge’ — when the law is broken? What are our church equivalents of the badge?
  4. From vv. 28-29: what is true circumcision, who performs it, and whose praise does the real Jew seek? Why can’t we do this to ourselves?
  5. 3:1-2 — if hypocrisy is so deadly, were the privileges worthless? What advantage still stands?
  6. 3:3-4 — does our unfaithfulness cancel God’s faithfulness? How does David’s confession in Psalm 51 (quoted in v. 4) model the right response when sin is exposed?
  7. 3:5-8 — what twisted logic does Paul reject? Where do we hear “grace means my sin doesn’t really matter” today?
  8. We rename our sins: gossip becomes “sharing a prayer point”, harshness “I’m just being honest”, temper “righteous anger”. What renamed sin do you need to call by its real name — at home, at work, or at church?

Live It Out

This term we are practising the KOG 3·2·1·0 rhythm together:

  • Pray for 3 — keep praying daily for your three people who don’t yet know Jesus.
  • Connect with 2 — have a real conversation with two of them this week; genuine interest, no agenda.
  • Invite 1 — invite one person to church, to your home group, or to morning tea after the 9:15 service.
  • 0 left unloved — like the sister who simply gave David a cup of water: one small, practised act of love at church this Sunday.

Weekly challenge: Confess one specific sin to the Lord this week using ACTS — and, where wise, to a mature brother or sister who will pray for you. No renaming; confess like David, not the objector.

Memory verse — “A person is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart — by the Spirit, not the letter. That person’s praise is not from people but from God.” (Romans 2:29)

Pray

  • Praise God that he remains true and faithful even though “everyone is a liar” — thank him that Jesus kept the law perfectly on our behalf.
  • Confess together, honestly, the gaps between what we profess and what we practise, asking the Spirit to keep circumcising our hearts.
  • Pray by name for each member’s 3 people, that God would draw them to Jesus — and for our church’s witness, that neighbours would honour God’s name because of us.

Leader’s Notes

Keep the tone warm and joyful — point to Jesus, don’t name and shame. Guests and not-yet-Christians may be present; many have been hurt by religious hypocrisy, so let them hear that God sees it too. Never corner anyone into sharing sin publicly.

Q2: v. 24 quotes Isaiah 52:5 — God’s people caused his name to be mocked. The watching world judges God by his people; our lives commend or contradict our message.

Q4: true circumcision is by the Spirit (Deut 30:6; Ezek 36:26-27). We can’t operate on our own hearts — this keeps the study from ’try harder’ and drives us to grace: Jesus kept the law for us.

Q6-7: God’s faithfulness stands despite our faithlessness; David confessed rather than excused. And grace never licenses sin — it transforms (Titus 2:11-12). Watch for both errors: badge-keeping legalism and cheap-grace licence.

Vision tie-in: Romans is taking us “from faith to faith” (1:17) — real faith shows in practised lives. That’s what 75 households serving (currently 52) looks like on the ground: not badges, but practice.
