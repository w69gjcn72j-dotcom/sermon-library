It was great to see celebrate New Year with many of you last week!

We had a great time at Church in the morning and many of us came back to make dumplings together afterwards!

Of course, the main reason for us to be here is not just to make dumplings; it was to bring new people to hear about Jesus!

It was a wonderful way to celebrate the New Year!

During New Year, we do things like greeting each other with special New Year greetings and have traditions like making dumplings.

But there are also things to we avoid saying and doing.

Things like, having a haircut.

Does anyone know why Chinese avoid having haircut during the Spring Festival?

It’s because the word for wealth sounds like hair, and we don’t want to cut our wealth, do we? Especially during New Year!

And don’t say anything negative, like death or pain.

We want blessings in the New Year, not curses!

However, in today’s passage, I want to show you that when we avoid hearing about curses, we miss out on getting the greatest blessing!

To hear about the greatest blessing this New Year, we need to hear about the curses, in fact, the worst of curses.

Why do we need to hear about curses, especially in times like the Chinese New Year?

When sin takes the driver’s seat

The Bible begins with the creation of the Heavens and the earth.

From Genesis chapter 1, we see God as the Creator of everyone and everything, and it was all good, very good.

God created men and women in his own image and the new couple enjoyed God’s blessings in a perfect place, under God’s perfect rule.

However, they were tempted by the devil, who came to them as a serpent.

The devil told them that they could be like God,

and even though God said that they would die if they disobeyed him,

the devil lied to them by saying that they wouldn’t.

The woman listened to the devil’s advice, and her husband followed her lead.

When they realised what they had done, they hid from God, but God found them, confronted them, and sent them away from his presence.

By the end of Genesis chapter 3, we see God banishing them from the Garden of Eden.

Here, in Genesis chapter 4, we see the first human couple begin a new life outside of the Garden, and it starts off looking promising!

Look with me at 4:1, “ The man was intimate with his wife Eve, and she conceived and gave birth to Cain. She said, “I have had a male child with the LORD’s help.”

This is the first birth recorded in human history.

Adam and Eve were made by God from the dust of the earth, but this first child was born of a woman.

The new child, whom they called Cain, must have brought great joy to the family.

He represented a new hope!

After Cain, Abel was born.

There are now two new persons, two brothers!

The world population has now officially doubled from two to four!

In time, Abel grew to become a shepherd who looked after the flock and Cain grew up to be a farmer who worked the ground.

The two brothers both gave an offering to God.

Cain gave some of the produce of the land and Abel gave the firstborn of his flock.

The two brothers both gave God an offering, but God accepted Abel’s and not Cain’s.

Why did God accept one and not the other?

We see the answer to that in Hebrews 11:4, “By faith Abel offered to God a better sacrifice than Cain did. By faith he was approved as a righteous man, because God approved his gifts,”

The Bible tells us that Abel’s offering was accepted because he offered in faith.

By faith, Abel offered the firstborn of his flock and their fat portions, trusting that God will provide for him later on.

Abel’s trusted God and sacrificed, whereas Cain also sacrificed but he didn’t trust God.

Maybe Cain only gave some of the produce instead of the firstfruits of his produce.

Whatever it was, we knew that though he offered to God, his heart was not in it; he didn’t offered to God in faith.

As a result, God didn’t accept Cain’s sacrifice.

When Cain saw that God rejected his sacrifice but accepted Abel’s, he was furious and his looked despondent, he looked sad.

And God himself intervened.

Look at what God said to Cain, in verse 6, ““Why are you furious? And why do you look despondent? 7 If you do what is right, won’t you be accepted? But if you do not do what is right, sin is crouching at the door. Its desire is for you, but you must rule over it.”

God says that sin is like an op-por-tun-istic animal, ready to pounce at the right moment.

Can you think of an animal like that?

One of the first animals that comes to my mind is the crocodile.

Crocodiles are very clever animals.

They often study other animals for a while before they attack.

For example, when a sheep comes to the same spot to drink every day, the crocodile would for the sheep the next day, and grab it when it is drinking, and at its most vulnerable.

That’s what sin is like.

It’s like a crocodile that’s always watching, always waiting.

It waits for the right opportunity to strike.

When the moment is right, it pounces on you and tells you,

‘You know you want to sin. Just do it already.’

God says that sin desires for you, it wants you.

When you don’t do what’s right, sin is ready to take over you.

Sin is ready to the driver’s seat in your life, as it were.

What does it look like to have sin take the driver’s seat in your life?

It looks like you trying to take control of your life.

It’s that simple!

That’s what sin wants; it wants you to take over the driver’s seat in your life, instead of God.

When God doesn’t accept your offering, it’s his problem, not yours.

Sin doesn’t want you to accept God’s decision; sin wants you to make your own decision.

That’s what it looks like when sin takes the driver’s seat: it makes you think that you make the decision and that you’re in control.

What happened when sin took over Cain’s driver’s seat?

Cain killed his brother, his little brother.

This little brother that he grew up with, ate with, worked with, laughed with, became the source of his hate.

Cain killed his little brother; it was horrible!

Why did he do it?

Was it because he hated Abel? Yes, but also no.

The Christian pastor Bonhoeffer asked, “Why does Cain murder? Out of hatred for God.”

Cain hated God and so he hated Abel when Abel did what was right in God’s eyes.

Evil hates righteousness, and when a person driven by sin sees a person acting according righteously, they are filled with hatred.

A person by sin hates others, hates even his own brother, because he ultimately hates God.

Hatred of others is linked directly with the hatred of God.

This is what we read in 1 John 3:11-12, “For this is the message you have heard from the beginning: We should love one another, 12 unlike Cain, who was of the evil one and murdered his brother. And why did he murder him? Because his deeds were evil, and his brother’s were righteous.”

Being hated feels bad but it doesn’t necessarily mean that you’ve done something wrong.

If you are righteous, and you trust God, the world will hate you.

When you trust God and do what’s right, you will be the object of hate; those driven by sin will hate you.

And when we’re tempted by sin, when we fail to do what’s right, we will be also tempted to hate.

We will try to rationalise our hatred.

Sin will whisper to us and say that those who we hate deserved to be hated, they deserve to be shamed, dishonoured, and despised.

When we let sin pounce on us, when we let sin takes the driver’s seat, it leads us to do all kinds of unspeakable evils.

In Cain’s case, it led to the first homicide in history, the murder of his own brother.

A story was told in the Chicago Tribune a number of years ago about a Texas mother who was so intent on making sure her daughter made the cheerleading squad that she was willing to hire a hit man to kill a competitor’s mother.

The woman’s hope was that her daughter’s thirteen-year-old competitor would be so saddened by her mother’s death that she would drop out of competition.

Sin has so overtaken the Texas mother that she hired someone to kill another girl’s mother!

That’s horrible but sadly it’s not an isolated case.

When sin takes control, there are terrible consequences on our society.

When sin takes control, we hate our fellow men and women.

We hate them, because we ultimately hate God.

This leads to the destruction of relationships and the destruction of life.

But it gets worse.

The curse of sin spreads far

The curse of sin spreads, and it spreads far.

When God asked Cain where Abel was, Cain outright lied to God and said, “I don’t know, am I my brother’s guardian?”

Cain basically said that he didn’t care about his brother’s whereabout; he wasn’t responsible for him.

But of course, Cain knew exactly what happened to Abel; he was responsible for his death.

God then confronted Cain by saying, ““What have you done? Your brother’s blood cries out to me from the ground!”

Cain might have killed Abel, shovelled the ground, and buried him in a grave.

But no grave is too deep for God.

For Abel’s blood cried out to God, as it were, from the ground.

It cried out to God for justice!

Both Abel’s blood and the ground belongs to God.

And now, the ground, having received Abel’s blood, became an unwilling accomplice to Cain’s murder.

And so, God pronounced this sentence on Cain, in verse 11,

“11 So now you are cursed, alienated from the ground that opened its mouth to receive your brother’s blood you have shed.”

Cain used to work on the ground but now the ground has become his enemy.

In fact, the whole ground has become his enemy.

Wherever Cain went, as long as he’s on the ground, he’s on enemy territory.

Cain will be a restless wanderer on the earth, all his life.

That will be his curse.

And it’s not just the ground who has become his enemy; people will hate him as well.

Cain said that whoever finds him will kill him.

Cain will be rejected by God, rejected and hated by men, and rejected by the ground.

Cain said that this will be too much for him to bear.

If I was there, I would say, “Oh, really, Cain? Really? You killed your brother, and you think God’s being too harsh on you?”

Cain was sad, but he wasn’t truly sorry; he was only sorry because he got caught.

But God is gracious even as he curses Cain.

God said that he would put a mark on Cain to protect him so that he wouldn’t be killed.

Even though Cain committed a horrible crime and most probably hasn’t repented, God still show mercy to Cain.

That’s God’s grace to Cain.

Cain went to the land of Nod to begin a new life.

He and his wife had a son called Enoch and Cain built a city for his him.

He wanted this city to be his son’s legacy

And as we read what Cain’s decedents have accomplished, we tread that they have left quite an impressive legacy after them.

One of them, Jabal, was the father of the nomadic herdsmen.

That is, he discovered how to domesticate and look after the flock.

Another, Jubal, was the father of all who play the lyre and the flute.

He was the first one to discover that you can make music through plugging strings and blowing through a tube.

He was the first guy to play a riff on his own version of the guitar!

And there was another guy, Tubal-cain.

He was a handyman; he made all kinds of bronze and iron tools.

They were a technologically and culturally advanced group of people.

They would’ve been clever and capable, smart, and artistic.

But something wasn’t right.

Look with me at verses 23-24, “Lamech said to his wives: Adah and Zillah, hear my voice; wives of Lamech, pay attention to my words. For I killed a man for wounding me, a young man for striking me. If Cain is to be avenged seven times over, then for Lamech it will be seventy-seven times!”

Lamech had two wives, something that God had not designed for marriage from the very beginning.

And more than that, he boasted that he killed a man; and not just a man, a young man, probably a kid or a teenager.

Cain’s murderous trait passed on to his descendent Lamech.

Whereas God limits the vengeance of anyone who killed Cain to be avenged seven times over,

Lamech multiplied that by seventy times!

He called for an avalanche of revenge for anyone who dared to kill Lamech.

Lamech was a violent man who killed and who wanted an escalation of violence should anything happen to him.

How horrible it would be for the two wives of Lamech to live with such a violent man!

And yet, that’s what happens when sin takes the driver’s seat.

It rejects God and his righteousness, increases hatred among fellow men and women, cursing the world and its inhabitants wherever it spreads.

Even though Cain’s descendants advanced technologically and culturally, they didn’t become better people.

Advances in technology and culture don’t change sinners into saints.

They just make better educated sinners, who get even better at sinning.

It wasn’t so long ago that bank robberies meant threatening the bank teller with a gun and ask them to hand over the cash.

Now days, you can rob banks and people’s bank accounts by accessing their log in details and transferring it to somewhere safe in the digital world.

People are getting smarter, but not better.

Even as we continue to grow as a society, the curse of sin continues to have its effect on human race.

We continue to see the spread of hatred both in us and around us.

Sin continues to sit at the door, and we continue to let it take the driver’s seat in our lives.

Are we hopelessly doomed to live with the curse of sin?

The cure for sin in Another Son

At the end of Genesis 4, we see a glimmer of hope in the birth of another son.

Look with me at verses 25-26, “ Adam was intimate with his wife again, and she gave birth to a son and named him Seth, for she said, “God has given me another offspring in place of Abel, since Cain killed him.” 26 A son was born to Seth also, and he named him Enosh. At that time people began to call on the name of the LORD.”

There is now a new line coming from Adam that doesn’t go pass Cain.

The line of Seth represents a new hope for humanity.

Whereas Cain’s line is known for advancement in technology and the arts, what’s Seth’s line known to be the pioneers of?

They were the one who began to call on the name of the LORD.

In the midst of the hopeless spread of sin,

when sin takes the driver’s seat for so many people in the world at that time,

God brings a new hope through the line of Seth.

I’ve often heard that people say that they don’t bring babies into the cruel world.

They say that they don’t want their children to experience a world filled with hatred and devastated by things like wars and climate change.

But I’ve also heard of mothers who gave birth in wars, in battles, and in times of despair.

These are women who live through extremely difficult times and who often don’t own a lot of things.

But what they do have a lot of abundance is hope.

They are hopeful that the child will have a future.

When Seth was born, there was a renewed hope for humanity as Seth.

As we continue to read the Bible, we know that this hope is real and is better than anything thing that Adam and Eve could’ve ever imagined.

For from Seth’s line will be born a Baby, who has come to give us a future.

He will give us hope.

This Baby is Jesus, the very Son of God.

He’s born not because there’s hope that humanity will get better; left to ourselves, we will always let sin win.

Rather, our hope is in Jesus, who alone who can save us from the curse of sin.

He bore the curse for us on the cross so that we can be set free from sin.

The Bible tells us that the blood of Jesus speaks better things that the blood of Abel.

Whereas Abel’s blood cried out to God for vengeance, Jesus’ blood cried out to God for forgiveness.

God heard Abel’s blood and cursed Cain.

God heard Jesus’ blood and will bless all those who trusts in Jesus for forgiveness.

And whereas Lamech sought an avalanche of vengeance against anyone who would kill him,

Jesus taught that his followers are to be ready to give an avalanche of forgiveness whenever anyone sins against them.

In fact, he uses a variation of Lamech’s words to teach his disciples about forgiveness.

When Peter asked Jesus how many times must he forgive his brother who sinned against him, Jesus said, “ “I tell you, not as many as seven,” Jesus replied, “but seventy times seven.”

Jesus has come to reverse the curse of sin and death.

Jesus is the greatest blessing that God gives to us as a human race.

To understand the blessing of God in Jesus, we need to understand the curse of God because of our sins.

Without understanding the curse of sin, you will never understand the blessing of God in Jesus.

This is something that all Christians need to hear.

For those of us who are new to Christianity, we need to hear what sin is and the danger that it brings.

We need to see its desire to take over us and take the driver’s seat and how quickly it can drive us to hatred toward God and man.

For those of us who have been Christians for longer, we need to be weary of the danger of being hardened by sin.

Don’t give sin an opportunity; don’t let sin take the driver’s seat.

We need to come back to the Bible and confess our sins to one other.

We need to rest our hope only in Jesus, for he is our only hope against the curse of sin and death.

And so, friends, Sin is crouching, pray for Jesus to slay it.

Sin tells you two lies.

Before you sin, sin lies to you by saying that that it’s no big deal.

Just go ahead and sin.

Then after you sin, sin lies to you again by telling you that it’s the worst thing that could ever happen.

You’re never going to recover from it.

The blood of Jesus smashes both of those lies.

Firstly, sin is serious.

It is a big deal. It’s such a big deal that God has to send his one and only Son into the world to deal with it.

Don’t mess with sin.

And second, sin is defeated.

Don’t sin, but if you do sin, God has sent Jesus to pay for your sins.

If you come before him and repent in the name of Jesus, your sins are forgiven.

The blood of Jesus cries to God for our forgiveness.

If you trust in Jesus, God will hear Jesus’ cry for you.

Only Jesus can cure us of our curse.

And so, trust in him! Slay sin!

Don’t trivialise sin and let him take the wheel for a second; don’t be tortured by sin by leaving him in your back seat.

Confess your sin and slay it in Christ!

Sin is crouching, pray for Jesus to slay it.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Who has the driver’s seat in your life? Sin or Jesus?

Who does sin tempt me to hate? Why am I tempted to hate them?

Is there a Christian brother or sister I trust who can I confess my sins to?

Friends, sin is crouching at the door, even now.

Don’t let it take over you; slay it in the name of Jesus by confessing your sins to him.

We thank you for sending Jesus to be the cure for our curse. Father, by your Spirit, help us to remember that sin is always crouching at the door, wanting to take over our lives. Help us to run to Jesus and confess our sins to him, so that it won’t take hold in our lives. By your Spirit, please slay the sin in our lives. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
