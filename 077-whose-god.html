Whose God?

Mark 12:1-27 · 2021
Big IdeaThe Father restores his vineyard and his creation through his Risen Son; honour him by giving him his due.

The Father’s own

Passage: Mark 12.1_27

Big Idea: The Father restores his vineyard and his creation through his Risen Son; honour him by giving him his due.

Whose vineyard is it?

Whose image is it?

Whose God is God?

A few years ago, one of my cousins from Hong Kong came to Australia to visit us.

At that time, the English Football team, Arsenal, was also visiting.

Since my cousin was a very devout Arsenal fan, we decided to go with him to watch a football game between Arsenal and the Western Sydney Wanderers.

My parents came, Catherine and Rupert came, and many thousands of families came as well.

It was an amazing atmosphere!

Every time Arsenal scored, the whole stadium erupted in cheer.

It was amazing that even here in Australia, on the other side of the world, Arsenal still has so many loyal fans.

These fans show their loyalty by what they wear, by attending the games and by cheering for Arsenal, not only when they win,

But whenever they get the chance.

For me and my family, we just tag along with my cousin because it’s fun.

But my cousin is the true fan.

He once even gone to the Emirates Stadium in London, home of the Arsenal Football Club.

He’s a true fan.

Arsenal is his club.

What are we loyal to?

Christians are called to be loyal to God.

We might show our loyalty by what we wear, maybe a cross.

We might show it by attending Church and we might even cheer for God every now and then.

But are we truly loyal?

Is God my God?

Are we true fans of God, so to speak?

In today’s passage, we’ll see people who pretended to be loyal to God but are not.

We’ll also see what true loyalty to God look like.

The passage can be divided into three questions:

Whose vineyard?

Whose image?

Whose God?

Whose vineyard?

Firstly, whose vineyard?

If you have your Bible there, please open up to Mark 12, beginning from verse 1.

‘He began to speak to them in parables: “A man planted a vineyard, put a fence around it, dug out a pit for a winepress, and built a watchtower. Then he leased it to tenant farmers and went away.’

Jesus is here telling a parable, a story that highlights a point that Jesus wants to convey.

At the end of the passage, we see that the point Jesus is making is against the religious leaders of Israel at that time.

Jesus is saying that these leaders had thought that they own the people of Israel rather than God.

And so, Jesus uses the parable of a man who planted the vineyard to demonstrate his point.

After the man planted the vineyard, he went away on a journey.

While he was away, he rented out the vineyard to tenant farmers.

These farmers would look after the vineyard and can use it to grow vines for themselves.

In return, they would pay a fee to the owner for the use of the vineyard.

It’s a little like setting up a franchise like McDonald’s.

You pay a certain initial fee to begin with.

You then agree to pay a franchise fee every year.

You use McDonald’s name to make a profit and you pay McDonald’s for the privilege every year in return.

This was happening all over Israel in Jesus’ time, except that rather than McDonald’s, they were renting out vineyards.

In Jesus’ parable, the owner sends his servants to collect some of the fruit, as you would expect.

But instead of paying the fee, the tenants beat him up and sent him away empty-handed.

This happens again and again, sometimes, they beat the owner’s servants, sometimes they kill them.

Finally, the owner sends his son, his beloved son saying, “they will respect my son.”

Would they?

No, instead they say to one another, in verse 7, “This is the heir, Come, let’s kill him, and the inheritance will be ours.”

There was a custom back in Jesus’ days that if the property that the tenants are staying on doesn’t have an owner anymore, the tenants can keep the land.

If these tenants can get rid of the son, the eventual owner of the land, they can get to keep the land in the long run.

So that’s what the tenants did.

Verse 8, “So they seized him, killed him, and threw him out of the vineyard.”

Jesus then asks them in verse 9, “What then will the owner of the vineyard do? He will come and kill the farmers and give the vineyard to others.”

The owner won’t give up on his vineyard.

He’ll avenge his Son, take the vineyard back and give it to others.

Why does Jesus tell them this parable?

It’s because the Jewish leaders are like the tenant farmers.

In the Old Testament, in Isaiah 5, God’s described as the one who had made the nation of Israel;

It’s God who had put the country of Israel together.

Israel belongs to God.

God gave the responsibility of looking after Israel to the leaders of Israel.

However, rather than giving God what is his due, they acted as if Israel no longer belonged to God.

They acted as if they were the owners of Israel, leading the people away from God.

They wanted God out of the picture.

God had sent servant after servant, prophet after prophet to them but some they beat up, others they’ve killed.

God’s been so patient with them!

God spent hundreds of years, sending so many servants to tell them to turn back to him.

But they’ve failed to listen.

Finally, God sent His Son, his beloved Son, Jesus Christ, to them.

What would they do to him?

Even though it hasn’t happened yet, Jesus knows that they will kill him, hang him on a cross outside of Jerusalem and throw him into a tomb.

And that’s what happened.

The leaders of Israel did kill Jesus, God’s beloved Son.

What Jesus had said in the parable came true.

While it’s shocking to see that the leaders of Israel will do that to the Son of God, it’s not just the leaders of Israel who want to get rid of Jesus.

Leaders, governments, kings, emperors, have wanted to get rid of Jesus for the past two thousand years.

That’s because all of us, in one way or another, are like the tenant farmers.

Just like the tenant farmers who doesn’t want to pay their due and who wanted to get rid of the rightful owner,

We don’t want to give God his due and get rid of him as the rightful owner.

James Edwards in his commentary says,

“If humanity can dispense with God, or even kill God, then humanity can become God. What is the sum total of human history if not the attempt to rid the universe of God.”

But what will the owner of the universe do?

He will come back to judge humanity, but he will also do something incredible for humanity.

In fact, he had already done it. Look with me at verse 10, “Haven’t you read this Scripture: The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone.11 This came about from the Lord and is wonderful in our eyes?”

Though the people had rejected and killed the son, Jesus Christ, God restored Jesus by raising him from the dead.

The resurrection of Jesus demonstrates that God has given up on humanity but had sent Jesus to rescue them.

He’s risen Jesus to be the cornerstone.

The cornerstone is the first stone that’s laid when a building is being built.

The whole direction of the building depends on where the cornerstone is set.

Jesus is the cornerstone of God’s kingdom; he will be beginning of a new people of God.

Though human beings have made a mess with God’s world, and the leaders of Israel made a mess with God’s people,

God is still in control.

He’ll restore all things through His Son, Jesus Christ.

And it’s something marvellous to behold.

A few months ago, I spoke with a Parish Councillor.

Let’s call him John.

John was a Parish Councillor of a Church in the 1960’s.

In fact, he was a Parish Councillor for ten years.

However, he told me plainly that even though he was a Parish Councillor, he was not a Christian.

It was not until after he left the Church and went somewhere else that he became a Christian.

He then went back to the Church and tries to explain the gospel to his old minister.

Rather than being encouraged, the old minister says, “Are you trying to convert me?”

Many years later, he called up the Church and asked the new minister what the Church now believes.

The minister says that we believe in Jesus, and that it’s only through him that we’re saved.

John was very happy to hear that after many, many years, and despite the old minister, God hasn’t given up on his Church.

Isn’t that amazing?

Though men and women reject God and continues to make a mess of his world, God’s is patient with us.

Don’t ever stop being amazed at God’s patience with us!

Don’t ever take what God has done in Jesus for granted!

And don’t be like the tenant farmers who refuse to give to the true owner of the vineyard.

Give God his due!

What does it mean to give God his due?

That’s what we see in the next part of the passage.

Before we know what it means to give God his due, we need to answer Jesus’ question - whose image is it?

Whose image is it?

Come with me to verse 13, “Then they sent some of the Pharisees and the Herodians to Jesus to trap him in his words.”

Back in Jesus’ days, the Jews were living under the Romans and had to pay taxes to Caesar.

And of course, the Jews didn’t like it.

These Pharisees and Herodians then came and asks Jesus whether it’s lawful to pay taxes to Caesar or not.

Why is this a trap?

Talk with the person next to you.

Text in your answer or if you’re here, tell us your answer.

You have twenty seconds.

If Jesus says no to paying taxes, then he can get arrested by the Romans for breaking the law.

If Jesus says yes to paying taxes, then he’s seen to betray his countrymen.

So what does Jesus say? In verse 15, ““Why are you testing me? Bring me a denarius to look at.” 16 They brought a coin. “Whose image and inscription is this?” he asked them.

“Caesar’s,” they replied.

A denarius was a coin that was worth about one day’s wage.

On the coin, it has the picture of the then Emperor, Tiberius Caesar, with the Latin inscription saying, “Tiberius Caesar Augustus, Son of the Divine Augustus.”

It was a Roman coin, with the face of the Roman emperor, with a pagan, religious description of the emperor.

That was Caesar’s coin – it belonged to Caesar.

Jesus then tells them, in verse 17, “Give to Caesar the things that are Caesar’s, and to God the things that are God’s.”

Basically, Jesus is saying since the coin has the image of Caesar on it, it belongs to Caesar.

Since the image on the coin show who the true owner is, and since the coin has Caesar on it, it’s right to give what is Caesar’s back to Caesar.

And so, Jesus is saying that there is a place for human government in God’s world, even if the head of that government is a non-believer like Caesar.

It’s right for Christians to pay taxes, obey laws and show respect to the government.

It’s right for the Christians to give the governments their due.

And as the same time, we need to give God his due.

If the image of Caesar on a coin shows that it belongs to Caesar, then men and women, bearing the image of God, show that we belong to God.

We might owe taxes to governments, but we owe a lot more to God.

We owe God all of our lives, all of our possessions, all of our loyalty, all of our love.

We are commanded to love the Lord will all of our hearts, all of our soul, all of our minds, all of our strength.

We owe him our all.

We have a duty to give God our all – that’s what it means to give God his due.

It’s to give him our all.

That’s our duty to God.

Our duty is to God first, then after that, our duty to our government.

Sadly, there are governments who want to replace God with themselves.

Just like the tenant farmers, they want to throw God out and act as the ultimate owners of what God’s given them.

In many countries, the government doesn’t want you to give to God what belongs to God but to give to Caesar what belongs to God.

They don’t just want you to pay taxes; they want you to worship and adore government and leaders before God.

They want to take what is due to God for themselves.

Earlier this month, the state of Victoria has adopted the Change or Suppression Practices Prohibition Bill 2020.

This Bill makes is a criminal offence for praying or speaking to anyone about changing their sexual orientation of gender identity.

If you pray for them, even if they asked you to, then you’re breaking the law.

This may result in a prison term of up to 10 years and a fine of $200,000.

Many Christians who believe what the Bible teaches are very concerned about this.

Even non-Christians, including the Australian Medical Association and the National Association of Practising Psychiatrists spoke against this.

Even still, the government has not listened.

How should Christians respond?

Give to Caesar what is Caesar’s, give to God’s what is God’s.

We’re to keep trusting God and keep obeying God.

We’re to remain loyal to God and keep preaching God’s word and keep praying for people, even at great cost.

And so, keep praying for the people of Victoria, especially the Christians in Victoria, that they might stay loyal to God.

Keep praying also for the political leaders of Victoria, that they might see the distress that this will cause many people.

Pray for the Christian leaders in Victoria, that they will be faithful in teaching God’s word and to keep encouraging people to do good.

Why would Christians continue to teach the Bible, continue to pray, continue to obey God, even when the cost is so high?

It’s because they know the living God.

Whose God is God?

Whose God is God?

God is their God.

Look with me at verse 18, “Sadducees, who say there is no resurrection, came to him and questioned him”

It’s now the Sadducees turn to question Jesus.

The Sadducees didn’t believe in the resurrection and thought that it was utterly ridiculous.

And so they thought of a scenario where a man married a woman but had no child with her.

According to the law of Moses, if a man’s brother dies, leaving the woman with no child, then a man would take the wife and have a child for the brother.

This is so that the family line can continue with the brother.

The Sadducees said that this happened to a man, who died.

After the brother married the woman, he died.

And then another brother married her and he died.

This kept happening until all seven brothers died and the woman died too.

What would happen when they all come back to life?

Wouldn’t it be a ridiculous situation to be in?

It would be ridiculous, but only if you didn’t know the power of God or His word!

Listen to Jesus’ response in verse 24-25, “Jesus spoke to them, “Isn’t this the reason why you’re mistaken: you don’t know the Scriptures or the power of God? 25 For when they rise from the dead, they neither marry nor are given in marriage but are like angels in heaven.”

When the dead are raised, there’ll be in a new creation.

The way the new creation works will be categorically different from the things in this creation.

In this creation, in our world, marriage is an important part of how our society works.

But in the new creation, people will neither marry nor be given in marriage.

In the new creation, our communities won’t run like the ones here.

Instead, we’ll be like angels in heaven; we won’t be angels, but we’ll be like angels.

The new creation will be whole new order.

It’ll be too marvellous to describe.

It would be like trying to convey the majesty of a sunrise to a blind person.

The power of God is beyond our wildest imagination.

The new creation, the resurrected life, is possible because of the power of God.

The Sadducees were making fun of the new order with their understanding of the current order.

Jesus says they were wrong because they have underestimated the power of God.

They were also wrong because they didn’t know the Scripture.

Jesus says that in the passage about the burning bush, God said to Moses, “I am the God of Abraham and the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob?”

Then Jesus says, “27 He is not the God of the dead but of the living. You are badly mistaken.”

God is not the God of people who no longer exists, but he is the God of the living.

Even though these people have died physically many years ago, they are still living, spiritually, in God’s presence.

When you are saved by God, you’re in a relationship with him forever!

He is your God forever!

As long as God’s alive, you’re alive!

And since God lives forever, you’ll be living with him forever.

Even your death can’t stop your relationship with God!

To call the one true God, our God, our Father, is a wonderful blessing for anyone who calls themselves a Christian.

I am my God’s and he is mine, forever.

That’s the Christian hope; it’s a hope in the resurrection.

For the Christian, we have the hope of the resurrection not only because God had alluded to it in the Old Testament,

But because Jesus himself had risen from the grave.

The resurrection hope is real because Jesus had risen from the dead.

We can know for certain that our God is real, that our hope is real, because Jesus Christ lives.

One of the hymns that I sang every Sunday when I was young was the song Because He Lives.

The person who wrote this song was Gloria Gaither.

At that time, Gloria’s husband, Bill, had been very sick.

As a family, they had been discouraged by people whom they thought were their friends.

And they were expecting a third baby to be born.

Gloria was very sad about the world and was thinking about what kind of world will this baby be born into?

What hope does this little one have?

Then God slowly lifted her eyes to the hope of the resurrection.

This is what she says, “Gradually, the fear left and the joy began to return. I knew I could have that baby and face the future with optimism and trust. It was the Resurrection affirming itself in our lives once again. It was LIFE conquering death in the regularity of my day.”

And she wrote down the words to the song Because He lives

“Because He lives I can face tomorrow, Because He lives All fear is gone; Because I know He holds the future. And life is worth the living just Because He lives.”

That’s the Christian hope.

Because he lives!

That’s what Christians marvel at.

We stand amazed at the hope that we have in the risen Christ.

And we keep diving back into God’s word, so that we know God’s word better, so that we know God’s power better, so that we can stand amazed at God even more.

God owns the vineyard, God owns us because we’re his image and God’s the God of his people forever.

What about us?

Is God our God?

Am I loyal to God?

There are Christian brothers and sisters right now in Asia, the Middle East and in Africa who were refused life-saving aids because they identify themselves as Christians.

They would rather be loyal to God then to receive help that would save their lives.

Would you remain loyal to God if the cost is getting life-saving medicine for you and your family?

Is God your God?

If God is your God, then think about what your loyalty looks like in what you give him.

Do you give him your highest love?

Do you give him your undying loyalty?

Do you trust him with your earthly treasures?

Here are the three questions for this week:

How do I respond to God’s patience and commitment to me?

How have I shown my loyalty to God?

How do I grow in my loyalty to God?

Whose God is God?

My prayer is that God will be our God, that we will remain loyal to him through the good and bad times,

That God will proudly say of us, I am their God, one thousand years, ten thousand years from now.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for Jesus. We thank you that because he lives, we live. Please forgive us when we follow the ways of the world, pretending to live in a world without you when you’re the owner, Creator, Sustainer and King of this world. Father, please keep us firm in the hope of the resurrection. By your Spirit, help us see beyond this world and look forward to the next. Please be our God forever. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
