Did God really say?

Passage: Genesis 3:1-8

Big Idea: Adam is our Champion Sinner, making us unable to love truly, but Christ is our Champion Saint, bringing us back to our Creator’s love.

Adam our champion defies the Creator’s order (3:1-8)

Total Inability to truly love God and fellow men (Jeremiah 17:9)

Christ our Champion restores the Creator’s order (Romans 5:17)

Our champion summons us to:

Read God’s word

Watch out for sin

Know: In Adam, we’re sinners incapable to love truly but in Christ we’re restored to God’s love

Feel: fear of sin

Do: Read God’s word, actively watch out for sin

Once there was a household of people… made of paper.

Mr and Mrs Paper and their three children lived happily together and there was really only one thing they couldn’t do.

The little girl who made them had said, “You must not go out in the rain. You aren’t waterproof. You are made to stay dry.”

One day the paper family was listening to the radio and what do they hear? Singing in the rain!

They thought about how nice it would be to go walking in the mist and the rain. They took umbrellas.

They’re sure that they’d be fine.

Of course they weren’t fine at all.

The water splashed them here and there.

And before they knew it they had become stained and soggy.

And life was never the same again.

The little girl had been right.

Paper people just weren’t made for the rain.

In Genesis 3 the first man and his wife did something that they weren’t made to do.

They rebelled against God.

The Bible calls this rebellion sin.

Today, we’re going see the first sin, the first act of rebellion in our history.

Adam our champion defies the Creator’s order (3:1-8)

We’ll see that Adam is our champion who defied the Creator’s order (3:1-8)

Last week, we talked about how God created the heavens and the earth.

We used this picture to describe creation: God is the Creator; we’re the creatures.

God created the world.

Everything that God has made was perfect.

In this perfect world, God gave man a command.

Look with me at 2:16-17 16 And the Lord God commanded the man, “You are free to eat from any tree of the garden, 17 but you must not eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, for on the day you eat from it, you will certainly die.”

Many of us who will know the story: into his perfect world comes the snake.

It asked Eve a question: “Did God really say, ‘You can’t eat from any tree in the garden’?”

A simple question, but also a sneaky question.

First, he twists God’s word completely.

God said that they are free to eat from any tree in the garden.

However, they cannot eat fruits from one particular tree.

But the snake asks, “Did God really say, ‘You can’t eat from any tree in the garden’?”

Can you hear how similar it sounds to God’s order, except that it’s not what God’s said?

The snake knew what the LORD God has said and then completely distorts it.

Listen to how Eve responds, ““We may eat the fruit from the trees in the garden. 3 But about the fruit of the tree in the middle of the garden, God said, ‘You must not eat it or touch it, or you will die.’”

She’s close, but she makes a mistake. Did you notice it?

She said God said “You must not touch it” but as far as we know, God didn’t say not to touch it.

Eve is like the prey who is slowly but surely being ensnared.

The snake then goes for the kill, so to speak, “You will certainly not die.”

Here the snake deceives Eve by contradicting what God has said.

God says that they will surely die, but the snake says they will surely not die.

He also said that when Eve eat it her eyes will be opened and she will be like God, knowing good and evil.

In effect he’s saying, “God doesn’t really love you, otherwise he would’ve told you about what this fruit really does.

The snake is saying. “Don’t trust God, trust me.

Don’t follow his orders, follow mine.

We don’t need to follow his order for the universe; we can create our own.”

Can you see what the snake did?

He distorts God’s word, he deceives the woman, leading the woman to doubt whether God loves her.

What does the woman do?

She takes the bait and falls right into the snake’s trap.

She defies God.

Look with me at verses 6 “The woman saw that the tree was good for food and delightful to look at, and that it was desirable for obtaining wisdom. So she took some of its fruit and ate it; she also gave some to her husband, who was with her, and he ate it.”

The man was right there beside the woman.

But instead of telling her to stop, he defies God’s order with her as well!

What happens when they defy God?

Look with me at verse 7, “Then the eyes of both of them were opened, and they knew they were naked; so they sewed fig leaves together and made coverings for themselves.”

The snake said that when they eat the fruit, they will not surely die, their eyes will be opened, they will be like God, knowing good and evil.

What happened now after they ate the fruit?

They didn’t die straight away, their eyes were opened.

Was the snake right, after all?

Wasn’t there some truth in what the snake has said?

Yes there was. There was some truth in what he said.

But the problem was that it was a half-truth, it wasn’t a complete truth.

The snake told a half truth about what will happen when they defy God.

And as the famous Anglican theologian JI Packer once said, “A half-truth masquerading as the whole truth becomes a complete untruth.”

This is what happens here.

The snake told a half truth, a complete untruth, that has led to devastating effects on all of God’s creation.

After they realised that they have sinned against God, the man and woman were covered with shame.

Before they’ve sinned, they were naked and felt no shame.

But now after they’ve sinned, they realised were naked and they felt ashamed.

They felt vulnerability and they wanted to hide.

They want to hide their vulnerability from each other, but they also wanted to hide from God.

They didn’t want to be naked before God; they’ve lost their innocence before God.

They feel shame before God.

Total Inability to truly love God and fellow men (Romans 3:23)

Because of Adam and Eve’s defiance against God, they were unable to love God in the same way that they’ve had since the beginning.

They hid from him.

Ever since then, every man and woman, boy and girl, had also hid from God.

By default, we don’t want to have a relationship with him.

Our rebellion against God, our denial of God, keeps us from loving him.

That’s what sin is.

God is our Creator and as creatures we should worship and obey him.

Sin is us denying who God is.

To love our Creator is to obey him

But like the first man and the first woman, we disobey God and so we also fail to love him, the very thing God created us to do.

God created us to love him and we have failed.

We fail because we sin.

Romans 3:23 says this “For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God”

All of us have sinned and failed to love God.

But how is it that just because Adam the first man sinned, we’re now sinners as well?

Why is everyone now a sinner because Adam had sinned?

As the First Man, Adam represented us.

He represented the human race; he was our representative, our Champion.

What he did, he did it on behalf of us.

It’s a little like this.

If 2020 was just like any other year, then many athletes from all around the world would be travelling to Tokyo for the Olympics.

With the Covid19 pandemic, it’s been postponed till next year.

And so, next year, God willing, we will be cheering for our athletes.

We’ll be cheering for them as they swim in the pools, run in the tracks and as they play against other countries in the fields.

We’ll be filled with pride when they win gold for our country.

Why are we filled with pride when they win?

Because they’re our champions; they represent us!

In a similar way, Adam represented us; He was our champion

The destiny of the human race rested on him.

If he wins, we win; if he fails, we fail.

And sadly, he failed.

Because our representative failed, we all failed.

We share in Adam’s sin.

Like Adam, we fail to love God, we’re unable to love God.

More than that, we’re unable to love one another.

Our sin against God causes us not only to hide from God, but to hide from one another as well.

By default, we don’t want to love one another.

Not only do we keep away from one another, we harm and even kill one another.

Sadly, we don’t need to look very far to see the hatred the people of our world have against one another.

Just turn on the news, look at the internet, log on to twitter and we’ll see the hate.

Left to ourselves, we’re incapable to truly love our neighbour, no matter how hard we try.

Left to ourselves, we’ll follow the steps of Adam, our fallen champion.

Christ our Champion restores the Creator’s order (Romans 5:17)

But God would not let Adam’s failure define us.

He sent another Champion, the Lord Jesus Christ.

Look with me at what the Bible says in Romans 5:17, “If by the one man’s trespass, death reigned through that one man, how much more will those who receive the overflow of grace and the gift of righteousness reign in life through the one man, Jesus Christ.”

In this new Champion, Jesus Christ, we receive grace instead of death.

This Champion obeyed God perfectly and so he loved God perfectly.

He also loved his fellow men perfectly.

Jesus the New Champion succeeded where the first champion had failed.

The First Champion failed in the Garden of Eden when he listened to the lies of the serpent.

The New Champion succeeded in the wilderness and resisted the temptations of Satan.

Whereas Adam was the Champion Sinner, Jesus is the Champion Saint.

The hope of our humanity now rests in Jesus, the new Champion.

It’s only as we come to Jesus that we can truly love God as God wants us to.

It’s only as we come to Jesus that we can truly love our fellow men as God wants us to.

It’s only in the Lord Jesus that our rebellion against God, our sin, can finally be dealt with.

Jesus is our only hope.

Jesus is the champion who will lead us to God.

We’re going to find out more about how Jesus leads us back to God over the next few weeks.

For now, this is what our champion summons us to do:

Read God’s word

Satan distorted God’s word, then he led the woman to doubt God’s word.

The woman was then deceived and she, along with her husband defied God.

Satan knows God’s word and he wants to distort it to make us doubt God.

And so, we need to make sure we read God word.

Keep reading God’s word and keep studying it .

When you come to Church, or watch the sermons online, get some paper, take notes.

When I read the Bible in the morning, I take notes.

They don’t have to be long; just reflections on what God says to me.

How about you? What do you do study God’s word?

Second, watch out for sin.

While some sins are obvious, they often start off being very subtle.

“It’s just a white lie, it’s not a sin.” “Yes, it’s not the whole truth, but it’s not a sin”.

That’s exactly how sin starts; it starts off subtle;

It looks attractive, it might even look like it’s the wise thing to do, just like the fruit that the woman took a bite of!

Satan comes in uninvited and tells us this lie, “Go on, do it, it’s nothing.”

After we sin, he tells us, “Look, it’s all over for you. You’ve lost everything.”

But that’s a double lie.

It’s a double lie because Jesus the new Champion has come so that there’s now forgiveness of sins for all those who turn to him.

Yes, we have sinned, but because Jesus has died and paid for our sins, we now have forgiveness.

That doesn’t mean that we can keep on sinning.

Jesus didn’t die so that we can keep on sinning.

But Jesus has died for us so that we won’t keep on sinning; Jesus has died for us and conquered sin on our behalf.

He’s our Champion!

Here are the three questions for today:

How do I read God’s word? Do I only do it as part of my religious duties on Sunday? Or do I listen carefully to what God’s saying?

How have I failed to love God and my fellow men?

How can I honour Jesus by staying away from sin?

Friends, we are like the paper people, soaked and damaged in sin.

Thank God that he hasn’t left us but has won us back to him through our Champion Jesus Christ.

Let’s pray,

“Father, you know how frail we are. You know that we are of dust. You know how we have failed you time and time again. Father, help us to be humble and help us to know our weaknesses. Thank you that in Jesus, you’ve restored our relationships. In Jesus’s name, Amen.”
