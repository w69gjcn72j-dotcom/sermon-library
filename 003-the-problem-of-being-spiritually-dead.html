The deadness of being spiritually dead

The Farewell is a 2019 movie about a Chinese-American family.

The young woman at the centre of the movie has a grandmother who only has a short while left to live.

And so, they decided not to tell her and schedule a family gathering before she dies.

When the young woman asks the doctor isn’t it wrong to tell a lie to the grandmother, he replies that everyone in China does it when it comes to death.

Chinese families don’t like to talk about death, especially if it’s about the potential death of someone elderly.

It’s not just the Chinese, but people of other cultures as well, who are also afraid to talk about death.

Why are we afraid to talk about death?

It’s because death puts an abrupt, unnatural end to our relationship.

Death always feels wrong.

Today, we’re going to see how death came into the world.

Before we do, let’s pray,

“Father, as we read your word, please open our hearts by your Holy Spirit, that we may know you more clearly, love you more dearly, follow you more nearly, day by day. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”

Over the past two weeks, we’ve learnt that:

God is the Creator; we’re the creatures.

God created the world.

Everything that God has made was perfect.

We’ve also learnt that:

God is the Holy One; we’re sinners

God gave us a command but we’ve defied him.

Today, we’re going to see the result of our defiance against God.

We’re going to see how sinners fracture God’s perfect world

Sinners fracture God’s perfect world

If you have your Bible there, please open up to Genesis 3, beginning from verse 9,

“So the Lord God called out to the man and said to him, “Where are you?”

After the man and woman defied God’s command, they went into hiding.

God actively went to look for them, not because he didn’t know where they were, but because he wanted them to explain what they’ve done.

Here’s the man’s explanation, “I heard you in the garden, and I was afraid because I was naked, so I hid.”

By telling God that he’s naked, he’s already shown that he’s done something wrong.

Before he defied God, the man and his wife were naked before God and they felt no shame.

But now, they realised they were naked, and they were ashamed.

The man himself gave the evidence for the wrong he’s committed.

God asks, “Who told you that you were naked? Did you eat from the tree that I commanded you not to eat from?”

That’s why the man feels naked and ashamed.

He’s eaten from the tree that God told him not to eat from and he’s now ashamed of who he is and what he’s done before God.

But does he admit that he was wrong? Does he man up?

No! Instead, he plays the blame game.

In verse 12, “The woman you gave to be with me—she gave me some fruit from the tree, and I ate.”

Who’s the man blaming?

Well, he’s firstly blaming God!

He blames God for giving him the woman!

The man says that it’s God’s fault for creating the woman in the first place!

Second, he also blames the woman.

It’s her fault for giving him the fruit.

She started it!

Instead of admitting that it was his fault, he blamed that on everybody else!

Have you met men like these? Men who blames everyone else for all the bad things that happen in their lives instead of owning their mistakes?

Have you met grown men who blamed God and their wives for how their lives turned out? Instead of looking at their own failures?

Adam, the first man, was such a man.

Rather than blaming on his wife, Adam should've been guiding and protecting his wife.

He was responsible for her.

God tells him, the man, not to eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil (Genesis 2:16).

Adam has the responsibility to teach the woman God’s command.

This is God’s created order: God, man (and woman) creation.

But what happened to the order?

The snake (creation) taught the woman and the snake deceived her.

Then the woman taught the man, who was supposed to be looking after her. He should have said, ‘Woman, you know that I love you, but what you’re doing is wrong. You need to put that fruit down, now.’

The man should have stopped the woman.

But he didn’t. He just let her do what was wrong.

God’s created order is reversed: Creation (snake), woman, man, God.

Letting his wife to sin against God is not love.

Letting his wife indulge in sin is not love.

It’s being irresponsible as a husband and it’s being disrespectful to God.

And letting the woman openly disobey God didn’t bring the two of them closer.

In fact it did the opposite; it fractured their relationship.

He blames the wife for his sin; their relationship will never be the same again.

The woman follows the husband’s lead.

Look with me at verse 13, “So the Lord God asked the woman, “What have you done?” And the woman said, “The serpent deceived me, and I ate.”

Rather than owning her mistake, she blames the snake.

How does the snake respond?

Well, there’s no record of the snake’s response, but the Bible does record God’s judgment on the snake.

God cursed the snake to move on its belly and eat dust all the days of its life.

Given that the snake tempted the woman to eat the forbidden fruit, it’s fitting that it’s condemned to eat dust.

There’ll also be hostility between the snake and the woman and between its offerings and hers.

They, too, will have a fractured relationship.

Their fracture relationship is symbolic of a far wider fracture.

Firstly, there will be a fractured relationship between humans, the descendants of the woman and the animal kingdom.

While some animals might get along well with humans, many don’t and are in fact quite hostile!

But more importantly, there will be hostility between good and evil.

The snake, symbolising evil and death, will continue to war against man.

The snake will strike the man’s heel.

But one day, the man will strike the snake’s head.

One day, the evil that the snake represents will be destroyed.

The son of woman will conquer the snake.

That’s God’s judgement on the snake.

As for the woman, God judges her by intensifying her labour pains.

There will be a lot of pain when she gives birth.

Childbirth is a wonderful thing to celebrate.

The birth of a new life brings great joy.

But as any mother will tell you, giving birth brings great pain.

In fact, giving birth is dangerous for most of our human history.

Throughout history, every 100th to 200th birth led to the mother’s death.

As recent as 2015, 830 women from all around the world die every day due to complications with birth.

And so, while childbirth is wonderful, since the fall, it’s painful, sometimes even fatal.

Not only is the experience of childbirth fractured, the woman’s relationship with the man is also fractured.

The woman’s desire will be for her husband.

What does that mean?

The word ‘desire’ here means the ‘desire to rule’.

You see it over in Genesis 4:7.

In 4:7 sin desires to rule over Cain but Cain is told he must master it.

In other words he must rule over sin.

Now back in Genesis 3:16 when it says the woman desire will be for her husband it means she desires to rule over him.

In other words, marriages are now turned into power struggles.

There will be frustrations in their relationship.

The wife will desire to have the man, to take over him.

The man will rule over her.

That doesn’t mean that there’s any room for the man to exert excessive rule on the wife.

There is no excuse of domestic violence of any sort in any marriage.

The husband has responsibility for his wife and ought to look after her, as a kind and responsible leader.

In the perfect world, both the husband and the wife will live in perfect harmony.

But in the fallen world, there will be stress and power struggles in marriages, even in Christian ones.

Finally, God judges the man by cursing the ground.

Instead of listening to God, the man listened to his wife, and so from now on, he will have to work hard for his food.

Instead of having a ready supply of food in the garden, the man will now labour and work hard for his food.

Man can expect his work to be difficult; the world will be a challenging place to work in.

Just as there will be always be weeds and thorns in the field, there will always be budgets to be balanced, there will always be illnesses to be cured, there will always be problems to be solved.

Work will be difficult and we’ll keep working and striving until we return to dust.

For God’s said, “For you are dust, and you will return to dust.”

After all our work, after all our toil, we’ll return to the ground.

We’ll die.

Dead sinners are condemned to death

That’s the consequence of our defiance against God.

We fracture our relationships with God, we fracture our relationships with each other, and we fracture our relationship with the world.

But these are more than just the natural consequences of our actions.

These are God’s judgements on our actions.

It’s God the Judge who judges us, who sentences us to death.

Look with me at verse 22, “The Lord God said, “Since the man has become like one of us, knowing good and evil, he must not reach out, take from the tree of life, eat, and live forever.”

As painful as the labour pain is, as stressful as the tension in a marriage may be, as hard as working in the fallen world is,

These don’t compare with God denying the man and the woman access to the tree of life.

Before they’ve sinned, they were forbidden to eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil,

But they were free to eat from the tree of life.

After they’ve sinned and ate from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, they were forbidden to eat from the tree of life.

By eating the forbidden fruit, they were forbidden to eat from the tree of life.

Instead, just as they were made from dust, they will return to dust.

When God breathed life into them, they became a living being, alive to God, made to love him and to be loved by him.

But now, a spiritual darkness comes upon them.

It’s a darkness that infects their minds, their heart, their souls.

It’s a spiritual darkness that fills every part of their being, making them spiritually dead to God.

They become spiritually dead beings.

Since the first man and the first woman defied God, every person is born as a spiritually dead being.

This spiritual darkness separates all of us from God.

What’s the mark of someone in spiritual darkness?

It’s the same mark that marks the ancient snake, the Devil himself.

It’s the desire to be independent from God, it’s the desire to run your own life without God.

That’s what he suggested to Eve, is it not?

“You will not surely die! You will be like God”

That desire to rebel against God is exactly what the Devil wants!

Whenever someone disobeys God by taking the forbidden fruit or telling a lie or not loving their neighbour,

They are not just making an independent decision.

When someone tries to run their own life and disobey God, they’re following the Devil’s footsteps.

To disobey God is exactly what the Devil wants you to do!

To turn our back on God, to leave God is what the Devil wants everyone to do.

What’s God’s punishment for the Devil and his angels?

God’s prepared an eternal fire for the Devil and his angels; it’s called Hell.

Contrary to popular belief, the Devil doesn’t rule in hell.

It’s the opposite; the devil and his angels will be prisoners in hell.

The one who rules in hell is ultimately God himself.

For hell is the place of God’s wrath and anger against the Devil and his angels.

It’s a place where the Devil and his angels will experience God’s anger forever.

It’s a place for those who are spiritually dead, spiritually separated from God.

And it’s a place for all the sons and daughters of Adam and Eve, if they remain in their spiritual deadness.

Those who share in the Devil’s spiritual darkness, who want their independence from God, who remain disobedient to God, are destined for eternal, spiritual punishment.

That’s the condition of the human race; disobedient and spiritually dead before God.

The deadness of being spiritually dead

We see this deadness described in Ephesians 2:1, “And you were dead in your trespasses and sins”

Left to ourselves, we’re spiritually dead before God.

We might look alive physically but we’re dead spiritually.

As spiritually dead people, we follow the ways of the Devil; we can’t help but disobey God.

As spiritually dead people, we’re not even aware of our spiritual deadness.

Someone once said to a preacher, “I don’t feel this burden of sin that you’re preaching about”

And the preacher replies, “You’re right, dead people don’t feel any burden.”

If we’re spiritually dead, we don’t know how offensive we are to God, we don’t feel how dangerous we are from God’s anger.

Indeed, when Jesus the light of the world, came into the world, the world didn’t recognise him.

As creatures of spiritual darkness, as spiritually dead people, we don’t recognise God’s radiant Son when we see Him.

The dead are called to turn to the Risen Christ

Christ is risen, fight against deadness

Christ is coming, offer hope to a dead world

But thanks be to God, because he has given us his one and only Son.

In the first three chapters of the Bible, we see God’s creation and man’s downfall.

But from here onwards, we see how God begins his rescue mission to save the human race from our spiritual deadness by sending us Jesus.

In Jesus, God has given us a new hope.

That’s what we’re going to explore next week and in the rest of the sermon series.

But here’s a quick glimpse of what we’re going to see.

There’s still a few weeks to go and we’re going to explore this more but I can’t wait to share this news with you.

Here it is: Jesus Christ has conquered death by rising from the dead.

By his death on the cross and by his rising from the dead, Jesus has conquered sin and death.

For those who trust in him, we’ve been transformed from being in spiritual darkness to spiritual light.

We’ve been transferred from the dominion of the devil to the Kingdom of God’s Son.

We were enemies of God but now we’re adopted into God’s family.

That’s what you are! You’re spiritually alive.

And so, don’t live as though you’re still spiritually dead.

While you’re still physically alive, you will still be tempted to leave God, you will still be tempted to disobey God.

But if you belong to Jesus, resist that temptation.

Fight the spiritual deadness that still wants to take over you.

Don’t follow the Devil’s schemes anymore, don’t follow him in disobeying God.

Follow Jesus, trust in Him, ask him for the strength to be a good soldier as you fight against the world, the flesh and the devil.

If you don’t know the rest of the story of God’s mission and want to find out more, please send me a message.

I’ll love to share this hope of Jesus with you.

This is what St Paul’s Kogarah is about.

We’re a Church that wants to share the hope of Jesus with our community and our world.

We’re a Church where people can see the gospel of Jesus, hear the gospel of Jesus and tell the gospel of Jesus.

Here are the three questions for this week:

When was the last time blamed someone else or something else for your sin?

What do you do to stay alert against the Devil’s temptations?

How will the hope that Jesus motivate you this week?

A few weeks ago, a minister sent me a message about what to say at a funeral of a non-believer.

Usually, this minister would give messages at funerals of believers.

At the funerals of believers, we can offer messages of hope, that we will see this brother or sister again.

But we can’t give the same hope to those who aren’t Christians.

It’s always sadder to attend the funerals of those who didn’t know Jesus.

What message will people give at your funeral?

Will they say a few sentimental things about you and bid their farewell?

Or will they have the certainty of seeing you again because you both believe in Jesus Christ, the risen Lord.

My prayer is that everyone here watching this will have this certain hope:

That they’re no longer spiritually dead beings, but spiritually alive, confident to enter into God’s presence,

Because they believe in Jesus.

Amen.
