The Roman Road: The Eager Servant Proclaims God's Gospel

Romans 1:1–15 · 21 June 2026

Big Idea: King Jesus sends his servants, who pray for and long to see fellow saints, to announce God's gospel so the nations may obey him by faith.

Outline

1. Paul, a servant of the gospel of God

2. Jesus, the King of the gospel of God

3. Beloved saints, fruits of the gospel of God

Introduction

Imagine you have one message to send to the whole world. Just one. What would it say?

Some of us here have lived through remarkable moments in history — years of hardship and wonder, of watching the world change many times over. Through all of it, what is the one message that truly matters?

The Apostle Paul knew exactly what it was. He called it the gospel of God. And in his letter to the Romans, he explains it with a passion and clarity that has changed the world for two thousand years.

Please open your Bible to Romans chapter 1, beginning from verse 1.

"Father, by your Holy Spirit, help us to see your truth more clearly, that we may follow Jesus more nearly, and love you more dearly, day by day. For Jesus' sake, Amen."

1. Paul, a servant of the gospel of God

Paul begins his letter with an introduction of himself — but it's not what you might expect. He doesn't list his achievements, his education, or his status. He writes: "Paul, a servant of Christ Jesus, called as an apostle and set apart for the gospel of God."

The word servant here can actually be translated as slave. Paul has surrendered himself to fully obey Jesus. When Jesus says "go," Paul goes. When Jesus says "stay," Paul stays. If Jesus says "Jump!", Paul asks, "How high?" That is what belonging to Jesus looks like.

But who was Paul before all this? Many of us have lived long enough to know that people can change dramatically. Paul is perhaps the most dramatic example in the New Testament.

He was originally called Saul, and he hated the Christians. He thought they were teaching lies about God by calling Jesus the Son of God. He had Christians arrested, beaten, even killed.

Then Jesus appeared to him on the road to Damascus. In an instant, everything changed. Saul's whole understanding of the world was turned upside down. He came to see that Jesus truly was the Son of God. He submitted his life to Jesus. And from that day, he gave the rest of his life to announcing the gospel to the whole world.

Paul didn't choose this. Notice what he says: Jesus called him, Jesus set him apart. It was Christ's choice, not Paul's. And that is the wonder of grace — that Jesus calls even his enemies and transforms them into his faithful servants.

What is the gospel Paul was set apart to announce? It is the gospel of God — not a human invention, not a religious programme, but God's own message to the world. God had been preparing this message for centuries through the prophets, promising that one day, a new king would come. The Kingdom of God would arrive. And it did — in the person of Jesus.

2. Jesus, the King of the gospel of God

Look at verse 3: "concerning his Son, Jesus Christ our Lord, who was a descendant of David according to the flesh."

The gospel is about Jesus Christ. At the very heart of the gospel is not a doctrine, not a programme, but a person — the Son of God.

Jesus was a descendant of David, the great king of Israel. God had promised David centuries earlier: "Your house and kingdom will endure before me forever, and your throne will be established forever." (2 Samuel 7:16). Jesus is the heir to that eternal throne.

But Jesus is more than a king in a human political sense. Verse 4 tells us he was "appointed to be the powerful Son of God according to the Spirit of holiness by the resurrection of the dead."

When God raised Jesus from the dead, he was declaring to the whole world: this is my Son. This is the true King. What Jesus claimed about himself is true.

Many of you have trusted that truth for decades. You have seen Jesus prove himself faithful — through illness, through loss, through long seasons when prayer felt unanswered, and yet he was there. The resurrection is not merely an historical event; it is the foundation on which your entire life of faith has rested.

Because Jesus rose from the dead, he has authority over everything — over governments, over our own hearts. He has more authority over your life than you do yourself.

That is not a threat. It is the most comforting truth in the world. Because the One who holds your life is the very One who died and rose again to save you. You are safe in his hands.

3. Beloved saints, fruits of the gospel of God

Now look at how Paul addresses the Christians in Rome. Verse 7: "To all who are in Rome, loved by God, called as saints. Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ."

Loved by God. Called as saints.

You may feel that your more active days of service are behind you. You may feel a long way from the busy rhythms of congregational life. But hear this clearly: you are beloved saints of God. That has not changed. It never will.

Paul had never even met the Romans. He'd only heard of them. And yet, verse 8 tells us: "First, I thank my God through Jesus Christ for all of you because the news of your faith is being reported in all the world."

Your faith is known to God. Years — even decades — of trusting in Jesus, of praying faithfully, of enduring hardship with quiet trust: that is not invisible. It has not been wasted. It has been witnessed by the One who matters most.

And listen to how Paul prayed for the saints he had never met. Verses 9–10: "God is my witness, whom I serve with my spirit in telling the good news about his Son — that I constantly mention you, always asking in my prayers that if it is somehow in God's will, I may now at last succeed in coming to you."

Paul longed to see the saints in Rome. He begged God in prayer to open a way. Can you hear the longing in his voice? "Dear God, it's me again, asking for the same thing. Please — if it is your will — let me go and see them."

That longing is not unique to Paul. Your brothers and sisters at St Patrick's long to see you. You are prayed for. You are thought of. The body of Christ feels incomplete when we cannot be together. And yet, even when separated by distance or circumstance, we remain bound together in Christ — praying for one another, carrying one another before the throne of grace.

Paul also knew this was not a one-way gift. Verses 11–12: "I want very much to see you, so that I may impart to you some spiritual gift to strengthen you — that is, to be mutually encouraged by each other's faith, both yours and mine."

Paul did not imagine himself going simply to give and receive nothing. He expected to be encouraged by the faith of the Romans. And so it is with you. Your years of faithfulness, your endurance through suffering, your quiet and unshaken trust in Jesus — these things encourage others more than you may ever know.

Conclusion

If God has one message for the world, what is it?

The answer is right here in Romans 1. It is the gospel of God: that King Jesus is the Son of God, risen from the dead, seated on an eternal throne. And now is the time to turn to him and trust him.

Paul gave his whole life to announcing this message — not out of duty alone, but out of love: love for Jesus, and love for the saints. Even though it was costly — long journeys, danger, hardship — he went, because he owed it to them. God had paid the great cost of his Son to save Paul's life. So Paul gave his life to bring others the same good news.

You have received that same gospel. You have trusted that same Lord. And the same grace and peace that Paul prayed for the Romans is yours today: "Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ."

King Jesus sends his servants, who pray for and long to see fellow saints, to announce God's gospel so the nations may obey him by faith.

Closing Prayer

Father, we thank you for calling us to be yours through Jesus Christ, your Son and our Lord.

Thank you for the gospel that has sustained us through all the years of our lives. Thank you that you do not love us because of what we can do, but because of what Jesus has done for us.

We thank you for the saints gathered here — for decades of faithful trust in Jesus, for prayers offered, for lives quietly lived in your service.

Help us to keep trusting you, to keep following you, even now. And when the time comes for us to see you face to face, may we find that your grace has been sufficient all along.

We pray for those in our church family who long to be with us. May they be encouraged, as we are encouraged by them.

For Christ's sake, and in his name, Amen.

Reflection Questions

1. Paul saw himself as a servant — even a slave — of Christ Jesus. How has that kind of surrender to Jesus looked in your own life over the years?

2. The resurrection of Jesus declared him to be the powerful Son of God. How has Jesus proven his faithfulness to you through difficult seasons of life?

3. Paul prayed constantly for the saints in Rome, even before he had met them. Who are you still praying for, and what does that tell you about the bond we share in Christ?
