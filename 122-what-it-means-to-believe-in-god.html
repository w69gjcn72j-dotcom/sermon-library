What it means to believe in God

Big Idea: To believe Jesus is to believe God, leading to salvation; to reject Jesus’ words is to reject God’s words, leading to judgement.

Let’s pray, “Father, your word is life. Help us to receive your word today. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

What do you mean when you say, “I believe in God”?

What do you mean when you say, “I believe in God”?

According to the latest data that we have, the majority of the people living in Australia believe in God.

According to a survey done in 2019, almost 6 in 10 Australians believe in God, spirit, or some sort of life-force.

When asked about what they thought that Jesus was real or fictional, 57% of Australians understood Jesus to be a real person.

And of course, of those who believe in God, they have very different views of God.

As a multicultural society, we have people of different religions.

Some are adherents of Islam, others are followers of Hinduism and still others follow the ways of Buddha.

In Kogarah, almost one in five people follow Hinduism and almost the same number follow Roman Catholicism.

Almost one in four people in Kogarah don’t have any religion at all.

And so, when someone says, “I believe in God”, it can mean very different things to different people.

The statement, “I believe in God” though short, is not a simple statement.

When people say, “I believe in God”, what do they really mean?

Are they saying they believe in God as a life force, or God as Allah, or God as an idea?

Also, we can look at it from the other way.

When others say, “I don’t believe in God”, what do they mean?

Are they rejecting the idea of God as a higher, spiritual force or are they refusing to believe that God is a Personal, relatable Being?

What one person believes or not believe about God, can be very different to what another person believes or not believe about God.

Now, a common way to go around this is the example of the elephant and the blind men.

Have you heard of it?

The story goes like this: A group of blind men who had never came across an elephant before found one.

And they had to try to describe what the elephant looked like by touching it.

Some touched the trunk and said that the elephant is like a snake.

Another touched the tusk and said that the elephant is like a spear.

Some touched the ear and said the elephant is like a giant fan.

And so the blind men couldn’t agree what the elephant is.

Of course, if they only had eyes to see, the elephant is bigger than all those things.

They could describe honestly the part of the elephant they could see but they were all ultimately wrong, because they couldn’t see the whole.

And so, the moral of the story is, religions are like the blind men.

They can only understand parts of the story about God.

If only they had eyes, they would see that they’re all talking about the same thing.

Have you heard of that before?

A lot of people use that to describe how all religions all point to the one reality, even the one real God.

It seems to make sense until you think more seriously about it.

What’s the problem with this?

The problem is with the person who claims to see the problem.

Basically, he’s calling everyone else blind and only he can truly see.

The person who says that all religions are the same thing had almost certainly never seriously studied the different religions.

Try telling a Muslim that he’s no different from a Christian, or a Hindu that he’s no different from a Buddhist.

He or she would be rightly offended because they don’t believe the same thing about God.

To think that you can see that all religion are the same and point to the same God is a very ill-informed and arrogant approach.

It’s claiming that you can see and everyone else who disagrees with you is blind.

To use the elephant example to describe different religions is a seriously flawed argument.

It’s an approach usually adopted by someone who doesn’t believe in God at all.

But let’s start from the other side.

What if there is a God?

What if God is real? And what if He wants to tell us something about himself?

How would he do it?

Well, we don’t have to speculate because He has spoken to us about himself.

He has spoken to us in the Person of Jesus.

Believing Jesus is believing in God

For thousands of years before Jesus’ birth, God has been speaking to his people.

He spoke to Abraham, to Jacob, to Moses, to Joshua and many others.

He has spoken through prophets who had written down what God has said to them and recorded them in the books of the Old Testament.

But now, God speaks to us through His Son, Jesus.

Look with me at John 12:44-45, “Jesus cried out, “The one who believes in me believes not in me, but in him who sent me. 45 And the one who sees me sees him who sent me.”

Jesus is saying if you believe in Him, you are really believing in God, who sent Him.

That’s a massive claim!

Listen again to what Jesus said, “The one who believes in me believes not in me, but in him who sent me.”

He’s saying that those who believe in Him are those who believe in God.

That’s Jesus’ definition of belief in God.

What would you do if you believe in God? You would believe Jesus.

To believe in Jesus is to believe in God.

It’s a little bit like this.

One of the hardest things that medical professionals had to deal with is patient compliance.

What’s patient compliance? It’s a patient agreeing with what the Doctor had asked them to do and for the patient to actually do them.

Studies have shown that in developed countries like Australia, approximately 50% of patients with chronic disease don’t follow what their Doctor tells them to do.

Extensive research and studies might have shown that a certain medicine works.

It might lower the Blood Pressure, for example.

If the patient believes that the medicine works, they would take it regularly.

If they don’t believe it, they wouldn’t.

The patient’s action depends on whether they trust their Doctor and ultimately, whether they believe in the science behind the medicine.

Unfortunately, 50% of those with chronic diseases don’t comply, resulting in treatments that are effective in clinical trials (where people comply) but not in clinical practice (where 50% of the people don’t comply).

In a similar way, those who believe in God, will comply and obey Jesus.

Jesus says that when we believe in Him, we’re really believing in God.

Those who believe in God will believe in Jesus and follow Jesus.

If you don’t listen or obey Jesus, if you don’t comply with him, then it shows that you don’t believe in God.

That’s what belief in God is like – To believe God is to believe Jesus.

What are the consequences of believing in Jesus? And what are the consequences of not believing in Jesus.

If you believe in Jesus, he will rescue you from darkness.

Look with me at verse 46, “46 I have come as light into the world, so that everyone who believes in me would not remain in darkness.”

By default, we’re in the dark about God; we don’t know much about God at all.

And more than that, we’re in moral darkness.

By default, we live in a way that is evil in God’s eyes.

But God sent Jesus into the world to be the light of the world.

Jesus has come so to rescue us from darkness and to bring him into his marvellous light.

Jesus is the light who has come to save the world.

If you believe in Jesus, you will be saved.

However, rejectors of Jesus will find themselves judged by Jesus’ words.

Look with me at verse 47, “If anyone hears my words and doesn’t keep them, I do not judge him; for I did not come to judge the world but to save the world.”

Two thousands years ago, Jesus came not to judge but to save.

However, that doesn’t mean that there is no judgement for those who reject Jesus.

Judgement will come and the judge will be Jesus’ words.

Look with me at verse 48, “48 The one who rejects me and doesn’t receive my sayings has this as his judge: The word I have spoken will judge him on the last day.”

The very words that Jesus has spoken will judge all those who have rejected him.

On that day, on that very last day, the words of Jesus will condemn the rejectors to everlasting darkness.

It’s a little like this.

A few weeks ago, I received a letter from State Revenue.

It tells me that I was fined for speeding on Princes Highway.

What? On Princes Highway? How?

Now, the section that it said I sped in was a section that I’ve travelled many times before and I never had an issue.

It said that I was driving faster than the speed limit of 40Km/hr.

I looked at the time and it said 1:50pm, that’s more than half an hour before school zone.

And I was sure there were no schools there!

How could they have charged me with speeding?

But just to be sure, I drove back to have a look.

As I approached, the section, I slowed right down, and sure enough, there were plenty of signs saying that the speed is 40KM/hr.

They were put there around the lockdown time and I haven’t travelled there much since then.

But now, they’re there, permanently, 24/7.

And so, I dutifully paid my fine.

The words of Jesus are like the signs.

For example, Jesus says “He is the Light of the World”.

We might say that’s just his opinion, or that’s it’s not true.

Or we might simply hear it and don’t do anything about it.

On the last day, those very words of Jesus will judge those who rejected Jesus.

It will be as if Jesus himself says to those who reject him, “Why didn’t you believe me in your lifetime when I say I am the Light of the world?”

“Why didn’t you believe me when I say, “I am the Way, the Truth and the Life.” “Why didn’t you obey me when I say Deny yourself, take up your cross and follow me?”

On that day, there will be no excuses.

We can’t say that we didn’t have the time or the opportunity in our lifetime to turn to Jesus.

No amount of arguing or pleading will save us.

The words of Jesus will judge you and condemn you for the rest of eternity.

To reject Jesus is a dangerous thing to do, and yet many of us do it.

Sometimes, rather than listening to God’s word, we look at the phone.

Sometimes, we talk with the person next to us throughout the Church service.

Other times, our minds might just wonder off and we start daydreaming.

It’s like seeing the screensaver, except rather than on the computer, it’s on people’s faces.

If we do these things instead of listening to God’s word, we are in real danger of rejecting what Jesus is saying.

We’re in danger of ignoring his signs and rightly getting punished and condemned on the last day.

Notice that the condemnation here is not only for the people outside the Church.

It’s for anyone who has rejected Jesus and not received his word, and so, it’s for anyone who had heard Jesus’ word, whether they heard it inside the Church or not.

For Jesus’ words are not Jesus Jesus’ words.

They are the very words of God.

It’s the Father who commands Jesus what to say.

Look with me at verse 49-50, “49 For I have not spoken on my own, but the Father himself who sent me has given me a command to say everything I have said. 50 I know that his command is eternal life. So the things that I speak, I speak just as the Father has told me.”

Jesus speaks just as the Father has told him.

Every word of Jesus comes from God the Father.

And the words that God the Father had commanded Jesus God the Son to say are eternal life.

It’s because of the words of God, given through Jesus, that we have eternal life.

To reject Jesus’ words is to reject the Father’s words.

And to reject the Father’s word is to reject eternal life.

That’s why it’s no small thing when someone say that they don’t believe in Jesus.

When someone say they don’t believe in Jesus, it’s far more serious than just a personal opinion.

According to Jesus’ definition of belief, what they are ultimately saying is that they don’t believe God.

To reject Jesus’ word is to reject God’s word.

And in so doing, they remain in darkness, rejecting the light.

In so doing, they have rejected eternal life.

Believe and receive Jesus’ words of eternal life

And so, it’s the most important thing you can do is to believe and receive Jesus’ words of eternal life.

There are five steps to this.

Mind, Heart, Hands, Words, Communion of the saints (信圣徒相通)

Mind

Use your mind to understand Jesus’ words.

Have a hunger for it; Know it and Memorise it.

Read your Bible daily. You can use devotionals like Our Daily Bread to help you read the Bible.

Join a Growth Group

Right now, almost half of our Church is in a Growth Group.

There are five sets of brothers and sisters meeting up in 1to1 groups to grow in God’s word together.

Don’t ever let your hunger stop.

Your hunger for Jesus’ words is a sign that you’re alive because it’s only people who are alive and growing who gets hungry.

Hunger for God’s word.

Heart

Belief in Jesus is not merely an intellectual exercise; it’s not just a course to pass.

Rather it’s a conviction.

To receive Jesus’ words is to receive them into your heart.

You’re convinced by it and willing to pay a heavy price for it because you believe in your heart that it’s true.

And so, don’t stop living sacrificially for Jesus.

When you live sacrificially, it’s a sign of true belief, for only people who are convicted in their heart about the truth of Jesus will give sacrificially.

Hands

If you believe Jesus’ words, you will do it.

You will show by your action that you believe Jesus.

If you trust in Jesus, you will obey him; Only people who trust in Jesus obey Jesus.

Obedience to Jesus is a sign that you believe in Jesus

Words

You won’t just do good deeds, but you will tell people why you are doing these things.

You will give glory to Jesus; you will tell people about him.

You will give Jesus all the credit, all the thanks for any good deed that you’ve done.

If you believe that Jesus is from God, and that he speaks the very words of God, you will speak and let other people know.

Glorifying Jesus with your words is a sign that you believe in Jesus.

Glorifying Jesus with your words is a sing that you believe in God.

Communion of the Saints

None of us follow Jesus alone.

Jesus calls a people to follow Him.

That’s what the Church is; Church is a group of people, made holy by Jesus to follow Him.

That’s what we call the Communion of the saints.

Christians are saints because they’ve been made holy by Jesus.

If you believe Jesus, you will walk with fellow believers towards Jesus.

As we combe before Jesus, we receive his words together as a group of believers, as the communion of the saints.

We help each other receive and believe the words of Jesus.

That’s a sign of true belief.

Those who have received Jesus’ words will help other saints to walk in the light.

Those who are not interested or not keen to help one another walk in the light shows that they are probably still in the darkness.

But those in the light of Jesus, those who knows how good it is to walk in his light, will be keen to bring others into his light.

And so, how do we receive and believe the words of Jesus?

With all of our minds, with all of our hearts, with all of our actions, with all of our words and with all of God’s people.

That’s what it means to believe in Jesus; that’s what it means ultimately to believe in God.

For to believe in Jesus is to believe in God.

And to reject the words of Jesus is to reject the words of God.

Listening and receiving the words of Jesus will lead to salvation.

Rejecting and dismissing Jesus’ words will mean staying in the darkness, and waiting for judgement.

What will you choose?

Friends, believe and receive Jesus’ words, for they are God’s words.

It might be extremely difficult, it might be very costly, but when we believe and receive Jesus’ words, we will show ourselves to be people who have been saved from darkness and brought into the light.

Vyacheslav Kogut is the executive director of Prison Fellowship Ukraine.

A few weeks ago, Vyacheslav was very angry.

His fellow Ukranian military had just liberated another village on the eastern front.

That was great! But what he was angry about was that there were Russian prisoners of war in the prison that he was looking after.

The warden of the prison told him, “We have Russian prisoners of war who need clothing,”

“I’ll bring them skirts and dresses,” Kogut shot back, grumbling.

He was angry because he had to leave his fellow Ukraians wounded while he looked after these Russian Prisoners of war (POW).

But then he remembered his Bible: If your enemy is hungry, feed him—as well as, I needed clothes, and you clothed me.

He then went into the storehouse that collected goods for displaced Ukrainians and took the best of its donated items to give to the Russian POW.

“It is a way to show many people, besides these prisoners, that God is love,” said Kogut. “And when they go back to Russia, they can never again return with guns and hatred.”

Kogut had so many reasons to not show the Russians love, and he felt almost disgusted at the thought of showing them love.

However, he received the words of Jesus and believed the words of Jesus, and so would rather obey Jesus than to withhold love from the Russian POW.

Friends, you heard God’s word today. What will you do about it?

Will you receive it? Or will you reject it?

It’s easy to reject Jesus’ words; you reject it by default by not doing anything about it.

You reject it by default by forgetting about it.

However, if you want to believe it, if you want to receive it, you will need to be intentional about it.

One way that I make sure my mind don’t wonder while I listen to God’s words is to make notes.

I would have a book or some paper and I would make notes as I listen.

That’s me.

For the children, it might be simply to draw things that you heard during the sermon.

For others, it might simply talking with other people about God’s word over morning tea.

It’s great to have conversations about God’s word straight after hearing it.

It’s great that Range’s group meet up to talk about it in his group afterwards.

It’s also great that Dr Chen’s group reads the passage and talks about it before Sundays!

If you want more ideas, take the book, “Listen Up!” at the back. I’ve printed some Chinese copies for us to take.

Do whatever you can to receive and believe the words of Jesus, for they are God’s words.

Friends, here are the three questions for this week:

What do I mean, when I say that I believe in God (if I say that I believe in God)?

What word of Jesus am I in danger of rejecting?

Where is my weakness in receiving Jesus’ words? Is it my mind? My heart? My hands? My words? Or my relationship with other Christians?

Friends, to believe in Jesus is to believe in God.

And so, believe and receive Jesus’ words, for they are God’s words.

Let’s pray, “Father, we thank you for giving us your One and Only Son, our Lord Jesus Christ. He is the Word who speaks your word. He is the Word who speaks words of eternal life. Forgive us when we listen and forget his words. Please help us to listen. Help us to receive his words with all of our minds, with all of our hearts, with all of our actions, with all of our words and with all of God’s people. May his word take hold of us and transform us, so that we might be more like Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
