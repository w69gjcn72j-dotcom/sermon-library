The proof of God’s love

Passage: Romans 5:6-11

Big Idea: Rejoice! For the death of Christ, through whom we’re justified and reconciled, is proof that God loves us.

1. The helpless enemies of God

2. Christ’s death is proof of the love of God

3. The Justified and Reconciled rejoice in God

Rejoice in God’s love

Receive with humility

In the last census, there’s an increase in the number of people who identify themselves of having no religion.

The number increased because of the way the census was presented, but even so, there were many people who have no religion.

They would argue that there’s no proof that there’s a God, even less proofs that there’s a God who loves them.

In today’s passage, we’ll see the proof that God himself gives.

And so, if you haven’t done so already, bring a Bible, a pen and a paper.

We’re going to see God’s proof of his love for us.

Over the past four weeks, we’ve begun our overview of the seven stars, the seven sets of teaching (or doctrines) that will help us have a better grasp of what the Bible is saying.

We learnt that:

“In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth.” Genesis 1:1

God is the Creator, we’re the creatures.

“Then the eyes of both of them were opened, and they knew they were naked” Genesis 3:7

God is the Holy One, we’re the sinners.

“So the Lord God sent him away from the garden of Eden” Genesis 3:23

God is the Judge, we’re the judged.

As a result of our sins, we’ve been separated from God.

That’s what’s happened in the beginning.

Since then, every man and woman, boy and girl, have been born separated from God.

And the situation has not evolved or got better over time; in fact, it’s gone worse.

There are many descriptions in the Bible about the state that we’re in.

In today’s Bible passage, there are four words that describe who we are.

1. The helpless enemies of God

If you have your Bible there, please open up to Romans 5:6.

We’ll see that we’re helpless enemies of God.

“For while we were still helpless, at the right time, Christ died for the ungodly”

The first description we have here is the word helpless.

We’re helpless, powerless to do anything about our relationship with God.

You might have heard the old saying, “God helps those who help themselves”?

If that’s the case, then God would help no one, because no one would help themselves.

There’s nothing in us that want to turn to God.

Left to ourselves, we can’t become a more holy person or make ourselves more acceptable to God.

We’re utterly helpless.

That’s because we’re utterly ungodly.

That’s the second word: ungodly.

Whatever godly character God has; we have the opposite.

To be godly is not the same as being religious.

By coming to Church, by having the Holy Communion, even by praying doesn’t make a godly person.

You can do all these things and still be an ungodly person.

That’s because being godly is a posture of worship before God.

It’s a constant awareness of how awesome God is and to always live a life that’s in line with his will.

We might think that we’re godly, but we’re not.

By default, we’re ungodly, we don’t worship God as we should and we don’t do his will.

So, we’re helpless and ungodly.

Thirdly, we’re sinners.

The Bible tells us in Romans 3:23 that “All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God”

We have all broken God’s laws and we’ve become tainted, broken people.

Fourthly, we’re enemies of God.

Again, left to ourselves, we don’t love God, we’re hostile to God.

We don’t want God to rule over every aspect of our lives.

We don’t want to follow God’s laws; rather we want to make laws of our own.

We rebel against God; we make God our enemy.

But it’s not just one-way; it’s not just us who made ourselves enemies of God.

There’s a sense in which God is hostile towards us.

Does God hate us, as much as we hate him?

Yes, and even more so, because He knows our darkness, our hate, even more than us.

He sees the depths of our hearts in a way that we can’t.

And God hates it; he hates us and our sin with a holy hatred.

God can’t turn a blind eye on our evil; that would make God unjust.

It’s an evil that he has to deal with.

When we’ve sinned against God, we’ve become God’s enemy and God has become ours.

As helpless, ungodly sinners, we’ve become enemies of God.

2. Christ’s death is proof of the love of God

Though we are helpless to save ourselves, God has not left us to ourselves.

God loves us and he took the initiative to save us!

Look with me at Romans 5:6, “For while we were still helpless, at the right time, Christ died for the ungodly.”

Jesus Christ, the Son of God, died on our behalf, died for the ungodly.

Jesus came at the right time, some 2000 years ago and died outside the city walls of Jerusalem.

Jesus came to die for us; he died in our place at the cross.

As enemies of God, we go into battle against God and we deserve to face God’s full vent against us.

But Jesus has come to take God’s anger, God’s wrath, against us.

God had sent Jesus, his one and only Son, to die in order to save us from himself.

God sent his One and only Son to die for the ungodly.

This is something that’s distinctive of God.

It’s God’s sacrificial love for man.

Don’t men die sacrificially as well?

Yes, look with me at verse 7, “ For rarely will someone die for a just person—though for a good person perhaps someone might even dare to die.”

Someone might die for someone who’s just, who does what is right.

Others might die for a good man, a kind man, knowing that this man would live to continue to do good to others.

But how does God show his love? Verse 8,

“ But God proves his own love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.”

God sent his son to die for helpless, ungodly sinners who are his enemies.

It’s a bit like this.

It doesn’t take a lot of imagination to see that we live in a divided world.

We live in a world where there’s increased tension between the East and the West.

We live in a world where there’s a deep divide between the politically right and the politically left.

We live in a world where something as simple as the colour of our skin can cause suspicion and mistrust.

I found an article this week that says, “Church, don’t let Coronovirus divide you.”

There are many different opinions among Christians about how to resume in-person gathering.

Sometimes personal opinions are so strong that Churches divide over the issue.

Now, I want you to think of someone that you don’t agree with.

Maybe it’s a person who don’t agree with your political view.

Maybe it’s someone that you’re competing with.

Maybe it’s even someone you’ve separated from.

Can you see that person in your mind?

Now, take all your money, all your properties, and each member of your family, and give it to this person.

It’s not because the person asked you and it’s certainly not because the person has changed.

You do it even when you find this person utterly repulsive because you love the person.

I don’t know about you, but I find the thought of doing this difficult if not humanly impossible.

Why would I want to sacrifice so much for someone I find so repulsive?

That’s what God’s done, except that we are over a billion times more repulsive to God.

God loves us, despite of how repulsive we are, and he proved his love by giving us his one and only Son.

The Christian writer John Stott puts it like this:

“The more the gift costs the giver, and the less the recipient deserves it, the greater the love is seen to be.

Measured by these standards, God’s love in Christ is absolutely unique.

For in sending his Son to die for sinners, he was giving everything, his very self, to those who deserved nothing from him except judgement.”

God gave everything to you and to me who deserve nothing from him except judgement.

What kind of love is this?

It’s an utterly incredible and magnificent love!

What happens as a result of Jesus dying for us?

There are two things listed here.

Firstly, we’re justified.

Look with me at verse 9, “How much more then, since we have now been justified by his blood, will we be saved through him from wrath.”

To be justified is to be declared right by the Judge.

It’s the Judge’s decision that the accused is not guilty of the crime he was accused of.

In our case, we are guilty of sinning against God.

We deserved God’s judgement.

But Christ died so that by his death, by his blood, we can be justified before God.

We deserve to die but Christ, the Sinless One, died in our place.

Christ’s death on the cross paid for ours.

So firstly, we’re justified by Christ’s death.

Second, we’re reconciled to God.

We were weak enemies of God; we were hostile to God.

But by the death of His Son, God reconciled us to himself.

We were his enemies but now he’s made us his family.

Through Christ’s death, God brought us back to him.

And so, whenever God looks at those who trust in Jesus, he sees us as justified and reconciled people.

He sees a people who had been justified, made right by the death of Jesus.

He sees a people who had been reconciled, brought back by the death and life of Jesus.

When God looks at those who are in Jesus, he sees Jesus.

But wait, there’s more!

These verses just don’t talk about being justified now and reconciled now.

These verse talk about the future as well.

The idea of these verses is that if God has done the hard work of justifying us and reconciling us by the death and life of Jesus, he will do the relatively easier work of saving us in the future.

Look with me at verse 9 again, “How much more then, since we have now been justified by his blood, will we be saved through him from wrath.”

One day, God will come back and judge the world.

On that day, the unbelieving and hostile world will feel the wrath of the Living God.

That Day is known in the Bible as the Judgement Day.

But because he loves the world, God has sent His Son Jesus Christ to save those who would trust in him.

If God has done the hard work of sending His Son to die for those who trust in Jesus, wouldn’t he also save us on the Day of Judgement?

Of course, he would.

Also, verse 10, “10 For if, while we were enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of his Son, then how much more, having been reconciled, will we be saved by his life.”

If God has done the greater thing of reconciling us to himself through the death of His son, wouldn’t he also save us, whom he’s already reconciled?

Of course, he would.

That’s the hope that Christian have.

Jesus has come, died our death, to save us from God’s wrath.

Though God’s Day of Judgement will come, we don’t need to fear, because God will save us on that Day.

How should we respond?

The Justified and Reconciled rejoice in God

Rejoice in God’s love

Receive with humility

As a justified and reconciled people, we rejoice in God.

Look with me at verse 11, “11 And not only that, but we also boast in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom we have now received this reconciliation.”

Firstly we rejoice in God’s love.

We praise God for what he’s done for us.

We say with confidence that God loves us!

We were God’s enemies but now we’ve been justified, we’ve been reconciled.

That’s why we praise him; that’s why we sing!

Christians are known as a people who sing praises to God because we rejoice in what God has done for us!

Unlike other religions, we don’t mumble endless prayers, we don’t go to temples to offer incense.

As Christians, we sing!

We praise God for what he’s done!

I’ve spoken with a number of ministers who are starting to come back together as Churches and one of the hardest things that they have to do is to not sing.

As one of the precautionary steps we need to take, we can’t have congregational singing when we come together.

That’s going to be hard.

But when we do come together, let’s rejoice, let’s smile.

Though we can’t sing together as a congregation at Church, I encourage you to join with us later on as we sing praise to God, each of us in our own home.

If you haven’t done so already, invite another family to come into your home and watch the Church service together.

Be a good host and help others rejoice in God.

How have you rejoiced in God recently?

Second, as Christians, we receive with humility what God’s given us.

Christians are confident in God’s love for them, not because they are better but because God is good and gracious.

Jesus died for us while we were still helpless.

And so, we don’t boast in our action but we boast in God.

It’s all God.

And so we must receive what God’s given to us in Christ with humility.

It’s a joy to see the humility of young men and women who humble themselves before God, wanting to know more about the reconciliation that they have in Christ.

It’s an even greater joy to see the humility of brothers and sisters, who are well-versed in the Bible but who still comes with humility, longing to learn from God’s word.

These brothers and sisters might not have everything, but in Christ, they have the most precious thing.

They have salvation, they have justification, they have reconciliation.

It’s a treasure that the world can’t give, and the world can’t take away.

Here are the three questions for this week:

When was the last time you expressed your joy in your salvation in Jesus? How did you express that joy?

How have you expressed your humility before God as you think about what He’s given you in Jesus?

What do you need to change in light of God’s love for you in Jesus?

Friends, we live in an utterly broken world.

It’s a world with very few reasons to smile about.

But it’s a world that God has loved so much, that he gave his one and only Son, so that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life.

It’s a world that badly needs to hear that God loves them.

It’s a world that needs to hear that God has already proved his love for them by the precious blood of Jesus Christ.

Let’s pray,

“Oh, the splendour of His greatness,

Oh, the beauty of His love,

Oh, the depths of His forgiveness,

The wonder of the Father's love.

Father, your love is beautiful. Your love is wonderful. It’s majestically rich and it’s more precious than anything else in the world. Father, please forgive us for the time when we’ve taken your love for granted. Please forgive us when we’ve cheapened your love for us in Jesus. Please humble our hearts before you, that we may always receive with humility the love you have for us and to rejoice in the salvation, the justification and the reconciliation that you’ve won for us in your Son Jesus Christ, in whose name we pray, Amen.
