Christmas Lesson: Luke 2 (Years 3–6)

8 October 2026

Lesson overview

Children will discover that the baby in the manger is God's promised Saviour, Messiah and Lord, sent by God's plan to rescue people who could never save themselves.

The lesson is framed as a detective case, "The Case of the Baby in the Manger". Students examine clues in Luke 2 to answer one question: who is this baby, really?

MaterialsBibles or printed passage, Case File worksheet, pencils, envelope of 5 clue cards, a small wrapped box, whiteboard

By the end, students should be able to:

  1. Name the three titles the angel gives Jesus and say what each means.

  2. Explain that God was in control of the details, even a Roman census.

  3. Explain why Jesus came: to save us from sin, not just to be a nice story.

  4. Respond like the shepherds: tell others and praise God.

Teacher's background notes

Luke 2 is not a sweet story about a baby; it is God announcing the arrival of the King who saves. Read these notes before class so the big truths stay central.

God is sovereign over history (2:1-7). Caesar Augustus thinks he is in charge, ordering a census of the whole empire. Yet his decree moves Joseph and Mary to Bethlehem, exactly where God promised the Messiah would be born (Micah 5:2). The most powerful man in the world serves God's plan without knowing it. Jesus is born in the town of David because he is David's promised Son (2 Samuel 7:12-16).

The King comes in humility (2:7, 12). The manger is the sign. God's Son lays aside his glory and comes as a real, helpless baby (Philippians 2:6-8). This is the incarnation: fully God and fully human, so he can stand in our place.

Grace goes to the unexpected (2:8-12). The good news goes first to shepherds: ordinary, low-status workers. Nobody earned this announcement. That is grace, and it is "for all the people" (2:10).

Three titles carry the whole gospel (2:11).

  • Saviour: he rescues us from our real problem, sin and God's judgement (Matthew 1:21).

  • Messiah (Christ): the anointed King God promised through the Old Testament.

  • Lord: the title used for God himself. This baby is God, and he rules.

God receives the glory (2:13-14, 20). Heaven's response is worship. Peace comes "to those on whom his favour rests": peace with God is his gift, not our achievement (Romans 5:1).

Two right responses (2:15-20). The shepherds go, see, tell and praise. Mary treasures and ponders. Children can do both.

Extension: Simeon (2:25-32). Simeon has waited his whole life. Holding Jesus, he says he has seen God's salvation, a light for the Gentiles too. This points forward to the cross (2:34-35) and includes children of every nation in the room.

Watch out for turning the lesson into "be kind like Mary" or "be brave like Joseph". The hero is God, and the focus is Jesus and what he came to do.

Lesson plan

The lesson runs in five parts over 30 minutes, moving from mystery to clues to verdict to response.

  1. Hook: The Mystery Box (3 min). Hold up the small wrapped box. Ask: "What could be inside? How could we find out without opening it?" Take guesses. Say: "Clues help us know what is inside. Today we are detectives on a much bigger case. A baby was born 2,000 years ago, and people have argued ever since about who he really is. Let's look at the evidence."

  2. Read the evidence: Luke 2:1-20 (6 min). Read aloud with volunteers taking parts: narrator, angel, shepherds. Ask students to listen for anything surprising. Briefly explain that Luke was a careful investigator himself (Luke 1:3); he checked his facts.

  3. Open the clue envelope (10 min). Pull out one clue card at a time. For each, read the verse, ask the question, then draw out the answer.

    1. Clue 1: The Emperor's order (2:1-4). Q: "Who do you think was really in charge here, Caesar or God?" Point: God promised 700 years earlier that the King would be born in Bethlehem (Micah 5:2). God used the emperor's census to keep his promise. God is always in control.

    2. Clue 2: The town of David (2:4, 11). Q: "Why does Luke keep mentioning David?" Point: God promised King David a forever King from his family. Jesus is that King.

    3. Clue 3: The manger (2:7, 12). Q: "Where would you expect a king to be born?" Point: The Son of God came low and humble. He became like us so he could rescue us.

    4. Clue 4: The shepherds (2:8-10). Q: "Why tell shepherds first, not the important people?" Point: The good news is a gift for ordinary people. No one earns it.

    5. Clue 5: The three names (2:11). Write SAVIOUR, MESSIAH, LORD on the board. Q: "What does each name tell us about this baby?" Point: He rescues us, he is God's promised King, and he is God himself.

  4. The verdict (5 min). Ask: "So, detectives, who is this baby?" Gather answers. Then explain: "Our biggest problem is not that we need presents or a nicer Christmas. Our problem is sin: we have all turned away from God and done things our own way. We cannot fix that ourselves. That is why we need a Saviour. Jesus grew up, died on the cross to take the punishment for sin, and rose again. Christmas is the start of the rescue."

  5. The response (6 min). Read 2:15-20. Ask: "What did the shepherds do after they saw Jesus?" (They went, saw, told others, praised God.) Move into the worksheet and closing prayer (see below).

Case File worksheet

The worksheet has four tasks; Years 3 to 4 do Tasks 1 to 3, Years 5 to 6 also attempt Task 4. Answers are at the end of this section.

Task 1: Crack the code. Use the key A=1, B=2, C=3 and so on to Z=26.

7 - 15 - 4 / 11 - 5 - 5 - 16 - 19 / 8 - 9 - 19 / 16 - 18 - 15 - 13 - 9 - 19 - 5 - 19

Task 2: Match the name to its meaning. Draw a line.

Name of Jesus (Luke 2:11)Meaning
SaviourGod himself, who rules over everything
MessiahThe one who rescues us from sin
LordThe King God promised long ago

Task 3: Promise kept? God made promises hundreds of years before Jesus was born. Find the matching verse in Luke 2.

God's promiseKept in Luke 2, verse...
The ruler will come from Bethlehem (Micah 5:2)
A King from David's family will reign forever (2 Samuel 7:16)
God will send salvation for all nations (Isaiah 49:6)

Task 4 (Years 5 to 6): Detective's report. In two or three sentences, write your answer to the case: "Who is the baby in the manger, and why did he come?" Use at least two clues from Luke 2 as evidence.

Finished early? Draw the shepherds doing one of the four things in verses 17 to 20, and write what they might have said to a friend.

Answer key

  • Task 1: GOD KEEPS HIS PROMISES

  • Task 2: Saviour = rescues us from sin; Messiah = the promised King; Lord = God himself who rules

  • Task 3: Bethlehem, verse 4 (or 11); David's family, verse 4 (or 11); all nations, verse 10 (or 31-32 for extension)

  • Task 4: Look for: Jesus is the Saviour, Messiah and Lord; he came to rescue us from sin; evidence such as the angel's words, Bethlehem, the town of David

Response, prayer and teaching tips

Close by pointing children to Jesus himself and giving them a simple way to respond like the shepherds.

Case closed (2 min). Open the mystery box. Inside is a card reading "A Saviour has been born to you" (2:11). Say: "This is the best gift of Christmas. Notice the angel says 'to you'. God offers this gift to each of us. A gift is not ours until we receive it."

Closing prayer. Invite students to pray along silently if they want to, or simply listen.

Dear God, thank you that you always keep your promises. Thank you for sending Jesus, our Saviour, Messiah and Lord. Thank you that he came to rescue us from sin. Please help us to know him and to tell others about him this Christmas, just like the shepherds. Amen.

Take-home challenge. Students tell one person at home this week: "Do you know the three names the angel gave Jesus?" This mirrors the shepherds spreading the word (2:17).

Teaching tips for the SRE classroom

  • Invite, never pressure. Use "Christians believe" and "the Bible says" language, and let students respond freely.

  • Keep the hero clear. Each clue should point to what God did in Jesus, not to a moral lesson.

  • Pace the clues. If time is short, cover Clues 1, 3 and 5 only; they carry the main line of the lesson.

  • Expect questions. If asked "Did it really happen?", point to Luke as a careful investigator who wrote down eyewitness accounts (Luke 1:1-4).

  • Mind the mix. Years 3 to 4 enjoy the box and the code; Years 5 to 6 can be stretched with Task 4 and the Simeon extension.

  • Be sensitive to students from other faiths or none. Welcome every question as part of the investigation.

Extension (10 to 15 min): Simeon's testimony (2:25-32). Read the passage. Ask: "What had Simeon been waiting for? What did he say when he held Jesus?" Point out that Jesus is a light for all nations, including every child in the room. Then link to verse 35: this baby would one day suffer, pointing to the cross.
