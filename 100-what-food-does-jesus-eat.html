What food does Jesus eat?

Passage: John 4.27_38

Big Idea: Christ’s obedience to the Father’s sustains us as we reap for eternal life.

Jesus sends his disciples to reap and rejoice

Our sustenance and our task:

Relying on Jesus’ obedience to the Father

Rejoicing with the sowers

Reaping for eternity

Today is Mother’s Day.

Let me take this opportunity to wish every mother a Happy Mother’s Day!

Usually, we would take the mothers in my family out for a dinner to celebrate Mother’s Day.

Our mothers have been feeding us since we were young; it’s only right that we take them out to eat on Mother’s Day.

In fact, not just Mother’s Day! Whenever you get a chance, take your mothers out to eat!

But this year, because of COVID-19, we won’t be able to take our mothers out.

Our family will probably buy some take away for our mothers and we’ll eat in instead.

I think many families today all around the world might do the same.

In today’s passage, Jesus’ disciples had gone out and brought some food, the ancient equivalent of takeaways for Jesus to eat.

They were urging Jesus, saying “Rabbi, (that is, Teacher), please eat!”

But Jesus wouldn’t eat because he had a very important lesson to teach the disciples.

If you have your Bible with you, please open up to John 4:27.

The Samaritan Missionary

Look with me at John 4:27, “Just then his disciples arrived, and they were amazed that he was talking with a woman. Yet no one said, “What do you want?” or “Why are you talking with her?”

The disciples see their teacher Jesus speaking with a Samaritan woman and they are amazed.

That’s because Jews don’t associate with the Samaritans.

Not only is she a Samaritan, she’s a woman!

The society in those days used to look down on women.

Some Jewish Rabbi actually taught that it’s an insult for a Rabbi to teach even his own daughter about the law of God.

And so, when the disciples see Jesus speaking to the woman, they’re amazed.

Nobody asks the woman, “What do you want?” and no one asks Jesus, “Why are you speaking with her?”

This woman brought a jar with her to the well to draw water.

But after speaking with Jesus, she leaves the jar by the well and goes back into town.

She really wants to introduce the people to Jesus.

She says, “Come, see a man who told me everything I ever did.”

At first, she doesn’t want to reveal much about herself to Jesus because she has a complicated background.

However, Jesus already knows about this woman’s background!

Not only did Jesus talk about the things that she did, he also taught her some incredible truths about God!

In the society that this woman belongs to, no one would take the initiative to teach this woman truths about God.

But this woman had learnt precious truths about God from Jesus.

Jesus teaches this woman about true worship.

He teaches true worship happens when someone worship the Father in spirit and truth.

A person must come before Jesus if they’re to worship God

After hearing Jesus’ teaching, she shares it with other people.

She asks, “Could this be the Messiah?”

Is this the one prophet they’ve been waiting many years for, the one who’ll teach them God’s word?

She doesn’t know but she hopes that other people will be able to come and know this Jesus.

Perhaps if they come, they can help her figure out whether this Jesus is the Messiah or not.

And so this woman tells the others, “Come and see”

This phrase, “Come and see” is very similar to the phrase that the disciples used in John 1 to invite people to meet with Jesus.

When Philip spoke with Nathanael about Jesus, he said, “Come and see”

And now the Samaritan woman says the same thing to the people in her town, “Come and see”.

This Samaritan woman’s become the first missionary to the bring the Gospel to the Samaritans.

She met Jesus and now she wants others to meet Jesus.

She didn’t debate with the people; she didn’t give them a lecture.

She’s just invited people to come and meet Jesus!

As many of you know, our Church has this motto: See the Gospel, Hear the Gospel, Tell the Gospel.

We invite people to come and hear the gospel, so that they’ll come to know the Lord Jesus.

Just like the Samaritan woman, this Samaritan Missionary, we invite people to ‘come and see’, not so that they can see how good we are, but so that they can see how good Jesus is.

Jesus’ sustained by obeying His Sender

Just as this woman is inviting people from the town to see Jesus, Jesus’ disciples are just outside of the town by the well urging Jesus to have some food.

At this time, Jesus is thirsty; he’s probably also hungry.

Jesus used his thirst as an opportunity to teach the Samaritan woman the meaning of worship.

He’s now using his hunger as an opportunity to teach his disciples about how he’s sustained by obeying his Father’s will.

Look with me at verse 32: “But he said, “I have food to eat that you don’t know about.”

The disciples asked, “Could someone have brought him something to eat?”

34 “My food is to do the will of him who sent me and to finish his work,” Jesus told them.

The one who’s sent Jesus is his Father

Jesus has come to do his Father’s will, he’s come to obey the Father’s will.

To obey the Father’s will is what gives Jesus satisfaction.

This is what the Bible teaches in the Book of Deuteronomy 8:3, “Man does not live on bread alone but on every word that comes from the mouth of the Lord.”

Man doesn’t just rely on food to live; man rely on the word of God to live.

And Jesus is the one who can fully obey the Word of God.

As Jesus trusts God, he finds his sustenance, his food.

As Jesus follows His Father, he finds His strength from God, he finds his sustenance.

Jesus’ obedience to God is like food, giving him sustenance.

Jesus lives not on bread alone but on every word that comes from the mouth of God.

Jesus has come to complete God’s work

God’s work is to see Jesus go to the cross, to die on the cross for our sin and to rise from the dead after three days.

That’s God’s will for Jesus; that’s God’s mission for Jesus.

And Jesus has come to complete the mission that God has for him.

His determination to complete God’s mission is like food, sustaining him, until his mission is complete.

In the Marathon event that was held in the 1968 Olympic games in Mexico city, there was a runner named Akhwari.

During the race, he fell and injured himself badly.

His leg was bleeding and the medical personnel were telling him to drop out of the race.

However, Akhwari kept going.

Out of the 57 runners, he finished last.

The winner of the marathon finished the race at 2:20:26.

Akhwari finished the race more than one hour later at 3:25:27.

The sun had already gone down by then and only a few thousand people remained in the stadium.

When he was interviewed afterwards and asked why he didn’t quit the race but kept going, he said,

“My country did not send me 10,000 miles just to start the race; they sent me to finish the race.”

God sent Jesus here not only for him to begin the work; God’s sent Jesus here to complete the work, that is the work to save the people of the world.

Jesus’ determination to complete his mission is what drives Jesus; that determination to do his Father’s will sustains him till the very end.

Jesus sends his disciples to reap and rejoice

Jesus then ties in what’s happened with the Samaritan woman with his own mission.

Open up with me to verse 35, “Don’t you say, ‘There are still four more months, and then comes the harvest’? Listen to what I’m telling you: Open your eyes and look at the fields, because they are ready for harvest.”

When a farmer sows, he needs to estimate how long it takes for the crop to grow.

For example, if he is growing wheat, he’ll need to wait between 8-9 months before he can expect results.

Jesus says that the Disciples say, “There are still four more months and then comes the harvest”

But Jesus says that the harvest is now.

This is something that’s about to happen.

They don’t need to wait; the days of harvest are here!

What are they harvesting?

Look with me at verse 35, “The reaper is already receiving pay and gathering fruit for eternal life, so that the sower and reaper can rejoice together.”

The reapers are receiving their pay and gathering fruit for eternal life, that is those who previously didn’t have eternal life are now being saved, and they now have eternal life.

Jesus has just spoken to the Samaritan woman, who have believed what Jesus’ said.

Not only does she listen to Jesus, she’s become a missionary, inviting people to see Jesus.

The gospel of God has come, and anyone who’s heard and received the gospel has eternal life.

Now’s the time to spread the gospel; now’s the time of harvest for eternal life.

Now’s the time for the reaper to do their work.

Now’s the time for a new dawn, a new beginning.

Jesus’ disciples will invite people to Jesus and establish a new Christian community.

In Jesus Christ, they’ll start an everlasting fellowship.

This everlastinf fellowship is the holy universal Church, this fellowship is the communion of the saints.

This is Jesus Christ’s Church.

Jesus said the sower and the reaper will rejoice together.

Those who sow the seeds are firstly the Old Testament prophets.

Inspired by the Holy Spirit, they wrote their prophecies about Jesus Christ

They’re now where God is, watching how Jesus has come to the world, fulfilling the words of their prophecies; they must be filled with great joy!

When these prophets see how the disciples proclaim the gospel to other people, and how these people receive the gospel and are saved, both they and the disciples rejoice together.

And so, the disciples must now do the work of the reapers.

They must go out and share the word of God, they must do their work of reaping.

Look with me at verse 38, “ I sent you to reap what you didn’t labour for; others have laboured, and you have benefited from[j] their labour.”

Others have done the hard work of sowing; now the disciples must finish their work by doing the work of reaping.

Those who had first sown the seeds are the Old Testament prophets.

But to a smaller extent, there are those in our lives who have sown the seed.

Maybe it’s the Christian teacher who shared the gospel with you.

Maybe it’s the Christian who’s given you a Christian tract on the streets.

Maybe it’s a Christian friend who’s invited you to hear the gospel at Church.

We need to also give thanks for these people because they have done the work of sowing seeds in your life.

They too, will rejoice because you have become a Christian, they too, will thank God because of you.

Our sustenance and our task:

In today’s passage, we’ve seen the sustenance and the task of a Christian.

Relying on Jesus’ obedience to the Father

Firstly, we need to rely on the obedience of Jesus to our heavenly Father as our sustenance.

The obedience Jesus has to the heavenly Father is a perfect obedience.

Because Jesus perfectly obeyed the Father, those who believe in him will have an everlasting hope.

Because Jesus has perfectly completed his Father’s work, those who believe in him has new life.

We’re saved by the obedience of Jesus.

We’re not saved by our own obedience.

If we rely on our own obedience to be saved, we’ll never be sure because our obedience is flawed, it’s imperfect, it’s incomplete.

But Jesus’ obedience is perfect; he was able to perfectly complete the mission God had for him.

Because Jesus has completed God’s mission for him, we’re saved.

And now, as believers saved by Jesus, God wants us to obey him.

Jesus has saved us and we’re now to live a life of obedience to him.

Jesus didn’t save anyone just so that they can continue to live a life of disobedience to God.

Though our obedience is imperfect, incomplete, God still wants us to obey him, not so that we’ll be saved.

We obey God because he has already saved us and our obedience to him flows out of our thankfulness to him, out of our worship of him.

Like Jesus, our obedience to God is like food to us, sustaining us.

Like Jesus, we want to see God glorified and praised.

May our heavenly Father receive glory and praise through our obedience to him, despite our imperfect obedience.

Rejoicing with the sowers

When we do God’s work, we’ll have a great sense of joy.

When I see a family come to Christ, I’m very glad.

A few weeks ago, a young child whom I’ve taught sang a song about Jesus that I’ve taught her; it made my day.

When I’m here, seeing the brothers and sisters who have faithfully joined us online to worship God together, it gives me great joy, great encouragement.

When we meet together briefly online after the service to see how each other are, to encouraged one another, to pray for one another, it’s a beautiful sight to behold.

As the prophets of the Old Testament sees the life that we have in Christ, when those who have introduced us to Jesus see the fellowship we have in him, we rejoice together with them in the Lord Jesus.

We share a great joy with them.

Reaping for eternity

We will rejoice together in the Lord Jesus because we see the reapers receiving their wages, we see the fruits of the reapers, we see the eternal life that comes about because people have received Jesus Christ.

And so we must continue with the work of reaping.

Now is the day of reaping.

Though due to COVID-19, there are a lot of uncertainties, there are a lot of changes,

There is one thing that we know for certain, that is, that God loves the world and has sent his one and only Son to save us.

The Lord Jesus has finished his mission; those who believe in him are promised to have an eternal promise.

Work will come and go; health will come and go, but life in Christ is everlasting.

The fellowship we have in Jesus is everlasting.

And so we mustn’t stop the work of reaping.

We mustn’t let COVID-19 stop us from the work of proclaiming the gospel.

Those of our family members who haven’t believed yet need to hear about he hope that Jesus brings now.

Those of our friends who haven’t believed yet need the joy that Jesus brings now.

Now’s the time of harvest.

Will we be like the Samaritan woman, inviting people to come to Jesus?

We can invite them to come to our online service or our online Bible study.

In fact, now’s a good time to invite people because they can just come online and join our service and Bible studies.

My hope is that as we welcome our friends to join us online, we can invite them to physically come to Church afterwards, when the COVID-19 outbreak is over.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How have I been thanking our heavenly Father for the obedience of Jesus Christ?

Who have done the work of sowing in my life? Who have done the work of reaping in my life?

On whom have I been doing the work of sowing? On whom have I been doing the work of reaping?

May our God transform our Church into one which ask people to ‘come and see’; may we be a Church that invites people to meet Jesus.

Let’s pray

“Loving Father, we thank you for sending your son Jesus Christ to save us. WE thank you for Jesus’ obedience to you. Because of his obedience to you, we now have the hope of eternal life. Father, by your Spirit, please equip us, that we might do the work of reaping. Today’s the day of reaping. Please give us the courage to share the gospel of Jesus Christ with our families and our friends. May you have all the glory, along with Jesus and the Holy Spirit. We pray this in the all-powerful name of the Lord Jesus, Amen.”
