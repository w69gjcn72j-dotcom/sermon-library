Meekness is a sign of growth

Passage: Genesis 13.1_18

Additional passages: Matthew 5:5, Gen 12:3, Matthew 6:33, 1 Pet 5:5-6

Big Idea / Take home sentence: To grow in faith is to bow before the Lord Jesus, meekly giving what’s yours away, trusting He who gave what’s His to us.

Abram’s unexpected meekness

Lot drawn to paradise on earth

To grow up is to bow down

Who has a brother or a sister in their family?

Did you ever have to share something with your family, like a cake?

How did you do it? Do I have volunteers?

Here’s a cake. How would you divide and share it? Have a go!

Well done!

If you ever want to see how good human beings are at estimating equal portions of food, get siblings to share food.

As humans, we want to make sure we get our fair share of food, our fair share of the good stuff!

But what happens when we don’t have enough to share?

What if the cake is not enough for all the siblings?

Will we still be generous when we don’t have enough to share?

That’s what we’re going to look at in today’s passage.

Please open up your Bible with me to Genesis 13, beginning from verse 1.

We’re going to see three things:

Abram’s unexpected meekness

Lot drawn to paradise on earth

To grow up is to bow down

Before we look into God’s word, let’s pray, “Blessed Lord, you have caused all Holy Scriptures to be written for our learning—grant us that we may in such a way hear them, read, mark, learn, and inwardly digest them; that by patience and comfort of your holy Word, we may embrace and ever hold fast the blessed hope of everlasting life, which you have given us in our Saviour Jesus Christ. Amen.”

Abram’s unexpected meekness

Look with me at verse 1, “Abram went up from Egypt to the Negev —he, his wife, and all he had, and Lot with him.”

Abram was coming up from Egypt and heading back through the Negev with his wife, all that he had, along with Lot,

And got back to the place between Bethel and Ai.

Do you remember what he did last time he was here? Look with me at 12:8.

He built an altar to the LORD there.

Now he’s back, probably at the same altar, and once again, calling on the name of the LORD there.

Now Abram was rich, with lots of livestock, silver and gold.

His nephew Lot has lots of livestock as well.

At first, when they had less, they could live together as a family unit; they could share.

But now, it’s much harder; there wasn’t enough food for both Abram’s livestock and Lot’s livestock.

The earth underneath them could not support them; maybe the land was still recovering from the famine that led Abram to go to Egypt.

Or maybe the famine was still there.

Whatever the reason was, the land didn’t have enough to support all the animals and so there was an argument between Abram’s herdsmen and Lot’s herdsmen.

Not only did the Lot’s herdsmen need to share with Abram’s herdsmen.

They have to make sure they look out for the Canaanite locals and the Perizzite locals as well.

To put it in economic terms, the demand was greater than the supply.

What happens? If it happened today, the price for the food will go up.

But this was before modern economics and so if there’s no food supply, the livestock dies.

And so, Abram’s herdsmen and Lot’s herdsmen get into an argument over the patches of grass their livestock have to share between them.

The lack of resources has been a cause for tension between people and between nations for thousands of years.

Nations will go to war with other nations because of a lack of resources.

When nations don’t have enough natural resources to feed their own people there’s a temptation for these nations to expand their borders, to invade other countries.

Things can quickly get out of hand when a nation is driven to argue with another nation; it can lead to nations going to war with another nation.

We’ve seen this before in history time and time again.

They do it all in the name of national interest; they do it for the prosperity of their own people.

The situation between Abram’s herdsmen and Lot’s herdsmen was escalating and Abram decided to step in.

Look with me at verse 8, “So Abram said to Lot, “Please, let’s not have quarrelling between you and me, or between your herdsmen and my herdsmen, since we are relatives.”

Abram was saying, ‘let’s stop our men from fighting, since we are relatives.’

The words translated relatives here as ‘we are relatives’ are quite interesting.

Literally, the words are, ‘we are men, we are brothers’.

The idea seems to be that Abram didn’t want to fight with Lot because they were men, grown men.

And grown men don’t fight over each other for patches of grass.

More than that, they were brothers.

Not literally, because Abram was Lot’s uncle, but they were family.

If men shouldn’t fight each other over patches of grass, then families definitely shouldn’t be fighting each other.

What should they do instead?

Should Lot simply submit to Abram because he was his uncle?

Should they divide the land and give the better part to Abram since Abram was older?

Should they divide the land evenly between the two of them?

Listen to Abram’s solution in verse 9. “Isn’t the whole land before you? Separate from me: if you go to the left, I will go to the right; if you go to the right, I will go to the left.”

Abram told Lot, “You pick, I’ll take the leftovers.

You take the left, I’ll take the right. And vice versa”

What did Lot see?

We don’t know exactly but it might have looked something like this.

These are photos that I took near the place where they were.

Anyone remember where they were? Between Bethel and Ai

If Lot was looking towards the East, and he looked to his right, looking south, he would see the plains of Jordan with places like this.

This was at En gedi national park, not far from the dead sea.

It had green vegetation as well as fresh water.

If Lot had turned to his left, looking north, he would’ve seen something like this.

This was taken near Jericho, a very rocky ground.

Abram said to Lot, “You choose, I take the leftover.”

Was this the same Abram that came out of Egypt?

Wasn’t this the same Abram who would do anything to ensure his survival?

Wasn’t he the coward who lied and sold his wife to save his own life?

Yes, it was the same Abram but he has now grown in his faith in God.

For him, survival wasn’t the most important thing anymore, at least for now.

For now, this Abram would let Lot choose first, out of his love for his family.

This Abram would trust God first, before trusting in his own survival instincts.

Abram was showing the characteristics of meekness.

In his meekness, Abram decided to let others go first, choosing to take the leftovers.

He was ready to be left disadvantaged for the sake of others.

A lot of times, meekness can be mistaken as a sign of weakness, as someone who was forced to take the lower position.

But make no mistake, meekness is a sign of faith.

It’s a sign of faith because you know that your heavenly Father will provide and so you willingly let others go first.

It takes great strength to say ‘No’ to our survival instincts so that other people will get a better outcome than us.

This strength comes from knowing the fact that we’re safe in our Father’s hands.

The meekest person of all is none other than Jesus Christ.

In Philippians 2 we read that Jesus, being in the form of God did not consider equality with God as something to be exploited.

Jesus didn’t grab on to the glory and the honour that comes from being God for his own benefits.

Instead Jesus emptied himself by taking the form of a man, humbled himself and became obedient to the point of death, even to death on a cross.

Jesus chose obedience.

Jesus chose meekness before God the Father.

Meekness is one of the most definitive characteristics of God the Son.

Jesus isn’t weak-willed and was forced into obedience.

Instead, it was because Jesus was strong-willed, because of his determination to obey the Father and to save us, Jesus chose meekness.

He chose to be weak, to suffer and to die, so that people like me and you can live.

Jesus’ meekness shows us what true godliness look like; Jesus’ meekness shows us what trust in God looks like.

Last week, I talked about pastors who were kicked out of churches because of their refusal to accept the false teachings that the bishops were teaching.

One of these pastors was clearing out the office with his son and his staff.

It was their last day at the office before they give the keys back.

However, as people were going, this old pastor started to vacuum the office.

Though he was unfairly treated, he would not let that get in the way of him acting according to godliness.

That’s a faithful Christian pastor; it takes great strength, great maturity, great meekness, to do what’s right even when wronged.

His faith was shown in his meekness.

Brothers and sisters, growing in your faith doesn’t mean you become mightier; rather growing in your faith means you become meeker.

Growing in your faith means letting others have the first choice.

We don’t fight among ourselves but willing to choose the path of meekness, because that’s what mature Christian men and women do,

Because that’s what mature Christian brothers and sisters do.

We are willing to take what’s left.

Lot drawn to paradise on earth

Abram was willing to take what Lot rejected.

What did Lot choose? Lot was drawn to paradise on earth.

Look with me at verse 10, “Lot looked out and saw that the entire plain of the Jordan as far as Zoar was well watered everywhere like the LORD’s garden and the land of Egypt. (This was before the LORD destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah.)”

Lot chose a place that looked like the LORD’s garden, a place like Eden, perfect and unspoilt.

The place also looked like Egypt, a place that provided food even when the rest of the world was in famine.

However, there was a note from the editor that tells us it’s not all good news.

This place looked like paradise before the LORD destroyed it.

The place Lot chose was close to Sodom and Gomorrah, two cities we will meet later on in Genesis.

This place will be bad news later on.

Lot chose a place that looked like paradise on earth; he was looking for Utopia, a perfect place where people live in perfect harmony.

From history, we see time and time again that trying to establish Utopia on earth is always a bad idea.

Take for example Penedo in Brazil.

In the 1920’s, about 100 years ago, a man by the name of Tolve Uuskallio travelled with his family from Finland to Brazil to a place called Penedo to start a Utopian society.

It was to be a place free from religious and political arguments.

There was no smoking and drinking of alcohol there.

It was hard to begin with but they eventually grew corn, bananas and other fruits.

They were trying to build a paradise here on earth, free from the worries of the world.

However, it all got too hard in the 1940’s and eventually they had to make it into a place for tourists.

Today, if you go to Penedo, you won’t see a utopia.

Instead, you’ll see someone dressed as Santa Claus, ready to take photos with tourists, all year around.

The idea of a commercialised tour spot with Santa Claus as its main attraction all year round was probably the last thing that Tolve and his family thought of when they were planning for a Utopia.

Trying to find a paradise here on earth is always a bad idea.

But that’s what Lot chose; he chose the entire plain of Jordan for himself.

He wasn’t thinking about Abram or God.

He would’ve known the promises God made to Abram, that God would bless the world through Abram.

He knew that God promised to give the land of Canaan to Abram.

But when Lot lifted his eyes and saw the abundance of Jordan, he left Abram behind.

His choice would take him right to the edge of the Promised Land, if not beyond it.

It would take him away from the household of Abram to the people of Sodom.

In verse 13, the Bible reminded us again, “(Now the men of Sodom were evil, sinning immensely against the LORD.)”

They were evil sinning immensely against the LORD; these sinners were about to be destroyed by the LORD.

The reformed theologian, John Calvin puts it like this, “Lot, when he fancied he was living in paradise, was nearly plunged into the depths of hell”

There’s an innate drive in all of us to live in paradise, isn’t there?

We want to live in a place where everyone’s happy and satisfied.

We want to live in a secure home, where we don’t need to worry about housing or food or jobs or crime.

That’s why our hearts sink when we hear about the shortage of housing, the rise of the cost of living, the rise in unemployment and the rise in youth crime.

I don’t know if you have been watching, but these were all on the news last night.

Friends, we must remember that the place we’re living in, as good as it is, will never be perfect.

There is no true paradise here on earth.

If anyone tells you that their country is utopia, that their place is perfect, then it’s either they’re lying or they’ve been lied to.

We live in a fallen world, with corrupt people, and corrupt governments.

And it’s not just people out there who are bad.

We, we ourselves, are the citizens of this fallen world; we contribute to its fallenness.

No matter where we go, we bring our corruption with us.

Though utterly fallen and corrupted, we still try to set up our own paradise, and make our own blessings.

However, the further we stray from God, the further we’ll leave his blessings behind,

and the closer we’ll be to the destruction that God has reserved for sinners.

Sodom and Gomorrah might have looked like paradise on earth but they were places doomed for destruction.

They promised life but delivered death.

The same is true today.

Today, we might think of a place, a community as the perfect place, the modern day Utopia, the new paradise on earth.

But don’t be fooled!

Don’t follow Lot’s walk to Sodom and Gomorrah.

Don’t leave the city of God for the city of man.

Don’t leave the way to the heavenly home for a fool’s paradise on earth.

Last year, when I travelled back to Hong Kong with my family, I noticed many schools have very bold Christian messages as part of their notices.

Here’s a screenshot of the homepage of my primary school in Hong Kong, Carmel Leung Sing Tak Primary.

It wasn’t a rich private Christian school; it was a school in a very ordinary part of Kowloon.

And yet, they have a memory verse, an Easter service, and a combined school outreach.

How good is that!

When I asked around, I found out that many years ago, many Christians would go and teach in the poor suburbs of Hong Kong, giving poor children an education that they otherwise couldn’t afford.

I don’t know who these Christians are, but having an education in those early days of Hong Kong could’ve landed them a job with much more pay, with much more security.

They could’ve enjoyed life in Hong Kong, but they chose the path of meekness and they served among the poor in Hong Kong.

They weren’t looking for paradise in Hong Kong or paradise anywhere on earth.

Rather, they were walking with Christ to their heavenly home.

How about us?

Do we look for paradise here on earth?

Or are we looking forward to our home in heaven?

Jesus is the Truth, the Life and the Way; he is the only way to the Father.

He is the One who came down from heaven to take us back with him one day.

To grow up is to bow down

Jesus came down to earth to bring us back up; down then up.

That’s what Christian growth looks like.

If we are to grow up in the Christian faith, we must bow down.

To grow up is to lower down.

The Bible tells us in 1 Peter 5:6, “Humble yourselves, therefore, under the mighty hand of God, so that he may exalt you at the proper time”

Who does God exalt? Who does God lift up? Those who humble themselves under the mighty hand of God.

That’s what we see at the end of our passage in Genesis 13:14-15, “After Lot had separated from him, the LORD said to Abram, “Look from the place where you are. Look north and south, east and west, for I will give you and your offspring forever all the land that you see.”

Lot lifted up his eyes and saw the plain of Jordan.

The LORD tells Abram to lift up his eyes and take a look around, North, South, East, West.

Abram asked Lot to choose a side but the LORD didn’t tell Abram to choose.

The LORD has promised to give all the lands Abram could see to Abram.

God says in effect to Abram “All four sides are yours, Abram!”

And there’s something very poetic here:

The earth couldn’t support both Abram and Lot.

But one day, Abram would have children as numerous as the dust of the earth.

Abram might have lost some earth to Lot but God would give him children as numerous as the dust of the earth.

God even told Abram to take a walk around, like someone who had just got the keys to his new home.

Take a look around, enjoy the view, this is now your land!

Abram humbled himself, he was willing to take the second place, and God lifted him up and gave him far more than he ever deserved.

It’s like standing up and giving your seat to someone on the train and the train driver announced on the speaker,

“Congratulations to the gentleman who gave up his seat in carriage four. The whole train network is now yours.”

In a similar way, though it was good of Abram to let Lot chose first, God gave Abram far more than he deserved.

Abram doubted God and failed in Egypt but he’s now slowing learning to depend on God, to trust in God.

As Abram learns to trust in God, choosing the path of meekness and obedience,

he would learn that God would give him far more than he could ever imagine.

That’s the way growth in Christian faith works: You grow up by lowering yourself.

Your growth in faith is seen in your growth of meekness not your mightiness.

To grow up is to bow down; To rise up is to fall on your knees, in humble dependence.

And as we grow in our humility, we’ll be more ready to give away what’s precious to us even in our time of need.

We will be generous even when resources are limited.

Yes, being generous include giving to the Church, but it means so much more.

Being generous means giving to people who are in need.

That’s why having the Mobile Community Pantry is such a wonderful ministry.

Through the mobile pantry, we help those in need.

This year, we’ll be starting a new English class for those who come to the pantry.

It’s another way for us to help those who in need.

As a church, our time and our resources stretched, but in humility and in meekness, we choose to give.

For we’ve been freely given, and so we freely give.

We give because God first gave his Son to us.

God has already given his most precious gift to us, His one and only Son.

Jesus has eternal life, and he’s given his life, his eternal life to us, who believe in him.

He’s already given us so much!

No matter how much we think we’re sacrificing, what we give can never be compared to how much he gave us.

And so, we give willingly, we act generously to others, because God had already given us Jesus.

I don’t know about you, but when I look back on my walk with Christ, I tend to grow more not when I have plenty, but when we don’t have a lot.

When we have a lot, we tend to trust in the things that we have, rather it’s money, or time, or people to help.

However, when we have little, we had to learn to trust in God.

And God’s been so good; he’s never let us down.

Even when we don’t know what the next day might bring, even when we feel like we’ve got no more resources and we’ve got nothing left to give,

God has always provided.

Not always in the way that we like, but we trust that if we don’t have what we asked for, it’s only because God has something better in store.

We didn’t know what God has planned at that time, but looking back, we thank God that he provided what we need according to his timing, not ours.

Our Lord Jesus humbled himself and God lifted him to the highest place, the place above every other name.

And so, find your security in the name of Jesus.

Trust him; follow in the way of meekness and humility.

Keep trusting him, even as you try to find ways to survivie.

And so, how can I be generous when I don’t have enough?

We will give, we will gladly give what’s ours, when we remember that we follow Him who gave us what’s His.

When we remember how much he gave us, we will in humility and meekness give away what’s ours, which had always been His anyway.

When we give, we are only giving away what God had given us in the first place.

We have freely received and so we freely give.

Because we are thankful for what He’s given us in Jesus, we can act in humility and meekness, willing to choose the harder choice, the poorer path, walking his way to his heavenly home.

Friends, to grow in faith is to bow before the Lord Jesus,

meekly giving what’s yours away, trusting He who gave what’s His to us.

If we are siblings sharing a cake, we won’t be diving the cake straight down the middle.

Instead, we’ll cut unevenly and give the bigger piece to our sibling.

If we are at Church, we will look for ways to humble ourselves to serve others rather than longing to be served.

If we are in an argument, we will look for ways to be generous with our words, willing to be humbled even if we think we’re right.

How will you choose the path of meekness this week?

If you want to grow in faith, remember that to grow in faith is to bow before the Lord Jesus, meekly giving what’s yours away, trusting He who gave what’s His to us.

Here are the three questions for this week:

When was the last time I willingly let someone take what was better?

In what way am I tempted to look for a paradise on earth?

What will you give away this week as you grow in your trust of Jesus?

To grow in faith is to lower yourself and bow before the Lord Jesus.

As you grow in faith, in meekness give away what’s yours, trusting he who gave what’s His to us.
