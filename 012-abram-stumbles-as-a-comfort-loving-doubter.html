In the movie, ‘Crazy Rich Asians’, Nick wanted to take his girlfriend Rachel to see his family in Singapore.

Rachel got to the airport, with packages of food all prepared because she didn’t want to pay for the food on the flight.

However, when they got on the plane, they were taken to first class, where they give you plenty of food (or so I was told).

Rachel then asked, “Is your family rich?”

To which he replies, “We’re…comfortable.”

And she responds by saying, “That’s exactly what super-rich people would say”!

Ah, to be the first ones in line to board the plane and then to sit comfortably in first class on a long plane trip.

Wouldn’t that be nice!

We like to be comfortable, don’t we?

It might be sipping a cold drink by the pool or taking a cruise out on the seas.

It’s comfortable and it’s nice!

However, comfortable things in life often come with a high price tag.

What’s the highest price you’re willing to pay to make yourself comfortable?

When does the price for comfort become much too high a cost?

In today’s passage, we see what happens when a man pays too high of a price for his own safety and comfort.

In fact, we’ll see his pursuit of comfort became something dangerous for both himself, and his family.

A doubting coward pursues comfort

Please turn your Bible with me to 12:10, “There was a famine in the land, so Abram went down to Egypt to stay there for a while because the famine in the land was severe.”

Last week, we read that Canaan was the land that God had promised to give Abram one day.

It was where God told Abram to go and where Abram was staying.

However, Canaan had a severe famine and so Abram was in survival mode.

He decided to take his family down to Egypt to stay there for a while.

However, there was a problem.

Verse 11, “When he was about to enter Egypt, he said to his wife, Sarai, “Look, I know what a beautiful woman you are.”

That a sweet thing for a husband to say to his wife, isn’t it?

It’s especially sweet considering Sarai was 65 years old!

And it’s not just ‘beauty is in the eyes of the beholder’ sort of beauty; she wasn’t just beautiful to Abram because she was his wife.

As we’ll see, many men agree with Abram; Sarai was beautiful.

She was one of those women whose beauty would stop men in their tracks.

In the 1800’s there was a milkmaid in Sweden called Pilt Carin Ersdotter.

Her job was to deliver milk to the city’s public square.

However, because she was so beautiful, crowds would turn up just to watch her deliver milk.

In 1833, so many people came to see her that she was actually fined for causing traffic chaos in the city.

Her beauty stopped the men of Stockholm in their tracks.

Sarai, Abram’s wife, had that sort of traffic-stopping beauty and Abram was afraid.

Look at what he says to Sarai his wife, “When the Egyptians see you, they will say, ‘This is his wife.’ They will kill me but let you live. 13 Please say you’re my sister so it will go well for me because of you, and my life will be spared on your account.”

Abram thought that Sarai’s beauty was actually a high risk for him.

He though Egyptian men would kill him to get to his beautiful wife.

And so, to save his own life, he told Sarai to tell a half-truth.

He told her to tell the Egyptians that she was his sister.

It was a half-truth because she was his half-sister; same father, different mother.

He told her tell the half-truth so that he would not die; it was Abram in survival mode.

It was a half-truth; he was only trying to survive, he was doing it for the good of him and his household.

How bad could it be? It was bad, very bad.

Why?

Because firstly, Egypt wasn’t the land that God promised to Abram.

Abram wanted to move to Egypt and stayed there for a while, because that’s where there was plenty of food.

But that wasn’t where God wanted Abram to stay; Egypt wasn’t the promised land.

Second, Abram didn’t believe that God was able to keep his promises.

Last week, we learnt that God promised to make Abram’s name great, and that he would be the father of a nation.

If Abram hadn’t had a child before he died, then God would’ve broken his promises.

Abram didn’t believe that God was able to deliver on his promises before he starved to death and so he went down to Egypt for his survival.

God’s promises sounded nice, but Abram didn’t trust God with his life.

Third, while it was half true that Sarai was his sister, the very important half was that she was his wife, his better half in the marriage!

Abram was supposed to protect his wife but now he exposed her life in danger.

What would happen if an Egyptian approached Abram and asked Sarai for marriage?

Would he finally tell the whole truth and defend her? Or will he keep the lie going to save his life by putting his wife at risk?

Look with me at verses 14-15, “When Abram entered Egypt, the Egyptians saw that the woman was very beautiful. 15 Pharaoh’s officials saw her and praised her to Pharaoh, so the woman was taken to Pharaoh’s household.”

Sure enough, the Egyptians noticed Sarai’s beauty and told Pharoah about her.

And Sarai was taken from Abram’s household to Pharoah’s household.

Did Abram come to her rescue? No!

But it gets worse, verse 16, “He treated Abram well because of her, and Abram acquired flocks and herds, male and female donkeys, male and female slaves, and camels.”

Not only did Abram continued with the lie about Sarai, letting Pharoah take her into his household, he received lavish gifts from Pharoah for her.

Almost like a gift that a groom would give to the bride’s family at the wedding.

Abram dishonoured Sarai by exposing her to danger.

And he also dishonoured God by doubting God’s promises.

He didn’t think that God could deliver on his promises.

And so he was cautious and he devised his own plan.

While it’s generally a good thing to be cautious and to be aware of danger, Abram wasn’t just being cautious.

He was being a coward.

He doubted God and didn’t trust that God could keep his life safe, and so he gave his wife away to keep his life safe.

He sacrificed his wife’s dignity, he sacrificed his marriage, to save his life.

He sold his wife for camels, sheep, and servants; he sold his wife for his own safety and comfort.

He was a coward.

As a pastor by the name of Bob Deffinbaugh once said, “Abram was clinging to his wife’s petticoat for protection and blessing, rather than to the promise of God.”

For the sake of comfort, Abram became a coward.

We can fall for the same danger of comfort too, can’t we?

Who wants to go through hard work and suffering when you can relax and enjoy creaturely comforts?

There is one who would go through suffering and hardship rather than enjoy heavenly comfort.

He’s no other than the Creator himself.

Jesus had all the comforts of heaven, comforts that are far beyond anything we can imagine here on earth.

But he left the comforts of heaven out of his love for this Father and out of his love for his people.

When he came on earth, he was born in a manger, spent many nights going from one place to another, with no permanent place on earth to call his home.

It wouldn’t have been comfortable.

In time, he would carry a heavy cross that he would be crucified on.

It would not have been comfortable but painful.

Jesus didn’t come to earth to be comfortable; he came because of his love for his Father and for his people.

Jesus chose faithfulness over comfort.

And he calls his followers, his people, to do the same.

He didn’t call us to be comfortable but to be faithful; he didn’t call us to be cowards but disciples.

A few years ago, I met a few faithful Anglican ministers who refused to accept the false teachings that were prevalent in their part of the world.

Sadly, it was their bishops who were teaching these false teachings.

And so, they had a choice.

They can stay in their church, live a comfortable life and receive a comfortable superannuation when they retire (and some of them were close to retirement age).

Or they can leave the Church, their comfortable life and all their superannuation behind.

They chose faithfulness over cowardice.

Friends, beware the enticing pull of comfort.

Don’t choose comfort over faithfulness; be a disciple, not a coward.

2. God rescues Sarai, the ‘sacrifice’

How do you respond to someone who’s a doubting coward?

If it’s not someone you know well, you might just call him names.

But what if that person is someone you do know and love?

What do you do when your loved one is the coward?

God loved Abram; God chose Abram and promised to bless him.

Though Abram made a cowardly decision, God wouldn’t let Abram’s cowardice derail his plan.

Look with me at verse 17, “But the LORD struck Pharaoh and his household with severe plagues because of Abram’s wife, Sarai.”

Though Abram put Sarai his wife in a dangerous situation, God rescued Sarai by sending severe plagues on Pharoah and his household.

Though Abram should’ve been doing the rescuing, it was God who rescued Sarai from Pharoah.

Pharoah then rightly called Abram out.

Verse 18-19, “So Pharaoh sent for Abram and said, “What have you done to me? Why didn’t you tell me she was your wife? 19 Why did you say, ‘She’s my sister,’ so that I took her as my wife? Now, here is your wife. Take her and go!”

Pharoah was a victim of Abram’s cowardice.

He took Sarai into his household with a clear conscience because he believed Abram and Sarai.

He thought Sarai was just his sister.

As a result, Pharoah and his family suffered.

And of course, Sarai herself was the victim who suffered the most because of Abram’s cowardice.

She was the price Abram paid for his life and comfort.

We haven’t heard a word from her in this account.

There was no record of her resistance.

As far as we know, Sarai went into Pharoah’s household willingly, sacrificially, obediently.

This is confirmed by what we read 1 Pet 3:1-2.

“In the same way, wives, submit yourselves to your own husbands so that, even if some disobey the word, they may be won over without a word by the way their wives live 2 when they observe your pure, reverent lives.”

Abram was a husband who did not obey God’s promises.

Her heard God’s promises but refused to obey God; he was a doubter.

However, even with such a disregard for God’s word and a disrespect for Sarai, Sarai obeyed Abram.

She was willing to put her life in harm’s way, so that she may save her husband.

In I Peter, Christian wives are called to submit to their husbands just as Christ submitted and obeyed his Father, living sacrificially, in order to win their husbands over.

When you see a Christian wife, out of her submission to God, submits to her unbelieving husband, you see a glimpse of what Christ has gone through for people like us.

1 Peter 3 includes Sarai (also known as Sarah) to illustrate this point. Look with me at verses 5-6, “For in the past, the holy women who put their hope in God also adorned themselves in this way, submitting to their own husbands, 6 just as Sarah obeyed Abraham, calling him lord. You have become her children when you do what is good and do not fear any intimidation.

Sarai might had been beautiful on the outside; but she’s even more beautiful on the inside, as she put her hope in God and submit to her own husband.

And so, what do you do with a loved one who is a doubting coward?

Don’t cancel cowards but love them.

Even in the midst of betrayals, even in the midst of the frustration and the angst, don’t fear, don’t feel intimidated.

Be faithful and keep trusting in God.

Doubting cowards are self-serving, self-preserving; they sacrifice others in order to do what’s best for them.

They are inherently difficult and they will make this hard.

During those difficult times, will you trust God to see you through?

Would you be like Sarai, willing to sacrifice yourself for a doubting, cowardly husband?

Would you do it not because you think he’s right, but because you know that if he continues to doubt Jesus, he will meet a hopeless end?

However, even if it’s not to the level of abuse, if you feel that you would like to talk with us, we’re here to listen and help.

It’s so hard to love a coward, isn’t it?

They have failed, they’re unreliable, they’ll probably fail again.

They won’t have your back, but they would run away and themselves first.

But aren’t we like that sometimes?

Don’t we make cowardly decisions?

We want to make our decisions sound logical and mature; we say that we have to make sacrifices for the greater good, but they are sacrifices at other people’s expense.

We don’t want to put ourselves in danger, which is a good thing to do.

But is it top priority? Must self-preservation always be the most important thing in our lives?

When we do, we will tend to choose cowardice instead of courage.

However, I like Ezekiel’s Whatsapp description of himself.

Does anyone know what it is? Jesus first, safety second.

Yes, I know it’s a joke, and of course you should take safety seriously.

As Ezekiel’s Dad, I tell Ezekiel to be safe all the time.

But there’s some truth behind this: Jesus first, safety second.

If there’s a choice between the two, which will you pick? Jesus or safety?

Will we choose faithfulness or cowardice?

We thank God that he hasn’t given up on cowards like us.

Though Abram wanted comfort more than faithfulness, God abruptly took Abram’s comfort away.

God cursed Pharoah because of Abram’s cowardice and Abram was eventually kicked out of the comfort of Egypt back into the discomfort of the wilderness.

It’s in the wilderness where Abram must learn to depend to trust God, even in times of discomfort and starvation.

3. Choose Christ over comfort

And so, friends, choose Christ over comfort.

Don’t be lured by comfort. Don’t be lured by luxury. Choose Christ, who is better by far.

On Thursdays, Ezekiel, Rupert and I are teaching the kids at KOGWorx about God’s Hall of Fame in Hebrews 11.

In Hebrews 11, there’s a long list of Old Testament believers who trusted God through both good and bad times,

Who keep trusting God even though they haven’t received what God had promised them.

Though they don’t have it yet, they wait patiently, trusting that God will keep his promises.

That’s what faith is! Faith is the reality of what is hoped for, the proof of what is not seen.

They know for certain that God will deliver on his promises and they live their lives based on that certainty.

That’s faith! That’s how we overcome our doubting cowardice.

All of us, without exception, will face fear in our lives.

All of us, whether you’re six months old or 60 years old or 106 years old, will face things that we are dreadful of.

Will we respond with cowardice, compromising our principles and faith so that we’ll be comfortable and survive for another day?

Or will we respond with faithfulness, trusting God to keep his promise, even if we don’t get to see it in this life?

We faced things we feared before; we will face things that we’ll fear in the future.

Will we respond with doubt and cowardice? Or will we respond with courage and faith?

Friends, when we are afraid, when we fear that all hope is lost, remember these wonderful words of assurance from Romans 8:37-39,

“No, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him who loved us. 38 For I am persuaded that neither death nor life, nor angels nor rulers, nor things present nor things to come, nor powers, 39 nor height nor depth, nor any other created thing will be able to separate us from the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord.”

That’s the assurance for those of us who trust in our Lord Jesus Christ.

When does comfort become dangerous?

When we would rather be comfortable than faithful, when we would rationalise our cowardice instead of confessing our cowardice as sin.

Christ-loving followers obey God sacrificially, even as they suffer for the sake of comfort-loving cowards who doubt God.

I thank God that he hasn’t given up on cowards like me and Abram.

I thank God that he has sacrificed his own Son to save us, from our own selfishness, from our own cowardice.

Please pray that God will melt away our cowardice and help us walk by faith in Christ.

Pray that we will love Christ above all else, even if it means sacrificing our comfort.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What kind of comfort am I most attractive to? Comfort of family peace? Comfort of financial security? Comfort of social recognition?

How have I responded to coward-loving doubters who have put me in danger?

What must I sacrifice in my life, so that I won’t fall into danger of being a coward instead of a Christ follower?

Friends, be Christ-loving followers obey God sacrificially, even as we suffer for the sake of comfort-loving cowards who doubt God.
