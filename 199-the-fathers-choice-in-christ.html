The Mystery of God’s Will: The Father choice in Christ

Passage: Ephesians 1:1-6

Big Idea: Blessed be God the Father, for he lavishly chose us in Jesus His Beloved according to his pleasure and purpose.

It was the year 1986.

I was in Year 6, and we were getting ready for a class test.

Before we began, my teacher said, read the instructions carefully before we begin.

And without warning, she told us to start.

And so off we go, writing as quickly as our little hands could.

However, after a while, the questions didn’t seem to make sense.

It got very hard, very quickly! We didn’t even study for this.

It was then that I looked at the instruction again.

The first instruction was: Write your name down and ignore the questions below.

If I read the instructions earlier, then I wouldn’t have wasted my time and stressed out on the rest of the test!

Has anyone else had that test before?

It was an important lesson to teach us to know what’s required of us, before blindly going ahead with the task before us.

That’s true of Year 6 exams, Year 12 exams, and life in general.

Many of us live very bust lives.

We’re busy doing many things, achieving many things, but we don’t ask what’s actually required of us.

We don’t ask whether what we’re doing is what we’re supposed to be doing.

What if we’re busy doing many things but they’re not what we’re supposed to do?

How do we know that we’re doing what we’re supposed to do?

As Christians, we turn to God to find out what we’re supposed to do.

But how do we know?

That’s what we’re going to look at over the next few weeks, as we go through the Letter to the Ephesians.

The letter to the Ephesians has a fairly clear and even division.

The first three chapters focus on revealing the mystery of God’s will.

The last three chapters focus on instructing our response to the mystery of God’s will.

And so please open up with me to Ephesians 1, beginning from verse 1.

If you don’t have a booklet to write notes but would like one, please put up your hand and the welcomers will give you one.

We’re going to see three things in today’s passage:

Does God the Father have a choice?

How God the Father makes his choice

The chosen ones praise the Blessed Father

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Father, by the Holy Spirit, please reveal the mystery of your will to us, that we may praise you for all that you’ve done for us in Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

1. Does God the Father have a choice?

Does God the Father have a choice?

As people, we make choices all the time.

We choose the type of food we eat, the subjects we want to study, the type of work we want to do.

Our world tells us that we can make all sorts of choices and be whoever we want to be.

That’s the basic storyline to most if not all of Disney children stories.

That’s the basic talk outline for many politicians around the world – you have the choice to be whoever you want to be!

However, we’re not as free as we might think.

Our choice of food depends on what’s available to us and whether we can afford it.

Many families will tell you that you can’t just go to any school that you want.

You might have to live in the local area, or pass a selective test, or pay a large school fee to get in.

And, sometimes, you might not get the dream job that you want.

You might have dreamt of being a lawyer working in the corner office when you were little but end up being a plumber, a job that you end up enjoying tremendously.

We can’t always get what we want because of factors outside of our control.

That’s who we are. We’re finite humans with limits in our decisions.

How about God? Does God have a choice to do what he wants?

A lot of people mistaken God to be a divine vending machine of some sort.

If you feed the machine an input, you’ll get a certain output.

For example, you sacrifice to God, God will automatically give you what you want.

Some religions in the world treat their gods like a machine.

Even some people who call themselves Christians treat God like a machine.

They do one thing and God will respond in a set way, a predictable way.

But God is not a machine!

He’s God! He has the freedom to do whatever he wishes.

Unlike us, He is not limited by anything.

He can be anywhere he wants and does whatever he wants.

God is truly free to make his choices.

Throughout the Bible, we see God making choices.

We see God’s choice in creating the world, in creating us.

We see God’s choosing certain people, like Abraham and Moses to be leaders.

We see God choosing to save people like Rahab the prostitute, and Jonah the disobedient prophet.

We see God’s choice to raise up a little nation like Israel and bring down empires like Rome.

Job rightly says of God, “He makes nations great, then destroys them; he enlarges nations, then leads them away.” Job 12:23.

Nations rise and fall, depending on God’s choice.

Our God, the God of the Bible, actively engages with our world with his choices.

Don’t you want to know God’s choice?

Wouldn’t it be great to know what God has in store in the future?

What if you know has chosen for the world, for your country, and for you?

When God created the world, he not only created the things of the world, the sun, the stars, the animals, and us.

When God create the world, he also created the history of the world, a history, that he has planned and designed.

God has already determined how history will turn out, whether we believe it or not.

God knows exactly what you will do next, even before you thought about it.

God already knows your last thought here on earth.

In fact, God already knows what will happen to you after your last breadth on earth.

God has a plan, God has made his choices, and He wants us to know His them.

But how? How do we know God’s plan? How do we know God’s will?

2. How God the Father makes his choice

We know God’s will because he has told it to us.

We know God’s will because He has revealed his plan to us.

Look with me at Ephesians 1:1-2,

“Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus by God’s will: To the faithful saints in Christ Jesus at Ephesus. Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.”

Paul was an apostle of Christ Jesus.

An apostle was a messenger sent by someone on a mission.

Paul was Jesus’ apostle, Jesus’ messenger, by the will of God.

Paul was sent by Jesus because that’s what God wanted.

Paul wrote a letter to the faithful saints.

A saint is a holy person, someone trusted in Jesus and so had been made holy by Jesus.

That is, someone who trusts in Jesus, a Christian believer, is in God’s eyes, a holy one, a saint.

And so, Paul wrote to the saints, the Christians, who probably lived either in or around the city of Ephesus.

Paul greets these saints by praying that God’s grace will come upon them and that they will experience peace from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

From verse three to verse fourteen is one long sentence, with 202 words in the original Greek.

In this one sentence, he will talk about the blessings from God the Father, God the Son and God the Holy Spirit.

Today, we’ll start by looking at the blessings we received from God the Father.

Look with me at verse 3, “Blessed is the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us with every spiritual blessing in the heavens in Christ.”

God is to be praised and honoured and thanked.

Blessed is God!

Whop is this God? He is the Father of the Lord Jesus Christ.

What has God the Father done? He has blessed us with every blessing in the heavens in Christ.

God has showered us with every blessing the heavens down to us in the Person of Jesus Christ.

That’s what the Bible means when it described someone as saved by Jesus Christ.

It means the person will receive every spiritual blessing in the heavens.

And we have all these because we’re in Christ.

The phrase ‘in Christ’ is seen in almost every verse from verse three onwards.

We have all these spiritual blessings only because we’re in Christ.

It’s in Christ, and in Christ alone, that we have all these spiritual blessings in the heavens.

If you want to have every spiritual blessing in the heavens, you must come to Christ.

The first blessing we see is God’s choice of us in Christ.

Look with me at verse 4, “For he chose us in him, before the foundation of the world, to be holy and blameless in love before him.”

In love, God the Father chose us in Jesus before the foundation of the world.

Before time and space existed, he had already chosen us.

He chose us not because of anything we’ve done, nor did he choose us for what we can potentially do.

God simply chose us.

But how, why us? How did God make his choice?

Look with me at verse 4 again.

God the Father chose us in him, in Christ, and God chose because of His love.

God’s choice of those whom he saves is never apart from Jesus.

God the Father didn’t choose us arbitrarily or clinically.

He chose us in love – it’s out of His love that he chose us.

He loves us because we are in Christ, His One and Only Son, who he loves.

This is very, very important to get right.

God chose us in Jesus.

God has always loved Jesus and for those who are in Jesus, who knows Jesus, who are loved by Jesus, who are saved by Jesus, those for whom Jesus dies, God loves them too.

As human beings, our focus tend to be on ourselves, on how important we think we are.

But we’re not the focus.

Jesus is the focus of God’s love.

We are loved by God because God loves Jesus, and we’re in him.

Those who are apart from Jesus, who are outside of Jesus, will not experience the love that God has for Jesus.

The last time I preached here, I mentioned that Jenny’s brother’s wife was about to have a baby.

Little did I know that even when I was preaching here two weeks ago, Jenny’s brother’s wife was going into labour!

In the early hours of Monday morning, she gave birth to Micah.

And our family loves him.

Long before he was born, we have loved him.

In fact, their older cousin Ezekiel and Kevin bought cars for him months before he was born.

We have loved him before he was born, and we love him now.

We loved him before he did anything good or bad; we chose to love him.

God the Father chose to love us, long before we were born, for we’re in Jesus, we belong to Jesus, whom God the Father loves.

Since God loves Jesus, he loves us also.

In fact, he loved us so much that he has adopted us into his family.

Look with me at verse 5, “He predestined us to be adopted as sons through Jesus Christ for himself, according to the good pleasure of his will”.

We are fallen, sinful, limited creatures, with no right to be anywhere near the holy, all-powerful God.

And yet, through His One and Only Son Jesus Christ, God has adopted us as his sons.

The Bible uses a very strong word here: We’re predestined to be adopted.

It’s not merely God knowing what will happen in the future, even though he knows what will happen in the future.

God predestined our future, actively and intentionally making it happen.

God the Father mapped out the history of the world, plan every minute details that will happen, so that those who believe in Jesus will be adopted as sons of God.

It’s all God’s choice and God’s plan; it’s all God’s initiative!

In God’s wisdom, God predestined the lives of all who would believe in Jesus Christ.

As we come to Jesus Christ, the Father loves us through him.

God chose us in Christ; God predestined us in Christ.

God loves us in Jesus Christ, His Beloved One.

That’s how God the Father chose us: He chose us in love and he chose us in Christ.

What’s God’s purpose for choosing us? Look with me at verse 4.

God chose us in Christ so that we will be holy and blameless.

God didn’t bring us in Christ only to have us fall back into a life of filthy sin again.

No, God chose us in Christ to be holy, just as he is holy.

To be holy means to be utterly devoted to God.

If you’re holy to God, you belong to God alone and you don’t belong to anyone else.

To be holy means to live a life that reflects his holiness.

We’re also to be blameless.

That doesn’t mean moral perfection, because none of us can be perfect on this side of heaven.

We will still fall short.

But we will live up to who Jesus has saved us to be.

We were sinful, but now we’ve made holy by what Jesus has done.

Since we’ve been made holy, we’re now to live out that holiness.

We want to avoid sin, we want to do the right thing, because that’s who God has made us to be.

God chose us in Christ to be holy and so we want to be holy.

We want to be holy is put there because God has made us holy.

Our desire to be holy is a sign that God has chosen you.

If we don’t have the desire to be holy and blameless, then we need to ask whether we’ve been chosen by God in the first place.

For God has not chosen anyone in Jesus just so that they can go back to a life of sin again, but to be holy and blameless.

The person who recognises God’s choice of them in Jesus will make choices in their lives light of God’s choice.

God’s choice to save you will direct the way you make choices in your life.

Here are two examples of how God’s choice will direct the choices we make.

Since God’s chosen me to be holy and blameless in Jesus, I’ll make our gathering with God’s holy people, a holy time for God.

We might meet with God’s people on Sunday, as most of us do.

Or it may be Saturday, or Monday nights.

The Holy God, my Father, wants me to gather with his holy people; that’s where I’ll be.

The Father’s choice to save you will direct what you choose to do with your time.

Since God’s chosen me to be holy and blameless in Jesus, I will serve God’s holy people, willing to give more than I take, wanting to serve rather than be served.

Just as Jesus, God’s Son, came not to be served but to serve, we also will want to serve.

That’s what holiness and blamelessness looks like in our lives – it will look like Jesus.

God chose us in Christ and we will make choices that reflect God’s choice in our lives.

3. The chosen ones praises the Blessed Father

We won’t be making these choice grudgingly but with great joy.

Look with me at verse 6, “to the praise of his glorious grace that he lavished on us in the Beloved One.”

God chose us in Christ, God chose us in His love, we live holy and blameless lives, all for the praise of his glorious grace!

It’s for the Father’s glory and honour.

When we see and understand how glorious is the Father’s grace, when we see how he lavishly, richly, abundantly pour out his grace towards us in Jesus his Beloved One, far more than we are able to receive them,

We will shout for joy!

We will lift our voice and sing, we will live our lives of holiness so that everyone around us can praise God.

A few weeks ago, Michael, Rohan and my family went to Sing Australia concert in the Opera House.

It was incredible!

Yes, the music was incredible but it was so good to hear the whole concert hall sing praises to God at the top of their lungs.

One of the most beautiful parts of the evening was when the whole concert hall was invited to sing a hymn together, acapella, without any instruments, without any lyrics.

It’s beautiful because so many people knew the song by heart.

It was the wonderful hymn: When I survey (奇妙十架).

It was so good to praise God with all our might for what he’s done!

The heavenly Father’s grace is a glorious grace.

God’s presence is shown in his grace towards us.

Later on Ephesians, we’ll read that when heavenly hosts see us, when the armies of heaven see us, they see the God’s wisdom on display.

They see how God’s predestination and purpose worked out in the Lord Jesus. They see how our salvation In Christ show the glory of God.

As we live our lives, let’s live it in praise of the One who has lavished his grace on us.

We’ll praise him at Church; we’ll praise him at home.

We praise him in public; we’ll praise him when no one’s looking.

We will praise him through songs, but we will also praise him through the choices we make in our lives.

If we have indeed understood the mystery of God’s will for us in Jesus, we will choose according to God’s choice.

We will do it gladly for we will praise God for what he’s done.

In a while, when we read the creed together, when we say Amen together, when we sing together, let’s do it with a heart full of thanks and praise for our God.

For me, the best times of praise is when I’m singing loud but I still can’t hear myself because the congregation is singing even louder.

When we sing, when we say Amen, when we testify who God is, let’s do it with all our heart.

To him be the glory!

God is the One who chooses.

He loves Jesus and he chooses to love those who are in Jesus.

Those who are chosen by Him will joyfully, wisely choose their lives according to God’s choice.

God has already made his will know to you, what are you going to do about it?

And so, friends, if you are chosen by God, praise God for His choice and make choices according to His choice.

After this service, as you leave this building, you will be making a choice.

Will you praise God with all your heart? Or will you holding back because you didn’t think God deserved it?

Will you choose to live a holy and blameless life, serving others, as Jesus himself did, or will you rather choose to live like the unholy world, looking out for your own interests rather than the interests of others?

If you know you have been chosen by God, then live your life based on God’s choice for you in the Lord Jesus.

If you don’t know whether you’ve been chosen by God, if you don’t know God’s will, then please come and see either me or Hien, or Po Ki or Range, and we would love to show you God’s glorious, abundant grace for us in Jesus.

If you are in Christ, praise God for His choice and make choices according to His choice.

Here are the three questions for this week:

1. How do I know that I’ve been chosen by God? If so, do I praise God for his grace?

2. If I belong to Jesus, how is God’s choice of me in him direct my choices in life?

3. Am I living the holy and blameless life that God’s chosen me to live in Christ? If not, what must I repent from?

Friends, praise God for His choice and make choices according to His choice.

Let’s pray,

“Blessed Father, thank you for your love for us in Jesus. Thank you that you’ve chosen us in Jesus before the foundation of the world to be holy and blameless. Father, thank you for adopting us as your sons. Please forgive us for our sinful desires, wanting to be like the world instead of wanting to follow you. By the blood of your Son, please make our hearts, our minds, our soul, all of our being holy, so that we will make choices to be holy and blameless, to the glory of your glorious grace which you’ve lavished on us in your Beloved One, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
