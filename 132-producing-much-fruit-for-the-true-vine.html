This month, the family on Jenny’s side of the family is very excited.

That’s because Jenny’s brother, Ken, is about to become a father for the first time!

There’s very high anticipation for this little one because this baby is the first one in the family since Caitlyn was born!

I still remember Ken and Ness announcing this big news to the family at a Christmas lunch last year.

They did it by showing a photo of the little one to all the cousins in their Christmas card.

Here’s a video of their reaction.

They were so happy! The photo in the Christmas card shows that this baby, this new life, is living inside of Ness.

The baby will come any time now!

That little photo was a sign of this precious little life that God has made.

It’s a joy to see life!

As Christians, we believe that God gives life to all living things.

In him we, live and breathe and have our being.

But Christians believe that in Jesus, God gives life that is truly life.

He gives what the Bible calls eternal life.

It’s not just life as we know it that continues forever and ever.

It’s a life that God gives to all those who trusts in Jesus, a life that’s lived for the glory of God, a life that’s given by God the Holy Spirit.

It’s a Spirit-filled life; it’s life that is truly life.

It’s a life that’s all about Jesus, for Jesus said that he’s the Way, the Truth and the Life.

How do we know whether we have this life?

If we have this life, what kind of signs will we see?

What are the signs of life for eternal life, the life that is truly life?

That’s what we’ll see in today’s passage.

And so, if you have your Bible with you, please open up to John 15:1-8.

We’ll see three things:

Two myths about disciples

True disciples remain in Jesus the true vine

True, living disciples produce much glorious fruit

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Lord Jesus, you said that if we ask anything in your name, you will do it. We ask you now to speak to us in your word. By your Spirit, burn and impress your words onto our hearts, that we may remain in you and you in us, Amen.”

1. Two myths about disciples

Over the Gospel of John, Jesus gave us seven I am sayings.

Let’s see if you remember them,

I am the Bread of Life (John 6:35)

I am the Light of the World (John 8:12)

I am the Door (John 10:9)

I am the Good Shepherd (John 10:11,14)

I am the Resurrection and the Life (John 11:25)

I am the Way and the Truth and the Life (John 14:6)

I am the Vine (John 15:1,5)

Today we look at the seventh, “I am the true vine, and my Father is the gardener.”

There are four words that keep repeating throughout this passage: Vine, Branch, Fruit, Remain.

Kids, circle these words in the passage and let’s see if you can count how many times each of these words appear in the passage.

Vine, branch, fruit and remain – you hear these words over and over again in this passage.

Jesus calls himself the true vine; he’s the One who brings true life to all those who belong to him.

And Jesus calls God the Father the gardener.

What does the God the Father Gardener do?

Verse 2, “Every branch in me that does not produce fruit he removes, and he prunes every branch that produces fruit so that it will produce more fruit.”

Here, Jesus destroys two myths, two misconceptions, about disciples.

The two myths are the fruitless disciple, and the problem-free disciple.

A fruitless disciple is a someone who calls himself a Christian who has no visible signs of growth.

He’s a Christian in name only.

He says he’s a Christian, but he has no growth, no spiritual life.

If we use the vine and branch analogy, he sees himself as a branch of the vine, just like the other branches.

However, there’s one big difference: The other branches are living and are connected to the vine ,but the fruitless branch isn’t.

One’s alive and the other is dead.

One has signs of life and the other has no signs of life.

God the Father, who is the Gardener, will remove the dead branches from the vine, for he doesn’t want any dead branch on his vine.

A few weeks ago, Hua and Mei Xiang (and I think Brother Chen), removed this dead branch in the car park.

It was hanging on the tree and if it was a windy day, it might have snapped off and damaged any car underneath.

It would’ve been dangerous to leave the branch hanging on the tree; it needs to be removed.

God the Father is the Gardener who will remove all the fruitless branches from Jesus, His Son, the true vine.

When God looks at our lives, will He see any signs of life in us?

If he doesn’t, he will remove us.

Before we look for signs of life in others, we must start with looking at the signs of life in our own life.

Where are my signs of life? Where are my signs of growth?

We must never be satisfied with being a fruitless Christian, a fruitless branch, for a lifeless, fruitless Christian will be removed by God himself.

There is no such thing as a fruitless Christian; there are only self-deceived non-Christians who see no problems with being fruitless.

That’s the first myth.

Here’s the second: The problem-free disciple.

In some circles, it’s taught that if you believe in Jesus, you will prosper, you will be healthy, your family live will be great, and you will be blessed!

Nothing bad will happen to you.

That’s often been called the Prosperity gospel; you believe and you prosper.

The flip side to this of course is that if you’re poor, if you’re sick, if your family is broken, then you’re cursed, for bad things don’t happen to good Christians.

But this, too, is false.

Jesus says God the Father prunes every branch that bears fruit so that they can bear even more fruit, so that they become even more fruitful.

Late Winter is a time when many gardeners prune their fruit trees.

He cuts away any dead branches and weak branches so that the stronger branches will receive more nutrients.

It also allows more light and air to come through, as well building a stronger branching system so that the tree will re-generate and reinvigorate in spring.

God, the Wise Gardener, often takes away parts of our lives so that we can be more fruitful in the part of our lives that really matter.

Hebrews 12:7 tells us “Endure suffering as discipline: God is dealing with you as sons. For what son is there that a father does not discipline?”

When we suffer, it’s God’s way of dealing with us as his sons.

Also Hebrews 12:11, “No discipline seems enjoyable at the time, but painful. Later on, however, it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it.”

Discipline is hard and painful, but it yields fruit of righteousness.

And so, our heavenly Father may discipline us by allowing us to live with consequences when we’re wrong, so that we can be more fruitful.

Our Father may take away the important things, even the good things we love in our lives, so that we can be fruitful in the best things, the things that truly matter.

The Father removes fruitless branches and the Father prunes fruitful branches.

Now notice that in both cases, it leads to a temporary shrinkage of the vine.

It looks smaller than it was before! But the Father does this, removes and prunes, so that the vine will become more fruitful.

I think that’s what’s happening to the Churches around our area at the moment.

I was told that many years ago, our Church had a Church bus that drove kids from around the area to take them to Church!

However, there are a lot less people attending Churches now.

Perhaps now’s the season of God’s pruning and removing.

Perhaps we’re going through the proverbial Winter, where we don’t see much growth.

However, God prunes the trees that do bear fruit so that we can be even more fruitful.

And there are signs of growth slowly coming back in.

Our Church has been experiencing growth, we’ve seen people being saved, we even have people driving local residents to Church.

Who knows, we might need a bus again one day!

God prunes those who are fruitful so that we might have more fruit.

But what does this fruit look like? How do we know we are bearing the kind of fruit that God is looking for?

2. True disciples remain in Jesus the true vine

Look with me at verse 3, “You are already clean because of the word I have spoken to you.”

Jesus is the true vine; if you’re a true branch, his word will be in you.

Jesus says his word has a cleansing effect on us.

The words of Jesus strip off our deadness and give us a new life.

Those who are true branches of the true vine will remain in him.

Just as there’s an organic relationship between the vine and the branch, there’s an organic relationship between Jesus and those who are his.

Just as life flows from the vine to the branches, life flows from Jesus to his followers as he speaks his words to them and they receive his words.

It’s only as they are in him that they have life,

The only way that they can produce fruit, much fruit, is to remain in Jesus and for Jesus to remain in them.

Here’s a branch from my orange tree at the back of my house with an orange on it.

If you brought some, bring them out!

The only reason why there are fruits on the branch is because they were attached to the tree.

Life flowed from the tree, through to the branches and to the fruits.

How do you know that these branches were at one stage attached to the tree?

By their fruits!

Jesus has said in Matthew 7:19-20, “Every tree that doesn’t produce good fruit is cut down and thrown into the fire. 20 So you’ll recognize them by their fruit

By their fruit you will recognise them.”

If you belong to Jesus, and if his words remain in you, you will bear good fruit in your life, you will have signs of life!

Remaining in Jesus is the only way that you will have the fruits, the signs of life.

On the contrary, you will be futile, fruitless, without Jesus.

You cannot bear fruits without Jesus; you can do nothing without Jesus.

You can’t bear fruits for Jesus without Jesus.

Without Jesus, you will have no signs of life.

Without Jesus, you’re a branch that is thrown aside, waiting to be gathered into the fires of judgement.

That is, friends, the way you live matters.

As Christians, we believe in salvation by grace alone, we’re saved by faith alone. However, some people have twisted this teaching to say that since we’re saved by trusting in Jesus, we don’t need to change, we don’t need to grow.

If you push them a little further, they might even say, since we’re already saved, we don’t need to obey.

They would say that obedience is nice but optional.

However, if there is no change in your life after you’ve become a Christian,

if you live your life in the same way that you were before you were a Christian,

Then, you are living as if you’re still dead.

If you were living as if you were still dead, then you don’t really have any signs of life.

And if you have no signs of life, if you have no fruit, then you’re still a dead branch.

Dead branches don’t belong with Christ; God the Father will remove the dead branches and throw them in the fire.

Dead branches who think they are living are suffering from a dangerous delusion.

It’s dangerous because they think they’re living when they’re dead.

How do you know whether you’re live branch or a dead one?

Take this test: Look at the fruit you’re bearing.

If you’re not bearing any fruit, then you could be a dead branch, living a dangerous delusion.

But for the living branches, taking the test and seeing that you’re bearing fruit is a wonderful assurance.

Seeing the fruits that we bear gives us the assurance that we are living in Christ and that we are remaining in Christ.

On Friday, I went on a conference where I met a young man called Cooper.

I taught Cooper many years ago at another conference called NextGen.

He still remembers me! Cooper came up to and asked how I dealt with certain aspects of growth as an older Christian.

It was great to see this young man remaining in Christ after many years and is now a father and a Youth Minister at his local Church.

If you’re an older Christian, talk to the younger ones and encourage to remain in Christ.

Share the stories of how you trusted Christ in difficult times.

And if you’re a younger Christian, don’t just talk with your friends.

Talk with the older Christians and ask them about how they remained in Jesus Christ.

Make the most of the next 30 minutes after the service!

We’re fruitful if we remain in Christ and we’re futile if we don’t have Christ.

3. True, living disciples produce much glorious fruit

And so, what is this fruit that Jesus has been talking about?

What am I looking for? Is it more people coming to Church? Is it growing stronger in faith? Is it the fruit of the Spirit?

What does fruitfulness look like?

Are you ready? You know you’re fruitful in Christ, you know you are a living branch, if your prayers are effective.

Let me explain it by going back to the plant analogy.

Did you know that trees have hormones?

Hormones are substances that organisms produce to regulate or stimulate certain tissues into action.

Like humans, trees have hormones too.

These hormones start, stop and control all sorts of functions.

For example, in spring, light shines on the buds of new growth in the trees’ branches.

This stimulates hormones to go down to the roots to boost growth.

These new roots then make a hormone that is sent back up to the buds to get them to open and grow.

Or to put it in simpler terms, if branches could talk, they would say, “Give us more food, so that we can bear more fruit!”

How do you know whether you’re producing fruit?

How do you know that you’re a live branch and not a dead one?

You know you’re producing fruit if you pray effective prayers!

Jesus says, “The one who remains in me and I in him produces much fruit”

Jesus also says, two verses later, “If you remain in me and my words remain in you, ask whatever you want and it will be done for you.”

There’s a direct correlation!

What do the two verses have in common?

They both talk about remaining in Jesus and for Jesus to remain in us.

What’s the outcome? The person will produce much fruit and the person will be effective in prayer.

The fruit that you look for, the fruitfulness that you look for, is the answers to your prayers.

Your effectiveness in prayer is the sign that tells you whether you’re a fruitful Christian or not.

Why is prayer, effective prayer, the sign of life?

Why is prayer the fruit that true branches bear?

When we pray, we show that we’re dependent on Jesus, that we trust Jesus.

That’s what you do if he does remains in you and you in him.

You will depend on him, and you will pray.

If you don’t pray, how can you say that you’re dependent on him?

You don’t even talk to him!

But if you belong to him, it’s the natural thing to do.

Branches talk with the vine.

Those who are in Jesus will talk with him in prayer; it’s organic.

Note that Jesus is talking about effective prayer, and not just a wish.

If what you ask Jesus to bring glory to the Father, Jesus will do it! Absolutely! If you remain in Jesus, you will want to bring glory to the Father.

If you ask Jesus brings glory to the Father, then ask whatever you want and it will be done for you.

Jesus will do it; your prayers will be effective.

Look with me at verse 8, “My Father is glorified by this: that you produce much fruit and prove to be my disciples.”

Jesus says that those who pray effectively will produce much fruit, fruit that will bring glory to the Father.

This will include everything, anything in your life that bring glory to the Father.

The result of an effective prayer that glorifies the Father will include obedience in your life, it will include seeing people saved, it will include loving one another just as Christ loved us.

In fact, if you are a true branch from the true vine, you will want your whole life to be one which glorifies the Father.

That’s what a living branch will pray for, and Jesus will do it.

When you live to the glory of the Father, you will be shown to be Jesus’ disciples.

That’s the fruit you’re looking for.

The fruit of the true branch, the true Christian, is Jesus’ answer to their prayer for the Father’s glory.

In our Men’s Growth Group, we’re praying big, bold prayers.

Here are some of them: We’re praying that there will be ten people saved this year.

We’re praying that five new families from local school communitie will come.

We’re praying that 100 people who have never been to Church will walk into ours.

We’re praying that every fortnight, 1 new member will join regularly.

We’re praying boldy because we want to see the Father glorified.

We’re not wishing that Jesus will do it; we’re trusting that Jesus will do it, though it will be done in his own time, not ours.

And as we pray these bold prayers, we are preparing ourselves for Jesus’ answer.

If we’re praying for rain, we’ll be bringing the umbrellas.

If we’re praying for more people to be saved, and if we believe Jesus will do it, then we will go out and share the gospel, and we will share the gospel again when they come.

We will also fixed the toilets so that we can be ready for the many new people who will come.

If we don’t believe the Church will grow, why bother fixing anything?

Friends, pray bold prayers for the Father’s glory.

Don’t pray like you’re wishing for something.

Don’t pray prayers that don’t expect answers.

Pray boldly, as Jesus has invited us to.

If we remain in him and he in us, we will pray boldly, and it will be done for the Father’s glory.

We will pray effective prayers for the Father’s glory.

That’s how we become fruitful, bearing much fruit for the Father’s glory.

That’s the sign of life that God looks for; that’s the sign of life that we, too, must look for.

Where are your signs of life? Where are your fruits?

If you remain in Christ, if you’re alive, you will prayer effective prayers.

They will be effective because you’re praying for the Father’s glory.

You will see the fruits of your prayers, you will see faithfulness and obedience as you remain in Christ.

But if you don’t see these fruits, if you have no desire to be fruitful, heed Jesus’ warning.

Every branch that bears no fruit is removed, thrown into the fire and burned.

If that’s you, if you don’t see fruits, if you don’t see signs of life in your life, then pray, and pray hard, for Jesus to remain in you and for you to remain in him.

Pray that you will have life that is truly life.

And so, Remain in Jesus, the true vine; be fruitful by praying effectively for God’s glory.

In English, there’s a phrase that says, “I thought you’d never ask”.

It’s the response of a person who was eager to answer in the affirmative.

I thought you’d never ask, of course I help you.

I thought you’d never ask, of course I’ve forgiven you.

“I thought you’d never ask”. It’s what you say when you’re very eager to say Yes!

Now of course, Jesus already knows what you ask him before you ask him.

Even so, he longs to hear you ask Him for whatever you need to bring glory to the Father.

I thought you’d never ask, of course I will lead you through this difficulty.

I thought you’d never ask, of course I will give you what you need at the right time.

As a Church, we’ve been preparing ourselves for growth especially over the past few years.

We’ve hired our first assistant minister in many years, we’re directly involved with teaching SRE in four schools, we’re meeting our neighbours at Mobile Community Pantry, and at KOGworx.

We almost finished upgrading the toilets, we have upgraded our sound system.

We’ve set up the 5G teams.

We’re ready for more growth.

Friends, would you pray boldly with us for more growth? Would you pray with us for the Father’s glory?

Pray that we will see 100 families coming to our Church, serving Jesus with us.

Pray that Jesus will do this, even if it means pruning from the Father.

Pray that when we’re pruned, we will give thanks to God for preparing us for more fruitfulness.

And the only way we will see Jesus answer our prayers, the only way we can be fruitful, is to remain in Jesus and for him to remain in us.

If you haven’t been praying for growth, both yours and the Church, if you haven’t been praying for the Father’s glory, then what have you been praying?

Have you been praying?

Remain in Jesus, the true vine. Let his words remain in you and pray boldly in his name.
