The confession of a stiff-necked people

Nehemiah 9:1-37 (vv1-21, 22-37)

Big Idea: Fellow stiff-necked blasphemers, let’s confess our sins to our gracious and awe-inspiring Lord

A national day of mourning

A gracious and awe-inspiring Lord

Will history repeat for a stiff-necked people?

Intro: Stiff neck?

Maybe it’s just me getting older, but over the past few years, I’ve noticed that I’ll been stiff necks more often.

Does anyone else get them?

Usually that happens if I had to work for long hours straight or if I had a bad night of sleep.

And I would ask Jenny or one of the children to massage my neck for me to help with the stiffness.

One year, they even got me a massager, so that I can massage my own neck, in my own time.

What I had was a physically stiff neck.

Of course, the phrase stiff neck has been used metaphorically as well.

It’s used to describe someone who’s unwilling to listen or change.

They can be used to describe a young child who refuses to listen to their elders or an old person who is too stubborn to listen to sound advice.

Of course, they often fail to see that they’re stiff-necked; rather they just happen to know something better than everyone else!

Their failure to see their own stiff-neckedness often cause a lot of grief to those around them.

In today’s passage, we see God’s people being described as a nation of stiff-necked people.

Not only have they caused grief to those around them, but they’ve also caused grief to God himself.

The Bible uses a strong phrase to describe what they did: they were committing terrible blasphemies (verse 18).

To blaspheme is to lie about God or to speak about God in an unworthy manner.

God’s very own people were described as stiff-necked blasphemers.

Not a very flattering description.

How do we make sure that as Christians, we don’t fall into the same trap of being stiff-necked blasphemers?

Do we even know that whether we are stiff necked blasphemers?

That’s what we’re going to see in today’s passage in Nehemiah 9.

In today’s passage, we’ll see three things:

A national day of mourning

A gracious and awe-inspiring Lord

Will history repeat for a stiff-necked people?

Before we look into it, let’s pray,

“Father, by your Spirit, please open our minds, move our hearts, and empower our hands, that we may humbly obey your word. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

A national day of mourning

Previously, in Nehemiah chapter 8, the people were commanded not to mourn but to rejoice in the Lord.

They were told that the joy of the Lord is their strength!

But here in Nehemiah chapter 9, they have come to a time of mourning.

Look with me at Nehemiah 9:1, “On the twenty-fourth day of this month the Israelites assembled; they were fasting, wearing sackcloth, and had put dust on their heads.”

They’re to have a National Day of Mourning.

Every now and then, when tragedy struck, the heads of nations will announce a day of mourning for their nation.

It may be the death of a President.

Or it may be the death of a royalty.

Last year, when the Prince Philip the Duke of Edinburgh died, the UK had eight days of national mourning.

In Nehemiah, the people of Israel had a national day of mourning not because a leader or a royalty died.

Rather, they mourned as a nation because of their sins and the sins of their fathers.

They read from the book of the law of the LORD for a fourth of the day, or about three hours, and then they confessed and worshipped the LORD for another three hours.

How did they confess and worship the LORD for three hours?

It started with praising God for who He is.

Look with me at the second part of verse 5 to verse 6, “Blessed be your glorious name, and may it be exalted above all blessing and praise. 6 You, Lord, are the only God. You created the heavens, the highest heavens with all their stars, the earth and all that is on it, the seas and all that is in them. You give life to all of them, and all the stars of heaven worship you.”

They worshipped God, the Creator of heaven, the earth and the sea and all that is in them.

They also praised God for being the Promise Maker.

God chose Abram and made a covenant with him to give him the land of the Canaanites and the land of other nations.

God’s covenant with Abram, whom God renamed Abraham, meant that God will have a special relationship with Abraham and his descendants.

And sure enough, God kept his promise to Abraham and saved the sons and daughters of Abraham from the Egyptians.

Afterwards God led them through the wilderness and provided them with guidance and food and water.

And so, God’s people praised God not only for being the Creator and the Promise-Maker.

They also praise God for being the Promise Keeper.

However, rather than being grateful to God for being the Creator, the Promise-Maker and the Promise-Keeper,

God’s people became a nation of stiff-necked blasphemers.

Look with me at the Bible, beginning from verse 16, “But our ancestors acted arrogantly; they became stiff-necked and did not listen to your commands. They refused to listen and did not remember your wonders you performed among them. They became stiff-necked and appointed a leader to return to their slavery in Egypt.”

They acted arrogantly towards God, like their Egyptian overlords.

They were a stiff-necked people; they refuse to turn from their ways.

They did not listen to God’s commands; they refused to listen to God.

They even made a golden calf with whatever gold they could find and called that thing their god, the one who saved them.

That’s blasphemy! A terrible blasphemy!

They spoke falsely about God because God was not that golden calf.

Rather than obeying the real God, they would rather make a god that they can control and manipulate.

That’s what an idol it; idol is a god that we make according to our own imagination.

It’s blasphemous!

Even though God’s people know who God is and how kind He’s been to them, they were stiff-necked and disobedient, committing terrible blasphemies against God.

That’s the theme that comes back again and again in the Bible.

God’s people had repeatedly been described as a stiff necked people who refuse to turn to God.

Before we point the finger too quickly to the Israelites, we need to take a good look at ourselves.

Are we arrogant and stiff-necked?

Do we refuse to obey God?

I’ve met many people who call themselves Christians who would worship God with their mouths,

Proclaiming, even singing that God is good and powerful,

And yet, at the same time, say that the most important thing in their lives is happiness.

Their top priority in life is to be happy and on their list of priorities, being happy is even more important than God.

When we place our happiness in our lives as being in a higher place over God, haven’t we made our happiness an idol?

Is it not idolatry?

When we say that God wants us to be happy rather than us being holy, aren’t we telling lies about God?

Aren’t we committing terrible blasphemies?

When we arrogantly, refuse to obey God and worship him as God, when we refuse to turn from our stiff-necked ways, we’re no better than the arrogant stiff necked Israelites who blasphemed God all those years ago.

I’ve mentioned placing happiness above God as one way that we show our stiff-neckedness.

In what other ways are we a stiff-necked people, refusing to obey God?

What else do we do that show that we’re a stiff necked people?

Talk to the person next to you.

If you’re at home, please send us an answer by whatsapp, wechat or Zoom.

What else do we do that show that we’re a stiff necked people?

You have twenty seconds.

May God help us see our stiff-neckedness and come before God in humble confession.

When we confess our sins before God, it’s not before a hateful, vengeful God but a gracious, awe-inspiring God.

A gracious and awe-inspiring Lord

Look with me at the second part of verse 17,

“But you are a forgiving God, gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and abounding in faithful love, and you did not abandon them.”

Even after God’s people committed terrible blasphemies against him, God was still being kind to them!

It truly boggles the mind!

How can God be so gracious to them after all the wicked things they’ve done against him?

Despite their blasphemy, God still looked after them, he still gave the people their land, just as he’s promised.

What God’s done is so different from the way our culture works.

A term that has come popular to describe our culture in recent years is the term “Cancelling culture”

If the people in the cancelling culture don’t like something, they just cancel it.

They don’t engage in conversations or in discussions with what they don’t agree with.

They just cancel it.

In the past, people cancel their opponent by burning their books.

Today, they cancel their opponents by erasing them from the internet, or by changing the history books or by taking down their monuments.

They just pretend you don’t exist.

God could’ve easily cancelled the Israelites.

In response to all their stiff-neckedness and all their disobedience, He could’ve easily erased them from existence.

But he didn’t!

God saved them time and time again.

It’s like the Israelites have this incurable itch to sin.

They sinned against God in the wilderness, they sinned against God even when they entered the Promised Land.

When they sinned against God, God rightly punished them by giving them over to their enemies.

When they cried out to God, God saved them again and again.

This cycle of disobedience, punishment, crying out to God and salvation happened for many, many years.

In their stubborn stiff-neckedness God’s people have sinned greatly against God but God’s gracious kindness and faithfulness is greater still!

Look with me at verse 31, “However, in your abundant compassion, you did not destroy them or abandon them, for you are a gracious and compassionate God.”

This is where God’s people are at when we come to verse 31.

They recognised that as a nation, they have been stiff-necked and that God was right to punish them.

Both their fathers and them did not listen to God’s commands and they’re rightly slaves in a land that God’s originally given as a gift to their fathers.

Look with me at verses 36-37, “Here we are today, slaves in the land you gave our ancestors so that they could enjoy its fruit and its goodness. Here we are—slaves in it! 37 Its abundant harvest goes to the kings you have set over us, because of our sins. They rule over our bodies and our livestock as they please. We are in great distress.”

And so the question remains:

Will God be gracious one more time?

Will God be kind to them even after they have failed God so many times?

T.J. Bengtson grew up in a Christian family.

He went to Church with his family, went to a Christian Private School and had wonderful Christian parents.

However, he began to turn away from God during his teenage years.

When TJ was in college, he spent his time clubbing, getting drunk, chasing girls, doing drugs, all the time thinking that he was having the best time in his life.

He even met a girl and was in a relationship.

However, he took advantage of the girl and the relationship was shattered.

He was bitter and angry at himself and everyone; he realised that he’s destroyed everything wonderful in his life.

And so, in his brokenness and agony, he cried out to God, and God answered.

He picked TJ up out of the rubble of self-destruction saved him from himself.

Though TJ was stiff necked and disobedient against God for many years, God was gracious and kind to TJ and restored TJ to himself.

That’s God’s incredible grace! God doesn’t give us what we deserve.

That’s what God’s grace is; it’s his extravagant, and faithful kindness to the unworthy.

God had shown his grace to his people Israel and God has shown his grace to people like TJ.

More than that, God has shown his grace to everyone who calls himself or herself a Christian.

If you want to find out more about God’s grace, don’t delay.

Speak to me, or Range, or Jenny, or Jiawu or Po Ki afterwards.

We’d love to share God’s grace with you.

Will history repeat for a stiff-necked people?

In today’s passage, we see a spiral that was repeated in Israel’s history.

A summary of the spiral looks like this: God’s good to the people, the people rebel, God punishes people, people cry out for God’s help, God saves.

It’s a spiral because it tends to get worse as time goes by.

When the people of Israel made the blasphemous golden calf, a portion of the people were destroyed.

When the nation of Judah committed blasphemies against God over many centuries, the whole nation of people was exiled.

This happens again and again throughout Israel’s history.

No matter how wicked the people had been, no matter how blasphemous the people had been in their worship, when God’s people cry out to God, God will answer.

That’s God’s incredible kindness!

However, some people might mistake God’s kindness as a licence to sin.

Their reasoning might go something like this:

Since God’s kind and gracious anyway, then I don’t really need to change and just keep on sinning.

Because if I do sin, I just ask God for forgiveness; if I get into trouble for my sin, I’ll just ask God for help.

No big deal.

However, what they fail to see is that they are still set in their stiff-necked ways.

By definition, to be stiff-necked is not turning to God for help and not repenting of one’s sin.

A stiff-necked person would prefer not to turn back to God; their posture is to literally refuse to bow down to God.

Their default is disobedience against God; their itching to sin against God.

And if we follow the spiral, the next step is God’s rightful punishment for their sin.

How many times must God punish us before we leave our stiff-necked ways?

If breakdowns in families, downfall of nations,

If natural disasters, floods, fires and pandemics don’t turn us from our stiff-necked ways,

What else can God do?

We’re like the dog in Proverbs 26:11,

“As a dog returns to its vomit, so also a fool repeats his foolishness.”

We have a tendency to be stiff necked fools.

We’re so stiff-necked, so stuck in our ways, that we keep repeating our foolishness, as a dog returns to its own vomit.

We know God’s good, we know God’s kind, we might even know God’s right, but we refuse to bow before him in obedience and serve him as we should.

For example, , there are times when we feel like we can do nothing but pray.

And we genuinely ask God for deliverance and safety.

However, when God does answer our prayers, we don’t acknowledge him or even thank Him for answering our prayers.

Take COVID, for example.

I’m sure that many of us have prayed for God’s protection and deliverance.

And I’m certain that God’s heard many of our prayers.

I’m sure that God’s protected some of us from getting COVID and he’s also healed some of us or our loved ones from COVID.

God’s heard our prayers and have answered our prayers.

And yet, are we thankful for what God’s done?

Or do we just take God’s kindness for granted, just like generations of Israelites did all those years ago?

In light of what God’s done, do we bow our heads in humble obedience?

Or do we continue in our stubborn, wicked, stiff-neckedness?

Friends, don’t be stiff-necked towards God.

But instead, bow your head in worship of the good, mighty and gracious God.

God doesn’t want us to remain stiff-necked forever, that’s’ why He’s sent Jesus.

It’s only in Jesus that our itch to sin is cured.

It’s only in Jesus, and in the power of Jesus, that we can be cured of our stiff-neckedness.

It’s only in Jesus that our stiff necks can be softened so that we can bow in worship.

It is through Jesus that God saves us from our spiral of sin and destruction.

This is what we read in Hebrews 12:28, “Anyone who disregarded the law of Moses died without mercy, based on the testimony of two or three witnesses. 29 How much worse punishment do you think one will deserve who has trampled on the Son of God, who has regarded as profane[f] the blood of the covenant by which he was sanctified, and who has insulted the Spirit of grace? 30 For we know the one who has said, Vengeance belongs to me; I will repay, and again,

The Lord will judge his people.[i]

31 It is a terrifying thing to fall into the hands of the living God.

Friends, God has sent Jesus His Son as the final and Greatest Saviour to save us from our spiral of sin and death.

Don’t trample on the Son of God, don’t profane what he’s done, don’t insult his Spirit of grace by taking what he’s done for granted.

There is no more hope for you if you don’t turn from your stiff-necked way and turn to Jesus.

All that waits for you will be the judgment of God and it’s a terrifying thing to fall into the hands of the living God.

What does this mean for us?

Don’t stiffen your neck before God; instead Bow your heads before Jesus.

A few years ago, a young woman went for an interview. During the interview, the interviewer started to explain the opportunities for advancement.

Before he had finished, she interrupted the interviewer to say, "The only job I want is yours!"

God is God the Creator, the Promise Maker, the Promise Keeper, our Saviour, our Lord.

Are we so arrogant that we want God’s job as God?

Friends, rather than being stiff-necked and arrogant, let us humble ourselves and see what God wants you to do for one another.

While I’m sure that they don’t want to be known as such, but I thank God that some of the most high profile Christians I’ve met are also some of the most humble Christians I’ve ever met.

These Christians don’t talk about how good they are but how good Jesus is! They get excited when they shared about how God’s saved them and they also get excited by hearing how God has saved other people.

Friends, what gets us excited?

Do we get excited when you get the praises?

Or do you get excited when Jesus gets the praise?

My prayer is that we won’t stiffen our necks before God but that we will bow our heads in humble adoration before Jesus.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Do I have any stiff-neckedness that I need to confess before God?

Do we as a Church have any stiff-neckedness that we together need to confess before God?

How am I depending on God weekly, daily and hourly?

Friends, don’t stiffen your neck before God; instead Bow your heads before Jesus.
