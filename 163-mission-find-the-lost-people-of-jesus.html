Hi everyone, it’s good to be back in Sydney after so many weeks away.

We flew to Hong Kong, Japan, Korea, then we flew back to Sydney from Korea.

Our flight from Korea was a night flight and so many of the passengers were sleeping for most of the flight.

During the flight, an older lady suddenly landed facedown next to Jenny, who was on the aisle seat.

She just lied there and didn’t move.

Jenny, being Jenny, called for help and sought to help her.

By now, quite a few people were calling for the crew to help.

It was a scary moment; she did not move.

The crew arrived to monitor the situation and got the oxygen tank out.

They got the lady back up, asked her a few questions (in Korean) and everything was ok.

She got back to watching Mission Impossible Dead reckoning straight afterwards (I was sitting directly behind her).

When someone’s life is in danger, many of us will want to help the person.

It’s a good response; a kind response.

However, some of us might be hesitant to help.

What if the person has a disease? Will I catch it too? What if I made things worse for her, am I liable? I’m not a professional; let’s leave it to the professional to help. It’s their job.

And so rather than helping, we are tempted to leave the person in danger.

How about you? What would you do?

When you see a person in danger, will you help?

What’s the price you are willing to take if you can save someone in danger?

What will you give to save a person’s life?

That’s what we will be looking at in today’s passage.

So please open up to Acts 16:1-10.

We’re going to see three things in today’s passage:

Freedom to give up your rights

Follow Jesus’ mission strategy, not yours

Find Jesus’ people, whatever it takes

Before we begin, let’s pray, “Almighty God our heavenly Father, we ask you from the bottom of our hearts, that this seed of your Word now sown among us, may take such deep root, that neither the burning heat of persecution cause it to wither, nor the thorny cares of this life choke it. But that, as seed sown in good ground, it may bring forth thirty, sixty, or a hundredfold, as your heavenly wisdom has appointed. Amen.”

Freedom to give up your rights

After Jesus died on the cross and rose from the dead, he sent his disciples, who were all Jews, to tell everyone about has happened.

That is, Jesus has come to die for our sins, so that whoever believes in him will not perish but have eternal life.

The disciples obeyed Jesus, travelled to many places around Judea and Samaria and told thousands of people about Jesus.

Some travelled even further and shared the message of Jesus to people who weren’t Jews.

A question then came up about whether a non-Jewish person must become a Jew to be saved by Jesus.

Look with me at Acts 15:1-2, “Some men came down from Judea and began to teach the brothers, “Unless you are circumcised according to the custom prescribed by Moses, you cannot be saved.” After Paul and Barnabas had engaged them in serious argument and debate, Paul and Barnabas and some others were appointed to go up to the apostles and elders in Jerusalem about this issue.”

God said that all Jewish men must be circumcised to mark that they belong to him.

That is, does a Jewish man need to be circumcised in order to be saved.

The Christians at Jerusalem debated and declared that you don’t need to become a Jew to be saved.

A non-Jewish person can be saved if they put their trust in Jesus.

They are freed from the practice of circumcision.

After this declaration from the Jerusalem council, Paul decided to go back to visit some of the cities where he shared the gospel, the good news of Jesus.

This is where our passage comes in.

Please look with me at Acts 16:1, “Paul went on to Derbe and Lystra, where there was a disciple named Timothy, the son of a believing Jewish woman, but his father was a Greek.”

Timothy was the son of a mixed marriage in two ways:

Ethnically, his mother was Jewish and his father was Greek.

And spiritually, it’s very likely that though Timothy’s mother (whose name was Eunice) was a believer, Timothy’s father most probably wasn’t.

Timothy would fit quite well in a Church like ours, where some of us have an ethnically mixed heritage and others are from spiritually mixed marriages.

More importantly though, Timothy was identified as a disciple, a follower of Jesus.

He was almost certainly converted when Paul came to Lystra to preach the gospel on his first missionary journey.

Other Christians in the area also affirmed him and spoke highly of him.

And so, Paul wanted to take Timothy along with him on the road.

However, Paul did something quite surprising before he took Timothy.

Verse 3, “Paul wanted Timothy to go with him; so he took him and circumcised him because of the Jews who were in those places, since they all knew that his father was a Greek.”

Paul did what?

He circumcised Timothy. Why would he do that?

Didn’t he just argued at the Jerusalem council that a non-Jewish person, a Gentile, doesn’t need to be circumcised to be saved?

Why did he circumcise Timothy?

Is it a little bit like companies that tells you how cheap and good their product is, until you read the fine prints?

You probably have seen this before: an internet companies tells you that they have the cheapest internet plan, until you read the fine prints, which says this only applies in a bundle. Have you seen that?

Is that like what’s happening here?

Is Paul saying that in theory you don’t need circumcision, but in practice you actually do?

Is he saying that a Christian is technically free, as long as they obey the law?

Is he pointing out the fine prints to Timothy?

Here’s another thing I learnt from my trip to Japan: When you want to say No, you cross your hands like this. ()

And so, is he pointing out the fine prints to Timothy? Absolutely not ()

Paul didn’t circumcise Timothy in order to save Timothy.

No, he circumcised Timothy not to save Timothy but to save others, many others.

Timothy was half Greek and half Jew.

A full Jewish family would’ve circumcised their son eight days after his birth.

However, Timothy’s Greek father most probably would’ve objected to the idea and so Timothy wasn’t circumcised.

This is ok with the Greek side of the family.

But the fact that Timothy wasn’t circumcised would’ve been a barrier for the Jewish side of the family.

They would’ve seen him as a Jew who denied his heritage.

Worse still, they would’ve seen him as someone who disobeyed God and so they would not have listened to him.

Timothy himself had the freedom in the gospel not to be circumcised.

If he wasn’t circumcised, he would’ve still been saved.

But he gave up rights to not be circumcised, in order to save the Jews.

That’s the mission – to save Jewish and non-Jewish person, to save every person, whatever it takes.

There was no guarantee for success, but Timothy and Paul would do whatever it takes to save some.

Paul himself wrote in 1 Corinthians 9:19-20, “Although I am free from all and not anyone’s slave, I have made myself a slave to everyone, in order to win more people. 20 To the Jews I became like a Jew, to win Jews; to those under the law, like one under the law—though I myself am not under the law —to win those under the law.”

This is a very important principle to remember: Jesus has saved us, and there’s nothing we can do to earn our salvation.

Salvation is free but following Jesus will cost you everything you have.

Jesus says that those who follow him must deny themselves, take up their cross daily and follow him.

It’s hard and tiring to carry a cross; it’s hard work.

But a follower of Jesus will do it not to be saved but because they’re saved and they share Jesus’ mission to save others.

Friends if you are saved by Jesus, you have a freedom to give up everything to follow Jesus.

You have the freedom to give away everything that you have, your money, your relationship, your future, your rights, and even yourself, because you have Jesus Christ.

And He’s more than enough.

Jesus gave up heaven , he gave up his rights, he gave up his life, to save people like you and me.

What he gave, is far greater than anything we can give back.

He saved us and gave us freedom to give up everything we have, to follow him.

Without him, we’ll still be slaves to our rights, slaves to our desire for our vision of freedom.

With him, we are truly free, freed from our lust for our rights, freed even from rights that we’re entitled to as a Christian, so that we might follow him as he sends us out to save others.

I still remember a few years ago, I went to a world-wide Anglican conference where there were many bishops from all over the world attended.

Some came with a whole group of ministers and people around them, ready to serve their bishop’s every need.

But among all these bishops, the Sydney bishops could be seen either wearing a welcomer’s badge, serving by welcoming people into the conference,

Or spending many sleepless nights preparing for all the unseen administration for the conference.

I’m thankful for our bishops, who were ready, and still are always ready, to let go of their entitlements in order to serve, in order to save.

What right would you surrender so that you can serve Jesus more?

What entitlement will you give up as part of following Jesus?

Paul and Timothy went to different towns to share the gospel, the message that brings freedom.

These Churches were strengthened in the faith and grew.

As their faith in Jesus grew, their numbers grew.

Faith in Jesus comes first; growth in numbers come second.

If you trust and obey Jesus, doing what he asks you to do because you trust him, then your faith will grow and you will want to go out and tell more people about Jesus.

That’s exactly what Paul and his team did.

They went on from Lystra to other Churches.

But then things get interesting.

They had a mission strategy, but it wasn’t Jesus’ strategy.

Follow Jesus’ mission strategy, not yours

Look with me at verses 6-8.

“They went through the region of Phrygia and Galatia; they had been forbidden by the Holy Spirit to speak the word in Asia. 7 When they came to Mysia, they tried to go into Bithynia, but the Spirit of Jesus did not allow them. 8 Passing by Mysia they went down to Troas.”

They were originally going southwest to Asia (not modern Asia but modern day Turkey).

It could’ve been a good strategy, sharing the gospel with many influential towns and centres there as they were travelling along the region.

But the Holy Spirit forbade them.

They then changed their strategy, how about going up north into the region of Bithynia, along the Black Sea.

They haven’t been there before and so that’s good missional place to go to.

But no, the Spirit of Jesus would not allow it.

They kept going west to the edge of the land.

They got to Troas, which was a coastal town. Now that’s a good and strategic place to do mission.

Many people coming in and out; news can spread quickly from here.

But no, this wasn’t the place either.

Verse 9, “During the night Paul had a vision in which a Macedonian man was standing and pleading with him, “Cross over to Macedonia and help us!”

They were to keep going west, across to Macedonia.

That would’ve been like us planning on a mission from Sydney, down the Hume Highway to Canberra and holding a massive rally at Melbourne, only for the Holy Spirit to tell us to take a sharp left turn to Wollongong and crossed the Tasman Sea to Christchurch New Zealand.

Paul and his team had a great strategy for mission, but it wasn’t Jesus’ strategy.

Jesus actively takes the role of the Mission leader.

We must trust his strategies, his directions, and we must turn to the people to whom he wants us to go.

It’s not our decision but his.

In this case, Jesus gave a very clear direction through the form of a vision for Paul.

In his vision, Paul saw a man begging to him to go to Macedonia.

This is NOT how Jesus usually gives direction; it’s rare to see this even in the Bible.

Jesus usually gives his direction to us as we read the Bible, study the people around us, understand the context we’re in, and as we pray!

If we don’t pray, then we’re no different from ad companies trying to promote a product.

If we don’t pray and follow Jesus’ lead, we won’t grow in Him.

In fact, I’ll go so far as to say that even if we do grow in numbers, it’s not the kind of fruit He’s looking for, if we don’t pray.

Paul might have had the strategies to reach the people of Asia and Bithynia.

He might even had the opportunities to reach out and talk to them.

It would’ve made perfect sense on paper.

However, at the end of the day, it’s God’s mission.

It’s his timing, not ours; God leads his mission, not us.

We must pray and trust his lead.

I don’t need to tell you this, but right now, we’re in the same city as Taylor Swift.

A lot of people have brought tickets to see her shows in Australia.

In fact, 570,000 tickets were sold in Australia to see Tay Tay and maybe even a glimpse of Travis, her boyfriend.

But you know what, back in 1959, nearly 3,250,000 people from all over Australia and New Zealand, that is nearly one quarter of the population of both nations, went to hear a Bible preacher called Billy Graham.

That’s more than five times the number of people who went to see Taylor Swift.

Of these 3.25 million people, 150,000 said that they gave their lives to Jesus Christ.

It was a massive win for God’s kingdom!

Did they have a really good strategy? Probably, pretty sure that they did.

But more importantly, they prayed. More than 40,000 fervent people prayed in 5,000 prayer meetings in 1959 believing that God would save people.

They knew it was God’s work from the beginning, during and after the event.

They prayed and God answered for they knew that the mission was ultimately Jesus’ mission.

Do we believe that the Church’s mission is God’s mission?

Do we think he’s in charge of his mission?

If you do believe, then why won’t we pray? Why won’t we?

One of the highlights of my week are the prayer meetings.

In the last Chinese prayer meeting we had eight people praying across the internet.

I was in Melbourne at that time and my connection was hopeless, but that didn’t stop me.

I went downstairs to the hotel lobby next door, which had a great internet connection, ordered coffee, and joined the prayer meeting.

It was great!

My English brothers and sisters, don’t get left behind.

We need to pray more, much more.

We need to ask God to forgive us for our faithlessness and our foolishness, when we think we can do his work without asking him in prayer for his guidance and for his help.

3. Find Jesus’ people, whatever it takes

As soon as Paul and his team concluded it was God’s will for them to go to Macedonia, it was easy.

They wasted no time but immediately sailed for there.

They went there to tell Jesus’ message the people of Macedonia.

More specifically they went there to Jesus’ lost people in Macedonia.

Jesus is in control of all things – he’s the Lord of all. He knows all those who are his, whether they’re Macedonian, or Greek or Jewish or Chinese or English.

Our job is to go and bring Jesus’ gospel to those who are his.

They might not believe him yet; they might not have even heard of Jesus yet.

But Jesus sends us, his people, to find his people who are still lost.

They’re lost because they don’t know Jesus yet.

However, if they are truly Jesus’ own, they will turn to Jesus when they hear the words of Jesus.

They will repent, believe and obey.

We don’t know who they are, and so we can’t choose who to go to.

But Jesus does and we must go where he sends us.

Jesus loves his own and he sends us to find them.

When we were in big cities like Seoul, our family would often get separated.

Every now and then, when my kids find me, they would yell out, “Dad, Dad!”

For whatever reason, I don’t hear them, probably because there were so many other people yelling Dad as well.

However, when they yell out ‘David’, I turn around because there aren’t that many Davids left in the world.

Who names their son David these days anyway?

And so, when my children call, I respond, I look for them.

God has already chosen those who are his from the beginning.

He has predestined them, he has chosen them, and he will call them.

And when He calls them, they respond by calling him back, they respond by praying to him.

And so, that’s our mission, we’re tasked to go where Jesus sends us and bring Jesus’ message to them, no matter the cost.

What does this look like in our Church?

Everything in our Church comes back to pointing people to find their hope and salvation in Jesus.

We have a team that’s especially tasked to do that. It’s the Go team.

The Go team gets together and organise events such as Open Days to encourage people to come.

We prepare posters and videos to let people know about the events where they can invite people to come.

While we want to reach people everywhere, we only have limited resources and we want to reach the people whom God has sent our way.

Over the past few years, we believe that there are three groups of people that he sends us to:

Children and Youth

Firstly, we believe God has sent us to reach the children and youth of our community.

We’ve got a school next to us and they gave us three classrooms to use on Sundays, for free!

We have a Children Centre where we can go and teach Bible lessons.

We have five schools within walking distance where we’re already teaching the Bible.

And God keeps opening doors for us to reach families in this area.

And so, this year, we really want to strengthen the KOG teachers and help them to promote KOG ministries around Kogarah.

Please pray for them as they pray and think through how to reach the children and youth of Kogarah.

Please pray for the Church, that we might be ready when God does answer our prayer, that we might welcome the families in.

Non-English speakers

In our Parish, less than 1/3 speak English only at home.

Most of us speak at least two different languages or know a language other than English.

For many immigrants who don’t know English, it can be quite scary to live in a place where you can’t understand people and people don’t understand you.

After coming back from Japan, I’m reminded of how frustrating it is not to know the local language.

And so, this year, our prayer is to start an English class that happens every time we have the Mobile Community Pantry.

Would you please pray that we will be able to reach our community as we share the love of Jesus in ways that they can understand?

And that when God does answer our prayer, they will be welcomed at our Church?

Mandarin speakers

I thank the Lord so much for all our Mandarin speaking brothers and sisters.

Even though I don’t speak much Mandarin, many are still coming, inviting their friends, to hear God’s word, to join Growth groups.

We even have a young family who will be joining our Church camp, even though they’re not believers yet.

My prayer is that God will provide a suitable Mandarin speaking worker who can help our Mandarin speakers grow.

I’ve been looking for someone for the past six or seven years, but we still couldn’t find a good fit yet.

We even have money set aside for this.

Would you pray that God will send his Mandarin speaking worker or workers into this harvest field?

And when God does answer our prayer, would you keep praying for them, that we will welcome them into our Church?

Keep praying for these things.

Pray for these things in your Growth groups.

Keep praying and find out God’s will about to whom God wants to send us.

If you’re not in a Growth group, come and see me or Range afterwards.

Without one another, it’s very easy to rely on ourselves, and not trust God.

Join us on prayer meetings; my prayer this year is that our prayer meeting will grow.

Join us on Street Evangelism as we meet and talk to the people of Kogarah.

We even got these lovely T-shirts designed by the Go team and printed by Hien and his team, all ready to go.

Join us as we bring the gospel of freedom, the gospel of hope, to the people of Kogarah and beyond.

And so, what will I give to save a person’s life?

Without Jesus, it’s a hopeless end.

With Jesus, it’s endless hope.

It’s either one or the other.

If you have endless hope, then do everything you can so that your husband, your son, your parents, will not have a hopeless end.

What will you do to save their life?

Brothers and sisters, Go to the people whom Jesus sends you, whatever it takes.

We might not have much, we might not know much, but let’s do everything we can to bring people to Jesus.

For whatever reason, God has placed Luca in Vina’s path, and so Vina gently brings Luca to Church.

Who has God placed in your path today?

Who does he want you to bring to him?

Pray and find out.

Once you know, go to the people whom Jesus sends you, whatever it takes.

By your Spirit, let your word burn in our hearts, that we won’t rest until we proclaim your word, that people of all cultures and nations will be saved. Please show us your will, Father, and when we discern it, let us do it immediately, whatever it takes. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
