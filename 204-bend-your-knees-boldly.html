Bend your knees boldly

Ephesians 3:8-21

Big Idea: Since God displays his multi-faceted wisdom through the Church, pray that the Triune God might enlighten you, the Church, to know the knowledge-surpassing love of Christ and be filled the fullness of God.

1. God displays his multi-faceted wisdom in the Church

2. Praying for the triune God to enlighten the Church.

3. Praying for the Church to be filled with the fullness of God

Good morning, my name is David Yung, the minister of St Paul’s.

It’s great to see you all at Church.

Over the past few months, we’ve been learning many great lessons from the Bible from various preachers here.

We celebrated our 154th Anniversary, we learnt about singleness, marriage and divorce.

We celebrated the baptisms and confirmations of our children and youths.

We went through a series of thanksgiving.

If you want to find copies of these sermons, you still can find them at the back of the Church in the shelves marked ‘previous sermons’.

Today, we come back to our series on the letter to the Ephesians.

If you have your Bible there, please open up to Ephesians chapter 2.

Here’s a recap of what we were up to.

In Ephesians chapter 2, we see the Church being described as the place of reconciliation between God and men, and between one fellowman to another.

There Paul talks about the reconciliation between Jews and Gentiles, that is people who weren’t Jews.

There was hostility between Jews and Gentiles back then and indeed, even for hundreds of years before Paul’s time.

Now the last time I preached on this was on 10 September, before the conflict that started on 7th Oct.

Since then, the hostility between the Jews and the Gentiles intensified.

The war between the two sides is intense; all sides are saddened by what is happening there at the moment.

The hostility between the Jews and Gentiles is still happening and all of us would’ve felt it to an extent, even in here in 21st Century Australia.

That’s why these words in Ephesians 2:16 are so amazing, “He did this so that he might reconcile both (Jews and Gentiles) to God in one body through the cross by which he put the hostility to death.”

They are even more amazing now than when I preached about it back in September.

Christ has come to bring us both, Jews and Gentiles, to God the Father.

In Christ the hostility between Jews and Gentles was destroyed!

Jesus has come to bring us peace; This is good news that we need to hear this more than ever!

But how can we be so sure?

I mean, the hostility between the Jews and Gentiles are still happening.

How can we be confident about the peace that the Bible says Jesus brings.

How can Christians, how can the Church, be confident about God and what he says?

What’s happening in Gaza seems to say that the hostility is still pretty much alive.

And yet, we Christians are still confident about who God is and what he said.

Where does a Christian’s confidence about God come from?

How can we be confident about our prayers to him, especially when the world is as hostile as it has ever been?

That’s what we’re going to explore in today’s passage.

Today, we’ll be looking at Ephesians 3, beginning from verse 8.

We’ll see three things:

1. God displays his multi-faceted wisdom in the Church

2. Praying for the triune God to enlighten the Church.

3. Praying for the Church to be filled with the fullness of God

Before we do, let’s pray, “Father, you are the one from whom every family in heaven and on earth is named. Open the eyes of our hearts so that we may see a glimpse of your glory as we hear from your words. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”

God displays his multi-faceted wisdom in the Church

Ephesians chapter three begins with the start of a prayer for the Ephesians, “For this reason, I, Paul, the prisoner of Christ Jesus on behalf of you Gentiles —”

But Paul then stops to explain the significance of the gospel to them.

He’s so excited about the gospel that he wanted to explain it to them again before praying for them!

He’s excited about the gospel because God has made him a servant of this gospel.

It’s God who has given him this grace, this gift, to tell the gospel, especially to the Gentiles, the non-Jews.

Look with me at 3:8, “This grace was given to me — the least of all the saints — to proclaim to the Gentiles the incalculable riches of Christ,”

Paul was chosen not because he was worthy.

He calls himself the least of all the saints, for Paul himself had persecuted Christians before he became a Christian.

He was what we would today call an extremist, chasing after Christians and hunting them down.

However, he’s now experienced God’s grace and God has assigned him the job of proclaiming the incalculable riches of Christ.

The riches of Christ is too much to calculate.

You can use your calculators, your computers, your supercomputers, but they’ll break before they can give you a figure on Christ’s riches.

It’s far too much to count.

And yet, this is what Paul was assigned by God to do.

Verses 9-10, “and to shed light for all about the administration of the mystery hidden for ages in God who created all things. This is so that God’s multi-faceted wisdom may now be made known through the church to the rulers and authorities in the heavens.”

In our Chinese translation, we have the words “使眾人都明白” which is true, but the original word carries the idea of shining a light, shedding a light on what was once darkness.

Paul’s job was to shed light about how the Creator God administers, how He manages his mystery, hidden for ages but now put on display to the rulers and authorities in the heavens.

These rulers and authorities are heavenly beings, some of which are enemies of God.

This mystery was hidden from us but now, we can see it because God has chosen people like Paul to shed light on it.

God wants us to see his wisdom, his beautiful, multifaceted wisdom.

It’s wisdom that shines like a diamond.

Now, I personally don’t have a diamond but I do have a crystal.

When I shine my light at the right angle, we can see different rays of colour displayed.

That’s what God’s wisdom is like, except it’s infinitely grander and infinitely more beautiful!

God’s wisdom has many dimensions, and many colours, and they all come together perfectly in Christ.

Where do we see God’s wisdom? Look with me at verse 10:

This is so that God’s multi-faceted wisdom may now be made known through the church to the rulers and authorities in the heavens.

The Church, as imperfect as it appears to the world, is what God shows as his multi-faceted, multicoloured wisdom.

For in the Church, you see people from European background, from Asian background, from African background, from South American background, coming together in the one Lord, the Lord Jesus Christ.

When people from different backgrounds, different cultures, different languages all praising, glorifying one God, filled with the Holy Spirit of God, giving thanks in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, there you see the multi-faceted wisdom of God.

We live in a world that’s utterly divided.

There are people who are pro-this and anti-that, loyal to one nation and deeply suspicious of all everyone else.

And yet, in the midst of these divisions, Christians have a message, a gospel, that declares that in Christ, we’re no longer enemies but family.

This is what happens when we come together in Christ.

We’re reconciled to one another because we’re reconciled to God, in Christ.

Christ came to die for the reconciliation between God and men, and between one fellow men to another.

However, that doesn’t mean Christians are perfect.

Far from it! We fail so miserably short of what God has called us to be.

There are still churches bitterly divided along the line of race, along the line of culture, even along the line of politics.

We still look down on the other ‘people’, the other ‘race’, even though Christ has died to bring us back together.

The problem is not with the gospel of Jesus but with us.

We fail to let the gospel take hold of our hearts; we fail to live up to what God has made us to be.

Does this mean that this reconciliation between people is merely only an ideal, a utopia that we will never reach?

Absolutely not!

3:11, “This is according to his eternal purpose accomplished in Christ Jesus our Lord.”

Despite our weaknesses, God has established the Church according to his eternal purpose in Christ Jesus.

God has planned this from eternity and He will fulfil it.

God will make it happen.

In Christ, all barriers will be broken down.

The hostility between God and men, fellow men with one another, as well as all hostilities in all creation, physical and spiritual will be destroyed.

No ruler or authority, be they physical or spiritual, no matter how powerful, will be able to stop God’s plan to bring peace to all things in Christ.

It will happen!

That’s why we can come before him with boldness and confidence.

3:12, “In him we have boldness and confident access through faith in him.”

We can speak freely and approach freely before the throne of God.

Just as Christ can speak and access God freely, we, the Church, as those in Christ, can come before him with confidence.

Is it perfect? No. We will still suffer.

Paul himself still suffered for the Christians at Ephesus.

But it’s for their glory.

He suffered so that they will see the glory of Jesus even more clearly.

Eph 3:13, “So, then, I ask you not to be discouraged over my afflictions on your behalf, for they are your glory.”

He calls his sufferings the Ephesians’ glory.

While not many of us suffer to the same extent, some of us do work hard for the sake of other Christians.

Some of us spent late nights to prepare for Church; others take long drives to Church.

Some of us take the risk of being ridiculed when things don’t turn out right as well as the risk of being humiliated when rejected.

Others suffered a loss of income and even a loss of homes.

But these afflictions, these sufferings are your glory.

We suffer so that you may obtain glory.

Don’t be discouraged by it; we thank God that we can suffer for your glory.

Friends, we live in a world full of hostility and divisions.

But in the midst of all these, we can come together as a Church.

What we have in the Church is so good, so unique.

We have different cultures and different generations coming together in the name of the Lord Jesus.

It’s not easy, but good things are never easy.

We come tougher because we want to be a Church that God can point to and say, “Everyone, this is what my wisdom look like. This is the Church my Son died for.”

Brothers and sisters of St Paul’s live up to what God has made us to be!

2. Praying for the triune God to enlighten the Church.

But how? What must we do? How can we possibly live up to it?

The first thing that Paul tells us is not about putting together a strategy or a plan.

The first thing is to kneel before the Father and pray for the triune God to enlighten the Church.

3:14-15, “For this reason I kneel before the Father from whom every family in heaven and on earth is named.”

You might notice that in front of you is a contraption that folds down.

They are not foot rests but cushions for you to put your knees on when you pray.

They are not foot rests but prayer pads.

For many, many years, members of our Church use the prayers pads when they pray to God.

Our Church is a House of Prayer and even the furniture is designed for us to pray.

We’re to pray to God the Father.

While He is especially the Father of the Lord Jesus and those who belong to him, there’s a sense in which he’s also the Father of every family in heaven and on earth in a more general sense.

Every person’s identity ultimately comes from the Father; all of us, Christians and non-Christians, owe our existence to the Father.

What do we pray for?

3:16-17, “I pray that he may grant you, according to the riches of his glory, to be strengthened with power in your inner being through his Spirit, and that Christ may dwell in your hearts through faith.

The Father’s glory is limitless and so ask God to generously, lavishly strengthen your inner person, your inner man, according to his limitless glory.

Your inner man is who you are on the inside, where the centre of your life is.

It’s deeper than your job, your family, what you’re good at, even what you love – your inner man is the heart of who you are.

Pray that your inner man will be strengthened to fight against sin, persevere despite your own weaknesses, love when hated.

Pray that your inner man will take up the cross daily and follow Jesus.

It doesn’t take much to sin, to listen to your flesh, to follow the ways of the devil and the world.

But it takes great strength to be a Christian; it takes great strength to follow Jesus.

And so, pray that your inner man may be strengthened by the Spirit!

Also, pray that Christ will dwell in our hearts.

Pray that Jesus will be at the heart of our being.

Pray that He’s there, at the centre of our lives, at the centre of our identity.

These two prayer requests, the Spirit’s strengthening and Christ’s presence in our heart are parallels of one another.

We have the Spirit’s strengthening because we have Christ living in our hearts.

Christ lives in our hearts because the Spirit strengthens us in our inner man to believe Christ.

This is what a Christian experiences, both the Spirit’s strengthening and Christ’s dwelling.

Jesus lives in the hearts of genuine Christians.

A Christian’s heart is Christ’s permanent home; He lives there by his Spirit.

Did you notice that all three Persons of the Trinity, the Father, the Son and the Spirit, are there in the prayer?

We are to pray to the Father, according to his glory, strengthened by the Spirit, as the Son lives in our hearts.

As someone who prays to, strengthened by and in whom the Persons of the Trinity dwells, Paul prays that the Ephesian Christians will be rooted and establish in love.

Christians are planted and rooted in the soil of the love of God, established and built as the temple of God.

For it’s only as we’re rooted and firmly established in the love of God, that we (in verse 18), “may be able to comprehend with all the saints what is the length and width, height and depth of God’s love,”

We need God, all three Persons of the triune God, to help us comprehend God’s love.

More specifically, we need the triune God to help us comprehend Christ’s love.

Verse 19, “and to know Christ’s love that surpasses knowledge, so that you may be filled with all the fullness of God.”

Christ’s love is beyond comprehension; it’s a love that surpasses knowledge!

And yet, Paul prays that the Ephesian Christians will know the knowledge-surpassing love of Christ.

How do you know something that’s infinite and incalculable?

We need God’s mighty power to understand the love of Christ.

We need to get on bended knees and ask God to help us catch a glimpse of it.

And even when we catch a glimpse of it, there’s still so much more to understand.

Now notices that we’re not asked to pray that we will love Christ more but to know Christ’s love more.

We need to pray that we might know Christ’s love for us.

For our lack of love for Christ it shows that we don’t know Christ’s love.

For when we know Christ’s love, we can’t help but love him.

And the more we know Christ’s love, the more we love him.

For those who know the love of Christ, his love for us is obvious and we can’t believe that others don’t see it.

The reason is because they don’t know Christ’s love and so they remain blind.

And so, we need God to lovingly, tenderly, open our eyes so that we can see the love of Christ.

It’s only when we get a glimpse of the length and width, height and depth of God’s love in Christ, that we can begin to love Christ.

On Friday, the Children at the Children Centre asked me – Does God really love us? How do we know?

I told them, “Come on Sunday!”

But they said, “No, tell us now, how do we know God love us?”

And I told them plainly the first thing that came in my head, “Jesus loves me this I know, for the Bible tells me so.”

I actually sang it, “Little ones to him belong, they are weak, but he is strong”

Yes, Jesus loves me, Yes Jesus loves me, yes Jesus loves me.

The Bible tells me so.”

God showed Christ’s love for us in His word, the Bible.

For the only way we can understand Christ’s love for us is for God to show it to us.

And so, we need to pray for that to happen.

Pray that we might know that length and width, the height and depth of Christ’s love.

For Until you know his love, you won’t love him.

Until you grow in your love for him, you can’t love him more.

3. Praying for the Church to be filled with the fullness of God

As well as praying for the Ephesian Christians to know Christ’s love, Paul prays that the Church will be filled with the fullness of God.

3:19, “and to know Christ’s love that surpasses knowledge, so that you may be filled with all the fullness of God.”

We know the knowledge-surpassing love of Christ so we might be filled with the fullness of God.

That’s a massive prayer point.

When we pray, we ask for better weather, or to pass exams.

Their scope of prayer is usually limited to these.

Sometimes, when I ask for pray points, people tell me they have nothing to pray for, probably because they don’t have exams, or the weather is good.

Everything is fine and so there’s nothing to pray for.

But here, Paul prays that the Christians in Ephesus will be filled with the fullness of God.

Not just filled with a little bit of God but to the fullness of God.

That’s a bold prayer; a massive prayer!

Can we pray a prayer this big?

Is that too big of a prayer point to ask for, for the Church to be filled to the fullness of God?

No, it’s not too big! It is an entirely appropriate prayer!

God wants to fill us with his Spirit, God wants to strengthen us; he wants Jesus to live in our hearts, he wants us to know the knowledge-surpassing love of Christ.

He wants us to be filled with his fullness so that He can point to us and say that we are the fruit of his multifaceted wisdom.

It’s not too much to all!

For we pray according to the riches of his limitless glory.

Since his glory is limitless, no prayer is too big for him.

3:20-21, “Now to him who is able to do above and beyond all that we ask or think according to the power that works in us —  to him be glory in the church and in Christ Jesus to all generations, forever and ever. Amen.”

God is able to do above and beyond anything we can ask or think of. He can fill us with all the fullness of him.

We ask for this not for our glory but God’s.

For all glory belongs to him.

And He has shown His glory is in the Church and in Christ Jesus.

Our prayer is that God will be glorified in his Church and in Christ Jesus for all generations.

Our prayer is that people will see the glory of God in Jesus.

Our prayer is that they will see the glory of God in the Church.

Friends, that’s what you have come to see today.

You have come to glorify God, you have come to be God’s glory.

People will see the glory of God in the Church, when we, the Church, grasp how the knowledge-surpassing love of Christ and live according to his love.

People will see the glory of God when enemies are reconciled to each other because they are in Christ.

People will see the glory of God in the Church when we proclaim the incalculable riches of Christ and believe Christ for themselves.

Last week, we were at the shopping centre collecting toys ‘n’ tucker, not for our own glory, but for God’s.

This coming week, a mission team from Macquarie university will come to speak with the people of Kogarah, not for their own glory, but for God’s.

Pray that we will be able to bring someone to Christ this week.

It might seem like a bold prayer, but God can do above and beyond all that we ask or imagine.

How can we be confident in our God?

To be confident in who God is and to trust him at this word, we must bend our knees and pray.

We must pray, for only God can show us who he is.

Don’t just pray small prayers; pray bold prayers.

Pray that God will show us the knowledge-surpassing love of Christ and to fill us with his fullness.

St Paul’s Kogarah, Bend your knees to the Father, and pray for His Spirit to strengthen our inner man, so that we may know the love of Christ His Son.

Use the prayer pads later on when we pray.

Bend your knees and pray bold prayers before God.

Use this prayer in Ephesians 3:14-21 as your template for your prayer this week.

You can either use word for word or to use your own words based on this passage.

Bend your knees to the Father, and pray for His Spirit to strengthen our inner man, so that we may know the love of Christ His Son.

Here are the three questions for this week:

In what area have I failed to live up to what God has called me to be?

Have I confessed it to God and ask for his forgiveness?

Will I pray boldly for God to fill us with all of his fullness?

Friends, don’t be satisfied with prayers that mean so little that nothing happens if you don’t pray.

Instead, bend your knees to the Father, and pray for His Spirit to strengthen our inner man, so that we may know the love of Christ His Son.

Let’s pray,

“For this reason, I kneel before the Father from whom every family in heaven and on earth is named. I pray that he may grant you, according to the riches of his glory, to be strengthened with power in your inner being through his Spirit, and that Christ may dwell in your hearts through faith. I pray that you, being rooted and firmly established in love, may be able to comprehend with all the saints what is the length and width, height and depth of God’s love, and to know Christ’s love that surpasses knowledge, so that you may be filled with all the fullness of God. We pray this according to the incalculable riches of Christ, amen.”
