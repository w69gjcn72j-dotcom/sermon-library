Happy New Year! 新年快乐

Chinese are very creative people.

In English, we usually only have one greeting for the New Year, 新年快乐

But there are so many more greetings for Chinese New Year.

Since we’re in the Year of the Tiger, there are many greetings that use Tiger in their greetings.

For example, we have 生龙活虎 shēng lóng huó hǔ

— Wishing you the drive of the Dragon and the vigor of the Tiger!

Wouldn’t it be great to have a dragon’s drive and the tiger’s vigor!

In other years, we might wish that people 金玉滿堂 Jīnyù mǎntáng

Wishing people to have gold and jade fill their halls!

Wouldn’t it be great if people have homes filled with treasures like gold and jade!

While most homes I know aren’t filled with gold and jade, they are filled with a lot of stuff.

Twice a year in Kogarah, we get a glimpse of what our neighbours had been filling their houses with.

Because twice a year, there’s a council clean up, when the council picks up large piles of your rubbish for free.

During those weeks, the streets often have a lot of rubbish: Old TV’s, old furniture, old toys.

These things might had been treasures at one stage, but soon they became worthless, rubbish that needs to be put out.

It can be a confronting sight, can’t it?

Things that we once treasured had turned into garbage.

Of course, there are other things besides furniture that we might treasure now in our lives.

Perhaps it’s our house itself, or our career, or our relationships.

We’ve spent a lot of our time and our resources on these.

They are our treasures.

But are they worthy treasures to keep? Or will I be throwing them away one day?

What have I filled my life with? How do they contribute to my life’s worth?

In today’s Bible passage, we’ll see that there is something good that we must fill our lives with.

Being filled with it will contribute to our life’s worth in ways far greater than a house full of gold or jade.

If you have your Bible there, please open up to Colossians 1:9-14.

Be filled with the knowledge of God’s will

Colossians 1:9, “For this reason also, since the day we heard this, we haven’t stopped praying for you. We are asking that you may be filled with the knowledge of his will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding,”

Whenever you don’t know how to pray for a Christian, take a look at the Bible and see how the writers pray.

Here, Paul’s praying for the Colossian Church, that they may be filled with the knowledge of God’s will.

It’s not like a Chinese New Year greeting; it’s not a wish.

It’s a prayer; it’s asking God to fill the Colossian Church with the knowledge of his will.

It’s something that Paul hasn’t stopped doing since he’s heard about them.

Paul prays that they’ll know God’s will, that they’ll know what God wants.

What is God’s will? What does He want?

If we can sum it up in one word, it’s Jesus.

One of the most influential book in Christian history is a book called Confession by Saint Augustine.

It’s generally recognized as one of the masterpieces of western literature.

It’s also unique in that it’s the first Christian autobiography ever written.

It’s Augustine recounting his youth, when he wasn’t a believer, through his Christian conversion, to how he views his relationship with God.

As you read Confessions, you begin to discover what kind of person Augustine was.

You begin to understand why he made those decisions and why chose the life that he did.

That’s what an autobiography does. It helps us know the writer.

The most important thing for us, both men and women, whether we are believers or not, is to know who God is and to worship him.

And God has graciously shown us who he is through his Word, the Bible.

The Bible is not a textbook, a practical guide to becoming a good person.

No, rather, the Bible is God’s book to us, God’s personal letter to us.

It’s his autobiography, as it were, and it’s a book all about Jesus.

If we want to know God and His will, we must come to know the Lord Jesus.

Paul’s prayer is not for us to know just a little about the Lord Jesus.

He wants us to be filled with the knowledge of his will.

He wants us to be filled with the knowledge of Jesus.

To be filled means there’s no room left for anything else.

We are to be full, filled to the brim, with Jesus.

Paul prays that as we’re filled with the knowledge of His will, we’ll also have all wisdom and spiritual understanding.

When we have wisdom and spiritual understanding,

we can apply our knowledge of God’s will in different situations,

in order to do what God wants.

For the Christian, it’s what God wants that matters.

There are many things that we want in our lives, but what does God want?

What does God want me to do in this situation?

How does God want me to choose, especially in light of what Jesus has done?

Paul prays that the Colossians will be filled with the knowledge of God’s will in all wisdom and understanding to make these decisions.

It’s a prayer that we ought to pray for one another in our day-to-day lives.

Recently, I read a story about a CMS missionary who worked with healthcare workers in a South East Asian country.

The country’s healthcare system was overwhelmed by COVID, especially during June last year.

After a horrible day at a particular hospital, she had a clear and strong urge to send a message to a doctor at that hospital.

She wrote back and asked, ‘Did you hear something about me?’

The missionary said no, but that she just felt God prompted her to pray for her at that moment.

It turns out she had just hours earlier been diagnosed with COVID, her X-ray showed pneumonia.

She was alone, in isolation because of COVID, and awaiting a hospital bed.

And then the missionary’s text came.

The young Doctor said how grateful she was that God had answered her cry in an unexpected way.

The missionary then send a COVID care pack later that day, which included Our Daily Bread. It was the ‘Our Daily Bread’ book she thanked for first.

It happened to be out of date, but her comment was, ‘His truth is never out of date’.

The missionary might had felt helpless, seeing so many people dying with COVID around her.

But she trusted God’s lead, and helped a young Doctor in her moment of need.

What does God want you to do with your life?

How do you know God’s will for your life?

You will find God’s will for your life in the Gospel of Jesus.

Join a Growth Group.

Pray that all of us will be filled with the knowledge of God’s will.

Pray that all of us will know Jesus better.

Live a life worthy of the Lord Jesus

Why do we want to know God’s will and know Jesus better?

It’s because we want to live a life worthy of the Lord Jesus

Look with me at verse 10, “10 so that you may walk worthy of the Lord, fully pleasing to him”

Some of us would’ve noticed that I had this digital piano up here since last year.

It’s a Yamaha Clavinova CVP-92 that I bought back in 1997, some 25 years ago.

It was one of the first thing I bought when I first started working full-time.

It has served me well over these 25 years and we’re still using it for ministry even today.

It’s a digital piano that gave me great joy! It was costly when I got it, but it was worth it!

Paul prays that his readers may be filled with the knowledge of God’s will not just so that we’re filled with head knowledge.

It’s not so that we can pass an exam.

He wants us to use the knowledge of God’s will to live a life worthy of the Lord, to be fully pleasing to him.

Just as the digital piano has given me great joy and has shown its worth over the years,

Christians are to give God great joy and to live lives worthy of the Lord, lives worthy of God.

God has shown us his will for our lives; now live it.

That’s the purpose of knowing God’s will in our lives.

You can’t separate the knowledge from the life.

You can’t tell me that you know God when you don’t live a life worthy of the Lord.

You can’t tell me that you know the Lord Jesus and that you know his will when you’re doing the opposite of what he says.

All of us, whether men or women, boys or girls, kindergarten or University lecturers, all of us are called to live a life worthy of the Lord.

God calls all of us to come to know him in the Lord Jesus.

God calls us to be filled with the knowledge of His will.

God calls us to live a life fully pleasing to him.

No matter how much we’ve achieved, no matter how much we’ve accumulate for ourselves,

if we’re not filled with the knowledge of his will, if we don’t live a life to please the Lord, then our lives aren’t worthy.

It may be full but it’s not worthy.

What does a life worthy of the Lord look like?

Paul lists four things:

You will bear fruit in every good work.

You will show do good works for the sake of other people in your life.

You will show kindness, care, generosity and love in your life.

You will grow in the knowledge of God.

You will always have a thirst to know God more through the Lord Jesus.

You will continue to read God’s word and find out what he wants.

It’s a positive feedback loop.

That is, A produces more of B which in turn produces more of A.

When you know more of God’s will and you will do more of God’s will, you will know even more of God’s will which leads you to do even more of God’s will.

When you are filled with God’s wisdom and spiritual understanding, and act upon it, you begin a spiritual positive feedback loop.

You will grow in the knowledge of God

You will be strengthened with all power.

We can only know God’s will and do God’s will if we’re strengthened with all power, according to God’s glorious might.

As the saying goes, God gives what he demands.

God demands us to live a life worthy of him, he’ll give us whatever we need to live a life worthy of him.

God gives us with the greatest strength possible, even greater than that of dragons and tigers,

In order to live a life worthy of him.

So depend on His strength.

Endure through the difficult times and be patient with difficult people in your lives.

He will give you the strength to last through.

Joyfully giving thanks to God the Father

It’s God who gives us our lives; it’s God who shows us how to live our lives; it’s God who gives us the strength to live worthy lives.

And so always give thanks to God the Father, with great joy.

“bearing fruit in every good work”; “growing in the knowledge of God”; “being strengthened with power”; “giving thanks with joy.”

There are the characteristics of a Christian whose life is worthy of God.

Last month, my family and I went to CMS Summer School, a camp where many missionaries share their stories.

One missionary couple was Jo and Michael Charles.

They were missionaries in Chile for 18 years.

Before they went, they had a fairly good and stable life here in Australia.

However, they sold everything, EVERYTHING, they had to go and become missionaries in Chile.

It was hard work in Chile; it’s not as comfortable as Australia, it’s been politically unstable and you don’t have many like-minded friends there, working for a small Bible college.

Was it a waste? Absolutely not!

Was it worth it! Totally!

Not only have they lives a life worthy of the Lord in Chile but they also led and taught others to live a life worthy of the Lord Jesus.

It’s a tough life but a life worthy of the Lord Jesus, a life pleasing to Jesus, a life of joyful thanks to the Father because He has given us so much more in the Person of Jesus.

How about us?

Do we live lives worthy of the Lord Jesus?

“bearing fruit in every good work”; “growing in the knowledge of God”; “being strengthened with power”; “giving thanks with joy.”

Are these the characteristics of our life?

Do people see the Gospel in us?

When we gather together as Church on Sundays, we gather to help each other live worthy lives for God.

We make sure our governance is in order so that the infrastructure of our Church helps us to live lives worthy of God.

And we use our gifts from God for God’s glory, for his pleasure.

That’s why one of our goals this year is to have 100 families at Church, not only to sit in pews but to actively serve in the Church.

Over the coming year, we’ll keep training people so that all of us are equipped to serve God.

Our prayer is that as more people serve God, more people will be rescued by the Lord Jesus, who rescues people by delivering.

The Lord Jesus rescues by delivering

Look with me at verses 12-13, “giving thanks to the Father, who has enabled you to share in the saints’ inheritance in the light. 13 He has rescued us from the domain of darkness and transferred us into the kingdom of the Son he loves. 14 In him we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins.”

God saves us be delivering us, transferring us from the domain of darkness to the Kingdom of the Son, whom he loves.

Some of us might remember that back in 2018, a small junior Soccer team and their assistant coach were trapped in the  Tham Luang Nang Non cave in Thailand.

Their entrance was four kilometres away and they were deep pools of water between them and the entrance.

Thousands of rescuers from all around the world joined in the rescue mission.

In the end, specialist divers had to sedate the boys to keep them calm and safely carry them from the darkness of the cave to the glorious light at the entrance.

They were rescued by the divers delivering them from one place to another.

That’s what God has done for us in Jesus.

All of us were at one time in a place far more dangerous than the cave.

We all used to live the domain of darkness and we were all under the control and influence of the darkness.

We were soaked in utter spiritual darkness.

We were living and breathing the darkness; we were so used to the darkness that we didn’t even know we were in the dark.

But God in his kindness and goodness has transferred us from a place of darkness to the Kingdom of the Son whom he loves.

God has rescued us and placed us in a safe place.

It’s a place of light, where we share in the inheritance of the saints.

What belongs to the holy ones now belong to us, because God’s delivered us from darkness to light.

We were once slaves of the darkness, but he has redeemed us.

With the price of his son, he brought us into the Kingdom of his Son.

We were once sinners, but he has forgiven us.

Because Jesus his Son has died for our sins, he forgives us and remembers our sin no more.

God saves us from darkness by delivering us into the Kingdom of Jesus.

Lin Lin was someone who moved to Hong Kong from China.

She had been worshipping idols all her life until she stepped into St Andrew’s Church in Hong Kong.

There she heard the gospel of Jesus and wanted to come to Jesus.

However, her house still had idols and she was afraid of them and so she asked the Mandarin minister to cleanse her home of idols.

As they were going to the house, Lin Lin was afraid of what these idols might do to her.

She asked whether destroying these idols would harm her.

However, when they got there, the minister along with a three other congregational members prayed, read the Bible and sang songs about the power of Jesus.

They then went around the house to destroy idols.

There were so many idols in the house that Lin Lin had forgotten that she had them!

She was living in fear of them and had been under their control.

But now, Lin Lin knows the love of Jesus and is filled with joy because she knows that Jesus has died for her.

She’s been transferred from the domain of darkness to the kingdom of the Son.

God has the power over every idol, over every domain of darkness in your life.

We can’t save ourselves from darkness but God can.

Only God can save us in the Person of Jesus.

As a Church, we’re here to Tell the Gospel.

There are many people like Lin Lin, living under the influence of the evil one.

Perhaps some of us here in person or online, are like Lin Lin, trapped in darkness, unable to escape.

Here at St Paul’s we want to tell you how God can rescue you in the Person of Jesus.

This is what the Gospel is; it’s how God rescued us in the Person of Jesus.

This is why we exist here at St Paul’s – it’s to tell everyone that God has provided the rescue, that there is true light beyond the darkness.

If you want to find out more about what it means to live in the light instead of the darkness, please come and see me or Range or Po Ki afterwards.

We would love to show you what God has done for you.

Don’t stay in the domain of darkness any longer; come into the Kingdom of the Son.

And so friends, be filled with the knowledge of Jesus, our deliverer, and walk in a way worthy of him.

That’s what we’re on about!

And so, what’s the take home message?

Here’s our today’s Chinese New Year greeting from our Church to you:

May our Church hall be filled with the knowledge of God’s will. May our Church life be strengthened by God’s glorious might.

My prayer is that all of us will be filled with the knowledge of God’s will and that we’ll walk according to his glorious might.

What will that look like?

When we make decisions, we’ll ask ourselves: what has God already said about the decision we’re facing?

How will our decision be fully pleasing to him?

What does God want us to do in this decision?

Does he want us to be generously give or to be greedily take?

Does he want us to serve others or to sit and be served?

Does he want us to do good or to do evil?

My prayer is that we will indeed be filled with the knowledge of God’s will so that we will walk in a way that’s pleasing to him, not only as individuals but together as a Church.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How am I looking forward to seeing God fill me with the knowledge of His will?

Is my life worthy of the Lord Jesus? Why or why not?

Does the way I live show that I’m living under the domain of darkness or in the kingdom of the Son?

Friends, May our Church hall be filled with the knowledge of God’s will. May our Church life be strengthened by God’s glorious might.
