The movie ‘Up in the Air’ starring George Clooney has this fascinating speech at the beginning of the movie.

He begins by saying this: “How much does your life weigh? Imagine for a second that you’re carrying a backpack. I want you to feel the straps on your shoulders. Feel ’em? Now I want you to pack it with all the stuff that you have in your life. You start with the little things. The things on shelves and in drawers, the knick-knacks, the collectibles. Feel the weight as that adds up. Then you start adding larger stuff, clothes, table-top appliances, lamps, linens, your TV.

The backpack should be getting pretty heavy now. And you go bigger. Your couch, bed, your kitchen table. Stuff it all in there. Your car, get it in there. Your home, whether it’s a studio apartment or a two bedroom house. I want you to stuff it all into that backpack. Now try to walk. It’s kind of hard, isn’t it? This is what we do to ourselves on a daily basis. We weigh ourselves down until we can’t even move. And make no mistake, moving is living.

Now, I’m gonna set that backpack on fire. What do you want to take out of it? What do you want to take out of it? Photos? Photos are for people who can’t remember. Drink some ginkgo and let the photos burn. In fact, let everything burn and imagine waking up tomorrow with nothing. It’s kind of exhilarating, isn’t it?

That was George Clooney; now back to us here at St Paul’s Kogarah

I don’t know about you, but if everything I ever own can fit in my backpack, and it’s set on fire, I would probably feel quite bad….at first.

All the things that I saved to buy, all the things that I collected over the years, all the tools and appliances I use for my day to day living, all gone in a fire.

I hope it doesn’t happen.

But we all know that it can happen; a fire can destroy everything we own.

And of course, a war or a conflict can certainly destroy everything a city or a country owns.

What we have won’t last; we might not think about it much, but we know that everything we have has an expiry date.

And so, why do we want all these things so much?

Do we really need and keep every single thing that we own?

Must we devote ourselves to earning more, so that we can buy more?

The Bible has a word for our desire, our devotion to get and to keep as much as we can: it’s the word “Greed”.

In fact, the Bible calls greed idolatry.

Over the next three weeks, we’re going to explore the idea that Greed is idolatry.

We’re going to see the godlike status of money today and its influence over us.

If you have your Bible, please open up to Matthew 6:19-24. We’re going to see two things:

Give us neither poverty nor wealth; feed us with the food I need. Otherwise, I might have too much and deny you, saying, “Who is the Lord?” or I might have nothing and steal, profaning the name of my God’. This we ask in the name of the Lord Jesus, Amen.”

Two treasures

Please open up with me to Matthew chapter 6, beginning from verse 19.

This is what Jesus’ said: “Don’t store up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust destroy and where thieves break in and steal.”

Jesus says that there two types of treasures: disposable treasures and indestructible treasures.

Jesus tells us not to treasure disposable treasures.

Don’t treasure things that will one day end up on the rubbish tip.

When my children were growing up, we would take them every now and them to McDonald’s (we call them Macca’s in Australia).

They would often get the Happy Meals because they often have cool toys that come with them.

You get things like Hello Kitty, Hot Wheels cars, Zoobies, Littlest petshop and even minions.

However, even though the children really loved them when they were kids, they’re not that interested in the Happy Meals toys anymore.

We just have boxes and boxes of them stored away.

When we move from one house to another, we would often throw them all away because no one wants them.

Of course, it’s not just toys that we throw away.

All of our latest tech and gadgets that we have, and pay thousands of dollars for, will also end up in the rubbish tip.

Check out this old ad I found.

It’s for a 10 Megabyte computer system for only $5995 US Dollars.

10 Megabytes is less than 0.01% of the memory of my phone (and my phone is 4 years old).

No one wants to buy a 10 Megabyte computer for $5995 for work; you won’t be able to get much done on that thing.

All our toys, all our computers, all the things we can buy are disposable.

All of them will end up in the rubbish tip one day.

Jesus tells us not to treasure these things; don’t make these your end goal!

Why spend so much on pre-rubbish material?

The comedian Jerry Seinfeld has this really clever insight about the things that we own: ‘All things on earth only exist in different stages of becoming garbage. Your home is a garbage processing centre where you buy new things, bring them into your house and slowly “turning them to garbage” over time.’

It’s kind of true, isn’t it?

We buy things into our home, and the only way they leave our home is usually when they’re turned into rubbish.

So what should we do instead?

Jesus says, “store up for yourselves treasures in heaven, where neither moth nor rust destroys, and where thieves don’t break in and steal.”

Jesus tells us to treasure the indestructible things.

Unlike anything we can buy for ourselves, these treasures won’t end up on the rubbish tip.

These treasures are in heaven!

Thieves can’t break into heaven to steal these treasures.

What are these treasures in heaven?

If we look back at back verses 1-18, we’ll see that Jesus has been talking about rewards from God the Father.

Jesus says that God the Father will reward those who give in secret, who pray in secret and who fast in secret. PPT

God the Father will reward those who obey him.

To obey God the Father we must first of all believe in Jesus.

That’s the first thing that the God the Father wants us to do.

That will mean trusting Jesus as Lord and to obey him in all that we do.

To be approved by the heavenly Father because of Jesus, to be rewarded by him because of Jesus is a heavenly treasure that the world can’t give and the world can’t take away.

Long for this treasure; treasure this heavenly treasure.

It’s far better; it will last far longer.

Jesus says, ‘For where you treasure is, there your heart will be also.’

Where you place your treasure is where you will find your heart.

Now notice that it’s not the other way around: it’s not where your heart is, there you will find treasure.

Your heart is not the starting point; where you place your treasure is your starting point.

That is, wherever you place your treasure, your heart will follow.

The New Testament scholar Don Carson puts it like this:

‘“Make sure you choose the treasure of heaven itself, the treasure of the new heaven and the new earth, because your heart will follow your treasure.”

If what you treasure the most is advancement at work, that’s where your imagination goes. That’s where your daydreaming goes. That’s where your energy goes. That’s where your creativity goes.

If what you value the most is the progress of your children, that’s where your heart goes. If what you want the most is a really fat retirement package, then you start thinking money. That’s where your heart goes.

What Jesus says is, “Choose your treasure, and make sure it’s eternal treasure, because that’s where your heart will go.”’

Where is your treasure? Is your treasure in heaven or on earth?

Is it a treasure one that will last a few decades, a few years, a few months, or

Do you have a treasure that lasts forever?

How do you know where your treasure is?

Here’s a simple exercise you might try.

All mature Christians, since they have understood how good God’s grace is, gives to support the work of telling people about Jesus.

Here’s a graph of what they give to their local church might compare with the rest of the expenditure.

Being in Sydney, they might have to use a large part of their income on housing cost.

They might also be very prudent and save a lot of what they earn.

They will need to spend money on shopping, transport, and entertainment, every now and then.

There are different reasons why different people use their money various ways.

However, what mature Christians will do is to see how much they give to their local church compared with what they give to other things.

Should a Christian spend more on food than on giving to ministries?

Should a Christian spend more time on entertaining themselves than to give to ministries?

I did this exercise and I must say I had to repent on how much I spend on myself.

I adjusted my spending so that I will put my treasure in the heavens, not on earth.

How about you?

Where are your treasures? What do you spend your time, your imagination, your money on?

Put your treasure in heaven, seek God’s approval in Christ first, for these treasures will last far longer than anything you can spend your money on.

Two eyes

Look with me at verses 22-23,

‘22 " “The eye is the lamp of the body. If your eye is healthy, your whole body will be full of light. 23 But if your eye is bad, your whole body will be full of darkness. So if the light within you is darkness, how deep is that darkness!’

The phrase ‘your eye is healthy’ can also mean ‘you have a single eye’.

That is, literally, if you have a singular vision, your whole body will be full of light.

Today, we might say that the person has their eyes on the ball. (PPT)

Here, Jesus is talking about focus. (PPT)

If we focus on God the Father, then our body, our life, will be full of God’s light.

If we set our eyes firmly on the things of heaven, then we’ll find our heart there.

We’ll have a longing, a drive, to live for the holy God.

Our focus is on him and we want to be where he is, in heaven.

This singular vision, this focus on God the Father, will give us the drive to live for him in our lives.

On the other hand, to have a bad eye is to take your eyes off God and be distracted by something else.

In ancient Eastern culture, to have an evil (or bad) eye is to be envious of other people or to be greedy.

If having a good eye means focussing on God and the things of heaven,

Then having a bad eye means not focusing on God and the things of heaven.

Instead, we let our eyes wander to the darkness.

We turn our attention on the things of darkness, we envy those who have what looks like treasures in the dark.

What we treasure will be in the darkness and our heart will delight in darkness.

When we focus on darkness and evil, our lives will be consumed with darkness and evil.

If that’s where our eyes are, if that’s where our heart is, we, too, will become evil.

It’s like the character of Gollum in the story the Lord of the Rings. PPT

Gollum was originally a harmless Hobbit-like creature called Smeagol.

He lived peacefully; he enjoyed fishing. He was a very ordinary guy.

Then one day, he came across the ‘Ring’. PPT

It’s an evil ring and it grants evil powers to its wearer.

Smeagol was completely taken by the ring and became the horrible creature Gollum.

The ring became the only thing that mattered to Gollum.

It didn’t matter that the ring turned him into a creature of darkness.

He would do anything to keep the ring; he would even kill to keep the ring.

The ring became his ‘precious’.

That’s what happens when we take our eyes off God the Father.

Instead of treasuring him and trusting him, we turn away to something else.

And though we might not know it, that thing consumes us, overtakes us.

That’s what happened to a guy called Paul Fung.

Paul started gambling when he was eight.

It started with playing card games with his families and friends, betting with 10c and 20c coins.

When he turned 14, he asked people to place bets on horses for him.

When he was 16, he got a fake ID to gamble at the TAB.

At 18, he started to gamble in a casino.

This is how Paul described he was feeling when he gambled, ‘It didn’t matter what it was, as long as I could put money down and get some back. But when you lose, you justify that bad feeling only lasted a short period of time. You look for the next win, which you convinced yourself will override that losing feeling.”

He continued gambling for the next 10 years.

He then had access to his brother’s home loan and his gambling habit got a lot worse.

He had lost more than a million dollars online.

As a result, his brother lost his house.

Paul was out of control!

Gambling had completely overtaken his life and sapped his money, his integrity and his life out of him.

Not only did gambling sapped his life; it sapped his brother’s life savings as well!

I don’t know whether Paul was a Christian or not, but his wandering started with a game of cards when he was eight.

That wandering started a heart driven by greed.

It was greed that drove Paul to want more money, using the little money that he has, as well as the money that wrongly took from his own brother.

Greed was a tyrant that consumed Paul.

But it wasn’t just Paul who was driven by greed.

The whole gambling industry, the venue that provided the pokies for Paul, the advertising that drove Paul to play the pokies, were all driven by greed.

They were greedy for the little that Paul had.

Paul’s love for gambling ended up costing him far more, both financially and relationally.

And Paul’s story is only one of many, many more.

The solution is not simply to have better regulations or even to ban pokies machines, as good and as important as these initiatives may be.

The solution is to replace greed with true, and lasting satisfaction.

Greed, of course, is a dissatisfaction.

Greed is a vacuum in our heart that’s not satisfied.

Greed is our feeble attempt to fill the vacuum in our hearts with things that we buy with our money.

However, this vacuum in our heart is infinite and cannot be filled by the finite things that we buy with our money.

St Augustine of Hippo once prayed, ““You move us to delight in praising you; for you have made us for yourself, and our hearts are restless until they rest in you. Amen.”

Our hearts will be restless, dissatisfied until they find their rest in the infinite God.

That is, only the infinite God can fill the infinite vacuum in our hearts.

And God has chosen to fill our hearts by his Son Jesus Christ.

It’s only through Jesus Christ that God fills any hearts.

For we can’t be satisfied, until God is satisfied with us.

And God is only satisfied with us, as we come to believe Jesus, the Only One who can truly satisfy God by his death on the cross for our sins.

When you come to find your satisfaction in Jesus, the only One who can truly satisfy God, it’s only because God is satisfied with you and filled your heart the He alone can fill.

The only true cure for our insatiable greed is the infinite satisfaction that God gives us through Christ.

Can you imagine a world where our community is not driven by greed, where thousands of lives aren’t ruined by the greed of both individuals and industries?

Over the past two years, I’ve moved motions in our Sydney Anglican synod, drawing our attention to the danger of gambling fueled by greed.

My prayer is that through our Churches, through you, we will point people to Jesus so that as the love of Jesus grips the heart of our community, we will see a drop in what we’re losing to greed.

And so, friends, don’t have a bad eye, don’t wander away from God.

Have a good, singular vision – keep your focus on God and the heavenly things.

For both this week and next week, I want you to remember this: Set your eyes on the Master of indestructible treasures, not the tyrant of expiring pleasures.

Be honest with yourselves: what’s precious to you?

Gollum called his ring ‘his precious’. What do you call as your precious?

What do you hold most dear to your heart?

My hope is that if you call yourself a follower of Jesus, then Jesus will be the most precious person in your life and serving him is the most precious thing that you want to do.

However, I also want us to be honest with ourselves.

What’s the evidence of our devotion to Jesus?

Where’s the proof? If I were to look at your expenditure, what will it really say what’s precious in your life?

May our Lord Jesus be the treasure above all other treasures in our lives.

And may we set our eyes on the Master of indestructible treasures, not the tyrant of expiring pleasures.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Where does the eye of your heart focus on?

What does your bank statement tell you where your heart is?

Which master’s voice are you answering to? God’s or Money’s?

Friends, let’s set our eyes on the Master of indestructible treasures, not the tyrant of expiring pleasures.

Let’s pray, “Father, we thank you for giving us what’s most precious to you, your One and Only Son, our Lord Jesus Christ. We thank you that you treasure us, you love us so much, that you would send what’s most precious to you to save people like us. Please forgive us for our ungratefulness and our greed for the things of this earth. By your Holy Spirit, please lift us to see the beauty, wonder and glory of Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
