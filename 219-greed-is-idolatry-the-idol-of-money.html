A couple went to see a counsellor one day and they started telling the counsellor what was happening in their lives.

As they were talking, the couple told the counsellor that the man works 15 hours a day, seven days a week.

People would call him late at night and he was looking sick.

“It’s just work,” he said.

And the counsellor said, “Can’t you just cut it down? You don’t look so good. Is there any chance of you finding another job?”

The man replied, “I did that last year, but they offered to raise my pay by 25%. How can I say no to that?”

The man didn’t want to work there, he knew that it wasn’t good for him.

But he couldn’t say no to the extra income.

He wanted to leave, they offered him more money, and he withdrew his resignation.

That’s the power of money.

Give a person money, and they would do what they usually wouldn’t do.

Give a person more money, and you’ll begin to see what they will surrender for the sake of money.

That desire for more money is what the Bible calls greed.

We heard what Jesus said about it over the past two weeks.

Jesus tells us not to store up for ourselves treasures on earth, that is, disposable treasures.

Instead, we are to store up for ourselves treasures in heaven, indestructible treasures.

Jesus tells us to think carefully about where we put our treasures, for where we put our treasures, our heart will follow.

Jesus tells us to have a good eye, that is, to put out eyes on God.

Finally, Jesus tells us that no slave can serve two masters; you will say Yes to your master and No to the one who’s not your master.

You cannot serve both God and money.

Today, we’re going to hear from the Bible and hear what God has to say on the topic of greed.

If you have your Bible there, please open up to Colossian chapter three, beginning from verse 1.

We are going to see three things:

Risen Christians steer minds to Christ, their life

Please open up to Colossians chapter three, verse 1, “So if you have been raised with Christ, seek the things above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God. 2 Set your minds on things above, not on earthly things.”

If you believe in Jesus, then you are someone the Bible describes as ‘risen’.

Christians believe that Jesus has died for our sins.

But more than that, if we believe that Jesus has died for our sins, we also believe that we now have died to our sins.

This is what the Bible tells us in Romans chapter 6, verses 1-2, “What should we say then? Should we continue in sin so that grace may multiply? 2 Absolutely not! How can we who died to sin still live in it?”

A Christian who has believed that Jesus died for them also believes that they themselves had died to sin.

Sin has no say in our lives anymore.

Sometimes, when people refuse to forgive someone else, they would often say things like, “Don’t talk to me anymore; you’re now dead to me.”

They want to break the relationship; the other person is as good as dead to that person.

(Just as a side note, if you are a Christian, do not say that to a person, even in the heat of the moment.

Not only is it a horrible thing to say, but Jesus teaches us that if you don’t forgive others, God will not forgive you.)

However, we can and ought to say it to sin.

“Sin, don’t talk with me anymore, don’t tempt me anymore, for in Christ, you’re dead to me.”

Not only are we dead to sin because Christ died for us, we’re now risen because Christ has risen from the grave!

Look with me at verse 3-4, “For you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God. 4 When Christ, who is your life, appears, then you also will appear with him in glory.”

When we put our trust in Jesus, we die to sin just as surely as Jesus has died on the cross for our sins.

And just as Jesus was raised from the dead, we too are raised to a new life.

Of course, for now, it’s referring to a spiritual death and a spiritual life, because we haven’t physically died yet, nor have we been raised to life in a new body yet.

However, we look forward to the day when Jesus comes back and will appear to the whole world in glory.

On that day, our life will appear with him in glory.

In the meantime, we’re to live a risen life, a life that seeks what’s above.

We are to set our minds, steer our minds, as it were, to the things above, to the treasures of heaven.

That’s where our focus is.

It’s like this.

Who can drive here? Who has a mobile phone here?

Don’t put your hands up, just tell me, should you drive while using your mobile phone?

No! Why not?

Because you’ll be distracted.

I think most people would agree that driving while using the phone is bad.

However, in New South Wales, about 18% of all drivers use their mobile phones while driving.

Even though some drivers might think it’s ok, it’s not ok and it’s illegal.

In fact, between 2017 and 2021, 13 people died and 163 people injured in car accidents in our state because someone was using their mobile phone while driving.

When you’re driving, you must focus on the road and steer your car safely.

For those of us who are Christians, we mustn’t take our eyes off the road to heaven either.

Don’t get distracted for you are steering something far harder than a car.

You are steering your mind.

Steer your mind on things above, where Christ is.

Steer your mind to Christ, who is your life, now hidden in God.

Why do we hide things? It depends on what it is, of course.

If it’s a treasure, you hide it to keep it safe.

You are precious to God, you are God’s treasure, and God keeps you safe by hiding you with Christ in him.

Nothing can take away your life in God, as long as you are with Christ.

We can’t make our way to where God is by ourselves.

Instead, God himself provided the way through the Person of His Son Jesus Christ.

Jesus is the Way, the Truth and the Life.

He is the Way to heaven, and His home is our Destination.

He invites us to walk with Him as he leads us back into his home, that is, heaven, as he leads us to God the Father.

A few days ago, Joe Mock, who came to speak at our Church camp, shared the news of someone who looked after him when he was a student.

Her name was Mrs Norma Dumbrell.

Mrs Dumbrell and her husband Wililam had served God faithfully here in Sydney, Singapore and Myanmar.

She would often have lunch with Joe and his friend over the years.

Sadly, Mrs Dumbrell died last Sunday.

In the memorial sermon, Bishop Peter Jensen asked ‘Where is Norma? She’s gone home.’

Norma walked with Jesus throughout her life.

She steered her mind on the things of heaven, where Jesus Christ is.

Her focus was on heaven, her focus was on Jesus, the King of heaven.

Her life was hidden in Jesus.

And now, she’s home with Jesus.

She’s now more alive that she’s ever been.

That’s the hope that all Christians share.

We have died spiritually in Jesus Christ and we have been spiritually raised in Jesus Christ.

We can look forward to the day when we will see Christ after our physical death.

However, even though we have died to sin, on this side of the grave, we will keep struggling with sin as sin continues to tempt us to go back to sin.

Even though we are risen, sin still tries to get the better of us.

Risen Christians slay the idol of greed

And so, we must slay sin, especially the sin that is the idol of greed.

Look with me at Colossians chapter 3, verse 5, “Therefore, put to death what belongs to your earthly nature: sexual immorality, impurity, lust, evil desire, and greed, which is idolatry.”

We are risen in Christ, but we must continue to slay what belongs to our earthly nature.

There are many sins that we need to think about slaying, but the one that we must focus on is greed.

We must slay the greed, which is idolatry.

The word “slay” is an old word; I used to only hear about it in fairy tales, as knights slay the mighty dragons, for example.

But the word came back to more common use again as teenagers say, “Slay”.

To them, it means “Good job”, or that you’re “Killing it”!

That is, the person had done an excellent job of some sort.

For example, the Bible readers today slayed and the Sunday dish is going to slay!

If you have died with Christ and have risen with Christ, then slay sin, especially the sin of greed, which the Bible calls idolatry.

Greed is one of the most prevalent, if not the most prevalent, sin in our human history.

Greed is everywhere, seen in every culture, practiced cross every generation.

Greed us probably also one of the most respectable of all sins.

It’s respectable not in the sense that it’s good, but it is respectable in the sense that sinners accept and often even admire those who are greedy.

Many greedy people work hard, do whatever it takes, to make money.

In our passage, of all the sins mentioned in this passage, Greed is highlighted as an idol.

Jesus has said in Matthew chapter 6 verse 24, you cannot serve both God and Money.

Here is Colossians chapter three we read Greed is idolatry.

Idolatry is a serious sin.

The second command of the famous 10 commandments is this: Do not make an idol for yourself.

God warns against people worshipping other god, and tells them that if they worship idols, God will obliterate from the face of the earth.

Idolatry is bad.

The Church Father Tertullian described idolatry as the "principal crime of the human race, the highest guilt charged upon the world, (and) the whole procuring cause of judgment".

To worship an idol is to commit principal crime of the human race.

And that’s what happens when we have greed in our hearts.

Our greed for money, becomes our idolatry.

One of my former teachers, the Anglican theologian, Dr Brian Rosner, compares greedy people with religious people.

Instead of worshipping God, those who are greedy worship money.

There are a lot of parallels between religion and greed.

People used to look to religion to find the answers to life’s problems.

Through religion, they want to find out how they can live a long life, to have hope and even how they can have a sense of security.

That’s what people also do through money.

People spend money to see how they can prolong their life and put their hope and security in money.

People would spend a fortune to find the answers to life’s problem, just like others do with religion.

There are people who would go to temples, even Churches, and pray for money.

There was an English presenter who hosted the British National Lottery who offered this prayer on national television: I know I’m a sinner but make me a winner!”

People depend on money, chase after money, look for answers in money and even pray for money.

It’s come from a heart of greed; it’s come from a heart of idolatry.

Dr Rosner then made a very insightful point: “Imagine the response of disbelief in the local church if it were revelated that the vast majority of its members were secretly worshipping other gods. Yet if our analysis of the religion of money is right, the unthinkable may not be so far from the truth.”

I would be horrified if most of us here who come here to worship Jesus actually worship a Chinese idol like Guanyin, or the Hindi idol Vishnu at home.

You should be horrified as well.

But chances are some, if not many, of us here worship the idol of money secretly here.

Many of us may serve money with far more zeal than God.

Many of us might have greed as our real religion.

How do you know whether your religion is greed or genuine Christianity.

If you are a Christian, you are called to love God above all things, you are called to trust God more than anything and you are called to serve God wholeheartedly.

However, greed, like all idolatry, tells us to devote ourselves to money, even more than our devotion to God.

Greed tells us to trust in the security that money can bring, rather than to trust God completely.

Greed demands your absolute allegiance and will not share your loyalty with God.

Dr Rosner summarises these three characteristics we have towards money as: inordinate love, misplaced trust, and forbidden service.

That’s not just for people who are earning money.

You may be a student and still be have the idolatry of greed, if you study instead of loving God, trusting God and serving God, in order to get rich someday.

Greed is a powerful idol.

We must remain vigilant about it in our hearts and in our lives.

Don’t let the prevalence of greed dull our sensitivity.

As long as we live on this side of heaven, greed will try to overpower us.

Greed will try to slay us.

But we must slay it.

If you believe in Jesus you have died in Christ and you have risen in Christ.

In the name of Jesus Christ, consciously, actively put the greed in you to death.

Do it every day if you have to.

Resist the urge to get rich.

For God’s wrath comes against the disobedient – including all who practice greed, including all who surrenders to money.

We say “No” to greed in order to say “Yes” to our service of God.

“Look kids. Your mum and I have been doing some thinking and praying about our holidays. We've decided that we won't be going away this year. I'm really sorry, but:

Since I've lost my job we are just going to have to cut down on our activities. When I find a new job, we'll reconsider things.

We probably use answer c) the most, and sometimes we’ll also hear answers A) and B), but answer D), giving money for Bible college? No so much.

But imagine growing up in a family that chooses to give money away for gospel purposes.

Imagine having parents who say “No” to greed and selfishness and “Yes” to Jesus?

Parents, grandparents, uncles and aunties, be good examples to our children by showing them what it means to love God by slaying greed.

Say No to greed and Yes to God.

For Jesus’ said, no man can serve two masters.

If we can say no to God, then God is not our Master.

If we can say no to money, then money is not our master.

The sin of greed is powerful but don’t let it win.

Examine your heart and by God’s strength, slay the sin of greed

Risen Christians swap the old person for the new

Look with me at verses 8 to 9, “But now, put away all the following: anger, wrath, malice, slander, and filthy language from your mouth. 9 Do not lie to one another, since you have put off the old self with its practices.

More sins for the Christian to slay.

These ones focus on our relationship with one another:

Put away your anger and your wrath against one another, don’t speak filthy words from your mouth.

And don’t lie to one another.

Don’t lie to me, don’t lie to the person next to you.

Why because you have put off the old self with its practices and have put on the new self.

Here, I’ll get some of the KOGKids to help.

KOGKids, let’s make a mess on the shirt today.

Let’s add some colour here and draw something here.

Who’s going to wear it?

Before we became a Christian, we’re identified by the things that belong to our earthly nature.

We are greedy, we get angry for no good reason, we gossip and we lie.

We didn’t deserve to live in heaven.

But the Bible tells us that when we have put our trust in Jesus, we died with Christ.

It’s like taking the old dirty shirt off.

We let go of our old way of life.

And we put on a new one instead.

We no longer identify ourselves as the old person, the one who loves money, the one who depends on money.

Instead, we identity ourselves as the new man, the one who belongs to Jesus.

We have a change in identity.

Look with me at verse 10, “And have put on the new self. You are being renewed in knowledge according to the image of your Creator.”

As a risen person, we’re being renewed in the knowledge of the image of the Creator of the new person; the new you!

God created the new you, so that you will be like Christ.

We can try to use money to buy youth, but God gives his people something far better.

God gives you a new you, a renewed you.

God continues to renew us by His Holy Spirit as we keep coming before his word.

If you are in him, your new identity comes first, the other identities follow afterwards.

Look with me at verse 11, “In Christ there is not Greek and Jew, circumcision and uncircumcision, barbarian, Scythian, slave and free; but Christ is all and in all.

If you are a Greek before you believed in Jesus, you are still a Greek after you’ve put your trust in Jesus.

But you’re a Christian first before you are a Greek.

You’re a Christian first before you’re a Chinese.

You’re a Christian first before you’re an Australian.

Being a Christian will mean more to you than your ethnic identity, and every other identity.

To be a Christian is to know and live in a community where Christ is all and in all.

Christ is all that we long for, the One whom we love, trust and serve above everyone.

Christ is in all- he’s not limited to one race of people but he is Lord to all people.

That’s why I love it when I come to Church, seeing people from different cultures who speak different languages, who represent different ages, whether we’re a toddler or a grandfather, all coming together because our deepest identity is the same: it is Christ. Where in our society will you find a community like this, where different people from different background genuinely loving one another?

Exclusive clubs only allow the elite group of people to go in.

Certain friendship groups might only invite certain people to their parties.

But in the Church, the true Church, is a community of people who have died and risen in Christ.

In the Church, we see a group of diverse people united under Jesus Christ.

Though we are not slaves of money, we are servants, indeed we are slaves, of God the Father, God the Son and God the Holy Spirit.

In Christ, we have swapped our old self for a new self.

Don’t let sin rule in your heart any longer.

Take it off, and put on the new self.

How do we know whether we’ve surrendered to greed?

It will depend on whether you’ve surrendered to Jesus Christ.

If you have surrendered to greed, you will love money above all else, trust money to be your final security and serve money with zeal.

But if you have surrendered to Jesus, you will love Jesus above all else, trust in him as your final security and serve him with zeal.

Remember the three S’s

If you are a risen Christian, then steer your minds to Christ, your life; slay the idol of greed; and swap the old person for the new

Steer, Slay and Swap.

Back on Sep 11, 2001, when planes were flown into the twin towers of New York as part of a terrorist attack,

Rumour has it that some of those on the ground were busy calling their stock broker to sell their shares.

Even as a disaster falls upon them, even with tragedies of injury and pain happening all around them, they were just focused on one thing: money, and how to keep their own money safe.

My hope is that none of us here will see their money as more important than the tragedies that others are suffering.

As 2025 approaches, and as you plan for next year, make sure you steer your minds to goals that will glorify God.

Make if a priority to glorify God first before you think about how you can make more money.

Ask God to help you slay the sin of greed.

Ask God to reveal to you the ugliness of greed.

Ask him to help you take off your old self, and to put on your new self.

Finally, discover more about your new self in Christ by reading the Bible daily, by listening to him, and speak to him daily in prayer, by speaking to him.
