Predictable Christmases

The first Christmas was a surprise!

God surprised the shepherds

God’s surprisingly good news

God’s peace to the surprisingly few

Be marvellously surprised by Jesus, always.

Predictable Christmases?

At this time of the year, many conversations sound something like this…

“Wow, can’t believe it’s the end of the year! Where did the time go?

“Can you believe it’s Christmas already?”

Have you had conversations like that recently?

Of course, we might feel like Christmas has suddenly crept up on us and we’re surprised that it’s already here,

But Christmas Day has been there for quite a long time.

While the very first Christmas Day might had been two thousand years ago, it was first celebrated officially on 25 December 336 in the year of Emperor Constantine.

We’ve had Christmas Day on the 25th December for almost 1700 years!

Of course, there are always some people who are super organised and started preparing for Christmas for months already.

Yes, I’m talking about Shopping Centres.

About 90 days before Christmas, shopping centres start preparing for Christmas.

They would organise Christmas sales, Santa Clasuses and 25 days’ worth of Christmas tunes, 200 hours of it, starting from 1st December.

Whenever you hear Mariah Carey’s “All I want for Christmas” in shopping centres, you know that Christmas is coming.

In fact, the song is so well known that there’s now a thing called “Defrosting Mariah”.

When you defrost Mariah from wherever she’s been and she starts singing All I want for Christmas, that’s the signal that Christmas is here.

Even Mariah Carey herself laughs at the fact by posting a video of herself singing the song, the day after Halloween!

So many things may change in our world, but Christmas Day always come at the end of the year.

It’s always there; it’s predictable.

For some people, it’s a day to look forward to celebrate the end of the year with friends and families.

For others, it’s just the same old; it’s just another day of the year.

For some people, dare I say it, might even find Christmas boring.

It’s predictable; it’s the same, year after year!

For those of us at Church, we usually know the songs we’re going to sing, the Bible stories that we’re going to read.

Our Christmases are predictable!

But that wasn’t what the first Christmas was like.

The first Christmas was anything but predictable!

That’s because the first Christmas a surprise, planned by none other than God himself.

And this Christmas, no matter how many times you’ve celebrated Christmas, God wants you to be surprised.

What’s left to surprise us at Christmas?

How can God possibly surprise us, when we know Christmases so well already?

To see how God can still surprise us, let’s go back to how he surprised people in the first Christmas.

The first Christmas was a surprise!

Look at the excerpt from the Bible that we’ve printed in our booklet.

It came from a book called the Gospel of Luke, beginning from chapter 2, verse 8.

It’s an account full of surprises.

There are at least four surprises here.

Surprise 1: God chose to announce the birth of Jesus to the shepherds.

Shepherds were people who looked after the sheep.

These shepherds were on the nightshift looking after the sheep near the town of Bethlehem.

They weren’t part of the elite in their society nor were they well-known.

They were obscure and unknown.

They would’ve been like the security guards I saw sitting in the Apple store a few weeks ago.

They were sitting in the shop, guarding the precious iphones and mac computer, throughout the whole night.

It’s an important job but you don’t usually expect them to give us any major breaking news, not unless it has something to do with the Apple store.

That’s what the shepherds were like.

If you want to know about how the sheep were going, you might go to them.

But not if you want to find out about what God was about to do in the world.

But of all the people in the world, God chose to announce the birth of Jesus to the shepherds.

Of all the people, in the world, God chose to announce his greatest news to theses shepherds.

That’s surprise one.

Surprise two: an angel of the Lord appeared to the shepherds.

Angels are messengers from heaven who bring God’s message to men and women on earth.

Now, you must remember that this was ancient Israel.

It was utter darkness and then an angel just appeared among them.

It would’ve been terrifying; it would’ve been like a jump scare in the middle of a horror movie.

And so, the angel begins by telling the shepherds, “Don’t be afraid (because they were probably terrified), for look, I proclaim to you good news of great joy that will be for all the people.”

What’s the good news?

“Today, in the city of David a Saviour was born to you, who is the Messiah, the Lord.”

The angel announced that his baby who was born could be described in three words:

Saviour, Messiah, Lord

Firstly, he’s the Saviour.

A saviour is someone who saves or rescues someone else.

The saviour is the person who grabs the hands of someone falling and says, “Don’t worry, I got you.”

One word to describe human beings is the word “fallen”.

When God made the first man and woman, they were in a perfect place, living in a perfect relationship, under the perfect rule of God.

However, when our first parents decided to rebel against God, we fall from perfection, we fall from grace.

The result, we face God’s punishment against our rebellion.

As a result of our rebellion, what the Bible calls sin, we’ve been falling our way into God’s judgement.

If you ever go to Canberra and you have a chance to visit Questacon museum, go to the Free fall ride.

It’s a very simple ride: you climb up the stairs, to about two storey high.

You then hold on to a bar, which is over a very steep slide.

And then you let go.

And for a brief moment, you go into free fall.

It’s usually fairly safe, but it’s also quite terrifying.

As human beings, we’ve been free falling into God’s judgement ever since we rebelled against God.

Just like falling down the free fall ride, we can’t do anything to stop ourselves from landing into God’s judgement.

But God doesn’t want to see us land into judgement.

And so, God sent Jesus to rescue us.

Jesus was perfect and didn’t deserve God’s punishment.

But Jesus took the fall for us, as it were.

Jesus came into this earth, born as a baby, in order to take our punishment.

He was born in order to die on the cross.

And so, from now on, anyone who trusts in him and what he did would be rescued.

Instead of falling to our judgement, we’ll land safely in Jesus’ arms, as it were.

As well as the Saviour, Jesus would be known as the Messiah.

The Messiah was God’s Chosen One, who would rule God’s people on God’s behalf.

Finally, Jesus would also be known as the Lord, which mean Master.

This baby, born in the town of Bethlehem, would be the Saviour, the Messiah and the Lord.

This baby, born in Bethlehem that night, would be the most significant baby ever born.

How would the shepherds know how this baby looked like?

This takes us to the third surprise.

Surprise 3. The baby would be wrapped tightly in cloth and lying in a manger.

You might have expected that a baby who was to be the Saviour, Messiah and Lord would born in a palace.

But no, God chose to have Jesus born in a manger.

The most significant birth of the human race took place in an insignificant stable, in insignificant circumstances.

At least that’s how it looked like from a human point of view.

But not God; from God’s point of view, it was significant; it was magnificent!

So much so that God sent many angels to celebrate Jesus’ birth.

Look with me at verse 13-14 in your handout, “13 Suddenly there was a multitude of the heavenly host with the angel, praising God and saying:

14 Glory to God in the highest heaven, and peace on earth to people he favors!”

The fourth surprise is in the song that the angels sing.

Did you see it?

Surprise 4: Glory to God in the highest heaven, and peace on earth to people he favors.

The angels praise and glorify God to the highest heavens; his glory is to fill the everywhere, even the highest heavens.

But God’s peace is to a very specific group of people: the people who him favours.

The peace of God is more than the absence of war; it’s the presence of God’s blessings and rule among his people.

It’s about have living in perfect relationships.

The Jews’ word for it is Shalom.

It’s a wonderful thing to have, but it’s not for all people.

The good news of Jesus is proclaimed to all people, but the peace that the gospel brings is for those on whom his favour rests.

God’s peace is for those select few whom he wants to give peace to.

This peace of God is not universal; not everyone will enjoy this peace, even in Christmas.

But this peace from God is given by God to those he favours.

This often comes to a surprise to people who have misunderstood the good news of Jesus.

The peace of God is not given by God to everyone.

The offer of peace is there, but it’s not automatic.

We don’t automatically received God’s peace; God’s peace only comes to those he favours.

True peace of God is not something that we can earn or manufacture or buy.

God gives peace, true peace, only to those who he favours.

True peace from God is a gift and he’s given this gift of his peace to us in Jesus.

If we want true peace from God, then we must come to Jesus.

Jesus is the Prince of peace; God’s peace come to us only through him.

Until you have peace with God in Jesus, there is no peace.

Unless you receive Jesus as Lord, Saviour and Christ, you won’t have the peace of God.

How do we know we are among those whom God favours?

Be marvellously surprised by Jesus, always.

You will be marvellously surprised by Jesus, always.

It’s like this.

I was teaching the Bible in the Children Centre on Friday afternoon.

When I was finished and about to leave when one of the children told me that she had a special treat for me.

I was curious and looked at Zoe to see if she knows what she’s talking about.

Apparently, her father gave all the staff of the centre a special treat.

It’s a macaroon; I love macaroons!

I was marvellously surprised by this little girl!

What does it mean for a person to be marvellously surprised by Jesus?

There are four signs:

Sign 1: Christians are always marvellously surprised by who Jesus has done for them.

The shepherds were marvellously surprised by the appearance of the angels who told them about the birth of Jesus.

Christians have even more reasons to be marvellously surprised because of what Jesus did as a grown man.

We’re always grateful for his death on the cross for our sins.

We’re always thankful for the hope that we have because Jesus rose from the dead.

And because Jesus both died on the cross and rose from the grave, Jesus can now offer God’s peace to those who trust in him.

And so Christians are thankful for the peace the Jesus won for them.

How do Christians know that we’re favoured by God?

Sign 2: Christians respond to the good news of Jesus by actively obeying Jesus.

The angels told the shepherds that they will find the baby lying in the manger.

And so, what did the shepherds do? They obeyed!

They said, “Let’s go and find this baby!”

Christians likewise obey Jesus because He is the Lord.

Sign 3: Like the shepherds, Christians will tell others about what God has done.

The shepherds reported what God has told them through the angels.

They told them the good news that the angels had proclaimed and sang to them.

Like the shepherds, we can’t keep God’s good news to ourselves.

We must share it; it’s too good to keep to ourselves.

And finally, sign 4: Christians glorify and praise God for what he’s done.

We keep praising and singing; we will never get tired of praising God, no matter what happens in our lives.

That’s why we sing, not only at Christmas, but whenever we get the chance.

We have peace with God, that’s why we can sing, and be joyful, even when things are tough.

That’s what I mean when I say that we’re marvellously surprised by Jesus, always.

It’s not that we forgot about what Jesus did and so we’re surprised by the gospel, again.

Rather it’s because we can’t believe that for reasons known only to God, he favoured us.

He chose to show us, as obscure and as unworthy as we are, to receive and believe the good news of Jesus.

And when I say that we’re marvellously surprised always, it’s not that we’ll always be happy, that life will always be good for us.

Life will be hard, even for faithful Christians.

But even in those hard times, Christians continue to be grateful for the marvellous surprise that God’s given us in Jesus.

We’re thankful for the peace of God that God’s given to us through Jesus.

That’s why the Good news is good.

That’s what we remind ourselves of each year at Christmas.

But for us, Christmas is more than just remembering the facts about Jesus.

It’s more than just singing the familiar carols.

For us, it’s the peace that we have with God, which comes through Jesus.

When we have peace with God, we respond with a marvellous surprise, always. If we don’t have peace with God, then Christmas will be mundane.

It’s not because Christmas itself is mundane, but it’s because you don’t know the peace of God yet,

It’s because you don’t know Jesus yet.

For if you do know Jesus, if you do know the peace of God, you can’t help but be constantly marvelling at what God’s done for you in Jesus.

You will thank God that Jesus is your Saviour, your Messiah, your Lord.

If you know Jesus and the peace of God, you will have plenty of reasons to be thankful, to be joyful, always.

And so, my prayer for you is this, that you will always find Christmas to be a marvellous surprise,

That you will find the good news of Jesus a marvellous surprise.

My prayer is that you will be marvellously surprised by God.

A restaurant critic once walked into a French restaurant.

The waiter, a friend of the critic, asked, “What would you like today?”

The critic replies, “Surprise me.”

Because he knew that the chef will give him a good meal, no matter what dish it may be.

When we come before God, as we read from his word the Bible, as we come before him in prayer, we can have the assurance of asking God to surprise us.

That’s because we know that God is good and he will give what’s good to us.

We can come with a joyful expectation that he will marvellously surprise us.
