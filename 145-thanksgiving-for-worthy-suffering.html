Thanksgiving for worthy suffering

Passage: Acts 5:40-42

Additional passages: Matthew 5:11-12, James 1:2, 2 Timothy 3:12

Big Idea: Be a worthy and joyful church that never ceases to proclaim Jesus, even when we’re humiliated for his sake.

An invitation to become a joyful Church

A joyful church must prove its worth

A joyful Church never ceases to proclaim Jesus

When two of my children finished primary school, their teacher told them to write a letter to their future self.

It was to be left unopened until the last day of their Higher School Certificate Exam six years later.

I won’t share what they wrote to their future selves, but I can tell you that as a father, I found it very moving.

It was good to see the dreams they had for themselves, and even better to hear their younger selves’ advice to their older selves to keep trusting in Jesus.

I thank God for how he had grown them over the years.

If you can talk with your future self, what will you say?

Will it be, “do you have children yet? Do you have grandchildren yet? Did you finally achieve your career goal?”

You won’t hear from your future self, until your reach that future, be it 10 years, 30 years or 50 years from now.

Now, imagine that, by God’s grace, you get to your future one day.

50 years from now, you open a letter from 2024 that was written to the 2074 you.

How would you respond?

“Yes, I got children and grandchildren. And yes, I did achieve that career goal, but it wasn’t what you think it would be.”

You know, this Church is 155 years old.

It has seen the dreams and aspirations of its members come and go.

Imagine being there when the church was opened for the first time and about 100 people came to hear the first sermon ever preached.

In fact, the doors were originally located behind me; the wall behind me was the front entrance of the Church and the current doors were at the back.

They turned the whole Church around to make it more ‘traditional’ (since Anglican churches traditionally faced the East).

If I could turn back time and have a chat with the Church back then, I would’ve told them, “No, don’t turn the Church around! It’s much better to face the road so people know where the entrance is!”

If someone from the future St Paul’s Kogarah could come back in time and speak to us, what will they say?

Will they tell us that we should’ve done more? Or will they give thanks to both God and the congregation here present for what we’ve done for them?

That’s what we’re going to explore as we come to the final sermon of our Thanksgiving series.

We’re going to look at Acts 5:40-42.

We’re going to see three things:

An invitation to become a joyful Church

A joyful church must prove its worth

A joyful Church never ceases to proclaim Jesus

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Father, we praise you, for from eternity to eternity, you are God. Please humble us as we come before your word, that we might see Jesus more clearly and trust him more dearly, day by day. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

An invitation to become a joyful Church

It’s so good to hear a snippet of many good things that have happened at Church this year!

We had new families who have joined the Church, we had two courses of LIFE Course, Ezekiel has been working at Church as part of his Year 13 experience, we have new SRE (Scripture) classes in school that have grown in Christ, and of course, we have Sunday Dish!

Out of all these, it’s great to see that people are saved from everlasting darkness to eternal life, because they trust in Jesus Christ our Lord.

Turn to the person next to you and ask them one thing that they are thankful to God for about Church this year.

If you are really shy, you can send me a text on Whatsapp and Wechat and our AV people will help us read them out.

You have 30 seconds.

What are you thankful to God for this year?

Over the past year, with the Parish Council’s support, I joined the Reach Australia Leadership development programme to help pastors like me to think about how we can be better pastors.

Together, Reach Australia has helped hundreds of Churches throughout Australia to be better Churches.

More than that, they helped to start many new Churches.

I’ve been going to their conference for three years now and it’s wonderful to talk with so many pastors and trainers.

They shared their good experiences as well as their bad experiences as Church leaders.

As part of the training, there was also a consultant session for our Church.

As part of their consultation, they got data from our Church that stretches between seven to ten years.

They came and visited our Church.

Our Church leaders then met with them, and they sent us a copy of their report for our Church.

The leaders have been looking through this over the last few months as we think about the future direction of the Church.

There are a lot of things in the report, but there’s a simple format: Now, Where, How

‘Now’ is a description of where we are now as a Church.

We look at all the data and ask ourselves honestly how we’re going.

Some of the data was great, others, not so great.

However, it’s important to be honest about them so that we know how we may grow.

Currently, our church is in maintenance mode.

That is we’re surviving, but we don’t want to stay in the maintenance mode forever.

Because if we don’t grow, we will fade and die.

This has happened many times before.

In fact, last week, Jenny and I visited an open home in Cronulla that was a Church from 1924 to 2016.

However, the congregation shrunk and it’s now a townhouse.

If you want to buy the Church townhouse, I can give you the leaflet.

Churches have closed down before, and it can happen again.

That’s not ‘Where’ we want our Church to be

Where do we want to go as a Church?

Of course, when we think about the future, all sorts of good and wonderful suggestions come up:

We want to see more people come to Jesus Christ (always good), we want to

improve the use of our buildings; we want ministries in different languages; we want to reach people of every age.

It was exciting to see our leaders brainstorming and talking about where our church could be.

However, as we plan all these things, it’s important for us, it’s important for me, to remember that ultimately our goal must be this:

It is God himself who leads us home as we follow Jesus on the path of the cross.

It is God himself who has commissioned us to be witnesses who lives to point people to Jesus.

Our destination is heaven – that’s where our Church is going.

I can’t emphasise enough how important it is that we get this right.

For in Colossians chapter 3 verse 1, we’re told, “So if you have been raised with Christ, seek the things above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God.”

Our goal is not here on earth, but in heaven.

Our destination of heaven orientates us.

That’s where the ultimate future the genuine Christians of St Paul’s Kogarah will be.

In the meantime, the closer future self, the one that hasn’t gone to heaven yet, wants to be a joyful church that points people to Jesus Christ.

We want to tell the world that he’s the Lord and that they should believe him.

What will our Church look like?

Look with me at Acts chapter 5 verse 42: “Every day in the temple, and in various homes, they continued teaching and proclaiming the good news that Jesus is the Messiah.”

That’s the kind of Church we want to be, don’t we?

The people of the Church loved to be with one another so much that they meet every day in the temple and in various homes.

Of course, the church building is not the temple so it’s not exactly the same.

But imagine being a Church that wants to meet together, all the time, as often as we can.

Imagine wanting to catch up with each other in each other’s homes, or in parks, or wherever, as long as we meet together.

And it’s not simply meeting up because we like each other, as good as that might be, but meeting together because Jesus loves us, and he wants us to love all those who are his.

That will be an incredible Church to be a part of, a Church that doesn’t get tired of meeting together to tell people about Jesus!

We’re beginning to see a little bit of that in our Church.

Over the past two years, we’ve been running something called the School of Christian Living.

Last year, I’ve planned for us to meet together once a month.

But this year, we’ve been meeting much more.

Basically, we try to meet together as much as possible so that we can get better at reading the Bible and teaching the Bible.

It’s tiring, and at times, we need to stay awake with coffee, but it’s good and it’s so encouraging to see a group of young people who wants to stay after church to study God’s word!

Wouldn’t it be good to be a Church that’s so joyful, so happy, that people will stay the whole day, even throughout the week, because they want to meet together and proclaim God’s word?

Wouldn’t it be good to be a Church that keeps sharing who Jesus is, day in and day out?

A joyful church must prove its worth

Before a church can be that joyful, it must prove its worth.

Look with me at today’s passage, starting from verse 40, “After they called in the apostles and had them flogged, they ordered them not to speak in the name of Jesus and released them. 41 Then they went out from the presence of the Sanhedrin, rejoicing that they were counted worthy to be treated shamefully on behalf of the Name.”

Before they are the joyful church of verse 42, it had to be a Church from verses 40-41.

It has to a church that’s proven itself worthy.

How do we know that a Church’s been proven worthy? It’s a Church that’s willing to be humiliated of Christ’s sake.

Arrigo Brovedani was an Italian fan who supports his soccer club Udinese.

In one of the games, he walked into a stadium of 15,000 fans but there was one problem.

He was the only Udinese supporter.

Arrigo had driven four hours to watch his team play but there was no other supporter there.

The other team’s fans were booing and shouting at the stadium when Arrigo’s team came out.

But Arrigo, though he was the only fan, shouted to support his team.

Not sure which was more humiliating, having only one fan cheering for you, or being the only fan on the stand.

However, the very fact that Arrigo was there, alone, even when his team was losing, shows that he’s a true and faithful fan.

That’s what genuine followers of Jesus are like.

Genuine followers of Jesus are the true fans of Jesus.

We love Jesus; we follow his lead.

Wherever he goes, we follow.

And Church is like the fan club.

When you come to church, you will hear about Jesus, you will see how good He is. We’ll keep talking about Jesus even when we’re booed, even when we’re shamed, and even when we suffer.

Because that’s what a true fan does.

But of course, we’re more than just fans of Jesus.

We belong to his household; we’re part of his family.

We have a bond with Jesus that’s far stronger than the most devout fan club of any soccer club in the world.

Because we don’t want to cheer Jesus on to win any Championship.

Championships last a year, and the world cup lasts only four years.

But we cheer for Jesus, we praise Jesus, because he’s won for an eternity.

Jesus has already won the greatest battle of all on the cross and he will remain the winner for all eternity.

On the cross Jesus saved us from the dominion of darkness into his wonderful light.

And so, we keep praising Jesus, we keep proclaiming Jesus, even when we suffer as a Christian, for

Jesus himself has already told us that we will face opposition and persecutions because of him.

He knows that his most loyal followers, his most loyal fans, will suffer for him.

And yet, he says that they are blessed.

Look with me at Matthew chapter 5 verses 10-12, “Blessed (or happy) are those who are persecuted because of righteousness, for the kingdom of heaven is theirs. “You are blessed when they insult you and persecute you and falsely say every kind of evil against you because of me. 12 Be glad and rejoice, because your reward is great in heaven. For that is how they persecuted the prophets who were before you.”

When you are treated badly because of Jesus, you’re blessed, you’re happy, because you’re on the same team as the true and brave prophets who followed God in times of old.

Suffering for being a Christian is hard, but don’t give up!

Being persecuted for Jesus is evidence that you’re living a good life.

Suffering for Jesus’ sake is proof that God himself has found you worthy.

For God will allow those he considers worthy to suffer for Jesus’ sake.

Even as we go through the suffering, he reminds us by his word that he allows our suffering to prove to us that we belong to Jesus.

By our suffering for Jesus, God shows us that we are the true fans of Jesus.

When the Apostle Paul and his friend Silas were thrown in a Philippian gaol for talking about Jesus, they prayed and they sang hymns to God.

They’ve shown themselves to be true fans of Jesus.

So much so, that later on, when Paul stopped his jailer from committing suicide, the Philippian jailer asked Paul and Silas, “Sirs, what must I do to be saved?”

Paul’s and Silas’ joy in their suffering for Jesus convinced the unbeliever that they were the real deals, that the Jesus whom they talked about was really the Lord of all.

A joyful Church never ceases to proclaim Jesus

That’s what my prayer our church will be.

My prayer is that we’ll be like Paul and Silas, that we will want to be a joyful church that never ceases to proclaim Jesus, even when we’re humiliated, even when we’re suffering.

That’s what I want our future Church to be thankful to God for.

There are many movies about travelling back in time.

People write stories about travelling in time machines and changing the past to change their present.

Time travel make great stories but it’s impossible.

You can’t go back in the past to change your old self.

We can’t change the past to change the present.

However, you can make changes in the present that will affect your future self.

You can make changes in the present that your future self will appreciate.

As a Church, we can make changes in the present that our future Church will appreciate.

Imagine ten years from now, looking back on this date, and thanking God for making our church better than what it was like back in 2024.

Can you imagine being a Church where we’re willing to suffer for Christ’s name.

And not just merely coping but actually rejoicing, being happy, that God’s found us worthy to suffer for Jesus’ name.

Some of us might be thinking we won’t be here in ten years’ time and they might be right.

Nobody knows where we’ll be in 10 years’ time.

But by faith, let’s help build a better future for our Church, let’s do it for the future members of our Church.

For just as others in the Church had sacrificed and made it possible for us to meet as Church this morning, hearing from God’s word, glorifying God,

let’s sacrifice and make this a better Church, a worthy Church, a joyful Church for those who would come after us.

A young man who was part of a rebellion that wants to overthrow an oppressive regime once asked an old man, “What did you do to sacrifice for their cause?”

The old man replied, “What is my sacrifice? I burn my life to make a sunrise that I know I'll never see.”

That’s a true believer in the cause!

And our cause is greater! Our Church lives and breathes the gospel of Jesus Christ.

But will we burn our lives to make a sunrise that we will never see?

By God’s grace, even if we don’t see it in our lifetime, we will see the ultimate sunrise when we see Jesus in all his glory.

That is the future of the Church. That’s the ultimate future of the Church, for 10 billion billion years from now, those who belong to the Church will be joyfully praising God.

We will praise God for we’re counted worthy because of Jesus.

That’s the ultimate future.

What will us, the present Church, do as we wait for that final day?

For the last few years, our vision is: We gather to build and to nurture a nation which lives and breathes the gospel of Jesus Christ.

For a long time I was thinking who is the One who gathers, nurtures and builds the Church. It’s God, isn’t it?

And so, our new vision is this: God gathers us to build and to nurture a nation which lives and breathes the gospel of Jesus Christ.

That’s the overall vision for the Church.

And so, I’ve put together three questions and answers to help us know the updated vision and the direction of our Church.

Question one: Why do we gather as Church?

God gathers us to build and to nurture a nation which lives and breathes the gospel of Jesus Christ.

Question two: How does God grow us as a Church?

God grows us as one body through servants of his word, who equips us to thrive in him, to love his own and to save the lost.

Question three: Where are we going as a Church?

God leads us home as we follow Jesus on the path of the cross, and he has commissioned us to be witnesses who lives to point people to Jesus.

We often wander from the path and so we strive to grow with the following checkpoints along the way:

We want to see 500 households around here become households of God

We want to see a loving community who loves by serving one another.

We want to see language specific fellowships and congregations grow among us.

We want to see more intentionally designed meeting spaces for us to gather in.

We want to have ministries designed specifically for young fathers and toddlers.

We want to welcome everyone who comes within two minutes of coming to Church.

We want to see three classrooms of KOGKids and a thriving KOGYouth ministry.

These are check points of how we’re going, not the goal themselves.

Our goal, our destination, is heaven, where our Lord Jesus Christ is, and we want to point as many people to Jesus as we can so that they can come with us into our Father’s house. That’s our destination; that’s where we’re going.

We mustn’t set our eyes merely on the things of this world.

We serve the God who is from everlasting to everlasting.

For as long as God allows us to be here at this Church, let’s never stop talking about Jesus.

How will the future St Paul’s Kogarah thank both God and the congregation here present?

My prayer is that they will thank us for the suffering and the sacrifice that we made so that they can hear about God and be saved by him.

Will your present-self do whatever it takes to help your future-self to follow Jesus? You can’t change the past, but what you do in the present can and will affect your future.

And so, be a worthy and joyful church that never ceases to proclaim Jesus, even when we’re humiliated for his sake.

Friends, if you have been at Church for a while, you will know that it took a lot of people a lot of hard work to improve the Children Centre that used our hall during the week.

It was hard and humbling to change the St Paul’s Children Centre around.

But God’s been good and it’s now not only viable but also a great place for us to share the gospel.

One of the highlights of the past week was when one of the children in the Children Centre was asked what the best thing was about after school care,

One of the children said, “If I don’t come to after school care, I wouldn’t know about Jesus.”

Isn’t that good?

Hard times will come if we follow Jesus, and so, don’t pray for the hard times to go away.

Pray that we will be thankful to God for finding us worthy to have them.

Even so, pray for those brothers and sisters who are undergoing persecution.

Pray that they will preserve and be faithful.

If y ou are going through a hard time as a Christian, share with other Christians.

The very presence of a good, faithful Christian friend in suffering helps us go through our sufferings.

Here are the three questions for us to think about this coming week:

Have you experienced humiliation for being a Christian?

How did you respond?

How will being humiliated for Jesus’ sake help us become a joyful Church?

And so, St Paul’s, be a worthy and joyful church that never ceases to proclaim Jesus, even when we’re humiliated for his sake.

Let’s pray, “Father, we thank you for calling us to be yours. We don’t deserve your kindness to us. Please forgive us for not trusting you as we ought, especially when we face sufferings that come from being a follower of Jesus. By your Spirit, please make us loyal fans of Jesus, willing to be humiliated so that his name will be proclaimed and glorified. Give us faith to live sacrificial lives so that both the church present and the church in the future will never cease to proclaim and praise the name of Jesus. Father, we pray by faith that you will greatly, exponentially add to your Church here in Kogarah, that through what we say and do, many will come into your kingdom. Establish the works of our hands, Father, for we pray in the name of Jesus, Amen.”

Soli Deo Gloria · St Paul's Anglican Kogarah
