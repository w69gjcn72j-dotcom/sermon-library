Joy: Good news for a doomed world

Passage: Isaiah 40:1-11

Big Idea: Shout with comfort and joy, for our mighty shepherd has won us, his prized, grass-like people.

1. The Lord is coming! Comfort for a doomed people

2. The Word of God remains! Comfort for a grass-like people

3. The mighty Shepherd wins! His comforted people shout for joy

It’s been a hot week, hasn’t it?

Some of us have to go to school, or to work outside.

While others get to work in Air-conditioned offices.

Spare a thought for these Santa Clauses in shopping centres!

Yes, there might be air conditioning in some shopping centres, but still, how hot would it be for them to wear the suit!

But more than that, they had to keep asking the same question to children (and even some adults): What gift do you want for Christmas?

I’m sure some might say things like world peace, but I guess most people would say things like “the latest game console” or the “biggest Barbie dollhouse” or it may simply be “a million dollars; that’s all”.

I’ve never been to one, so maybe you can help me.

What do you say to them? What do they say back to you?

Is it something about whether it depends on whether you’re naughty or nice?

I wonder if anyone has tried to ask him the question, “What do you want for Christmas?”

Everyone asks him to give stuff to them but who gives stuff to Santa?

What kind of gift does Santa want for Christmas?

If I was one of the guys dressed up as Santa, I’d say, “All I want for Christmas is to not wear this suit in summer ever again!”

Kids, I’ll let your parents tell you what they think about Santa.

But what I can tell you and everyone here, is what God, the real Giver in Christmas, wants for Christmas.

Please open up your Bible to Isaiah chapter 40 beginning from verse 1.

We’re going to see three things in this passage:

1. The Lord is coming! Comfort for a doomed people

2. The Word of God remains! Comfort for a grass-like people

3. The mighty Shepherd wins! His comforted people shout for joy

What gift does God want for Christmas?

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Our heavenly Father, giver of all good things, please help us see what it is that you want this Christmas. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”

The Lord is coming! Comfort for a doomed people

Today, is the second Sunday of Advent, and it’s a reminder that the Lord is coming!

And with his coming, there is comfort for a doomed people.

Please open up your Bible to Isaiah chapter 40.

Isaiah is a long book in the Old Testament, with 66 chapters.

For many of the first 39 chapters, God has told his people that they have failed God miserably.

Though as God’s people, they should’ve been obeying God, they disobeyed him, time and time again.

And so, God said he would send foreign forces to punish Israel.

However, by the time we come to chapter 40, there’s a change in tone.

Look with me at Isaiah 40:1

“Comfort, comfort my people,” says your God.

“Speak tenderly to Jerusalem, and announce to her

that her time of hard service is over, her iniquity has been pardoned,

and she has received from the Lord’s hand double for all her sins.”

They were hopelessly trapped in hard service because of their own fault, because of their own sin.

They deserved it! But now, a time of comfort has come for God’s people.

They were doomed and hopeless, but here’s now hope, because her iniquity (that is her sin) has been paid for.

They owed God a payment for what their disobedience against him, but that payment has been paid.

It’s not just paid; it’s been doubly paid.

Whatever sin they committed, their punishment had been doubly paid for.

They’re now free from their forced labour!

It’s a little bit like this:

One thing that gets me nervous as a driver is not what happens on the road, but what happens at the car park.

That’s right, I’m nervous about losing the parking ticket.

On Thursday, I drove Jenny to the airport because she’s going to visit her village with her sister, her parents and her brother’s young family.

Even as I was driving her there, she said, “Don’t park there, just drop me off. It costs too much to park at Sydney airport.”

And you know what, she’s right! It’s expensive!

And so, as I was about to leave, I searched around for my parking ticket.

I always have at least 50 to 100 microseconds of anxiety about losing my ticket.

Because according to rule 3 of the Parking terms and conditions: We may not permit your vehicle to exit the car park until: where available, a valid parking ticket is presented to the attendant and the applicable fee is paid.

I will not be permitted to leave the airport if I didn’t have a ticket.

That’s what happened to the Israelites of old.

It was their fault they disobeyed God but they couldn’t pay back the penalty for their disobedience to God.

They had to stay where they were against their will.

However, in the passage, God tells his messengers to comfort, to comfort his people.

He says it twice to emphasise it.

The people are to be comforted because their debt to God had been paid.

Somehow, God was satisfied with an unspecified payment.

The passage then tells us more about how this payment was made.

Look with me at verse 3, “a voice of one crying out: Prepare the way of the LORD in the wilderness; make a straight highway for our God in the desert.

Every valley will be lifted up, and every mountain and hill will be leveled; the uneven ground will become smooth and the rough places, a plain.”

Someone announces that God is coming, so make way for him.

Clear the mountains; straighten the paths.

God has a destination to get to and nothing will stop him.

But it’s more than that, isn’t it?

Whatever obstacle it is, it needs to get out of God’s way.

He doesn’t just conquer mountains and fill up valleys.

As immovable as the mountains and valleys are to humans, these are moved to make a straight path for God.

God is coming and all flesh, everyone, not just Christians, will see his glory.

Now notice that verse three begins with a human crying out about God’s coming and verse five ends with the Lord speaking.

That’s because the messenger on earth merely speaks what he hears from the Lord.

The Lord spoke in heaven, and the messenger on earth echoes.

The messenger on earth spoke because God’s spoken.

The messenger brings a message of comfort for a doomed people.

And the message is this: Get ready, God is coming.

It is a message of Good News for doomed people.

What makes the message of the Bible the Good News is that it begins with the bad news.

The sinful and wicked Israelites were doomed, but it’s not just they who are doomed.

We, too, are doomed by default.

We, too, also have iniquities and we, too, have sinned against God.

This little thought of greed, that little thought of forbidden lust, and all those little gossips against other people – these are sins that are more than enough to doom us to hell.

Think about it, is there anything in the world worth disobeying God for?

Like the Israelites of old, we’re doomed.

But the good news is that it doesn’t have to end with the bad news: God is coming for his people, and nothing can stop him.

All flesh will see his glory, whether we believe to God or not.

God will come and do something about our doomed state.

But until we see our impending doom, we won’t be able to see the glory of the good news.

It’s hard to hear that we’re bad and doomed; we hate it! But until we understand our doom, we can never understand God’s glory.

And the glory of the good news is so good that it’s, not just good news but it will always be the better news.

It will always be better than any other news that you will ever hear!

Some of the most joyful, most happy Christians I know are those who ironically recognise their own wickedness.

They see their own sinfulness and they thank God for rescuing them out of it.

They know that they’re doomed, but they know that God has a message of comfort for them.

And so, they look forward to Christmas for Christmas is a reminder that God’s has come in the Person of Jesus.

And they’re joyful; they’re happy.

On the other hand, those who only see the wickedness and sinfulness in others, while ignoring their own, are the saddest people I know, whether they call themselves Christians or not.

They refuse to admit their greed, their arrogance before God.

And so, they often harbour a lot of bitterness and resentment to other people, and even towards God.

When all you see is darkness, you naturally become blind to the coming glory of God.

I’ve seen this happened in Churches that don’t confess their sins.

Friends, we mustn’t fall into that trap.

Instead, we must confess our sins and our failures before God.

May God help us to see our doom without him so that we can see be comforted by his good news.

The Word of God remains! Comfort for a grass-like people

We then see the second reason for us to be comforted.

We see that there’s hope even for those of us who are vulnerable and doomed for destruction.

Since the Word of God remains, there’s comfort for a grass-like people.

As you hear these words, count how many times grass is mentioned.

Look with me at verses six to eight:

A voice was saying, “Cry out!” Another said, “What should I cry out?”

“All humanity is grass, and all its goodness is like the flower of the field. The grass withers, the flowers fade when the breath of the LORD blows on them; indeed, the people are grass. The grass withers, the flowers fade but the word of our God remains forever.”

How many times was grass mentioned?

What is grass compared to? Human beings.

The Bible tells us that humans are like scarecrows.

Farmer make scarecrows out of straw to scare away crows and other birds.

Scarecrows don’t last long – they last only a few days in the sun.

In the history of the world, and definitely in God’s eyes, we don’t last much longer than a scarecrow.

Our lives wither and fade away, relatively quickly.

We have a relatively short expiry date.

That doesn’t mean that it’s pointless to be live.

No, there’s goodness and beauty that comes with being alive.

The word translated goodness here is often translated as God’s loving kindness when it refers to God.

God promises his goodness, his loving kindness, to his people forever and ever.

God can promise that because he lives forever and ever.

However, even though we might promise to love someone forever, we can’t because we don’t live forever.

And often, even in our short time on earth, we often fail to be faithful to someone we love for a few years, let alone the rest of our lives.

Our expiry date is short, and our goodness and faithfulness to one another in is even shorter.

Though we might try, we’re often not reliable.

We’re frustratingly unreliable to other people.

Can you imagine how frustratingly unreliable we are in the eyes of God?

How many promises that you’ve made before God have you already broken?

How many of these promises have you already broken today?

Even though we should be trustworthy, even though we want to be trustworthy, our promises, our words, often means very little.

That’s because we’re grass people; we’re temporary, making outlandish promises despite our short expiry date.

But not so God’s word; God’s word can be trusted.

God’s word will remain forever and ever.

Like us. everything that our hands have made will one day turn into dust.

Everything that we own will turn into dust.

However, if we have believed in God’s word and treasured them in our hearts, if we believe God’s word about Jesus,

then we will carry God’s word into eternity, we’ll carry God’s word back to God’s home.

God’s word is the only thing we have here on earth that we can take with us to heaven.

Do you have God’s word in your heart?

Does God’s word have your heart?

As we approach the end of the year, think about how you can treasure God’s word in your heart next year.

Memorise God’s word in your hearts.

One of the most helpful things I did when I was growing up as a Christian was that I memorised the Bible.

The biggest part of the Bible I memorised was Philippians.

I read and memorised about three verses a day and ended up memorising the whole letter of Philippians in about a month.

Now I can’t tell you every verse in Philippians, but I have a fairly clear idea of the context of each verse in the letter.

Every time I go back to Philippians is like visiting an old friend.

If you are on holidays, or even if you’re not, try memorising the Bible.

Get a group of friends to memorise a passage or a chapter together.

It’s hard but it’s good once you have it in your hearts.

You won’t regret it, because you’re memorising something that you will carry with you into eternity, if you belong to Jesus, the mighty shepherd.

The mighty Shepherd wins! His comforted people shout for joy

Jesus is the mighty Shepherd who has won.

In response, his comforted people shout for joy.

Look with me at verse 9, “Zion, herald of good news, go up on a high mountain. Jerusalem, herald of good news, raise your voice loudly. Raise it, do not be afraid! Say to the cities of Judah, “Here is your God!”’

If you have good news, you shout it out.

It’s like this introduction by the actor Matthew McConaughey at the 2005 Daytona 500.

“Alright, Alright, alright. Gentlemen, start your engine”

I am not into car racing but now I just want to get into my Kia and drive down Princes Highway (within speed limit of course!).

Matthew McConaughey was excited by the race and he got everyone excited by announcing its start.

God’s people are excited about God’s coming and there are excited to get to tell others about God’s coming!

Why should they be excited about God?

Verse 10, “See, the Lord GOD comes with strength, and his power establishes his rule. His wages are with him, and his reward accompanies him.”

We’re excited about God because God is mighty and powerful.

The word translated power here is the same word as arm.

It’s God’s arm that establishes his rule.

God’s arm is powerful and it’s by his arm that he wins over his enemies.

Nothing can defeat God’s arm.

What does God get for winning and defeating his enemies?

A trophy? Treasures? Fame?

Look with me at the last verse for today’s passage:

11 He protects his flock like a shepherd; he gathers the lambs in his arm and carries them in the fold of his garment. He gently leads those that are nursing.”

What does God get for his prize? He gets a flock.

For all the strength that God’s displayed, for all the mountains that were levelled and all the valleys that were filled in his presence,

He gets a frail, weak flock, some of whom might even be injured.

Some of them are young or and others have to nurse their own young.

These are the ones who have wandered away from him and found themselves trapped.

God went through the wilderness, he stopped at nothing, to save those who are his.

He did it for his prize, and his prize is us.

The same powerful arm that wins battles and establishes God’s rule is the same powerful arm that protects the weak and the vulnerable

This is what Jesus did, when he came all those years ago, on the first Christmas.

Jesus Christ, the Son of God, was born as a weak and frail man.

He lived through the hardships and the weaknesses that comes from being a human.

He was wrongfully accused and unlawfully executed at the cross.

Those strong arms that made all the stars in heaven were cruelly nailed to a cross.

From a human point of view, Jesus was weak for he was nailed to the cross as a criminal.

But look a little deeper and you’ll see that the arms of Jesus weren’t at their weakest at the cross but they were at their strongest.

He could’ve come down easily from the cross and no one could’ve stopped him.

However, his strong arms held him there upon the cross because he was determined to save us, and nothing was going to stop him.

Jesus, the mighty Saviour, saves us by his outstretched arms.

Are we worth it? No way!

We did nothing remotely close to deserve what Jesus has done for us, and we can never do anything to repay what Jesus has done.

Even if you could live ten thousand life times for his him, it still wouldn’t be enough to repay him for what he’s done for us.

But Jesus saved us with his outstretched arms, because he loves us.

It wasn’t the nails that held him on the cross but his mighty love for his own.

His love held him on the cross.

If you are a Christian, it was Jesus’ love for you that held him on the cross.

That’s why celebrate Christmas with great comfort and joy.

Jesus, the mighty Shepherd, has come to save a people like us.

I remember a long time ago, an influential member of another church (not ours) got really upset with his people in his own Church, saying, ‘our church is full of weak and useless people’.

To be fair, he spoke out of frustration, but I was saddened by it.

Just exactly what kind of people was he expecting to come to Church?

No, over the years, I’ve grown to love and treasure everyone at Church, especially those who appear to be weak people, especially those who appear to be imperfect, because Jesus has come to save them.

Are they frustrating? Yes! Are they often wrong? Yes!

But that’s the prize Jesus won.

Jesus came to die on the cross for these very people.

These are Jesus’ rewards for Jesus’ victory over sin and death.

Jesus loved them and saved them, by his loving and powerful arms.

Who am I to judge them?

No, Jesus’ commanded me and everyone who follows him, to love those who are weak, frustrating, and even those who are a pain,

because Jesus loves them.

He has commanded us to love them as he has loved us.

Do they deserved to be loved? No.

But neither are we.

We don’t deserve Jesus’ love any more than them.

In fact, the moment that we think we deserved to be loved because of how good we are, is the moment we have undermined the goodness of God’s good news.

And so, love them, for Jesus loves them.

And so, what gift does God get at Christmas?

God gets us!

God looks forward to having us at his home in heaven, not because we deserve it, but only because he loves us.

Though we should know better, we still fail; we still wander away from Him.

We must keep praying for ourselves to keep his word in our hearts, and to stay in his mighty, protective arms.

Friends, brothers and sisters, shout with comfort and joy, for our mighty shepherd has won us, his prized, grass-like people.

In Jenny’s village, when it’s time to it, the mother, or the grandmother would often shout out in a loud voice, “Sik fan lah! (食飯啦!),

Legend has it that Jenny’s grandmother’s shout will be heard clearly more than one kilometre away.

Friends, our mighty shepherd has won us, his prized grass-like people.

Let’s shout with joy.

In fact, let’s have a go right now.

Let’s say together, “Alright, alright alright. Ladies and gentlemen, here comes Jesus”

Are you ready?

“Alright, alright alright. Ladies and gentlemen, here comes Jesus”

Now let’s shout it like you mean it.

“Alright, alright alright. Ladies and gentlemen, here comes Jesus”

Shout with comfort and joy, for our mighty shepherd has won us, his prized, grass-like people.

Here are the three questions for this week.

What does God get for sending his Son Jesus to die for someone like you? Are you worth it?

How do you respond to what God’s given you?

What would it look like for our Church to shout with joy at Christmas?

Shout with comfort and joy, for our mighty shepherd has won us, his prized, grass-like people.

Let’s do some shouting this Christmas.

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you for sending Jesus to save a people like us. Thank you for saving by your powerful and gentle arm. Please forgive us for our foolishness, for wandering away from you and heading towards eternal destruction, the doom that we deserve. Please save us from our journey to everlasting darkness and be turned back towards you. If there is someone here now, who hasn’t been genuine in their faith with you, please move their hearts to believe and trust in you, that they will have real joy, a joy that springs from knowing you, and knowing that they are loved by you. Dear Lord Jesus, bring the wandering back, gather the young, lead those who are nursing. Amen.”
