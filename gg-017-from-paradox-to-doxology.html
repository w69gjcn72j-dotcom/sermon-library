Home Groups (adults)

From Paradox to Doxology

Luke 5:1–26 · Sunday 1 November 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

For adult home groups and midweek fellowships · Sermon by Rev David Yung

Big Idea: Glorify God for the incredible Jesus, who recruits the unworthy, cleanses the unclean, honours the faithful friends and forgives the immovable sinner.

Aim: by the end of the study, group members will

see how Jesus recruits the unworthy Peter, cleanses the unclean leper with a touch, and honours the faithful friends by forgiving their paralysed friend;

name where they think themselves too good for Jesus, or too unclean for him, and where they pray safe, vague prayers;

pray one clear, faithful prayer for a friend whose greatest need is forgiveness, and plan with others to bring that friend to Jesus.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

1. Connect (10 min)

  • Think of a time you worked hard at something you’re good at — a project at work, a meal you’ve cooked a hundred times, a job around the house — and it simply failed. How did you feel when someone else then offered you advice?
  • When did you last see something you’d call “incredible” — hard to believe at first? How did you respond?

2. Read: Luke 5:1–26 (CSB)

1 As the crowd was pressing in on Jesus to hear God’s word, he was standing by Lake Gennesaret. 2 He saw two boats at the edge of the lake; the fishermen had left them and were washing their nets. 3 He got into one of the boats, which belonged to Simon, and asked him to put out a little from the land. Then he sat down and was teaching the crowds from the boat.

4 When he had finished speaking, he said to Simon, “Put out into deep water and let down your nets for a catch.” 5 “Master,” Simon replied, “we’ve worked hard all night long and caught nothing. But if you say so, I’ll let down the nets.” 6 When they did this, they caught a great number of fish, and their nets began to tear. 7 So they signaled to their partners in the other boat to come and help them; they came and filled both boats so full that they began to sink.

8 When Simon Peter saw this, he fell at Jesus’s knees and said, “Go away from me, because I’m a sinful man, Lord!” 9 For he and all those with him were amazed at the catch of fish they had taken, 10 and so were James and John, Zebedee’s sons, who were Simon’s partners. “Don’t be afraid,” Jesus told Simon. “From now on you will be catching people.” 11 Then they brought the boats to land, left everything, and followed him.

12 While he was in one of the towns, a man was there who had leprosy all over him. He saw Jesus, fell facedown, and begged him: “Lord, if you are willing, you can make me clean.” 13 Reaching out his hand, Jesus touched him, saying, “I am willing; be made clean,” and immediately the leprosy left him. 14 Then he ordered him to tell no one: “But go and show yourself to the priest, and offer what Moses commanded for your cleansing as a testimony to them.” 15 But the news about him spread even more, and large crowds would come together to hear him and to be healed of their sicknesses. 16 Yet he often withdrew to deserted places and prayed.

17 On one of those days while he was teaching, Pharisees and teachers of the law were sitting there who had come from every village of Galilee and Judea, and also from Jerusalem. And the Lord’s power to heal was in him. 18 Just then some men came, carrying on a stretcher a man who was paralyzed. They tried to bring him in and set him down before him. 19 Since they could not find a way to bring him in because of the crowd, they went up on the roof and lowered him on the stretcher through the roof tiles into the middle of the crowd before Jesus.

20 Seeing their faith he said, “Friend, your sins are forgiven.” 21 Then the scribes and the Pharisees began to think to themselves: “Who is this man who speaks blasphemies? Who can forgive sins but God alone?” 22 But perceiving their thoughts, Jesus replied to them, “Why are you thinking this in your hearts? 23 Which is easier: to say, ‘Your sins are forgiven,’ or to say, ‘Get up and walk’? 24 But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins”—he told the paralyzed man, “I tell you: Get up, take your stretcher, and go home.”

25 Immediately he got up before them, picked up what he had been lying on, and went home glorifying God. 26 Then everyone was astounded, and they were giving glory to God. And they were filled with awe and said, “We have seen incredible things today.”

3. Context

Luke 5:1–26 follows straight on from Jesus’ teaching and healing in Capernaum (4:31–44), as his popularity grows. Luke gives us three encounters. Jesus calls the fisherman Simon Peter after a huge catch of fish; he reaches out and touches a man covered in leprosy, someone the law kept apart from all human contact; and, with Pharisees and teachers of the law watching, he forgives and heals a paralysed man whose friends lower him through a roof. Each encounter is a “paradox” — something hard to believe — and each leads people to glorify God.

4. Discuss

  1. (vv.1–5) Simon had fished all night and caught nothing; now the Teacher tells the fisherman how to fish. What do you hear in Simon’s reply, “But if you say so”? Where are we tempted to think Jesus is good with the Bible but doesn’t know better than us about our work, family or money?
  2. (vv.6–9) Why does an enormous catch make Peter say, “Go away from me, because I’m a sinful man, Lord!”? What has he realised about Jesus — and about himself?
  3. (vv.10–11) Rather than leaving the unworthy Peter, Jesus recruits him. How is this different from the way an employer recruits? What does “catching people” mean, and what did it cost Peter and his partners?
  4. (vv.12–13) What did leprosy mean for a man in Jesus’ day? Why is it so striking that Jesus reaches out and touches him? Who in our street, our workplace or our church feels “unclean” or on the outer?
  5. (vv.12–16) “Lord, if you are willing, you can make me clean.” What does the leper’s prayer teach us about praying clearly, and about God’s will? Be honest: what “safe” prayers do we pray without really expecting God to act? How do we come to know his will?
  6. (vv.17–20) The crowd blocked the door, so the friends broke the roof, and Jesus “saw their faith”. What did faith look like for these friends? What stops us bringing our friends to Jesus?
  7. (vv.20–24) Why did the scribes call Jesus’ words blasphemy? Which is easier — to say “Your sins are forgiven” or “Get up and walk”? What does Jesus prove by healing the man?
  8. (vv.25–26, application) The crowd said, “We have seen incredible things today,” and glorified God. Why do we so often see failures, outcasts and the powerless in church? How could we show the newcomers God brings us the paradox of grace this month?

5. Live It Out

KOG 3·2·1·0 — pray for 3, connect with 2, invite 1, 0 left unloved.

  • Pray for 3 — three people at work, in your street or in your extended family who don’t yet know Jesus. Pray clear prayers for them by name: “Lord, if you are willing ...”
  • Connect with 2 — a coffee, a meal or a walk with two of them this week.
  • Invite 1 — invite one of your three to the Welcome Home — Discover course, to Christianity Explored (in Mandarin), or to our Community Christmas Concert on 13 December (we are praying for 120+).
  • 0 left unloved — at morning tea, find the person on the edge: the newcomer, or the one who feels like a failure or an outsider.

This week’s challenge: When the crowd blocks the door, break the roof. Choose one friend whose greatest need is to be forgiven. With one or two others from your group, pray for them by name every day and plan one concrete way to bring them before Jesus this month — a meal, an invitation to the Christmas concert, a Gospel read together.

Memory verse: “Don’t be afraid,” Jesus told Simon. “From now on you will be catching people.” (Luke 5:10)

6. Pray

  • Praise God for the incredible Jesus, who recruits the unworthy, cleanses the unclean and forgives sinners. Confess where we have thought ourselves too good for Jesus, or too good for his church.
  • Pray clear, faithful prayers according to God’s will: for the households in the Discover course, for Christianity Explored in Mandarin, and for the Christmas concert. Before chasing numbers, pray “Lord, if you are willing” over each household on your list.
  • Pray by name for your 3 — that they would see that their greatest need is forgiveness, and that God would bring them before Jesus.

7. Leader’s Notes

Framing. Joyfully point to Jesus. Guests and not-yet-Christians may be present; never corner anyone. The good news is not “try harder to be worthy” but that Jesus welcomes the unworthy. Let people own their failures and uncleanness without shame: Jesus is not afraid to touch them.

Q1 — Fishing was Simon’s profession and he had just failed at it all night. His “if you say so” is reluctant respect. We too doubt that Jesus knows better than us about our own work and life.

Q2 — No fish a few hours ago, fish everywhere now: not normal, not coincidence, but God at work. Peter thought Jesus wasn’t good enough to tell him about fishing; in fact Peter wasn’t good enough to be near Jesus. He now calls him “Lord”, not merely Master. The irony: to come before God, you must first realise you are not worthy to come before God.

Q3 — Employers recruit the worthy — the right qualifications, experience and attitude. Jesus recruits someone both of them knew was unworthy: the paradox of grace. Catching people means proclaiming the good news of God’s kingdom with Jesus, to save people before they are lost forever. They left everything — they “burned their boats” (Cortes, 1519). Jesus recruited Peter not when he was ready or happy, but when he recognised Jesus as Lord.

Q4 — Lepers were cut off from human contact and had to cry “Unclean” within fifty paces of anyone; Josephus called them “in no way differing from a corpse”. Jesus’ touch is a reverse contamination: he isn’t made unclean; touching him makes us clean.

Q5 — The leper knew exactly what he wanted, and knew it was a gift, not a transaction. If we pray and don’t expect God to do anything, is it still prayer? We know God’s will by his word and by praying; when unsure, pray. Jesus himself withdrew to pray (v.16): make prayer the engine of your walk with God.

Q6 — Giving up because they couldn’t get in wasn’t faith; breaking in through the roof is. “Their” faith is plural — most probably the friends’ as well as the man’s. “When God closes one door he opens another” isn’t in the Bible; this is: when the crowd blocks the door, make your own opening through the roof.

Q7 — Blasphemy is saying something false about God. Only the one sinned against can forgive (if someone stole your phone, I can’t forgive him). Sin is ultimately against God, so Jesus is claiming to be the one sinned against. Both are easy for God, but only the healing can be seen — so Jesus heals to show that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins.

Q8 — “Incredible” is paradoxa (paradox); “glorify” is doxazo (doxology). Every paradox here leads to praise. We see failures, outcasts and the powerless in church because Jesus is willing to heal them: rejected by the world, wanted and loved by Christ. At the cross Jesus — rejected, betrayed, seen as a failure — took our uncleanness and sin so that unworthy sinners could hear, “Your sins are forgiven.”

Our vision. “From faith to faith”: faith is handed on, but it is real only when each of us comes to Jesus as an unworthy sinner and follows him.
