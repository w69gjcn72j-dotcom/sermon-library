One of the most popular movies in the 90’s was Home Alone.

Has anyone seen it?

It’s about a family in America who planned for a trip to France.

However, on the way to the airport, because one of them miscounted, they left one of their sons, Kevin, behind.

The classic scene from the movie was when the mother was trying to remember what she had forgotten.

And suddenly, she realizes, puts her hands on her face and says, “Kevin!”

Poor Kevin was left home alone and had to cook, clean and fight off robbers, all by himself.

I won’t ask you to put your hands up, but have you ever forgotten about someone in your family, and left them behind?

I hope you haven’t; I hope you will always remember your family, especially if you’re going away on holidays!

While I’m sure Kevin would’ve appreciated if the family remember to take him to holidays, staying at home wasn’t all too bad for him.

He had a nice house, he had food, he had all that he needed, except for his family.

However, it would’ve been another story if the family was escaping from gunshots in a war.

It would’ve been horrible for a mother to forget and to leave a child behind in a war.

The child would have little or no chance to survive on his own!

The whole family needed to stick together for safety, and they mustn’t leave anyone behind!

But what if it does come to that?

What if you have a family of six and you must get in a car of five to escape, and one of you must stay behind?

There was no way to fit another passenger in the car and one must be left behind.

Who is it going to be?

Which family would you leave behind?

Would you leave anyone behind?

In today’s passage, we’ll read about a global disaster that happened in history.

Not everyone will make it; some people will be left behind.

Why do some survive through the disaster and others don’t?

The LORD saved Noah his righteous one….and Noah’s family

Firstly, The LORD saved Noah his righteous one….and Noah’s family.

Look with me at Genesis 7:1, “Then the LORD said to Noah, “Enter the ark, you and all your household, for I have seen that you alone are righteous before me in this generation.”

Throughout Genesis, we see God as one who sees.

In Genesis 1, God saw all that he had made and it was very good.

In Genesis 6, God saw that men’s wickedness had spread throughout the world and that every inclination of the human mind was nothing but evil all the time.

And now, in Genesis 7, God saw that there was one man who was good, and his name was Noah.

And so, God saw a good world, God saw a world gone bad, and God saw one righteous man in this bad world.

Now notice that God saw there was only one righteous man, Noah.

There was only one man in the world who was righteous before God. One!

God didn’t say that he had a righteous family, or righteous sons, or righteous daughters-in-law.

God didn’t even say that Mrs Noah was righteous!

God had seen only one righteous man and his name was Noah.

Now yesterday, I sent some late homework out.

The homework was this: Were any of Noah’s ancestors still alive when the floods came?

Did anyone do it?

If we go through the family tree in Genesis 5, then we’ll see that Lamech, Noah’s father, died at 777 when Noah was at the ripe old age of 595.

Interestingly, Lamech’s father, Noah’s grandfather, Methuselah, was still alive!

In fact, Methuselah died in the same year that the flood came, at the ripe old age of 969.

Methuselah, Noah’s grandfather, died in the year of the flood!

But interestingly, the Bible doesn’t mention Methuselah’s name at all in this passage!

It’s possible that Methuselah wasn’t like Noah, because if he was, then you would expect God to mention him as well, but there was no mention of Methuselah at all.

And so, though related to Noah, Methuselah was not a righteous one, like Noah.

Traditionally, thought, the Jewish people taught that he died just before the flood came.

If he did, then he didn’t need to be ‘saved’.

However, if he was alive at the time of the flood and if he didn’t associate himself with Noah, he too, will die, even though he was Noah’s grandfather.

In God’s eyes, it was only Noah alone who was righteous in the world.

God would save him and anyone else associated with him.

It’s only Noah and those associated with him who were saved.

Those who were with him weren’t righteous; only he was!

Even the animals that were with him were saved.

Last week in our Monday Men’s Growth group, Terry asked a great question about the clean and unclean animals.

Why did God ask Noah to take seven pairs of clean animals, a male and a female, but only one pair of unclean animals, male and female?

And why seven pairs of birds, male and female?

Later on, after the flood receded, Noah sacrificed some of the clean animals and some of the clean birds to God, to thank God for saving him and his family.

If there were only two of the clean animals and two of the birds, they would become extinct straight away!

Also, as we’ll see next week, Noah sent birds to find out whether there was any dry land.

If one of the birds don’t return or gets lost, then those birds would extinct also!

And so, the extra clean animals and extra birds were to ensure that they would continue after the flood’s gone.

Notice of course, there’s a strong emphasis on male and female throughout the list.

You need both male and female of all the animals, of all the birds, and of all the crawly creatures and both male and female of humans in order to begin the new world.

Both male and female are needed to continue life in the world after the flood.

And so, the future of all humankind, the future of all the animals on earth, the future of all the birds, rest on their association with one man.

The future of the life of every man and woman, every male and female animal, depended on their association with righteous Noah.

They were saved by their association with Noah.

In time, Noah got two of all the animals, the birds, and the crawly things, as well as the members of his household inside the ark, just as the Lord commanded.

Noah’s righteousness was seen here again in his obedience to God.

Look with me at verses 15-16,

“Two of every creature that has the breath of life in it came to Noah and entered the ark. 16 Those that entered, male and female of every creature, entered just as God had commanded him. Then the LORD shut him in.”

Here, the phrase “the breath of life” reminds us of what God did in creation, when he breathed the breath of life into man and he became a living being.

Here, two of everything with the breath of life in them entered the ark, just as God commanded Noah.

The God who gave them life will now preserve their life.

And God preserves their life by telling them to go to Noah’s ark, for Noah’s ark will be the only place where it’s safe.

When everyone was on board, it was the Lord himself who shut them in.

If someone should ask those on board, “Hey you, why are you saved from the flood?”

They would simply reply, “It’s because I’m with Noah”.

That’s a glimpse of Christian faith.

Christians are saved by their association with Jesus.

I’m sure in that there are people who aren’t Christians who are far more capable people, far smarter people, far more generous people, and even far kinder people, than me.

Just as there would’ve been people who weren’t in the ark who were far smarter, far kinder people than Noah’s family.

Christians, just like Noah’s family, aren’t saved because we’re better.

Christians are saved because we’re associated with Christ, just as Noah’s family were saved because they were associated with Noah.

To be associated with Christ is to believe in him and what he has done.

To be associated with Christ is to belong to him, and to be found in him.

It’s salvation by association.

If someone should ask a Christian in heaven, “Hey you, why are you doing here? Why aren’t you in hell?”

They would simply reply, “It’s because I’m my Lord’s; it’s because I’m with Jesus”.

No one is in heaven because they were good.

No one is in heaven because they tried to be good.

The only people in heaven are those who are associated with Jesus.

A Christian’s association with Jesus starts when they believe in Jesus and what he’s done.

When they start trusting in Jesus, Jesus himself promises to be with them to the end of the age.

God himself promises that he will never leave us nor abandon us.

When we trust in Jesus, God invites us into his family, his household, so that we can call God our Father.

God calls those who belong to Christ his own, and God will not leave us behind.

That’s the comfort that Christians can have – God will never leave us behind.

But for those who don’t belong to Christ, those who don’t trust in him, the danger of being abandoned by God is very real.

Again, we see a glimpse of this in the flood event.

In the flood, we see God’s judgement in de-creation

God’s judgement in de-creation

This is an Extinction Level Event.

Look with me at verses 11-12, “In the six hundredth year of Noah’s life, in the second month, on the seventeenth day of the month, on that day all the sources of the vast watery depths burst open, the floodgates of the sky were opened, 12 and the rain fell on the earth forty days and forty nights.”

It was a complete destruction over all over the Planet Earth.

The floodgates of heavens poured out water from above and the watery depths were burst open from below.

Rain continued for forty days and forty nights.

It would’ve been horrific!

Look with me at verses 19-20,

“Then the water surged even higher on the earth, and all the high mountains under the whole sky were covered. 20 The mountains were covered as the water surged above them more than twenty feet.”

Even the mountains were covered; in fact all the high mountains under the whole sky were covered.

And we read this in verses 22, “Everything with the breath of the spirit of life in its nostrils—everything on dry land died.”

Just as God gave life to human beings and to all creatures by giving us the breath of the spirit of life, God has now taken his breath back from those who died the flood.

Noah’s ark was only place in the whole world where it was safe.

Anywhere outside of the ark meant death.

It would look something like this!

The whole world covered with water, with every mountain covered.

Now some of us who are sceptical might find it hard to believe.

Come on, every mountain, including Mount Everest, covered with water?

You’re talking almost nine kilometres above sea level!

Even if you melt all the ice in Antarctica, you probably get a 70 metres increase in sea level, still a long way off from the 9000 metres needed for what’s described here.

And so, some people simply dismiss the flood as a fairy tale, a myth.

And yet, it’s only fairly recently that scientists have discovered that there was a lot more water in the world that we realise.

Geologists have discovered a diamond that came from 660km below the surface, between the upper mantle and the lower mantle.

It’s a fragment called ringwoodite and it’s saturated with water.

If the minerals in the deep Earth are saturated with water, it’s very possible that they could hold up to 4.41 times the amount of water that we see in all of our oceans today.

If they were all released, there would’ve been enough to cover the whole earth in water, maybe enough to cover Mount Everest!

In fact, this picture that I had shown wasn’t drawn to show what the world would look like in Noah’s flood.

It was drawn by a scientist who thinks that at one stage the whole world was covered with water.

It may be a surprise to the scientists but it shouldn’t surprise those who believe that the Bible is the Word of God, for in Genesis 1:2, we read, “Now the earth was formless and empty, darkness covered the surface of the watery depths, and the Spirit of God was hovering over the surface of the waters.”

The waters were there at the very beginning of creation.

Here in Genesis 7, we read that the waters are there again.

While the Bible doesn’t tell us exactly that these waters are from the mantles deep inside the planet, it does tell us that there are sources, reservoirs, as it were, ready to be released at God’s command.

And in Genesis 7, these reservoirs burst open as God commanded

Every living thing outside the ark was destroyed.

They were all wiped out.

It was almost a reverse of the creation process.

At creation. there was only darkness and water at the beginning.

Then God created the lights, the seas, the land, the birds, the fish, the animals and the humans.

Now, the humans, the animals, the birds, the lands were all covered by water again.

It was a de-creation.

Was God fair to destroy the earth?

God was very fair; he was certainly not discriminatory.

No matter whether you’re wealthy or poor, young or old, male or female, whatever and however you identify yourselves,

If you’re outside the ark, you die.

And God gave them many warnings.

They saw Noah build the ark for 100 years.

They had plenty of time to turn back to God.

But not one of them did, except for Noah, who alone walked in righteousness. Their condemnation was well deserved.

God has again promised that he will destroy the world again.

And once again, God provided the way of salvation.

The path of salvation, the only one path of salvation and it’s through Jesus Christ His Son.

Jesus is the only way.

There is no other name under heaven given to men by which we’re saved.

If you’re outside of Jesus, you will die.

And God remains non-discriminatory:

No matter whether you’re wealthy or poor, young or old, male or female, whatever and however you identify yourselves,

If you’re outside of Jesus, you die.

You must associate with Jesus to survive.

And it must be a direct association with Jesus.

It doesn’t matter if are associated with a Christian friend, or even a Christian Church.

It’s great that you’re associated with them, but unless you are directly associated with Jesus himself, you won’t be saved.

Unless you trust in Jesus personally, you will not be saved.

It’s no good saying that your parents are Christians, it’s no good saying that you’re seventh generation Christian, as good as that is.

Do you know Jesus?

If you don’t, if you don’t personally trust him, you will die, you will perish.

One of the hardest things for Christian parents to see is that their son or daughter turning their backs to Jesus.

The former Archbishop of Sydney Anglicans, Peter Jensen and his wife Christine Jensen are lovely, kind, wonderful Christians.

They have helped countless Christians around the world through many years.

They would’ve brought their sons and daughters up in God’s word from the very first day of their lives.

But their youngest son, Dave, was a rebel.

He would get drunk, he would fight, and he would get into all sorts of trouble.

When he was 20, he got his girlfriend pregnant.

At that time, his father Peter Jensen had just become the Archbishop.

Listen to how Dave says the moment when he told his parents about it,  “My mum stood up and walked into the pantry and banged her head against the wall. Dad just sighed, but then literally 30 seconds later they were saying ‘How is she? How are you? Are you OK? What can we do? How can we help?”

It must have hurt his parents so much, to see their son gone astray.

It would’ve hurt Peter and Christine so much to see their son distancing himself so far from Jesus.

They would’ve known that it didn’t matter that Dave was the son of the Archbishop.

If he wasn’t associated with Jesus, he wouldn’t be saved.

Dave himself admitted that he was a terrible husband and soon got divorced.

Dave then continued to drinking a lot of alcohol as well as getting into a lot of fights.

One day, after waking from a hangover, he was watching a sermon by John Piper about not wasting your life.

He heard it all before but it suddenly dawned on him that he was wasting his life.

He knew that he needed to get back to God.

And so, he confessed and became a Christian.

That didn’t mean he became perfect he next day.

He still had a lot of struggles but he learnt to keep trusting in God.

Today, Dave is a minister and he share the good news of Jesus with thousands of people.

He’s bringing the many people before Jesus, so that they can trust in him, so that they can be associated with him.

He wants them to be God’s own.

I am certain that it would’ve brought much joy to both Peter and Christine.

They would’ve done as much as they could to talk with Dave for many years.

It would’ve seemed helpless and frustrating, but they kept trusting in God.

And God, in his providence, saved Dave their son.

Friends, I know that it can be very hard to share the good news with your family members.

I think for many of us, speaking to complete strangers about Jesus is hard, but speaking to family members is much harder, especially if they are hostile to Christianity.

But can I encourage you to keep praying for them?

It might seem hopeless, just as Dave seemed hopeless.

But God is good.

As long as they still have the breath of life in them, there’s still a chance.

And you don’t have to do it alone.

Invite them to Church where there will be others who might not know them, who might find it easier to speak with them.

There are people from all sorts of backgrounds here who can relate to your family member in different ways.

And if you’re a member of the Church and you see someone bringing their family member to Church, go out of your way to welcome them!

Someone has spent a lot of time praying, convincing, even begging to bring their family member to church.

If you see a new person, drop what you’re doing, and speak to them.

You can eat that morning tea afterwards, you can talk with your friend afterwards.

Drop everything and share the gospel with the new friend.

If you can, encourage them to a Christianity Explored Class.

Don’t let anyone leave the church without hearing about the love of Jesus.

Do not leave anyone behind!

The people of the world had 100 years of watching Noah build the ark.

They would’ve heard Noah telling them about the impending flood for 100 years, and yet not one of them turned to God.

The people of our world had now over 2000 years of hearing about the Good News of Jesus.

We thank God that some have turned back to God, but many have still had their backs turned on God.

If they are not with Jesus, they will perish in God’s Extinction Level Event, they will perish in God’s judgement.

But if they’re with Jesus, they will be safe from God’s judgement.

They will be home with God in heaven.

Friends, God does not and will not leave his own behind.

Friends, don’t leave any of our own behind.

If you’ve got someone in your home who’s not with Jesus yet, pray that he or she will turn to him now.

Don’t leave them behind.

Don’t leave your family in sin; lead them to Christ.

Over the years, we’ve seen parents who led their children to Christ as well as children who had led their parents to Christ.

Yes, sharing the gospel with the family is hard.

But when they do believe, it’s beautiful!

Even now, I am hearing wonderful stories of how some of our Christian sisters have had such a transformed life since they’ve become Christians that their family members are noticing.

Why isn’t she so quick to anger anymore?

Why is she so calm even though things have been so tough?

Even though they might not believe yet, they have witnessed how their family members are slowly becoming more like Christ.

May our Lord be merciful and through our words and deeds, lead our families to Christ.

Don’t leave your family in sin; lead them to Christ.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Do I believe that Jesus is the only one who can save anyone from God’s judgement?

Do I find it hard to speak to my family members about Jesus?

How can I get other people to help me share the love of Jesus with my family?

Friends, it’s only by trusting in Jesus that we are saved from God’s judgement.

There’s no other way.

Don’t leave any one behind.

Don’t leave your family in sin; lead them to Christ.
