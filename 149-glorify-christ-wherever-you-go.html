Glorify Christ wherever you go

Passage: Acts 8:1-25

Additional passages: John 4:9, Luke 9:52-56

Big Idea: Glorify the great name of Christ wherever you go and condemn the great sin of simony.

The Church glorifies Jesus even when it’s scattered

The Holy Spirit comes even upon former enemies

The leaders of the Church must condemn simony even among new ‘believers’

One of the most famous landmarks in Hollywood is the Hollywood Walk of Fame.

Here’s a photo of me and Ezekiel at the Bruce Lee star at the Walk of Fame.

I always thought that it was nice for Hollywood to honour some of their famous stars on this Walk of Fame.

It might be nice, but it’s not free.

In fact, to put a star on the walk of fame, you need to pay $250 for the application.

If you’re successful, you’ll need to pay $75,000 to have your star made and installed on the walk of fame.

It costs a lot to have your name printed on the pavement of Hollywood!

$75,000 might sound like a lot for most of us, but some people will pay for it, because it means that they’ve now made it.

They’re great and they’re worth it.

They’re famous and they’ve got a star in Hollywood to prove it.

How about you? How much would you pay to have a good name?

In today’s passage, we will meet someone who’s willing to pay for a good name.

But was it worth it?

Please open your Bible with me to Acts 8, beginning from verse 1.

We’ll see three things in today’s passage:

The Church glorifies Jesus even when it’s scattered

The Holy Spirit comes even upon former enemies

The leaders of the Church must condemn simony even among new ‘believers’

But before we look into it, let’s pray, “Father, by your Holy Spirit, please open our ears and our hearts that we might by faith see more of the glory of Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

The Church glorifies Jesus even when it’s scattered

Over the past few weeks, we read that Stephen was pointing people to Jesus and telling them how they have failed to recognize how glorious he is.

In response, the people were thrown into a rage and stoned Stephen to death.

Here’s what happened afterwards, beginning from Acts 8:1, “Saul agreed with putting him to death. On that day a severe persecution broke out against the church in Jerusalem, and all except the apostles were scattered throughout the land of Judea and Samaria.”

Saul led the charge against the Church in Jerusalem.

He would go from house to house to hunt down Christians.

Many, many Christians either fled Jerusalem or risk being thrown into prison.

The Bible said that Paul dragged men and women into prison.

Every Christian fled Jerusalem except for the apostles.

It would’ve been a horrible time to be a Christian in Jerusalem.

I still remember watching the film ‘Schindler’s List’ many years ago.

The film was based on what happened to the Jews when the Nazis came and literally dragged men and women, boys and girls into concentration camps.

In one scene, the Nazis would go from house to house, door to door, even cupboards to cupboards, to find any Jew hiding from them so that they could drag them out.

That would’ve been what the Christians were experiencing when Saul came to hunt them down.

There would’ve been thousands of Christians in Jerusalem, all gathered together in the Jerusalem Church.

But now, they were scattered throughout Judea and Samaria.

Any gathering they have now would seem tiny compared with what they had when they were together in Jerusalem.

They might have felt like the growth of Christianity was already over.

However, though it might appear to be a setback, in reality, under God, it was the next step in their growth.

This is what Jesus said in Acts chapter one verse 8, “But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come on you, and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth.”

They were witnesses of Jesus in Jerusalem and now they are witnesses of Jesus in all Judea and Samaria.

Even as they were on the run from Saul, they were telling people about Jesus.

Though they were no longer one big Church in Jerusalem, they were now many smaller churches spread across Judea and Samaria.

The Bible now focuses on one Christian who went to Samaria.

His name was Philip. Philip was one of the seven Christian men who helped looked after the Greek-speaking widows.

Like Stephen, he was chosen because the Church recognised him to be someone full of the Holy Spirit.

Like Stephen, Philip spoke to people about Jesus, that Jesus was the Messiah.

And like the apostles, Philip was performing signs and acts of power.

Those who were paralysed and lame were healed and those who were possessed by demons were set free.

As a result, there was great joy in the city.

When we think about Church growth, we might think about strategies and we plan outreach.

For example, we have vision statements, and we set goals.

We even have Church consultants who come to help us think about how we can do church better.

And there’s a place for that in Churches.

However, we must remember that it is God who leads his mission and that we must follow his lead.

When the Church in Jerusalem was thinking about Church growth, they almost certainly would’ve never thought that the death of their dear brother Stephen would mark the start of a new phase of growth for their Church.

As Saul was hunting Christians down and chasing them out of Jerusalem, they probably wouldn’t have seen it as part of their strategy for growth.

It probably wouldn’t have crossed the minds of the leaders of the Jerusalem church that the gospel was about to expand into all of Judea and Samaria.

Who would’ve thought that these people who fled Jerusalem would continue to proclaim God’s word, wherever they went.

And yet, that’s exactly what happened.

Under God’s providence, they were scattered.

What does God want them to do as they were scattered across the region?

God wanted them to tell people about Jesus.

God wanted them to live such lives among the world that all those around them will see the gospel in them and that they will hear the gospel from them.

They were forced to scatter; they were placed in a situation that they didn’t foresee.

And yet, that’s exactly where God wanted them to be.

And God wants them to tell the people there about Jesus.

Sometimes, we might be thrown in a situation that we don’t want to be in.

Sometimes we end up far from what we had planned for ourselves.

What are we supposed to do when things don’t turn out the way we planned?

Talk to people about Jesus.

This doesn’t apply just on a personal level.

This applies at a Church level as well.

Sometimes, our Church might not end up exactly how we planned it.

It might not be where we want it to be.

However, it’s not our version of great Church that counts but what God considers to be a great Church that counts.

What does God consider to be a great Church?

It’s a Church that talks about Jesus; it’s a Church that glorifies Jesus.

It’s a Church that brings honour and glory to Jesus’ name.

That’s what God wants His church to do, wherever the Church may find herself.

Nissa is a young mother with three out of her four daughters enrolled at her local western Sydney public school.

She used to be shy and not wanting to talk to anyone when she walks into a room.

But now, she’s learning to talk to other parents at school and to actively take part in the local school community.

She says that in order to be a light, you need to be visible.

As she continues to help out in the local community, people soon came to realise that her family is a Christian family.

I know many of us might feel quite shy and not wanting to talk to people, especially if it’s about Jesus.

However, wherever you are, God’s placed you there for his reason.

You may be exactly where you want to be, or you may dread to be where you’re currently at.

But wherever you are, God’s placed you there to tell someone about the good news of Jesus.

Bring the joy of Jesus to those around you; bring the joy of Jesus to your part of the city.

Bring joy to those who are your friends; bring joy to everyone, even those who are your enemies.

The Holy Spirit comes even upon former enemies

What kind of people lived in the city that Philip visited?

Samaritans.

The Samaritans and the Jews didn’t get along, at all.

The Jews consider them to be impure, for even though they had the same ancestors many generations ago, the Samaritans’ ancestors married people from other nations.

They even changed the way they understand the Bible.

Whereas the Jews believed in the whole of the Old Testament, the Samaritans only believed in the first five books of the Bible.

The Jews and Gentiles don’t share utensils, they don’t eat together.

And so, when Jesus was travelling near the Samaritans, and Jesus asked a Samaritan woman for a drink, she said, “How is it that you, a Jew, ask for a drink from me, a Samaritan woman?” she asked him. For Jews do not associate with Samaritans. (John 4:8)

And when Jesus was travelling through Samaria another time, and Jesus wanted to enter their village, the Samaritans refused entry to Jesus.

James and John then asked Jesus whether they could pray for fire to come down from heaven to destroy the Samaritan village. (Luke 9:52-56)

James and John might have over-reacted by a little bit.

Jesus rebuked James and John and they left.

Even Jesus’ disciples didn’t have a good impression of the Samaritans!

And so, when news came to the apostles in Jerusalem that the Samaritans had received God’s word, they might had been a little surprised.

They didn’t plan a mission trip to Samaria; they didn’t send Philip there and yet, they believed in God’s word.

Look with me at verse 14 to see their response, “When the apostles who were at Jerusalem heard that Samaria had received the word of God, they sent Peter and John to them.”

The Jerusalem had to find out for themselves what really happened.

The persecution from Saul didn’t make them leave Jerusalem but the news of Samaritans believing the gospel was enough to make them send two apostles down to investigate.

It would be something like this.

Even today, to say that the Jews and their modern-day neighbours don’t really get along is an understatement.

They are at war with one another.

It’s as if the hatred from over two thousand years ago kept burning even to this very day.

If God in his mercy does bring about the conversion of many Jews in Jerusalem, a Palestinian Christian might ask, “Would God really save the Jews?”

Or if God in his mercy brings about the conversion of many Palestinians in Gaza, a Christian Jews might ask, “Would God really save the Palestinians?”

That’s what the first century Church would’ve said about what was happening in Samaria, “Would God really save the Samaritans?”

When the Apostles went down to see for themselves, they saw these Samaritan believers, and they prayed that they might receive the Holy Spirit.

These Samaritans have already believed in Jesus and they were baptized but they haven’t received the Holy Spirit yet.

The Book of Acts records four episodes of the Holy Spirit coming down upon His Church.

The first was at Pentecost, then here in Samaria, then in the household of Cornelius and finally in Ephesus.

Each of these episodes were key steps in the spread of the gospel.

At the Pentecost the Jewish Christians were filled with the Spirit.

Here, in Acts chapter 8, the Samaritan Christians were filled with the Spirit.

In both of these episodes, the coming of the Holy Spirit marks a new step in the growth of the gospel, first to the Jews at the Pentecost and now to the Samaritans in a Samaritan city.

And all these episodes happened corporately, in a group setting.

Look with me at verse 15, “After they went down there, they prayed for them so that the Samaritans might receive the Holy Spirit because he had not yet come down on any of them.”

The apostles Peter and John laid their hands on the Samaritans, and God answered their prayer by giving them His Holy Spirit.

We don’t know for sure how Peter and John knew that the Holy Spirit has come upon them.

Whatever it was, it was probably similar to what they saw at Pentecost.

The witness of the apostles confirmed that they were indeed genuine Christians, and that they, too, were filled with the Holy Spirit, just like the Jewish Christians.

Now notice that this is not the norm.

In the very next passage we will read that an Ethiopian man became a Christian, but he had no laying on of hands.

Even so, everything in the passage suggests that he also had become a Christian.

The coming of the Holy Spirit upon Samaritans happened at a specific point in the history of the Church.

We should not try to replicate it.

The Holy Spirit is not a power that comes upon you when a bishop or a minister lays his hand on you.

The Holy Spirit is not a spiritual experience or a set of skill sets that you must show to prove that you’re a genuine Christian.

When you become a Christian, the very Spirit of God, the Holy Spirit, comes on you.

How do you know?

You would want to praise Jesus, you would want to glorify Jesus, you would want to serve him as Lord.

It is the Holy Spirit who drives you to follow Jesus and to praise Him.

You don’t need to look for a second spiritual experience to confirm that you’re a Christian.

Don’t miss the big picture of what the Bible is saying here: Jewish Christians now see Samaritan Christians as genuine Christians, saved by the Lord Jesus.

We who were once enemies are now brothers and sisters in Christ.

It will be like Jews and the Palestinians coming together as one.

It will be like Russians and Ukrainians coming together as one.

It will like all Chinese, whether they are Cantonese, Mandarin, Hakka, Fujian, Hunan, Shanghainese, every Chinese dialect coming together, not for political reasons, not because one group triumphed over another, but because we all have one Lord.

We come together because we’ve all been baptized in the one Holy Spirit and we all worship the one heavenly Father.

This is a unity and a peace that the world can’t give; it’s a unity and a peace that only through the gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ.

A few weeks ago, Range and I went to the Sydney Anglican Chinese Ministries Fellowship.

There were about 30 representatives from all over Sydney who serve in Chinese Churches in our Sydney Anglican diocese.

While we do work hard to reach the Chinese, we also had news of our Chinese churches wanting to help the Vietnamese ministries to grow.

We heard about how we’re supporting our Korean brothers and sisters to grow.

And of course, we heard about how we want to support our English speakers to grow as well.

Over the next 12 months, we’ll be working to help our South Asian brothers and sisters to grow.

As Chinese Churches, we’re learning to love across cultures.

We want to love those who are different from us.

After telling his disciples to love his enemies, Jesus said, “For if you love those who love you, what reward will you have? Don’t even the tax collectors do the same? 47 And if you greet only your brothers and sisters, what are you doing out of the ordinary? Don’t even the Gentiles do the same? 48 Be perfect, therefore, as your heavenly Father is perfect.”

Don’t just love those who love you; love those who are not like you, even those who are you enemies.

Who do you find hard to love?

That’s hard to think about isn’t it?

Let me put it another way.

Who won’t you have dinner with? Who won’t you talk with?

Who are you angry with?

God loves even them.

Jesus tells us to love them. The first church also loved them.

If we are following Christ, if we follow the footsteps of the first Church, we’ll do the same.

That’s one way we can tell the true believers from counterfeit believers.

The true believers will genuinely follow Jesus and love their enemies.

The counterfeit believers won’t.

The leaders of the Church must condemn simony even among new ‘believers’

Counterfeit believers aren’t new in the Church.

They were there in the first century Church, even in the first Church in Samaria.

The Bible focuses on a Samaritan called Simon, also known as Simon Magus.

Look with me at Acts chapter 8 verse 9, “A man named Simon had previously practiced sorcery in that city and amazed the Samaritan people, while claiming to be somebody great. 10 They all paid attention to him, from the least of them to the greatest, and they said, “This man is called the Great Power of God.”

Simon Magus amazed people with his sorcery.

Unlike many magicians today, Simon Magus wasn’t doing special tricks to fool people.

He probably did engage with some spiritual powers in the spiritual world.

He called himself to be somebody great; everyone called him the Great Power of God.

Imagine that, calling yourself great, and accepting the title the Great Power of God.

He reminded me of what Cassius Clay, also known as Mohamad Ali, said about himself, “If Cassius says a cow can lay an egg, don't ask how. Grease that skillet. He is the greatest. When I say two, there's never a third. Betting against me is completely absurd. When Cassius says a mouse can outrun a horse, don't ask how. Put your money where your mouse is. I am the Greatest”

Simon Magus would probably be happy to say that about himself.

Simon was the Greatest in the Samaritan city, until Philp showed up.

When Philip came to the city, the people were amazed by Philip and turned their attention to Philip instead.

The people believed Philip’s message and they were baptised.

Even Simon, formerly the greatest, believed and followed Philip.

He, too, was amazed at Philip’s signs and miracles, just like the crowd who used to be amazed at Simon’s own miracles.

When Simon saw that after Peter and John laid their hands people, the people would receive the Holy Spirit,

Simon offered the apostles money so that he, too, can be like them.

Simon wants whoever Simon lays his hands on to also receive the Holy Spirit.

He wanted to give the apostles money in order to become an apostle also, so that he, too, can give the Holy Spirit.

Traditionally, the act of buying or selling something spiritual with money is called simony.

In many instances, it refers to someone giving money to the church in order to have a position in the Church, whether it’s to buy your way to become a minister or a bishop.

This sin of simony is named after Simon: simony.

How did Peter refer to Simon Magus’ offer?

Look with me at verse 20, “But Peter told him, “May your silver be destroyed with you, because you thought you could obtain the gift of God with money!”

In other words, to hell with you and your money.

It’s a very strong rebuke; in fact, it’s a curse!

Simon thought that the Holy Spirit was like some kind of magic and the apostles were just superior magicians.

Simon wanted this type of power so that he can wow the Samaritans again.

He wanted to use religion, he wanted to use God, to make a name for himself, to make himself great.

Peter saw through Simon Magus’ intention and condemned it immediately.

Look with me at verses 21-22, “You have no part or share in this matter, because your heart is not right before God. 22 Therefore repent of this wickedness of yours, and pray to the Lord that, if possible, your heart’s intent may be forgiven.”

Peter said that Simon Magus is to have no share in the apostolic ministry because his heart is not right before God.

There was something seriously wrong with Simon’s heart.

Instead of trying to buy his way into ministry, Peter tells him to beg God for forgiveness.

And maybe, just maybe, God will forgive you.

It’s not often we see this when the apostles tell people to repent.

Peter didn’t say that God will forgive Simon for sure when Simon begs God for forgiveness.

Peter is saying that it’s a possibility that Simon is forgiven and not a certainty.

This leaves the possibility that even when Simon asks for forgiveness, it’s possible that Simon might not be forgiven.

Wow! Why was there a possibility that God might not be forgiven even when Simon begs for forgiveness?

It’s because there’s a possibility that his repentance won’t be real.

Remember, Simon had confessed that he was believer before but his repentance was fake.

And so, Peter is telling Simon Magus not to take God for granted again.

Why? Why would Simon Magus take God for granted? Why would his repentance be fake?

Verse 23, “For I see you are poisoned by bitterness and bound by wickedness.”

It’s as if bitterness flows through Simon’s veins.

Even though on the outside he appeared to be friendly, and even devoted as a Christian.

On the inside he was bitter with Philip and the gospel message.

Philip, the preacher of the good news of Jesus, is getting all the attention he used to be get.

Simon bleeds bitterness and his purposes are wicked.

Even though he wants to be a leader in the Church, even though he was willing to pay a large sum for it, he was driven by wickedness, not thankfulness.

And there are still people like Simon Magus today.

They would give money, even very large sums of money, so that they would be recognised as a spiritual leader.

For them, it’s a small price to pay for the prestige.

Sometimes, the price they’re willing to pay is not an immediate upfront sum of money, but the time and the money they’ll spend on becoming ministers.

Every now and then, people would me and other ministers what we think of certain men who are considering working in a Church.

As much as we would love to have more people to serve as ministers at Church, there are times when we have to say “No”.

For the sake of the integrity of the Church, we have to say ‘No’ to people who wants to become a minister for financial stability or even family stability.

I even heard a minister who was once asked why they entered into ministry and the first thing they said was that they get a discount when they send their kids to a Christian school.

That man does not belong in ministry.

Finding the right leader to serve at Church may be hard, and saying No to potential candidates can be very hard.

But to have a wrong leader at Church is toxic.

To have leaders who offer financial incentives in order to have a position at church is outright disastrous.

It’s happened before, and it will happen again.

Look out for these modern-day Simon Magus.

How did the original Simon Magus respond?

Simon asked Peter to pray for him so that the things that Peter said would not happen.

We’re not sure what happened to Simon aftewards.

Strictly speaking, he didn’t ask for repentance.

The Bible seems to have left what happened to Simon Magus hanging.

What we do know is that after this episode, word of the Lord continue to spread.

Though it would’ve been a hard thing for Peter to say, he had to say it to stop the Church from being corrupted and for the gospel to continue to grow.

And so, how much would you pay to have a good name?

You might be willing to pay a lot.

Simon Magus was willing to pay money to be called great again.

However, the problem was Simon’s focus was on himself.

Friends, don’t be a Simon Magus.

The better question will be this: how much would you pay for Jesus to have a good name.

We’re not talking about improving our brand; we’re talking about how we can tell people about how great Jesus is.

It may involve money, it may involve time, it may involve travel, it may even involve you being in a place you’d rather not be.

What prices are you willing to pay that price for the name of Jesus?

Whose fame is more important? Yours or Jesus’?

Friends, glorify the great name of Christ wherever you go and condemn the great sin of simony.

What has Jesus been doing in your life this week that you’re thankful for?

Tell someone about it!

Glorify the great name of Jesus!

At the same time, watch your conversation lest you praise yourself more than you praise Jesus.

When you get more joy hearing people telling how great you are instead of how great Jesus is, then you must step back, repent and turn back to Jesus.

When you like the attention you’re getting more than the praise that Jesus is getting, then you are walking down the path of simony.

Glorify the great name of Christ wherever you go and condemn the great sin of simony.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What gives the Church a great name?

What would a Church that makes Jesus’ name great look like?

What might the sin of simony look like in how we serve Jesus?

Glorify the great name of Christ wherever you go and condemn the great sin of simony.

Let’s pray, “Father, we thank you for the great name of Jesus. We thank you that Jesus is the name that you raised higher than any other name, that at the name of Jesus, every knee should bow and every tongue confess, that Jesus Christ is Lord. Father, please forgive us for wanting more praises than Jesus himself. Forgive us for any bitterness within us that takes away from the glory of Jesus. Please let us glorify the name of Jesus, wherever you might send us. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
