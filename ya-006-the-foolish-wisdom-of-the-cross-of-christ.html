Young Adults (19–30)

The Foolish Wisdom of the Cross of Christ

1 Corinthians 1:16–25 · Sunday 16 August 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

For young adults (19–30) · preached by David Yung

Big Idea: Don’t be a wise-sounding fool but a foolish-sounding sage, for God is pleased to save all who believe his message of Christ crucified.

Aim: By the end of tonight, we want each person to:

Name where they edit their faith down — on campus, at work, or in the group chat.

See from verses 18–25 that the cross only looks weak from the outside.

Lock in three names to pray for and one person to invite before the end of term.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, phones face-down. Allow 60–75 minutes.

1. Connect (10 min)

  • What is an opinion you would happily post publicly, and one you would only say out loud in this room? What makes the difference?
  • When did you last watch someone get pulled apart online for saying the wrong thing? What did that teach you about when to speak up?

2. Read — 1 Corinthians 1:16–25 (CSB)

16 I did, in fact, baptize the household of Stephanas; beyond that, I don’t know if I baptized anyone else. 17 For Christ did not send me to baptize, but to preach the gospel — not with eloquent wisdom, so that the cross of Christ will not be emptied of its effect.

18 For the word of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but it is the power of God to us who are being saved. 19 For it is written, I will destroy the wisdom of the wise, and I will set aside the intelligence of the intelligent.

20 Where is the one who is wise? Where is the teacher of the law? Where is the debater of this age? Hasn’t God made the world’s wisdom foolish? 21 For since, in God’s wisdom, the world did not know God through wisdom, God was pleased to save those who believe through the foolishness of what is preached.

22 For the Jews ask for signs and the Greeks seek wisdom, 23 but we preach Christ crucified, a stumbling block to the Jews and foolishness to the Gentiles. 24 Yet to those who are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God, 25 because God’s foolishness is wiser than human wisdom, and God’s weakness is stronger than human strength.

3. Context

Corinth ran on clever talk. Public speakers drew crowds, and your social standing depended on which teacher you were seen backing — think influencer culture in togas.

The Christians there were doing exactly that, forming factions around their favourite preachers (1 Cor 1:10–17).

Paul refuses to play. If he wins people by eloquence, he loses them to eloquence. So he leads with the least marketable thing he has: an executed Messiah.

4. Discuss

  1. Verse 18 splits the world in two. What decides which side someone is on, and what does that rule out as an explanation?
  2. Verse 17: how could clever presentation actually empty the cross of its effect? Where do you see that pressure in how Christianity gets talked about online?
  3. Verses 22–23: signs or wisdom. Which does your world default to — “show me it works” or “make it make sense to me”? Which one pulls at you?
  4. Verse 20 asks where the wise, the scholar and the debater are now. What does that do to the assumption that the smartest people in the room have already ruled God out?
  5. Verses 24–25: the same message is foolishness to one person and power to another. If God’s calling is what changes that, how should it change the way you pray for your housemates and mates?
  6. Where do you shrink the gospel down — a tutorial, a work lunch, a group chat, a first date? What are you actually afraid will happen?
  7. The people who look too clever, too religious or too good for the gospel are exactly the ones who need it. Who is that person in your life, and what would it take to say something real to them?
  8. What is one specific thing you will do before we meet again to stop being a wise-sounding fool and start being a foolish-sounding sage?

5. Live It Out — KOG 3 · 2 · 1 · 0

  • Pray for 3 — Three names — a housemate, someone from your course or team, an old friend. Set a daily reminder and actually pray for them by name.
  • Connect with 2 — Two real-world moves this week: a coffee, a gym session, a meal at your place. Not a DM — a face.
  • Invite 1 — Invite one of them to church, to this group, or to ALIVE South Sydney on 12 September. Our 157th Anniversary on 20 September is another easy front door.
  • 0 left unloved — Who has dropped off from this group or from church? Text them tonight. Nobody at St Paul’s should quietly disappear.

Memory verse: “For the word of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but it is the power of God to us who are being saved.” (1 Corinthians 1:18, CSB)

6. Pray

  • Thank God that he chose to save through something the world calls stupid, so that no one can boast — including us.
  • Pray your three by name. Ask God to call them, and ask him for the nerve and the words when the opening comes.
  • Pray for those here carrying study pressure, job hunting or family expectations, that Christ crucified would be what they build their identity on.

7. Leader’s Notes

Keep the tone warm and honest. This passage names something young adults feel constantly — the cost of being publicly attached to an unfashionable message. The good news is that God is pleased to save, and he does it through people who look ordinary.

There will likely be guests or people still working things out. Never put anyone on the spot about their faith or their sin — ask about “people our age” before you ask about “you”, and let people pass.

Brief answers to the key questions

  • Q1: Perishing or being saved — decided by response to the word of the cross, not by IQ, education, or how together someone’s life looks. Both verbs are present tense, so nothing is locked in yet.
  • Q2: Paul is not banning good communication — he refuses anything that makes the messenger the point. The online version: apologetics as debate-winning, faith as aesthetic, or a gospel edited until it survives a comments section.
  • Q4: Verse 19 quotes Isaiah 29:14. God is not against intelligence; he demolishes the self-assured wisdom that keeps people from him. Plenty of very clever people have knelt at the cross.
  • Q5: The message never changes — God’s call changes the hearer. That takes the pressure off you to be persuasive and puts it where it belongs: on God, in prayer.
