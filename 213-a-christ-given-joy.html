Philippians – Making my joy complete

Today, I want to talk about how we bring a smile to somebody’s face.

How do we bring joy to someone?

How do we bring a smile to somebody’s face?

In a time like ours, it can be hard to find things to smile about.

You might tell them a joke; you might tell them a feel-good story.

In today’s passage, the Apostle Paul wants his readers to be happy, but he’s not going to tell them a joke.

He’s not going to tell them a feel-good story.

He’s going to tell them what makes a Christian happy.

That's what Philippians is about.

It's about how to make someone's joy complete.

The Apostle Pauls’s going to tell us what makes Christians smile.

2. The affection of Christ drives prayer

Open your Bible and look with me at Philippians 1:3-4,

‘3  I give thanks to my God for every remembrance of you,[a] 4 always praying with joy for all of you in my every prayer,”

Paul smiles when he prays for them.

A while ago, I went to a prayer meeting which prayed in four different languages.

There was a man who prayed in a language that I wasn’t familiar with.

It was either Assyrian or Aramaic; I wasn’t sure.

But what I was sure of was that he prayed with joy.

I opened my eye during his prayer to see what he was doing.

He was praying with such joy! There was a big smile on his face.

He was smiling when he was praying.

I can imagine that’s what Paul’s face must have looked like when he prayed for the Philippian Church.

I’m sure he would’ve had a big smile on his face.

Why such joy? Why the smile?

Look at the next two verses,

‘because of your partnership in the gospel from the first day until now. 6 I am sure of this, that he who started a good work in you will carry it on to completion until the day of Christ Jesus.”

The Philippian Church is his partner in the gospel.

He led them to believe in Jesus Christ and now they’re his partnering with him to bring the gospel to the people of Philippi and beyond.

Paul smiles every time he prays about his partners in the gospel in Philippi.

Paul says it is right for him to feel this way about them because he has them in his heart.

They’re not just another Church; just another group of workers.

Paul has them in his heart.

He loves them; he longs for them.

Paul knows that these are brothers and sisters who will stick with him, through thick and thin.

And he’s not faking it.

He’s serious about having them in his heart.

In fact, he’s so serious that he calls God to be his witness.

Look at verse 8,

‘8 For God is my witness, how deeply I miss all of you with the affection of Christ Jesus.’

God knows how I long for all of you.

Is this intense longing that Paul has for others a gift that’s unique to Paul?

After all, God wires us differently.

Some of us might be more emotional than others; some of us are more logical than others.

Maybe Paul and other Christians like him are wired to be happy and emotionally attached to other Christians and other people aren’t.

Some are glad to see other Christians while others are not.

Is that why Paul is so emotional about the Philippians?

The Bible doesn’t tell us that Paul was a particularly affectionate person before he became a Christian.

However, the Bible does tell us is that after he became a Christian, Paul is a new person.

He belongs to Jesus Christ.

His whole person has changed.

His thinking has changed to think like Jesus Christ, his direction in life has changed to follow Jesus Christ.

Even his emotion has changed.

Paul longs for the Philippians not with the affection of Paul, but with the affection of Christ Jesus.

It’s the affection of Christ Jesus that drives Paul to love the Philippians.

Paul is moved by the affection of Christ Jesus.

Paul can say, ‘I feel this way because Christ feels this way’

‘I long for you because Christ longs for you.’

‘You make me smile because you make Christ smile.’

The affection of Christ Jesus shapes Paul’s emotions.

The affection of Christ Jesus shapes Paul’s joys, Paul’s feelings.

And so, Paul longs for the Philippians with the affection of Christ.

It also helps him to respond to two difficult situations that he both he and the Philippian church are facing at the moment.

The first difficulty that Paul’s facing is the fact that he’s in jail.

The affection of Christ focuses on the gospel

He’s stuck in jail; he’s being watched by the prison guards.

He can’t walk to wherever he wants to preach the gospel.

He’s really in isolation.

This has got to be bad news for someone who wants to spread the gospel, right?

How can anything good come from this?

Well, everybody throughout the whole palace guard, all those prison guards watching him, know that he’s in there because of Jesus.

People outside know that Paul’s in jail because of Jesus.

As a result, many brothers have been encouraged to speak the word of God more courageously and fearlessly.

Rather than hiding because of fear, Paul’s imprisonment encourages them to go out and preach the gospel.

Many of them are now stepping up to the challenge to preach the gospel.

They preach out of love, knowing that Paul’s put there for preaching the gospel.

However, Paul knows that not everyone is preaching out of a good and kind heart.

Some preach Christ out of envy and rivalry.

Now that Paul’s in jail, they are out in force.

They’re making the most of Paul’s absence.

Maybe they wanted to build up their ministry while Paul’s ministry declines. Who knows?

That’s what’s happening to the church in Philippi.

Paul’s in prison and some people are taking advantage of it.

How does Paul feel?

Look with me at verse 18, ‘What does it matter? Only that in every way, whether from false motives or true, Christ is proclaimed, and in this I rejoice. Yes, and I will continue to rejoice’

Paul says, ‘So what? Big deal! Who cares?’

As long as Christ is preached, I rejoice!

They’re preaching the gospel of Jesus.

People are coming to know Jesus!

Paul’s affection of Christ Jesus helps him to focus on the gospel of Christ Jesus.

It was never about Paul and his ministries.

It was never about how successful Paul would become.

It’s always been about Christ Jesus and his ministries.

It’s always about how the gospel of Christ is proclaimed.

Since Christ is preached, Paul rejoices, he’s happy, he has a smile on his face.

Paul then assures the Philippian church that things will be OK.

God is in control. Through their prayers and through the help of the Spirit of Jesus, things will be OK.

If anything, he hopes that he doesn’t let Jesus down.

He hopes to stay strong to the very end, so that Jesus Christ will be exalted, whether by life or death.

The affection of Christ longs for growth in others

After all, what’s Paul’s life about?

Look with me at verse 21,

‘For me, to live is Christ and to die is gain.’

Paul’s life is all about Christ!

Paul’s life is about Christ and nothing else!

It’s never about Paul’s glory, but Christ’s glory!

It’s never about how this life would make Paul look good; it’s always about how his life would make Jesus look good!

It’s all about him; it’s all about Jesus.

Every aspect of life, every moment of life, belongs to Jesus.

Everything about his life belongs to Jesus.

For Paul, to live is Jesus Christ.

To see his life lived out for Jesus is what makes him smile.

And so, Paul was willing to go through trials, to go to prison; he’s willing to be attacked, to be beaten, to be killed, all for the sake of Jesus.

To live for Jesus brings him joy; to live for Jesus makes him smile.

In fact, to him, death is gain.

If he dies, he would be where Christ is, he wouldn’t have to suffer in jail anymore.

He would be with the One Person that he’s being living for; he would be with Christ.

It would be far better than anything that this world has.

Of course, If he stays living, it would be good; his life would be fruitful.

But to be with Christ is better by far.

That was his dilemma.

What will he choose?

In the end, he chose to stay so that he can stay with the Philippians.

It’s the necessary thing for him to do.

He wants to stay so that the Philippian believers will grow in their faith.

He longs to see their growth.

Look with me at verses 25-26,

‘Since I am persuaded of this, I know that I will remain and continue with all of you for your progress and joy in the faith, 26 so that, because of my coming to you again, your boasting in Christ Jesus may abound.’

He’s staying for them.

He wants them to have the joy that he has.

He wants them to have the affection of Christ.

He wants them to smile because of Jesus.

The affection of Christ in us

How about us?

Do we have the affection of Christ?

If we have the affection of Christ, we will long for four things.

Firstly, if we have the affection of Christ, we will long for the people of Christ.

We will want to see them,

We won’t be content just to see them virtually; we’ll long to see them in person.

While for some of us, it’s not possible just yet, even so, the longing is there.

We long to see other Christians.

We’ll also long to pray for them.

Even though we might not see them, we will pray for them, with a smile.

When you pray, do you constantly pray for other Christians, or is it mainly for your own needs?

Friends, if we have the affection of Christ, we’ll be praying constantly for other Christians.

We will thank God for them, constantly, with a smile.

If we have the affection of Christ, we will also long of the Gospel to be proclaimed.

In a time of pandemic like ours, there’s a temptation just to think about self-preservation.

We want to be safe physically, safe financially and safe spiritually.

While it’s good to stay safe, there are many who may be at risk of getting infected by COVID, like medical personnel and police officers.

There are those who are financially vulnerable because their jobs are affected.

But worst of all, there are countless who are spiritually doomed, because no one’s telling them about the Gospel of Jesus Christ.

They’re hopeless without Christ and there’s no one to tell them that the hope that could be theirs in Christ.

Friends, if we have the affection of Christ, we will tell them the Gospel.

We will have a longing for the Gospel to be preached.

We thank God that over the past few weeks, many opportunities to proclaim the Gospel have been opened once again to us.

We can teach SRE at the local three schools again; we have started a Communion Service in the local Retirement Village.

The next step is for us to open our Church.

Over the past few weeks, we’ve been working hard at preparing our Church to open for public again.

I’ve longed for the relaunch for our Church for the past twenty-two weeks.

Long for that day, when we can all come back and tell the gospel of Jesus.

Finally, if we have the affection of Christ, we’ll long for growth in others.

Paul would rather be with Christ, but he chose to be with the Philippians so that they’ll continue to grow.

Paul stayed and he suffered and he was persecuted, so that the Philippians will grow in Christ.

How about us, what we will we give to see growth in other Christians?

I thank God that over this past few weeks, a number of people at our Church have stepped up to help others grow.

We have people helping with preparing the internet broadcast, we have people recording the Bible readings and the Prayers, we now have four Bible studies happening throughout the week.

Praise God! That’s great to see!

But don’t stop there.

We must keep longing to see growth in others.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Do I have the affection of Christ? Does what bring Christ joy bring me joy?

Do I long for the people of Christ with the affection of Christ?

Do I long for the lost with the affection of Christ?

Friends, may all of us have the affection of Christ, that we’ll long for what he longs for.

May what brings Christ joy bring us joy.

Let’s pray,

‘Father, we thank you for your love for us in Jesus. Father, Jesus has become the Man of Sorrow so as to bring us joy in you. Father, by your Spirit give us the affection of Christ Jesus. Help us to find joy in what brings him joy. Help us to long for that same joy in others. Let us live for Christ and Christ alone, who alone is our joy and in whose name we pray, Amen.’
