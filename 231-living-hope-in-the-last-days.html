On Tuesday, while I was driving back after dropping my children at school, I heard someone asked a Melbourne resident this question:

“Are you worried about raising up your three children, with your spouse.”

This was the morning just before Melbourne goes into Stage 4 lockdown.

Many businesses will be forced to close for six weeks.

Some will not come back.

It is very worrying for many Melbournians.

However, this young Melbourne Dad gave this reply:

““Ah, no, I should be worried but I’m a Christian and I have faith.”

I don’t know this Christian man, but I was very much encouraged by the honesty and faithfulness of his answer.

Yes, he should be worried but he’s not because he has faith in Jesus.

Why would someone who have faith in Jesus not worry when faced with Level 4 restrictions?

It’s because his faith is grounded in a living hope.

Open up your Bible with me to 1 Peter 1.

Hope is in the risen Christ

Over the past few weeks, we’ve been looking at seven doctrines

They are like seven stars to help us see the bigger picture of the story of the Bible.

The first one was creation.

God is the Creator and we’re the creatures.

The second one was on the Fall.

God is the Holy One and that we’re the sinners.

The third is the doctrine of Judgement.

God is the Judge and that we’re the Judged.

The fourth is the doctrine of salvation.

God is the Saviour and that we’re the saved.

The fifth is the doctrine of resurrection.

Christ is risen and we are those who are risen in Christ

The sixth is the doctrine of the Spirit.

God’s sent His Spirit and that His Spirit lives in us.

The seventh is the Doctrine of the Last Things

God’s focus on the Last Day is Christ; our hope on the Last Day must also be Christ.

The theme of Hope comes again and again throughout the Bible.

It’s what God’s people have been doing from the very beginning.

The hope that the Bible refers to is not wishful thinking, hoping for the best for something even though there’s no reason to.

Hope is not an educated guess, trying to make sense of data and numbers.

No, the hope that the Bible refers to is firm and sure.

It’s a hope that’s based on the fact that the historical Person of Jesus Christ has risen from the dead.

The fact that Jesus has risen from the dead is central to the Hope that Christians have.

That is, the Christian Hope is grounded on the resurrection of Jesus Christ.

What does it the Christian Hope grounded on the resurrection of Jesus Christ look like?

If you have your Bible there, open up with me to 1 Peter 1.

Here, we see that Christians have hope as Chosen Exiles

Hope as Chosen exiles

Look with me at 1 Peter 1:1,

“Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ:

To those chosen, living as exiles dispersed abroad in Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia,”

Christians are chosen exiles.

These two words don’t seem to belong together. What does it mean to be a chosen exile?

To be an exile is to live in a country that’s not your own.

It’s living as a stranger in a foreign country and knowing that you don’t belong there.

Have you ever had that feeling?

I’m sure those of us who have migrated to Australia had experienced this feeling in one way or another.

Today, there are over 70 million around the world, including those here in Australia who are known as displaced people.

Displaced people are people who have been forced from home because of conflicts and persecution.

Right now, there are 70 million people around the world who have no place to call home.

They are the exiles, the strangers.

That’s how the Bible describe Christians: Exiles.

But Christians are also God’s chosen ones.

We’ve been chosen by God to belong to him.

Listen to how the Bible describe Christians in verse 2,

“chosen 2 according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, through the sanctifying work of the Spirit, to be obedient and to be sprinkled with the blood of Jesus Christ.”

Christians are chosen according to God’s foreknowledge.

This doesn’t mean simply that he knew what we’re going to do before we do them.

God’s foreknowledge means that he had a special relation with his chosen people even before creation.

God’s foreknowledge is a loving, fatherly knowledge.

It’s like a father who knows his child even before she was in the womb.

Just as a father chooses to love his unborn child, before the child has done anything good or bad,

Our Heavenly Father chose to love us, before we’ve done anything good or bad, even before the creation of the world.

The Spirit of God also sanctifies us, cleanses us.

God the Father chose us, God the Spirit cleanses us, so that we will obey God the Son, so that we obey Jesus.

That’s the goal of the Triune God: it’s so that people will obey Jesus and to be sprinkled with the blood of Jesus Christ.

To be sprinkled with the blood of Jesus is to be cleansed by him, made holy by him.

As a holy people, we’re to obey Jesus – that’s God’s purpose for us in Jesus.

And it’s all God’s work. Look with me at verse 3,

“Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ. Because of his great mercy he has given us new birth into a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead.”

It’s only because of God’s great mercy that we have this living hope.

Just as a baby can’t make itself into being, Christians don’t make themselves into being.

We don’t make ourselves Christians.

When we hear the good news of Jesus and believe Jesus, it’s God himself who brings our new birth about.

We’re given a new birth into a living hope.

It’s a living hope because we have a living God.

Our God is a living God, who raised Jesus from the dead.

The hope that we have is so alive that even death can’t stop it!

For as long as God lives, and God lives forever, we have this living hope.

That’s how sure we can be of our hope in Jesus.

Our hope is that one day, we will live with God in his home in heaven.

That’s our inheritance, that’s what God has in store for us.

When my grandfather (father’s side) passed away, I inherited a watch.

Just before my grandmother (father’s side) passed away, she gave me a watch.

I treasure both watches and they both bring me great memories of my grandparents.

As precious as they are, these watches will break one day; they will perish.

But the inheritance we have from God is one that’s imperishable, undefiled and unfading.

And unlike what usually happens with inheritance, we’ll enjoy our inheritance with the ever-living God.

God will give us our inheritance when he receives us into heaven.

We’ll enjoy God’s inheritance in heaven in God’s presence forever.

But will we last until heaven?

It’s great that God has the inheritance in store for us, but will we make it to heaven?

Look with me at verse 5, “You are being guarded by God’s power through faith for a salvation that is ready to be revealed in the last time.”

God himself will protect us, guard us, until the Day we see him in heaven.

We need to trust that God can take us to heaven.

Can God protect us and guard us until that great day?

Of course he can! He’s the Creator, the Judge, the Saviour, the One who raised Jesus from the dead.

He is God Almighty! Surely he can protect and guard us.

That’s the hope that Christians have! It’s a powerful, living hope in the powerful living God.

Over the past few weeks, we have heard many people with many different opinions on how to deal with the pandemic.

Some say our best bet is on furthering our restrictions to limit the spread of the virus.

Others say that a better bet is to let loosen the restrictions with the hope of that the virus will eventually be somehow controlled.

Some of the opinions are at best educated guesses while others are simply wishful thinking.

That’s not what the Bible talks about when we talk about the hope that we have as Christians.

We have hope because ours is a faithful, loving and powerful God.

We will arrive in heaven, not because of how clever we are but because God is trustworthy.

We might find ourselves in a very difficult, very worrying situation.

But God will get us through. Trust Him!

So firstly, we’re chosen exiles.

We’re also joyful grievers.

Hope as joyful grievers

Look with me at verse 6, “6 You rejoice in this, even though now for a short time, if necessary, you suffer grief in various trials.”

As Christians living on this side of heaven, we will still grieve, we will still feel sad.

If you are a Christian and you love God and your neighbour, you will grieve for the things that are happening around the world.

I don’t need to tell you how hard 2020 has been for many millions of people around the world.

The COVID-19 Pandemic has caused a lot of distress among many people, Christians included.

Even during this difficult period, when everyone in the community ought to help one another, there are still many persecutions of Christians around the world.

Churches were forcibly knocked down; Christians had their houses burned down; Christians have been targeted and killed because they were Christians.

In the terrible blast in Beirut on Tuesday, a number of predominantly Christians neighbourhoods were destroyed.

Christians are not immune from very difficult, very trying times and it is right for Christians to grieve, it is right for Christians to weep.

But at the same time, Christians are called to rejoice even when they’re weeping.

Here, the joy is not a superficial ‘everything-will-be-OK’ sort of joy.

Rather, it’s a decision to rejoice in God, even in the midst of difficulties, because God is faithful, even in difficulties.

Notice the two small but important words in the verse, “if necessary”.

God won’t give us any difficulties if he didn’t think it was necessary.

He’ll only allow these difficulties to happen only if he thinks it’s necessary.

Why would God allow any difficulty at all?

It’s because he wants to refine us.

Look with me at verse 7, “7 so that the proven character of your faith—more valuable than gold which, though perishable, is refined by fire—may result in praise, glory, and honour at the revelation of Jesus Christ.”

God has allowed difficulties and trials to happen in our lives to show the value of our faith.

Since the start of this year, the value of gold has risen by 34% as people think of gold as a safe place to store their wealth.

Gold, as precious as it is, is a perishable product.

Though refined by fire, it’s still perishable.

But genuine faith will prove to be even more valuable than all the gold in the world put together.

Just as gold is refined by fire, our faith is refined by trials.

Though in the eyes of the world, our faith is not very precious.

It’s not even considered as an essential service.

But God sees our faith in him as being far more precious than even the purest gold bar and far more essential than any government official.

God loves it when he sees your faith shine in the midst of a fiery trial.

It’s because he loves seeing you trust in him.

Just as a soldier’s worth is seen in the heat of a battle, a Christian’s worth is seen most brightly as they trust God in the heat of a trial.

One day, when Jesus Christ is revealed as the Lord of all, your faith in him, especially during trials will result in praise, glory and honour.

Angels in heaven will praise God because of your faith in him through the trials.

What’s more, God himself will praise you and say, “Well done, good and faithful servant.”

I know things are hard, I know things are tough, but stay true to God for that day.

Don’t quit on Jesus Christ when things get tough but stay true to him in the trials.

I promise you, and more importantly God promises you, it will be worth it.

As Christians, we live in the tension of being both chosen ones and exiles, we live constantly with both grief and joy.

It’s because we hope, love and trust in Jesus in the now and not yet.

Hope, Love and trust in the now and not yet

Look with me at verse 8, “Though you have not seen him, you love him; though not seeing him now, you believe in him, and you rejoice with inexpressible and glorious joy,”

Trust (or faith), hope and love are often seen as the three pillars of Christian faith.

And they are here in this verse.

We hope for someone whom we do not see.

The apostles, including Peter the writer of this letter, has seen Jesus face to face.

But the readers here, along with us, haven’t see Jesus.

We place our hope in him.

We place our hope in him because we love him.

We love Jesus because we trust him.

Though we don’t see now, we believe in him, we have faith in him.

How can we hope in Jesus, trust in Jesus and love Jesus when we haven’t seen him?

It’s because God’s sent His Holy Spirit and has so transformed our hearts so that when we hear the good news of Jesus, we receive it.

It’s by the Holy Spirit that we’re born again so that we can love Jesus, trust Jesus and hope in Jesus.

What does loving, trusting and hoping in Jesus look like?

It’s an inexpressible and glorious joy.

It’s not a superficial happiness but a genuine, deep-seated, glorious joy.

The Christian commentator Wayne Grudem describes this joy in this way:

“Joy that has been infused with heavenly glory and that still possess the radiance of that glory.

It is the joy of heaven before heaven, experienced now in the fellowship with the unseen Christ.”

One day, we’ll experience an incredible joy in heaven.

It’s a joy that we haven’t fully enjoyed yet.

And yet it’s a joy that we’re experiencing now when we come to know Jesus.

This joy is something that we enjoyed now and not yet.

Now and not yet, is a phrase that’s often used to describe the Christian experience.

We experience now so many blessings that comes with being born again, and yet, we don’t fully experience all of them yet.

We see this idea of now and not yet in the final verse, verse 9, “9 because you are receiving the goal of your faith, the salvation of your souls.”

As Christians, we are receiving the goal of our faith, the salvation of our souls.

And for that we rejoice!

And yet, we are still called to have faith as we look forward to that final day when our faith shall be sight, when we’ll finally see Jesus face to face.

On that day, there will be no social distancing, no restrictions on congregational singing.

On that day, we, along with all the angels in heaven, will praise God with great joy.

What does this mean for us while we wait for Jesus to return?

Until that day, remember that we have a living hope.

Here are four things that we can do:

We have a living hope, and so we

Hope in what lasts

Don’t put your trust in perishable things.

Don’t put your trust in things that will fade in time.

Instead, put your hope in the resurrection of Jesus.

When you think about what to invest your time in, when you think about which house to buy, when you think about what job to work in,

Don’t think like the world, thinking only in how much you’ll get.

That’s investing in the perishables.

Think about how to make decisions and invest in projects and people which will bring about eternal benefits.

What you invest in shows you where your hope is at.

Invest in what building up faith among God’s people; that’s an investment into something that will never perish, never fade.

Rest in God’s power by faith.

When you undergo trials, don’t doubt but trust that God will lead you through.

Rest in his power.

Don’t trust in your own godliness or your cleverness to carry you through, lest you take pride in yourself.

Rest in God’s power.

God’s power is greater than our doubt and more assuring than our pride.

Rejoice in the joy of heaven

I look forward to meeting Jesus in heaven, don’t you?

Rejoice in what God has in store for us.

There will be times in our lives when we grieve, and rightly so.

But remember that this is not the final chapter for God’s people.

Jesus will return.

And so, don’t be overwhelmed by the trials, don’t be discouraged by the difficulties.

Trust that God will take you through to the final chapter.

Rejoice that God is allowing these trials to refine you for that final day, when the true worth of your faith will be shown for what it is.

Love, trust and hope in Jesus

Don’t lose your love for Jesus; don’t quit your faith in Jesus; don’t let your hope in Jesus grow dim.

When you’re tempted to leave Jesus, turn your eyes to him.

Pray that God the Father will give you the power to keep trusting Jesus.

Pray that God the Spirit will continue to make you holy for Jesus.

Pray that God the Son will always be your hope and your joy.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What’s my hope when I feel hopeless?

What gives me joy when I face difficulties in life?

How do I love Jesus more, trust in him more, hope in him more?

Friends, in a difficult time like ours, as chosen exiles, as joyful grievers, find your hope, find your joy, in Jesus Christ our Lord.

Let’s pray,

“Father, thank you that you have given us a new birth in a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead. In him we have the hope of receiving an inheritance that will never perish, never defile and never fade. Yet, Father, forgive us for the times when our love for Christ had grown cold, when our hope in Christ has grown dim. Please strengthen our faith by your Holy Spirit. Fill us with the inexpressible and glorious joy that comes from knowing Jesus. Take us safely to your home in heaven. We pray this in Jesus’ name. Amen.
