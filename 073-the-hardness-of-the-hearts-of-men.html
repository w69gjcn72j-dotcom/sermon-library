The Hardness of the hearts of men

Mark 10:1-16

Big Idea: Hard hearts tear apart what God’s joined; only child-like hearts can receive God’s Kingdom.

Hard Hearts tear apart what God’s joined together

Facing the hardness of hearts in marriage breakdowns

Only child-like hearts can receive God’s Kingdom

Intro:

In 2019, a movie came out called Marriage Story.

Though the name sounds like it’s a story about marriage, it’s actually a story about divorce.

It starts with the couple talking to a counsellor about their difficulties in marriage.

Throughout the movie, you see that they still love each other but after a series of events, they eventually decided to divorce.

It’s a beautifully moving movie and well acted.

Even towards the end, you think that they’ll get back together but they chose to part ways.

Marriage story was a tragic divorce story.

What does the Bible say about broken marriages?

What does God have to say about broken marriages in our community, among my friends and even in my own family?

Some of us here or at home might know someone who has gone through a divorce.

Some might have parents and even children who have gone through divorce.

Sadly, I have people whom I cherish, people whom I consider to be closest friends, who have gone through divorce.

What they went through still saddens me, even to this day.

Some people respond to divorces by ignoring it.

Others say that it’s the couples’ business and we should just stay out of it.

While we do want to respect a couple’s privacy, we also need to hear what God says about divorce.

While there’s a sense that divorce mainly affects the couple in question, it has an impact on everyone who knows the couple.

More than that, God has expressed his opinion on the couple’s divorce or their intention on divorce, and they need to hear it.

We will see God’s opinion in the words of Jesus in Mark 10:1-16.

We will see three points.

Hard Hearts tear apart what God’s joined together

Facing the hardness of hearts in marriage breakdowns

Only child-like hearts can receive God’s Kingdom

Today’s passage may raise a lot of questions and issues that I might not cover during the sermon.

And so, I’ll be available after Church to speak with those who might have further questions.

For those of us who are facing this issue, or has someone close who is facing this issue, please hear that we are here to care for you and not to judge you.

And so before we begin, let’s pray that God will lead us, confront us and comfort us as we read his word.

“Father, we thank you for your love for us, despite all our imperfections. Please help us to understand your word on marriage and divorce. Please prepare our hearts to obey your word. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”

Hard hearts tear apart what God has joined together

If you have your Bible there, please open up to Mark 10

Jesus is once again teaching the crowd when the Pharisees came to him.

Look with me at verse 2, “Some Pharisees came to test him, asking, “Is it lawful for a man to divorce his wife?”

This was a loaded question!

The Pharisees came to test Jesus with their question on divorce.

How is it a trick question?

Well, not long before this, Jesus’ cousin, John the Baptist, spoke against one of the rulers about his divorce.

The ruler in question was Herod Antipas, who divorced his wife, Princesss Aretas, to marry Herodias, his brother’s wife.

John the Baptist had told Herod Antipas that it was not lawful for him to have his brother’s wife.

The result? John the Baptist was eventually beheaded.

The Pharisees now ask a more general question on divorce but one which can potentially lead to the same result.

Is it lawful for a man to divorce his wife?

A sneaky, loaded question.

It’s a loaded question then and it can still be a loaded question today.

A few years ago, when we could still meet together, many representatives of Anglican Churches all around Sydney got together to talk about divorce and remarriage.

It generated a lot of responses.

Some people were saying that we should make it easier for couples to divorce while others say that we shouldn’t make it too easy, or words to that effect.

As a minister, I’ve been approached a number of times about the issue of divorce.

Generally speaking, no one thinks that divorces are a good but they do ask, is it right for me, in my circumstance, to divorce?

It’s what the Pharisees ask, “Is it lawful?”

How does Jesus respond?

He refers to the Bible. “What did Moses command you?”

To which the Pharisees reply, “Moses permitted us to write divorce papers and send her away.”

And they’re half right, because Moses does permit divorce in Deuteronomy 24.

But they’re only half right because the reason why Moses gave them permission to divorce was the hardness of their hearts.

Because of their stubbornness and selfishness, Moses permitted them to divorce, though, that wasn’t part of God’s original intention for marriage.

God was the one who designed and made marriage.

When God made marriage in the beginning, there was no provision for divorce.

Listen to what Jesus says from verse 6, “But from the beginning of creation God made them male and female. 7 For this reason a man will leave his father and mother 8 and the two will become one flesh. So they are no longer two, but one flesh.”

When God made them male and female and put them together, when God married them, they were to become one.

They become a new unit.

They are no longer two, but one flesh.

They are one body.

That’s what God made them to be.

And so, Jesus says, in verse 9, “Therefore what God has joined together, let no one separate.”

Marriage is something that God has put together; man (and woman) shouldn’t tear it apart.

It’s like this.

I’ve got a picture here of a man and a woman.

I’ve stuck them together – it’s now one.

Marriages are like that.

God has joined the man and the woman together in marriage.

And they are joined at every level; they belong to each other.

They are now a new social unit together.

That’s how God made them: a married couple.

But when divorce happens, when a man and a woman choose to part ways, they want to unmake marriage.

They are ripping apart what God has joined together.

And so, Jesus’ implication is clear.

Don’t let the hardness of your hearts take over.

Don’t divorce.

Don’t tear apart what God has put together.

Facing the hardness of hearts in marriage breakdowns

To stay married and not divorce is what God had intended for marriage.

But we live in a fallen and imperfect world where everything, including marriage, has been stained by sin.

Even though we like happy marriages, divorces still happen.

In fact, the latest figures show that in 2019, there were 49,116 divorces in Australia.

That’s almost 100,000 people who had gone through the pain of divorce in that year alone.

Sadly, there are indications that over the past 12 months, because of COVID-19 lockdowns, the divorce rate in Australia will go climb even higher.

That’s not including those who are living together in de facto relationships.

People who live together but not married have a much higher breakup rate.

What does Jesus have to say about those who have chosen to take the path of divorce?

Look with me at verses 11-12, “He said to them, “Whoever divorces his wife and marries another commits adultery against her. 12 Also, if she divorces her husband and marries another, she commits adultery.”

The man who divorces his wife commits adultery against her.

To commit adultery is to be unfaithful to your spouse.

At the very least, it certainly means committing sexual sins against your spouse.

That is, when you have sexual relationship with anyone who is not your spouse, you are committing adultery against your wife.

Jesus also teaches elsewhere in Matthew 5 that committing adultery is not limited only to physical, sexual intimacy.

He says that if you look at a woman lustfully, you have already committed adultery with her in your heart.

The Bible has a very, very high view of marriage.

It’s the relationship where you can enjoy love and safety, knowing that your spouse will be faithful to you and that you will be faithful to them.

That’s what marriage is – it’s a solemn pledge to be faithful and pure.

It’s a high calling.

When a man divorces his wife, he breaks his promise to his wife, he fails to be faithful to her, he commits adultery against her.

I’m not talking about forgetting to do the chores or having the occasional fights.

Being married doesn’t make you perfect.

Being an imperfect man or woman means that you will be an imperfect husband or wife.

No, Jesus doesn’t mean that being in a difficult marriage is cause enough for divorce.

It’s when someone is habitually, constantly unfaithful to his wife, often sinning against her by his fascination with someone or even something else besides his wife, that he approaches the sin of adultery.

This is especially the case when he divorces his wife to marry another woman.

In the ancient world, as in many parts of the modern world, a person wants to divorce in order to marry somebody else.

It might be presented as a romantic story in today’s world, or in a pagan’s world, but in God’s eyes, when someone divorces his wife and marries another, he commits adultery.

This also happens with the woman as well.

If a woman divorces her husband and marries another, she commits adultery.

And it’s not merely a change in life partners.

To commit adultery, to be unfaithful to your spouse, is a serious sin.

The Bible tells us in 1 Corinthians 6, beginning from verse 9, “9 Don’t you know that the unrighteous will not inherit God’s kingdom? Do not be deceived: No sexually immoral people, idolaters, adulterers, or males who have sex with males, 10 no thieves, greedy people, drunkards, verbally abusive people, or swindlers will inherit God’s kingdom.”

We live in communities that are so used to sexual immorality, idolatry, adultery, lies, that we forget how serious these sins are.

These people will not enter God’s kingdom.

They are banned from God’s kingdom.

And so, if divorce leads to adultery, then don’t divorce!

However, at this stage, I must speak about another sin which ties in with this.

I’m talking about the sin of domestic violence.

There is no justification for any man or woman to be violent towards their spouse.

The Bible strongly condemns it.

There is no place for threats, intimidation or violence in marriage.

Marriage should be a safe place where both the husband and the wife can experience loving care.

While both the husband and the wife contribute to this, it is especially the husband’s responsibility to ensure that the marriage is safe.

There is no excuse for domination or control.

There is no place for abuse in marriage.

I’m not just talking about physical abuse, like hitting, punching and breaking things deliberately, but also sexual abuse, financial abuse, spiritual abuse and intellectual abuse.

Abusers have no place in God’s kingdom.

There will be times when the abuser shows remorse and even ask for forgiveness but then they go back to the cycle of violence.

While forgiveness is important and it’s commanded by Jesus, there may be times when the abused spouse is taken to safety.

Sometimes the separation may be prolonged for the sake of the abused spouse and their children, if they have any.

Christians are not required to endure domestic abuse.

This is where the Church can shine as a wonderful witness of God’s love.

The Church needs to be what God has called us to be, namely, a loving community saved by the Lord Jesus,

A nation that lives and breathes the gospel of the Lord Jesus Christ.

We need to encourage support to grow and nurture strong marriages as well as helping those who had broken marriages.

We also need to help those who are single.

We need to help people see that their identity is not based on whether they have a happy marriage or not.

Having a happy marriage is great but even more important that a happy marriage is whether the person is holy and faithful to God.

As a community of believers, we need to help each other see that.

We need to spur and encourage one another to be holy and faithful, whether they are single, separated or married.

In my old Church, I had the opportunity, no, the blessing to meet a young man.

Let’s call him John.

John’s parents were divorced when John was a young boy.

Understandably, he was frustrated at his father, who left his mother, him, and his younger brother behind.

But John’s family kept coming to Church.

John’s mother was loved by the community and she’s been an active member of the Church for many years.

John himself grew up trusting in the Lord Jesus in a loving community of believers.

I was at John’s wedding a few years ago and it was like watching a son getting married.

I was so happy to see him married.

Though he was from a broken family, he grew up in God’s family and now he has a new family of his own.

The hard heartedness of his biological father didn’t stop him from being loved by his heavenly father.

I thank God for the loving family that John grew up in.

That’s what all churches, every community of God’s people, including our own here at St Paul’s Kogarah are called to be.

We’re called to be a loving community where people’s hard-heartedness is confronted and where those broken by men’s hard-heartedness can find healing in the gospel of Jesus Christ.

How does someone receive this healing that comes from the gospel of Jesus Christ?

Only child-like hearts can receive God’s Kingdom

Only people who have child-like hearts can receive the gospel.

Only people who have child-like hearts can receive God’s kingdom.

Mark doesn’t tell us whether this was immediately after Jesus’ teaching on divorce but in the passage that comes straight afterwards,

People were bringing little children to Jesus, but Jesus’ disciples rebuked them.

Maybe the disciples thought that the children shouldn’t disturb Jesus while he was teaching important things like divorce and remarriage.

But when Jesus saw them, he was angry.

And he said to them, in verse 14, ““Let the little children come to me. Don’t stop them, because the kingdom of God belongs to such as these. 15 Truly I tell you, whoever does not receive the kingdom of God like a little child will never enter it.”

Unlike the adult disciples, Jesus wants the little children to come to him.

In English, there’s a saying about children.

Children are to be seen and not heard.

Though it’s good to see children, we shouldn’t hear from them.

They’re noisy, they’re undisciplined, they’re messy and they don’t belong with the adults.

But Jesus says no!

Don’t leave the children out! Bring them in.

The Kingdom of heaven belongs to such as these.

Jesus wants them to come in because the kingdom of God belongs to such as these.

It’s people who are like little children who will receive God’s kingdom.

It’s not because these people are noisy or messy like little children.

Rather, like little children, who have nothing to offer, people who receive God’s kingdom have nothing to offer God but simply receive what God has given them.

Children generally trust parents unconditionally and accept whatever the parents give them.

That’s what we’re called to do if we’re to receive God’s kingdom.

We’re to unconditionally receive and accept what God gives us.

That’s the condition to enter God’s kingdom; it’s to unconditionally accept by faith what God’s given us.

This is especially the case in light of Jesus’ teaching on divorce and remarriage.

In a world that promotes our happiness as the most important thing in the world, it’s often hard to choose God’s way.

The world would have us choose happiness when God would have us choose his holiness.

But true happiness, genuine happiness comes when we walk in holiness before God.

Genuine happiness is a by-product of walking in God’s holiness.

This is especially true when it comes to marriage.

If we want to have a happy marriage, it must start with us walking in God’s holiness and following his way instead of ours.

A number of years ago I was preparing a young couple to get married.

As the wedding day was approaching, I discovered that there were a number of difficulties with the man’s family.

They were not keen for the girl to be married to the man.

Soon, when the girl’s family met the men’s family, they weren’t too keen to have the girl married to her either.

In fact, it got to a stage where I called up a lawyer to see any legal issue if someone gets up to object to the wedding during the wedding ceremony.

It was that serious!

I was thinking, how is this marriage ever going to work?

But under God, the man and the woman worked through their difficulties between their families.

And I’m really glad to see that the couple is growing well in the Lord and now they have a lovely daughter together.

Praise God!

The first few years of their marriage wasn’t easy; it was difficult!

But they trusted in God and they grew.

They chose holiness over happiness and now they have a happy marriage.

Both of them have a child’s heart when it comes to obeying God.

That’s the one condition to receiving God’s kingdom; to enter God’s kingdom, we must have a child’s heart.

However, we can’t soften our hard hearts, let alone our spouse’s hearts, even if we try.

And here’s where the gospel comes in.

God can soften our hardened hearts.

And so, if your hearts are hard, especially against your husband or your wife, ask God to transform your hardened hearts into child-like hearts so that you might receive God’s kingdom.

What does this mean for us?

Love your spouse and love your single friends

If you’re not married, help others love their spouse and love your single friends.

Marriages is lovely and is created by God to be a wonderful union between a man and a woman.

But it takes hard work to make it work.

And so, this week, surprise your spouse with something good.

Surprise them according to their love language.

For some of our spouse, their love language might be speech.

And so, tell them how much you love them.

You might do it through speaking or writing.

If speech is your spouse’s love language, tell them!

For others, service might be your spouse’s love language.

If that’s the case, serve them!

Do something special for them.

For still others, gifts might be their love language.

If that’s the case, surprise them with a gift to show them how much you appreciate them.

If you don’t have a spouse, help one of the married couples here to surprise their spouse!

I’m thinking especially the children here.

Help your Dad to show love to your Mum; or help your Mum to show love to your Dad.

Also, show love to those who are single among us.

You might take them out for coffee or give them a call.

There may be something we haven’t seen for a while at Church because of COVID.

Get in contact to tell them that you care, that you love them.

Even when I was preparing this sermon, a good friend called to tell me that they’ve been praying for me!

It’s always a welcomed joy to hear from an old friend!

Have a heart that love your spouse and your single friends.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What can I do to show love to my spouse?

How can I help those with spouses to show love to their spouse?

How can I show love to my single friends?

Friends, ask God to transform your hard hearts into child-like hearts, ready to receive God’s kingdom.

Let’s pray,

“Father, thank you for showing us your love for us in the Person of Jesus Christ. Father, please forgive us when we come treat others with hardened hearts. By your Holy Spirit, please soften our hearts, that we might genuinely, truly, love one another, as Christ has loved us. We pray for those who are going through difficulties in their marriage now. Father, please be merciful and help them love each other once more. And for those whose marriage are broken, Father, we pray that your healing hand might be upon them, that they might find mercy and comfort in Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
