Don't Pray to Be Dumb; Pray to Be Bold!

Don’t pray to be dumb; pray to be bold!

Passage: Acts 4:23-31

Additional passages: Psalm 2:1-2, Isa 37:16-20, Isa 66:1-2

Big Idea: Servants of God, when you’re threatened to be dumb, pray for boldness to speak God’s word about Jesus.

God makes, God speaks, and God moves

The servants of the Holy Servant threatened to be dumb

Praying to speak God’s word about Jesus with boldness

Who likes to watch cartoons?

Many of you are too young for this, but when I was growing up, we usually read cartoons, in newspapers!

In one of the old Peanuts cartoon, Lucy told her brother Linus to change TV channels and then threatened him with her fist if he didn’t.

“What makes you think you can walk right in here and take over?” asked Linus.

“These five fingers,” said Lucy. “Individually they are nothing, but when I curl them together like this into a single unit, they form a weapon that is terrible to behold.”

“What channel do you want?” sighed Linus.

Turning away, he looked at his fingers and said, “Why can’t you guys get organized like that?”

What would you do if you were Linus?

How would you have responded to the threat that is Lucy’s fist?

Even from a young age, we have to learn how to respond to threats that come our way.

It might be our older brother or sister, it might be a bully at school, or it might even be a bully at work.

Last week, we read in the Bible about a group of bullies who were threatening the followers of Jesus.

They were threatening them to not speak about Jesus, or else!

And so, last week, Peter, who was leading with me, asked a very good question.

How should Christians respond when we’re threatened?

How do you respond to someone who tries to silent Christians?

That’s what we’ll be looking at in today’s passage.

And so, please open your Bible with me to Acts 4, beginning from verse 23.

You can use the companion booklet to take down the notes on page 12.

In today’s passage we’re going to see three things:

God makes, God speaks and God moves

The servants of the Holy Servant threatened to be dumb

Praying to speak God’s word about Jesus with boldness

Before we begin, let’s pray, “Father, may your words enter our ears and into our hearts. May your words flow from our hearts to our hands, our feet and out mouths. And may people around us hear your words come out of our mouths as we speak the words of the gospel of Jesus Christ, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

God makes, God speaks and God moves

Let’s say Acts chapter four verse 23 together, “After they were released, they went to their own people and reported everything the chief priests and the elders had said to them.”

Peter and John were arrested by the leaders of their people.

Why? What did they do? Let’s listen and find out:

“Hi Peter, Hi John, great to see you! What happened to you?”

“When we went to the temple to pray, we saw a lame man on the way.

He held out his arm and asked for cash. But instead of giving him money, we told him to get up and walk, in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth.”

“And then what did he do?”

“He went walking and leaping and praising God!” “What?”

“Walking and leaping and praising God!”

“It was an obvious sign from God that the name of Jesus was powerful.

It proves that Jesus didn’t stay dead but rose from the dead!

God wouldn’t do this in the name of a liar, but God would do this in the name of someone who was the Son of God!

But the leaders of the people didn’t believe!

Instead they put us in and told us to be stay quiet about Jesus.

But how can we? Should we listen to God or listen to man?

We can’t help but to speak about Jesus!

But now, we’ll be facing the threat of being arrested again in prison, or worse, the threat of being killed, just as they’ve done to Jesus.”

What are they going to do?

Their lives might be in danger!

What did they do?

They didn’t hide nor did they run away.

They didn’t put a plan together to protect themselves nor did they prepare themselves to fight back.

What did they do? They prayed!

They raised their voice together in prayer.

They got together to pray, probably not everyone speaking different prayers at the same time, otherwise, we wouldn’t have a record of the prayer that they prayed.

They might say some words together in unison, like what we do when we pray the Lord’s Prayer.

They probably all said the first two verses of Psalm chapter 2 together, as recorded in their prayer.

However, they most likely would’ve have one person to pray on their behalf, someone who had prepared the prayer beforehand, someone who led everyone together in prayer.

That’s what we do every week here at Church on Sunday.

Someone leads us in prayer, and we say Amen, together afterwards.

When we say Amen after prayer, it means that we agree with the prayer.

You can also say Amen if you agree with what I’m saying?

Amen? Amen.

And so, when they were threatened, the followers of Jesus prayed to God.

They address three aspects of who God is:

Firstly, they address God as their Master Maker.

Look with me at verse 24, “When they heard this, they raised their voices together to God and said, “Master, you are the one who made the heaven, the earth, and the sea, and everything in them.”

Prayer is not talking to a genie.

You know how genies work, right?

They ask you for three wishes and you tell them what you wish for.

The genie’s job is to fulfil our wishes.

Some people pray to God as if He was a genie.

They pray to God to fulfil the wished.

But our God is not a genie; He is the Master.

It’s not our will but our Master’s will that must be done.

And more than that, he’s the Master Maker.

He is the Almighty Creator who made all things.

He made the church building you’re sitting in, he made the chairs you’re sitting, and he made you.

He’s the Master and He’s the Maker.

Second, they addressed God as the Holy Speaker.

Look with me at verses 25-26, “ You said through the Holy Spirit, by the mouth of our father David your servant: Why do the Gentiles rage and the peoples plot futile things? The kings of the earth take their stand and the rulers assemble together against the Lord and against his Messiah.”

God is the Holy Speaker.

By his Holy Spirit God spoke words through the mouth of God’s servant, David, who recorded it down in Psalm 2.

David wrote this psalm, this song, one thousand years before Jesus’ followers referred to it here.

In this psalm, God, through the mouth of David, asked, why it is that the Gentiles (that is people from nations other than Israel), why are they so angry?

And why do they plan futile plans, plans that will not work?

Why will their plans be a waste of time?

It’s because they had all come together against the Lord and His Messiah (that is, his anointed one, his chosen ruler).

It’s futile, an utter waste of time, to fight against the Lord and His Messiah.

How can you fight against God and his Messiah and expect to win?

It’s true from David’s time but even more importantly, it’s a prophecy, a glimpse of the future from David’s point of view.

This psalm would be fulfilled in the life of Jesus Christ.

What happened to Jesus would happen just as God has spoken thorugh David because of the third aspect about God in this prayer.

God is the Destiny Mover.

The kings of the earth moved against Jesus, the Holy Servant of God, God’s anointed, God’s Messiah.

Look with me at verses 27-28, “ “For, in fact, in this city both Herod and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles and the people of Israel, assembled together against your holy servant Jesus, whom you anointed, 28 to do whatever your hand and your will had predestined to take place.”

The Gentiles and their leader, as represented by Pontius Pilate, and the people of Israel, and their leader, as represent by King Herod, conspired against Jesus.

They killed him by nailing him to a cross.

What happened to Jesus wasn’t bad luck.

It wasn’t because the Gentiles and the Israelites were clever or that Jesus was merely a victim of circumstance.

It was all within God’s plan.

This is what Christians call the sovereignty of God.

God’s in control of everything.

God moves destiny.

He moved the destiny of both the Gentiles and the Israelites to against Jesus.

He moved Jesus to the Cross. It was part of his plan.

God was, is and always will be the Destiny Mover.

But what happened to Jesus didn’t end on the cross.

Death couldn’t keep him down.

Jesus rose from the dead.

The disciples saw the risen Jesus with their own eyes!

How do we know that they were telling the truth?

It was by the name of Jesus that God raised the paralysed man from the dead.

There’s power that comes at the very mention of Jesus’ name because he is the Holy Servant of God.

He is the One who died and rose again.

It happened exactly according to God’s will.

And it’s because we believe that God’s sovereign in everything that we pray.

When Christians face threats, we pray.

We come in prayer before the One who is the Master Maker, the Holy Speaker and the Destiny Mover.

When we get together to pray, we shouldn’t focus on how big the problem is and we don’t pray to tell God about what our plans will be.

Instead, when we come together to pray, our focus must be God first.

The disciples begin their prayer by focussing on God the Master Maker, God the Holy Speaker and God the Destiny Mover.

God’s the focus and the centre of our prayers, not us.

Someone once said that when he went to Church, he often hears what he described as organ recitals.

Organ recitals are special performances with the musical instrument the organ.

However, the word organ in English can also mean a part of the body.

And so this man said that when he hears prayer at Church, it’s often organ recitals: us telling God the problems we have with our body organs, like the heart, or the liver or the feet.

That’s not to say that we shouldn’t pray for healing for people with health issues.

However, the problem comes if that’s all we pray for; that problem comes if the focus of our prayer is always on us.

When we pray, the question is not whether God can help us; of course he can!

Instead, when we pray, we are to pray to help us focus our eyes on him.

That’s what faith is.

Robert Murray McCheyne’s once said: “You wish to humble a man? Ask him about his prayer life.”

How often we fail by praying with a focus on ourselves rather than a focus on God.

God’s sovereign, he has a plan and God’s plan will work.

Knowing and trusting God’s sovereignty is the foundation of our prayers as well as the foundation of our faith in him.

Amen? Amen

And so, these followers of Jesus prayed, lifting both their eyes and their voices to God in light of clear and present danger.

What was the threat they were facing?

The first Christians were threatened to be dumb.

2. The servants of the Holy Servant threatened to be dumb

They were threatened to not talk about Jesus.

They were threatened not to speak; they were threatened to be dumb.

In English, the word dumb can have two meanings.

One, it can mean someone who’s stupid or foolish.

For example, “What he did was dumb!”

And second, the word dumb can also mean someone who’s unable to speak.

For example, “We were struck dumb at the size of the catastrophe!”

The leaders of the Israelites threatened the Christians to be dumb, to stay silent about the death and resurrection of Jesus.

Even though the evidence of the miracle has shown that God approved of message, even though they couldn’t deny the facts about the miracle, the still refused to believe that Jesus has risen from the dead.

They wanted to make the disciples dumb.

Sometimes, we wish that God will give us a powerful sign, a spectacular miracle, that proves beyond doubt that Jesus is true.

We want a sign that is so obvious that no one can deny that it’s a sign from God.

That will make our job as Christians easier, wouldn’t it?

Well, as it turns out, it won’t make our job easier.

The first disciples’ miracle had a lame man to walk in the name of Jesus in full view of the public, but it didn’t convince everyone.

Many saw it but still didn’t believe it.

They had a stubborn refusal to believe, despite the evidence.

More than that, they threatened the disciples to not speak about Jesus again.

They used force before and they’ll use force again.

They wanted to make the Christians dumb.

Contrast this with what God’s done.

God spoke through David his servant by his Holy Spirit.

God spoke through his Holy Servant, Jesus Christ.

Jesus spoke truthfully about who he is.

He is the Messiah; He is the Word of God; He is the Way, the Truth, and the Life.

His enemies tried to silence him by putting him to death on the cross.

But God raised him up to show that Jesus spoke the truth.

And now the followers of Jesus are sent by Jesus to speak the truth about Jesus.

God speaks, Jesus speaks, Jesus’ followers speaks.

But the world wants to silence Jesus’ message; they want to make Jesus’ followers dumb (that is, not speaking).

It happened back then, and it still happens today.

In a recent report that was released in April 2024, it found that Australian teenage students face the second highest level of bullying among 24 OECD (Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development) countries.

What we they bullied for?

In a separate study, the social researcher McCrindle discovered that one in four Australians (26 per cent) have experienced discrimination because of their religion or religious views.

Younger generations are far more likely to face discrimination because of their religious views, with 33 per cent of Gen Z (7 to 22 years old) and Gen Y (23-38) experiencing this.

In the study, it’s not just Christians who were bullied but people of other faiths as well.

Even so, even in the modern nation of Australia, where much of our modern history is tightly linked to Christianity, Christians, even young Christian students are threatened to be silent.

Even young Christian students are threatened to be dumb about the story of Jesus.

If you are a student in a public school, or if you have a child who’s a student in a public school, make sure you sign up for the Special Religious Education Classes.

It’s a place where students can learn about their own religious heritage.

It’s not just for believers but for those who have no formal beliefs as well.

It’s a time in our public schoolsystem where we intentionally teach students to have respect for all other faiths and cultures.

If you’re sure how to enrol, talk with me or your school about it.

Of course, the threat to make Christians dumb doesn’t just occur in schools but in the marketplace, in governments and in social media as well.

The world had always tried to make Christians dumb.

If we stay silent about Jesus, then we are dumb, we would’ve done exactly what the world wanted.

3. Praying to speak God’s word about Jesus with boldness

But we don’t want to be dumb Christians.

We want to be obedient Christians. Amen? Amen

And so, like the first followers of Jesus, we pray for boldness.

Let’s say verse 29 together, “And now, Lord, consider their threats, and grant that your servants may speak your word with all boldness”

We want to pray to speak God’s word about Jesus with boldness.

God spoke through his Word in the Old Testament, God spoke through Jesus and now Christians want to speak God’s word about Jesus to everyone.

We know that we are not the smartest people in the world.

We’re not the strongest, the most powerful, or the bravest.

We’re weak, foolish and often act like cowards when we’re face with threats.

That’s why we need to pray for boldness.

This boldness not something that we’re born with or something that we gained because of our goodness.

This boldness comes when our gaze is heavenward to the God who’s more powerful than anyone or anything that threatens to silence us.

The famous Christian Pastor, Timothy Keller once said, “The only person who dares wake up a king at 3:00 AM for a glass of water is a child. We have that kind of access.”

That’s the boldness that Christians have as children of God.

No matter what threatens us, we can ask the most powerful King in the world for anything, even at the most inconvenient time.

That’s why we don’t pray for the threats to go away.

Instead, we pray for boldness to obey God, to keep talking about Jesus, even when we face the threats.

We pray for boldness because God is greater than any threat that we will ever face.

We pray for boldness for God is more important than anything that that are threats can take away.

We have this boldness to speak to God only because of what Jesus has done.

We’re not worthy to come before God’s presence.

We’ve sinned against God, that is, we trusted in ourselves or something or someone more than God.

We’re not good enough to come before God.

But because Jesus died and rose, those who believe in him, are forgiven.

In Jesus Christ, our sins are forgiven.

It takes humility to admit that we’re wrong, that we’re not good enough.

However, it’s only as we humble ourselves that we can come before God’s presence.

It’s only as we humble ourselves before God that we can speak boldly before men.

We’re bold not because we’re good or powerful, but because God is good and powerful.

It’s because we’re humble before God that we can be bold before men.

That’s why we pray.

As well as boldness to speak God’s words, the first Christians prayed for healing, and signs and wonders.

They prayed that God will keep doing the impossible and the miraculous so that people can believe the power of the name of Jesus.

Should we expect the same today?

While we do see the first followers of Jesus perform signs and wonders like the healing of the paralytic,

We see less and less of that as the Christian Church continues to grow.

That doesn’t mean that the Christian Church is now lesser because there are less of the miraculous signs.

No, the signs are there, just in a wonderfully different form.

Do you see the Christian next to you? She’s a miracle of God.

Can you see the man who sits in front of you, the man who trusts in Jesus? He’s a sign from God.

These people have been saved from death to life, not because of their goodness but because God’s saved them!

God has done the miraculous in them.

The leaders of Jesus’ day saw the miracle but didn’t believe.

Can you see the miracle that is the new birth of a Christian?

Do you trust God enough to see it?

What happened after they ?

Look with me at verse 31, “When they had prayed, the place where they were assembled was shaken, and they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak the word of God boldly.”

God heard their prayer and he shook the house to confirm that he has heard his prayer.

As the ancient Christian Chrysostom says, “‘The place was shaken,’ and that made them all the more unshaken.”

So, how do you respond to someone who tries to make Christians dumb?

Pray humbly before God.

Don’t pray for the threats to be taken away but pray to be bold to speak about Jesus always.

Servants of God, when you’re threatened to be dumb, pray for boldness to speak God’s word about Jesus.

John Roberts, Chief Justice of the United States Supreme Court, gave an address at his son's middle school graduation in June 2017.

Here is an excerpt from his speech:

“From time to time in the years to come, I hope you will be treated unfairly, so that you will come to know the value of justice. I hope that you will suffer betrayal because that will teach you the importance of loyalty. Sorry to say, but I hope you will be lonely from time to time so that you don't take friends for granted. I wish you bad luck, again, from time to time so that you will be conscious of the role of chance in life and understand that your success is not completely deserved and that the failure of others is not completely deserved either.”

He's right; life is hard.

And living the Christian life is hard.

But often it’s during the hard times that we learn life’s most valuable lessons.

When Christians face threats and hardship because they are speaking the words of the Gospel about Jesus, our faith is strengthened as we come humbly before God in prayer.

Don’t pray for the threats to go away.

Instead, pray for boldness to speak God’s word about Jesus.

For some of us, this is a live question.

Have you been threatened to not come to church?

It might not be as obvious as saying if you go to Church, you will go to jail.

However, it might be more subtle.

If you go to Church, you won’t go well at school.

If you talk about Jesus, you won’t get that promotion.

If you talk about Jesus, I won’t be your friend anymore.

How will you respond when you’re threatened to be dumb Christians?

Friends, don’t be a dumb Christian.

Instead, because you are Servants of God, when you’re threatened to be dumb, pray for boldness to speak God’s word about Jesus.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Have you shaken (trembled) at God’s word about Jesus?

Who has tried to stop you from speaking about Jesus?

How will you speak boldly about Jesus this week?

Servants of God, when you’re threatened to be dumb, pray for boldness to speak God’s word about Jesus.

Amen? Amen!
