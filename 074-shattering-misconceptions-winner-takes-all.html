“What do you want me to do for you?”

“What do you want me to do for you?” PPT

It’s such an or-di-na-ry question, isn’t it?

It’s the kind of question that you expect at the service counter.

You might expect something similar at a fast-food drive thru.

‘Welcome to MacDonald’s drive thru, what would you like?”

A son yells out ‘Mum, I need your help!’

And Mum answers, “What do you want? What do you want me to do for you?”

“What do you want me to do for you?”

In today’s passage, we see this question asked not just once, but twice.

And the person asking this question doesn’t work behind a counter.

It’s Jesus who asks the question, ‘What do you want me to do for you?’

He asks this in response to two different requests.

In his responses, we see how he turns our concept of greatness upside down.

And so, if you have your Bible, please open up with me to Mark 10:35.

The Sons of Thunder seek greatness PPT

We see two disciples coming before Jesus with a request for greatness. PPT

These two disciples are so tone-deaf to Jesus’ message about his death, that they askes Jesus to make them into someone great.

They’re James and John, two brothers who've been following Jesus since the start of his public ministry.

Jesus called them the Sons of Thunder.

That’s a great nickname isn’t it?

Hi we're James and John, but you can call us the Sons of Thunder.

Here’s their question, ‘“Teacher, we want you to do for us whatever we ask."

That’s a fairly bold request!

It’s like asking Jesus to sign them a blank cheque.

Jesus replies with a question, ‘What do you want me to do for you?’

That’s a reasonable question.

They reply, "Let one of us sit at your right and the other at your left in your glory."

In other words, give both of us places of honour when you are crowned king.

They know that Jesus is the Messiah, the Christ.

He’s the Chosen One, the King, that God’s been promising for hundreds of years.

More than that, Jesus often refers to himself as the Son of Man.

The Son of Man is a term that the prophet Daniel uses to describe the One who will receive authority from God.

According to the prophet Daniel, the Son of Man will be given an ev-er-las-ting dominion and a kingdom that will never be destroyed.

And here they are.

They are travelling with Jesus, the Son of Man to Jerusalem, the city of God.

God’s promises are about to be fulfilled! Jesus is about to be crowned King of God’s kingdom!

And they want to be a part of it. They want to share in Jesus’ glory!

James and John are both very ambitious people.

They want to be great in God’s kingdom; they want to share the honour with Jesus.

In today’s terms, if Jesus is the CEO of a new company, then they want to make sure they too, are just under Jesus on the corporate ladder.

They don’t want to be on the bottom of the ladder.

They don’t want to be one of those disciples whose name nobody remembers.

They want to be great!

They want to be recognised, they want to be respected, they want to be remembered.

They’re not too different from us.

We want to be respected; we want to be recognised as someone important.

We want to be great, whether it’s in our families, or at work.

Some of us even want to be great at church. (slow)

What’s wrong with having ambition for greatness?

What’s wrong with the Sons of Thunder wanting to step up and become someone great in God’s kingdom?

Well, it’s true that Jesus is about to be crowned King of God’s kingdom.

The promises that God gave through his prophets are about to come true.

Jesus himself had just told them that he will rise again from the dead. (slow)

But before he rises from the dead, he has to die.

In fact, just before the Sons of Thunder made their request, Jesus told them what’s going to happen to him when he comes into Jerusalem.

He told them that seven terrible things are about to happen to him, in Mark 10:33-34,

The Son of Man will be betrayed to the chief priests and the teachers of the law.

They will condemn him to death

They will hand him over to Gentiles.

They will mock him.

They will spit on him.

They will flog him and

They will kill him.

It’s as if James and John selectively only heard the good part, the glory part and chose to ignore the suffering that comes before the glory.

Which part of the sentence ‘they will kill him’ didn’t they understand?

They were utterly insensitive to Jesus and what he’s about to go through.

Jesus tells them, "You don't know what you are asking,

Can you drink the cup I drink or be baptized with the baptism I am baptized with?"

The cup that Jesus refers to here is a symbol of suffering and death.

The baptism that Jesus refers to also carries the idea of death.

In Jesus’ days, when people were baptised, they were fully immersed under water.

Jesus is about to be fully immersed in Death.

b. A request denied (PPT)

Jesus asks James and John whether they can go through his suffering with him.

Without thinking about it, they say, ‘We can’

They really have no idea what's about to happen to Jesus.

Even so, Jesus tells them, ‘You will drink the cup I drink and be baptized with the baptism I am baptized with’.

One day the Sons of Thunder will suffer for being followers of Jesus.

They will drink the cup that Jesus drinks.

But for now, Jesus denies their request. (slow)

Look with me at verse 40,

‘but to sit at my right or left is not for me to grant. These places belong to those for whom they have been prepared.’

Jesus doesn’t decide who gets to sit at his right or his left.

God the Father has already prepared people for these places.

And so, the request of James and John are denied.

As James and John bow their heads in shame and walks back to the rest of the disciples,

you can imagine the rest of the disciples giving them death-stares.

The Bible says that the rest of the disciples are indignant.

They’re angry at James and John!

‘Who do these two think they are? What right do they have to claim the seats next to Jesus?

Aren’t we as good as them?’

Jesus then gathers them together and gives them one of the most important lessons they’ll ever learn.

He tells them that the Son of Man is a Servant and that he comes serving.

The Son of Man comes serving PPT

A misconception of greatness PPT

Jesus says to them, "You know that those who are regarded as rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them, and their high officials exercise authority over them.”

The disciples know all about how the Gentiles rule.

Gentiles refer to people who aren’t Jews and in the disciples’ case, they’ve seen how the ancient Romans have ruled.

They’ve seen how the Romans treat their slaves.

They treat them like animals.

They’ve seen what the Romans do to those who oppose Rome.

They’re killed; they’re sometimes even crucified.

For those in positions of power, they have tre-men-dous control and authority over other people.

That’s the order of things; that’s the way things are.

While we’re not living in Ancient Rome today, we still see how powerful people have tre-men-dous influence over others.

If you have a look at CEOWOROLD Magazine’s Most powerful people in 2020, they’re people who have an enormous influence over millions of people.

The top three on the list are Xi Jiping, President of the People’s Republic of China, Valdmir Putin, President of Russia and, until recently, Donald Trump, the President of the United States of America.

Their words and their decisions can affect millions of people.

They are very powerful and influential people.

On a smaller scale, employers can have a powerful and sometimes unreasonable influence over their employees.

A few days ago, a viral video was shown around the internet which shows a fight in a Bubble Tea Store in Adelaide

It was between a young Chinese worker, who disputed about her pay and her Chinese boss, who refused to pay her.

When she asked about it, the boss slapped her on the face.

She was later taken to Adelaide hospital and the boss was taken to the Police Station.

That’s how the world works.

Powerful people lording, high officials exercising authority.

That’s what greatness is. (slow)

You’re in control, you’re honoured, you’re feared.

Jesus says, you know that’s how the world works.

But not so with you.

‘Instead,’ says Jesus, ‘whoever wants to become great among you must be your servant, and whoever wants to be first must be slave of all.’

Does that even make sense?

How can someone who is great be a slave?

Here, Jesus goes against the order of the world completely.

The First among you must be a slave. (slow)

Service, doing the things of a servant, is the mark of the Christian.

As the Bible scholar James Edwards once said, ‘the preeminent virtue of God’s kingdom is not power, not even freedom, but service.’

Why is ‘service’, of all things, the mark of the Christian?

Talk to the person next to you here at Church or at home and ask yourselves, why is service the mark of a Christian?

Text your answer in via Whatsapp or Zoom!

(20 seconds)

It's because the First One in God’s Kingdom, the Greatest One in God’s Kingdom, is Jesus Christ.

The Greatest One served and gave (PPT)

And Jesus is a servant; Jesus himself is the slave of all.

Look with me at v45,

‘For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many.’

James and John were partly right.

Jesus is the Son of Man; Jesus is the King of God’s Kingdom.

But as Son of Man, he’s come not to be served but to serve.

Who is he serving?

He’s come to serve God; he’s come to do God’s will.

He is the Servant of God.

But he’s also come to serve us.

Jesus has come to be the Servant of All; that includes us.

That’s such a contrast to how the world works, isn’t it?

The God whom we pray to, the God whom we worship has come to serve us.

How does our God serve us?

In the last part of verse 45, ‘he gave his life as a ransom for many.’

Jesus Christ, served us by drinking the bitter cup, by enduring the wrath of God’s anger, to give up his life, as a ransom for ours.

We humans have always wanted to be great.

We want to be in powerful positions, ruling over others.

We want to be as great as God; no we want to be greater than God.

We want to reverse God’s order.

We want to put ourselves first and God last.

And so, we’ve put ourselves directly against God; we made God our enemy.

God has every right to destroy us.

But out of his great love for us, he sent Jesus.

Jesus came to drink the bitter cup, to die in our place.

He’s given his life as a ransom for ours, to save us from God’s wrath.

That’s Jesus’ great service to us!

The Greatest One in God’s Kingdom, the Son of Man, gave his life as a ransom for people like us.

That’s how Jesus serves us.

When we come to church, we often call this gathering, what we do here on a Sunday, a service.

This is the 9am Bilingual, intergenerational service.

In Chinese, we often the word Chóngbài崇拜 to mean religious gathering and the word Shì fèng 事奉to mean serve

But In English, the word service can have two meanings: an act of service and a religious gathering.

When we come to this service, we do both.

We worship God and we serve.

There are people who serve God in different ways. (pause)

We serve by welcoming, we serve by playing music, we serve by praying and we serve by preaching.

And praise God for these people who serve among us.

But when we gather together at church, the primary reason we get together is to remember Jesus’ service for us.

We remember how Jesus has served us.

We remember that he gave his life as a ransom for us.

We celebrate Jesus’ service for us.

We serve because he first served us. (slow)

That’s what we do here, in this service. (pause)

But before we can truly celebrate and worship Jesus, we must personally accept his service for us.

We must accept who he is and what he’s done.

This is shown beautifully by someone who’s also made a request of Jesus.

In the last section of today’s passage, we see the Son of Timaeus, who comes begging.

The Son of Timaeus comes begging PPT

On their way to Jerusalem, Jesus and his disciples passed through the city of Jericho.

As they were leaving the city, there was a blind man by the name of Bartimaeus (li-ter-al-ly the Son of Timaeus), begging.

When he heard that it was Jesus walking past, he shouted, ‘Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!’

In the Bible, God promised to King David that one of his sons will have a kingdom that will continue forever.

And when Jesus is within an earshot away, he shouted, ‘Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!’

People told him to be quiet but he shouted all the more!

‘Son of David, have mercy on me!’

He wouldn’t stop shouting until Jesus stops walking.

And Jesus stops.

Jesus says to him, ‘What do you want me to do for you?’

That’s the same question that he asked James and John earlier.

What’s Bartimaeus’ request?

A request to see (PPT)

He only has one, simple request, ‘Rabbi (teacher), I want to see.’

Simple, but impossible.

How can anyone make the blind man see?

But Bartimaeus believes that Jesus can do the impossible.

He trusts in Jesus.

A request granted (PPT)

Jesus says to him, ‘Go, your faith has healed you.’

Bartimaeus trusts Jesus and asks Jesus to do the impossible.

And Jesus said to him, Go, your faith has saved you.”

When Blind Bartimaeus’ eyes were opened and he sees Jesus, he follows him.

That’s what genuine faith in Jesus looks like.

When you trust in Jesus and sees Jesus for who he really is, he saves you and you follow him.

Just as Blind Bartimaeus sees Jesus and starts to follow him along the road.

“What do you want me to do for you?” PPT

How about us?

If Jesus were to ask us, ‘What do you want me to do for you?’ What would we say?

What do we want Jesus to do for us?

James and John asked for greatness for themselves.

The blind Bartimaeus asked to see.

What do you want Jesus to do for you?

For some of us, we might be like Bartimaeus, we might be spiritually blind and can’t see Jesus.

If that’s us, then maybe we also want to say to Jesus, ‘Lord Jesus, have mercy on me. I want to see.’

We might feel that we’re not be able to trust Jesus on certain things. Maybe we need to say to Jesus, ‘Lord Jesus, have mercy on me. I want to trust you.’

But before we ask Jesus to do new things for us, remember what He’s already done for us.

Jesus, the Greatest One, has come to serve us.

Jesus, the King of God’s Kingdom, has come to serve a people like us.

He’s come to give his life as a ransom for a people like us.

And so, when you pray, when you ask Jesus, don’t seek greatness for yourself because the Greatest One has come to serve us.

The three questions for this week are:

In which area of my life do I feel blind and uncertain and in need of Jesus’ guidance?

In which area am I tempted to seek greatness for myself whether than the greatness of Christ?

What do I want Jesus to do for me?

Friends, Jesus, the Greatest one, has come to serve us.

Would you accept what Jesus has done?

Would you accept his service for you? If Jesus were to say to you, “What do you want me to do for you?”

What would you say?

What’s the one thing you want him to do?

Don’t seek greatness for yourself when the Greatest One has come to serve us.
