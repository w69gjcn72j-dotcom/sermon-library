Our Family and Our Older Brother

Sermon Title: Our family and our older Brother

Passage: Hebrews 2:10-13

Big Idea: In Christ our Brother, realign our fallen families to the glory of God our Father

“It’s going to be OK, because we’re Family”

A race of fallen Families

Hope in Christ our Brother

A family that glorifies God our Father

“It’s going to be OK, because we’re Family”

In the Fast and Furious movies, the theme of family runs throughout the series.

Throughout the movies, the hero of the movie series, Dom, keeps talking about the importance of family.

In one of the scenes in the movie, he tells his brother, “It’s going to be OK, because we’re Family.”

Earlier this year, my son had shown me these funny pictures from the movie Fast and Furious.

They show how Dom pushes the importance of family to the extreme.

For example, in one picture, it shows Dom about to do something ridiculously dangerous.

When asked about the danger, he replies, I don’t have gravity – I got family.

Who needs gravity when you have family?

It’s a funny picture but it stresses an important point that many people agrees with.

Families are important.

We see this in both the East and the West.

I know that often, people in the West are perceived to be individualistic without much concerns for their family.

But that’s a gross stereotype of the West.

I’ve seen many people in the West who love their families and wants to spend a lot of time with them.

The COVID lockdown had brought sadness to a lot of families in the West because they couldn’t see each other.

Of course, there’s a strong focus in the East on families too.

In Confucianism, one always loves one’s family first and then one moves outwards.

While both the East and the West agree on the importance of family in theory, it doesn’t always appears to be the case in practice.

For example, for many of us from Asian backgrounds, we know of the incredibly high pressure that many families are facing.

Often, the adults had to work incredibly long hours to make ends meet.

This would mean less time with the family and the children.

This might have contributed to the currently very low fertility rate of 1.3 in China.

That is, a couple will only produce 1.3 children on average.

The change in the laws regarding one child policy in 2016 has caused a small spike but not enough for the fertility rate to increase back to sustainable levels.

Even if it’s economically viable and legal to have a family, many are choosing not to have one.

A report has shown that many no longer see marriage and families as necessary passages in life or the essential ingredients of a happy life.

Families are no longer considered as important.

Of course, this doesn’t happen just in the East.

It also happens in the West as well.

The latest fertility rate in Australia is currently 1.59.

The research here has shown that young people in Australia feel insecure in our world, especially in our time when so many things are uncertain.

They’re not sure if they can afford to have housing and a family.

Even for those who have families, many of them have experienced brokenness within the family.

Some children go through different foster homes, sometimes not knowing who their real parents are.

For those children who have experienced brokenness in their families, the emotional, financial and social impact might last a lifetime.

Is family still the answer? Is everything still going to be OK because of family?

For many people around the world, family can’t be the answer because it’s so broken.

And so family is not the answer.

Nor is the answer leaving or getting rid of the idea of family.

Because the problem is not with the idea of family itself.

The problem is that we are from a race of fallen families.

A race of fallen families

The Bible is very honest about the fallenness of our families.

Just as in English we hear the phrase, “Skeletons in the closet” and in Chinese we have the phrase “家家有本難唸的經”,

The Bible is tells us that every family has deep flaws, starting from the very first family in the Bible.

Star 1: Creation

In the beginning, God created man and women, as well as the whole world.

God created the world to be a place where men and women live in harmony as God’s people, live under God’s rule, in God’s perfect world.

The first family consist of the first man and the first woman, living in a perfect relationship in God’s world.

Star 2: Fall

However, when men and women reject God’s rule, their relationship with God was shattered.

You can read about this in Genesis 3.

This break in their relationship with God led to a fracture in their own relationship.

This is how God describes the relationship between men and women.

Speaking to the wife, God says, “Your desire will be for your husband, yet he will rule over you.”

Their relationship will no longer be perfect; there will be a tension between the man and the woman.

Christians call this the Fall of mankind.

It’s a term that describes our brokenness before God.

This brokenness is also known as sin.

Star 3: Judgement

As a result of their disobedience, they were punished by God.

They were banished from God’s perfect garden of Eden.

The man has to work hard to find food to eat.

The woman will endure pain when she has children.

This punishment from God didn’t stop with the first couple, Adam and Eve.

The brokenness in their relationship was passed to their sons, Cain and Abel.

Out of his hatred for his brother Abel, Cain killed him.

Cain killed his brother, Abel.

A family doesn’t get more broken than that!

This brokenness keeps getting passed onto each generation.

Even the great man of faith Abraham wasn’t immune.

The Bible has recorded many great things that Abraham has done in his life, including many acts of faith.

But it also recorded one of the lowest points in Abraham’s life.

You can read about it in Genesis 12.

To keep himself safe, he lied about his wife, Sarai, and told people that Sarai was his sister so that people won’t kill him to take Sarai.

What a coward!

What’s worse, his son Isaac faced a similar situation.

You can read about it in Genesis 26.

Isaac lied about his wife Rebekah because just like his father Abraham, he didn’t want to be killed if a more powerful man wanted to take her as his wife.

Like his father, he, too, falter at that moment.

And don’t get me started on Jacob, Isaac’s son!

He lied through most of his life!

You can read about his life and his lies from Genesis 27 all the way to Genesis 43.

If you keep reading, you’ll see it this pattern of brokenness in the families of God’s people throughout the centuries.

Can you think of other broken families in the Bible?

If you can, send a message to us via Whatsapp, WeChat or Zoom.

Can you think of other broken families in the Bible?

You have twenty seconds.

There are many broken families in the Bible!

Leaders and Kings are not exempt.

Even the great King David had a lot of issues in his family.

Like others before him in his ancestry, King David failed and broke his family.

You can try but you won’t be able to find a perfect family in the Bible because it’s not there.

Each family has a skeleton in its closet, each family has a book that’s hard to read because

Each family is broken.

Each family is broken because us, the members of the family, are also broken.

The human race is a race of fallen families.

Hope in Christ our Brother

But there is hope.

Despite our brokenness, God has not given up on us.

God has sent Jesus to rescue us.

It reminds me of the story of a young six years old boy named Bridger.

Last year, Bridger was playing with his younger sister at a friend’s house.

Suddenly, a dog lunged at the younger sister.

Bridger quickly jumped in front of his sister to protect her.

As a result, the dog bit his face and he had many, many stitches.

To hide his stitches, he wore a Captain America helmet.

The actor who played Captain America, Chris Evans, gave him a special Captain America shield to Bridger and told Bridger that

“You’re the hero. You’re so brave. Your little sister is so lucky to have you as her big brother.”

Bridger’s little sister is safe because of the sacrifice of her big brother Bridger.

In one sense, we’re like the little sister and Jesus is like our Big brother.

Jesus sacrificed himself to make us safe.

But the difference is that God the Father intentionally sent Jesus, his one and Only Son, to save us and Jesus obeyed and came to save us.

Another difference, of course, is that we weren’t Jesus’ brothers or sisters.

We were in fact God’s enemies; we were the ones who rejected God.

We’ve turned our backs on God.

In Chinese there’s a saying, “Recognising the thief as the father” 認賊作父.

It’s turning to an enemy instead of your father.

That’s what we’ve done when we turned to sin.

We recognise the enemy as our father and rejected God instead.

We became God’s enemies.

But God sent his Son to rescue us, his enemies, to make us into his sons and daughters.

God didn’t send Jesus into a perfect family.

In fact, God’ sent Jesus into the messy family we’ve just seen.

God’s sent Jesus into the family of the coward Abraham, the liar Jacob and the adulterer David.

It was a messy family.

Jesus’ own family wasn’t that great either.

His brothers didn’t believe him; even his own mother tried to control him.

When Jesus was preaching and teaching, his family came to call him.

They thought they had priority over Jesus’ ministries.

He replied to them, “Who are my mother and my brothers?” 34 Looking at those sitting in a circle around him, he said, “Here are my mother and my brothers! 35 Whoever does the will of God is my brother and sister and mother.” (Mark 3:33-35)

Jesus redefines who the family is.

Jesus’ family members are now those who do God’s will.

Jesus himself had come to do the Father’s will.

What was the Father’s will? It was for Jesus to die on the cross to save us from our brokenness, our sin.

God had sent Jesus to die in our place to save us from God’s own punishment against our sins.

Because Jesus has died, those who believe in him are no longer his enemies but God’s sons and daughters.

Look with me at today’s passage in Hebrews 2:10,

“For in bringing many sons and daughters to glory, it was entirely appropriate that God—for whom and through whom all things exist—should make the pioneer of their salvation perfect through sufferings.”

God wants to bring many sons and daughters to glory.

To do that, he perfects Jesus by leading him through the sufferings that came with the cross.

The road to glory is marked by suffering and Jesus has paved the road ahead for us.

Jesus saved us by suffering and dying on the cross and he leads us on the road to glory.

The road that Jesus takes us on will be marked with suffering as well; it will be difficult.

But Jesus himself has suffered the worst already on our behalf already on the cross.

On the cross, Jesus has already suffered the full wrath of God on our behalf.

The path that we travel has suffering too but it’s suffering from the wrath of the devil and the wrath of man, neither of which is worth comparing with the glory that Jesus has won for us.

Star 5: Risen

And we know that Jesus has glory in store for us because He has risen from the dead.

Jesus’ resurrection from the dead shows us that what he’s said is true.

What Jesus has said about God is true and that we can trust him to lead us to God.

Look with me at Hebrews 2:11, “For the one who sanctifies and those who are sanctified all have one Father. That is why Jesus is not ashamed to call them brothers and sisters”

Jesus sanctifies us; that is, he makes us holy.

We now belong to Him.

And together, we belong to God.

He’s saved us, made us holy and makes us sons and daughters of God the Father.

Jesus brings us into his family.

And as our older Brother, Jesus is not ashamed to call us brothers!

We don’t call many people our brothers and sisters.

We only call those who are actually our brothers, brother.

We might even call those who are close to us our brothers.

But Jesus calls us brothers and sisters.

Jesus calls you brothers and sisters.

He’s our family and He’s not ashamed to call us his brothers and sisters.

In the ancient world, Christians were shamed for their beliefs.

Indeed, even in the modern world Christians are shamed for their beliefs, often by families.

But Jesus is not ashamed of us.

By his death and resurrection, Jesus made a new Family.

Listen to how the writer of Hebrews describes what Jesus does among his new family, in Hebrews 2:12, “I will proclaim your name to my brothers and sisters;

I will sing hymns to you in the congregation.”

Jesus proclaims and sing praises among his brothers and sisters and in his Church.

When we gather in the name of Jesus at Church, when we hear the words of the Bible, God’s word, when we sing God’s praises,

Jesus is there among us, proclaiming God’s name, singing his praise, right in the midst of us!

When we come together, Jesus worship God our Father with us.

Star 6: Spirit

Galatians 4:6 ‘And because you are sons, God sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts, crying, “Abba, Father!”’

We can worship the Father with Jesus because God has sent the Spirit of His Son into our hearts.

Star 7: Hope

As the sons and daughters of God, we long for the appearing of our Big Brother, Jesus Christ.

That’s what the Spirit of God longs for in us.

That’s what heaven is: heaven is about being in fellowship with God the Father and Jesus the Son forever.

It’s about being part of God’s family forever.

For those who have the Spirit of the Son of God, we long to be with our brothers and sisters in Christ so that we can worship our God the Father forever.

Over the past few weeks, a number of people have asked me about when Church will begin.

People are looking forward to coming back together to worship God with the rest of God’s family.

They long to see the brothers and sisters come back together to worship God.

That’s what the family of God longs for.

Jesus our Brother had died and rose from the dead to bring us into God’s family.

Jesus gave his Spirit so that we can be his brothers and sisters and for God to be our Father.

In Jesus we have a new family who will one day be made perfect.

Jesus is the Hope for our fallen families.

However, on this side of heaven, though not perfect, we still need to be a family that glorifies God the Father.

A family that glorifies God our Father

There are three things that we need to remember as we think about what it means to be a family that glorifies God our Father.

Avoid worshipping the family

Family is very important; it’s a gift from God.

But it’s not the giver; God is the Giver.

Very important to not make family into a god.

When you place anything, anyone above God, even if it’s family, it becomes an idol.

Don’t place anything above your worship of God.

Though family is important, we must avoid describing it in such a way as if we’re praising God.

For example, don’t describe the family as the sun and for its members to be like plants.

Families are important, but only God is the one who can truly give us life and make us grow.

Sometimes you will have to choose your loyalty

Jesus himself has said in Luke 14:26-27, “26 “If anyone comes to me and does not hate his own father and mother, wife and children, brothers and sisters—yes, and even his own life—he cannot be my disciple. 27 Whoever does not bear his own cross and come after me cannot be my disciple.”

Jesus is not saying that we’re to actively hate our family; we must love them and there are numerous passages in the Bible that tells us to love our family members.

But there will be times when we must choose between following Jesus and following our father or mother, our wife and our children, our brothers, and our sisters.

When those times come, and they will, a true disciple will choose to follow Jesus above their family.

We love our family, but we love Jesus even more.

And because we love our family, there is nothing we want more than to see them made members of the family of Jesus.

For it’s only in the family of Jesus that our fallen families have any real, lasting hope.

3. Be a family that glorifies God

As families which had been made new in Jesus, we mustn’t fall back into our fallenness and our brokenness.

We need to realign our families so that together, we glorify God the Father.

To help us think about how to glorify God as a family, we can use our 5G’s.

Gather: We gather as a family together on Sundays to worship God.

That’s the absolute basic of what we can do as a family that worships God.

We gather as a family before God, together with other families, as the family of God.

Encourage one another to keep gathering especially when they don’t feel like it.

Governance: We make sure we manage our household well.

For the fathers and mothers of the household, we want to make sure that our household is well-looked after.

We want to make sure the children are looked after, the house is well looked after, and our extended families are well looked after.

To manage our household well is a pre-requisite of being a leader in God’s household.

Growth: Make sure you help each other to grow in God’s word.

Encourage each other to grow in our knowledge of God.

Giftedness: Use your gifts at home to serve one another.

It might be the gift of cooking, or cleaning up, or building things.

Use the gifts that you have to serve other members of the family.

Who knows? You might be able to use these gifts at Church one day.

Go: Go and invite people into your family home.

Be hospitable with other people, whether they are singles or families.

Share your family life with others!

Friends, if you are a Christian, Christ has died and risen to bring you into his family.

And so, in Christ our Big brother, realign your fallen families to the glory of God the Father.

What does this mean for us?

Instead of saying that it’s going to be OK because of family, we say

“It’s going to be OK, because Jesus is our Brother.”

A pastor by the name of Ray Ortland has this wonderful illustration:

What does it mean to worship Christ instead of the family?

‘It means we sometimes say to our wives,

“Honey, I’m going to my discipleship group now.

I know it’s been quite a day with the kids.

And I will make this up to you tomorrow.

But the Lord will give you extra strength this evening.”

Then, as you’re walking out the door, she turns and says cheerfully to the kids, “Hey kids, aren’t we lucky to share our daddy with others, for Jesus’ sake?”

And the kids growing up in that family understand that Jesus is the centre of the universe, and they are better off with reality arranged that way.’

Let your wife and your children know that it’s going to be OK because Jesus is our Brother.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How am I in danger of worshipping my family instead of Jesus?

Think of a difficulty you’re facing now. How am I trusting Jesus my Brother and my Lord to lead me through it?

What would it like to be in Jesus’ presence as he leads in praise among his brothers and sisters?

Friends, God has sent His Son, our Big brother, to save us and to bring us to glory.

May we, by our words and deeds, bring our families to him, so that together, under Jesus’ lead, we’ll bring glory to God our Father.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for sending Jesus to us. We thank you that you’ve sent him into our messy world and into our messy lives. Father, please by your Spirit help us see the glory of Jesus and the glory that he’s leading us into. Help us to want that glory not only for ourselves but also for our families as well. Please give us a hunger to see our families come before you so that together with them, we will behold your glory forever. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
