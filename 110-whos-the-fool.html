Are you fooled too?

Fooled by boasts and biases

Marvel at Jesus’ words

Many of us like to think of ourselves as free thinkers.

We like to consider ourselves to be able to read the facts for ourselves, sort out the facts from the errors and arrive at some level of truth, all by ourselves.

However, more often than not, the decisions we make are often the result of social pressure around us, whether we notice it or not.

This has what’s often known as social conformity.

Jennifer Wosmek, a psychology instructor, was explaining this to her class.

To test this out, her class did an experiment.

They went to the shopping centre and organised different students at the doors of different levels in the shopping centre.

At ground level, two of them would go in and, without any explanation, face the back of the elevator.

The other ‘normal’ shoppers might look at them strangely but face the front of the elevator.

However, at the first floor, other students would come in and face the back of the elevator also.

At this, some of the other ‘normal’ shoppers would start to turn their backs to the elevator, even though it’s a strange thing to do.

This keeps going through as the elevator rises through the levels and quite a few would conform to what the students were doing.

They would conform to what the society is doing around them, even though it didn’t make much sense.

And it’s understandable, isn’t it?

We don’t want to be the odd one out.

We don’t want to look like a fool, facing one way while the rest of the society faces the opposite.

But that’s what it can feel like for many Christians.

What Christians want are often completely opposite to what the rest of the society wants.

The society says to put yourself first; the Bible tells Christians to put Jesus first.

The society says to seek treasure on earth; the Bible tells the Christian to seek treasures in heaven.

And so, often, the world would tease and mock Christians for being fools for believing Jesus.

They frown upon Christians and laugh at our beliefs.

How should Christians respond?

How do we respond when our friends and families call us fools for believing in Jesus?

That’s what we’re going to explore in today’s passage.

If you have your Bible there, please open up to John 7:45-52.

We’re going to see three things:

Are you fooled too?

Fooled by boasts and biases

Marvel at Jesus’ words

Before we look into it, let’s pray.

“Father, please open our eyes so that we may marvel at the words of Jesus. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

Are you fooled too?

By the time we get to John 7, Jesus was already a well-known figure among the people.

He had healed people miraculously, walked across the sea, fed thousands of people with five loave of bread and two fish, and he’s taught many things to many thousands of people.

In fact, he had made extraordinary claims about himself.

He said that he’s the Bread of Life.

He’s said that God the Father had sent him and that those who have him will have eternal life.

The priests and pharisees of the day, who were religious leaders of the Jews, saw Jesus as a threat.

And so, they’ve sent servants of the temple, also translated as temple police in some Bible translations, to arrest Jesus.

According to them, Jesus was too dangerous to be allowed to teach in the temple!

However, these temple police returned empty handed.

They failed in their mission to arrest Jesus.

Why did they fail?

Look with me at verse 46, “The servants answered, “No man ever spoke like this!”

Imagine the group of temple police, pushing through the crowd, maybe even with clubs ready just in case Jesus and his disciples resist arrest.

Their objective was simple: seize and arrest Jesus.

They had one job: it was to arrest Jesus.

But once they got there, they stopped.

They heard what Jesus’ said and they were stunned.

“No man ever spoke like this!”

Now these temple police were no ordinary thugs.

They would’ve been Levites with special religious training.

When they heard Jesus, they recognised that he wasn’t just a preacher with crazy ideas.

He spoke words about God and about himself like no one has ever done before.

And so, they wondered, was the warrant for Jesus justified?

Now at this point, they were probably not believers yet.

They were just wondering: Was it wrong to arrest Jesus?

Look at how the Pharisees reply in verses 47-49, “Are you fooled too? 48 Have any of the rulers or Pharisees believed in him? 49 But this crowd, which doesn’t know the law, is accursed.”

The Pharisees called these temple police fools!

They were stupid because they’ve been deceived.

The Pharisees saw themselves as the wise ones; they were the ones who knew the truth.

They were the ones who knew their stuff.

Surely, they argued, if Jesus was telling the truth, then rulers and Pharisees would believe him.

But, said the Pharisees, no ruler of Pharisee had believed Jesus.

They go even further to say that this whole crowd, the common people, didn’t know the law.

That’s why they were confused about Jesus.

In fact, they went further and said that the crowd was accursed.

To be accursed by God means that they would be going to hell.

They were saying the crowd, who didn’t know the law, would be going to hell!

How did they know the crowd didn’t know the law?

They were fooled by Jesus.

In contrast, the Pharisees, who knew the law, weren’t fooled by Jesus.

They knew better.

Then something interesting happens.

Nicodemus, who was one of the Pharisees, asked a very sensible question, in verse 51, “Our law doesn’t judge a man before it hears from him and knows what he’s doing, does it?”

Surely, God’s law allows a man to be heard and understood before it judges him, right?

Look at the Pharisees’ response in verse 52, ““You aren’t from Galilee too, are you?” they replied. “Investigate and you will see that no prophet arises from Galilee.”

Not only where the Pharisees saying they know better.

The Pharisees were basically saying, “We are better!

We’re Jews from Jerusalem, the centre of Jewish religion.

But these Galileans like Jesus are on the fringe.

What do they know?

No prophet arises from Galilee!

And so, don’t side with Jesus the Galilean. Side with us!

We are better!”

They claimed to know better; they claimed to be better.

And so, they felt justified in being hostile and aggressive against Jesus of Galilee.

That’s what our world is like today, isn’t it?

We live in a world that’s increasingly hostile to Christianity.

Earlier this week, the top news item was seven rugby players who refused to wear a jersey that promotes sexual ethics contrary to their religious beliefs.

I don’t know whether these rugby players are Christians.

But the world was saying, how dare they for not compromising their religious beliefs!

The pressure for Christians and other people of faith to comply to social conformity is great,

Whether it’s at school, promoting certain ethics or within professional bodies where accountants, lawyers, scientist are compelled to sign off on certain sexual contrary to what the Bible teaches.

Christians are increasingly seen as the bad guys, people who should be arrested.

We are fools with dangerous beliefs!

That’s how the Pharisees saw Jesus.

They saw him as someone with dangerous beliefs.

The world, today, sees Jesus’s followers as people with dangerous beliefs.

Like the Pharisees of old, there are those in the world who would claim that they know better.

They’ve done the research; they’ve read the philosophers.

They know their stuff!

They would claim that Christians are fools, people who don’t know science or history.

Like the Pharisees of old, they would ask us to see who’s on the Christians’ side?

No one! No ruler, no philosophers, none of the important people.

Like the Pharisees of old, they would claim, “We know better”

But more than that, they would claim, “We are better!”

Christianity is not for smart people; they’re for simpletons and fools.

It’s embarrassing and cringey.

Don’t side with them; side with us.

We are better!

How do Christians respond?

We thank God that this is not a new problem and Christian writers throughout history have thought about it.

In fact, we see how Christians should respond in today’s passage.

Did you see it?

Fooled by boasts and biases

If you look more closely, you will see that it’s not the temple police or the crowd who were fooled.

Rather, it’s the Pharisees and the chief priests who were fooled, by their own boasts and biases.

This is a passage full of irony.

What’s irony?

According to one definition, irony is a state of affairs or an event that seems deliberately contrary to what one expects and is often wryly amusing as a result.

It’s when someone thought they knew better but they didn’t.

Often, it’s quite funny to see.

It’s a little like this.

Last week, I was in isolation because I contracted COVID.

I’m thankful for God’s mercy and for all your support.

I’m feeling much better now.

But while I was in isolation, I needed a hairdryer after I had a shower.

I couldn’t go out to look for it because of isolation.

I was getting a little annoyed because just a few weeks ago, we bought a second hairdryer because hairdryers can be in high demand in our household.

How can we lose two hairdryers?

In time, Jenny found one and put it at the door.

Finally, I thought, I can blow dry my hair and go back to sleep.

As I was about to plug their hair dryer in, I noticed that there was already a hairdryer in the socket.

The hairdryer was in my room all along!

How ironic!

That’s what this passage in John is like.

It’s full of irony!

Firstly, we see a boast undermined.

Remember when the Pharisees asked whether any Pharisee has believed Jesus, and assuming that none of them did?

Well, actually, one of them had had a long, respectful chat with Jesus.

It was none other than Nicodemus himself.

That’s how he was introduced in verse 50, “Nicodemus—the one who came to him previously and who was one of them—”

Nicodemus was known as a teacher of Israel, a learned man who was highly respected in Israel.

He in turn, was highly respectful of Jesus, whom Nicodemus called a teacher who has come from God.

Nicodemus marvelled at Jesus, just like the crowd, and just like the temple police.

It’s ironic that the Pharisees didn’t know that one of their own marvelled at Jesus’ words.

And what’s even more ironic is that God doesn’t necessarily shows himself to the rulers and the Pharisees.

In fact, Jesus says in Matthew 11:25 that God has hidden these things from the wise and intelligent and has revealed them to infants.

We can only understand God as God chooses to reveal himself, not because of how clever we are.

The fact that rulers and pharisees don’t believe in Jesus doesn’t necessarily mean that Jesus is not from God.

Second, the Pharisees pride themselves as someone in the know.

They claim to know the law, but do they, really?

Nicodemus subtly challenge their boast about the law by asking whether the law hears from the person and knows what he’s doing first.

The law verifies whether a person is right or wrong before arresting and condemning the man.

Nicodemus knew the law.

However, the Pharisees either were ignorant of the law or they chose to ignore the law.

And so, their boast in the law was undermined.

They didn’t know the law, and if they did, they chose to disobey the law.

Thirdly, their bias was exposed.

They were saying that Nicodemus had sided himself with Jesus of Galilee, a place known to be inferior because no prophet had come from Galilee.

But is it true though?

The Bible lists both Jonah and Nahum, prophets from the Old Testament, as being from the region of Galilee.

Should it be a surprise that God has raised another prophet from Galilee?

Besides, Jesus wasn’t originally from Galilee.

He was born in Bethlehem, the very place that God’s said that Messiah would come.

The crowd has rightly alluded to this only a few verses earlier in verse 42, “Doesn’t the Scripture say that the Messiah comes from David’s offspring and from the town of Bethlehem, where David lived?”

The Pharisees were so biased against Jesus that they didn’t even think about asking Jesus where he came from.

They just assumed Jesus was from Galilee.

Their bias against Jesus was exposed.

Again, we see the boasting and bias against Jesus exposed in today’s world.

In Australia today, only 49% of people think that Jesus is a real historical person.

Most Australians don’t believe that Jesus existed.

However, Dr John Dickson, who has a PhD in Ancient history said “I wager that 99 per cent of professional ancient historians — atheist, Christian, Jewish, or whatever — would agree with this minority view.”

That is, even though many Australians might sneer against Christianity and boast that Jesus never existed, many professional historians, even non-Christian ones, can demonstrate that Jesus existed in history.

For many of us who weren’t born in a Western country, we might have a bias against Christianity and say that it’s a western religion and so don’t want anything to do with it.

However, Christianity didn’t start off as a western religion.

It started from Israel in the Middle East and had travelled to both the East and the West.

In fact, it was ridiculed by the West for the first 200 years.

People who called themselves Christians were heavily persecuted and killed in the West for the first 200 years.

But by God’s grace, Christianity grew and had greatly influence the West over the past two thousand years.

Over time, Christianity had travelled to the East, and had a wonderful impact on many African and Asian countries.

In fact, most Christians are not powerful, white, Western men.

A typical Christian today is a non-white woman living in the global South, with lower-than-average levels of societal safety and proper health care.

Don’t be conformed to the society’s bias against Christianity.

Instead, be like Nicodemus and ask respectful questions that exposes the bias and boasts against Christianity.

For example, when they tell you that Jesus is not historical, ask them, “Thank you for sharing that, but how did you come up with that? Who told you that Jesus is not real?”

When they tell you that Christianity is a western religion, ask them, “Thank you for sharing that, but how did you come up with that? How many western Christians do you know? How many non-western Christians do you know?”

Marvel at Jesus’ words

But even more importantly, help them to marvel at Jesus’ words.

When the temple police came back to the Pharisees they reported, “No man has ever spoken like this.”

The last thing that Jesus was recorded to have said prior to their statement was this, beginning from verse 37, “If anyone is thirsty, let him come to me and drink. 38 The one who believes in me, as the Scripture has said, will have streams of living water flow from deep within him.”

Listen to the audacious words of Jesus.

Jesus says those who are thirsty, who has a spiritual thirst that can’t be satisfied by the world, will find satisfaction in him.

Jesus is not just a teacher who points to God as the source of satisfaction.

Jesus points to himself as the source of satisfaction.

Jesus is in fact saying, “I am the answer to your thirst!”

It’s an audacious thing for Jesus to say!

Jesus has many more audacious things to say, about himself and about God.

No one has spoken like him before.

And so, when we come before him, we need to prepare ourselves and be honest when you come before him.

We need to lay aside our boasts and our biases.

When we come before Jesus, we need to ask whether we have any boasts and biases that stop us from coming to him.

That’s why we must come before him in prayer as we come to hear his words.

As we come before him in honesty and in humility, as we lay down our boasts and biases, we will agree that no man has ever spoke like this.

We’ll see that there’s no man like him.

There’s no one like Jesus.

CS Lewis, the great Christian writer once said, “Either this man was, and is, the Son of God: or else a madman or something worse.

You can shut Him up for a fool, you can spit at Him and kill Him as a demon;

or you can fall at His feet and call Him Lord and God.

But let us not come with any patronizing nonsense about His being a great human teacher. He has not left that open to us. He did not intend to.”

(Mere Christianity, 55–56)

Jesus didn’t give us an option to just merely listen to his words as a teacher.

The Pharisees understood that but chose to reject him.

They were fools who were blinded by their own boasts and biases.

Don’t be like them.

Don’t be like their equivalents today, people in society who would dismiss Jesus based on their boasts and biases against Jesus.

Don’t bow to the society’s pressure to conform in their boasts and biases against Jesus.

They don’t know better; they are not better.

Jesus, the Son of God, knows best. He is the Best.

Listen to His words.

Listen to his words as we come together as Church on Sundays and as we meet in Growth Groups throughout the week.

As we come together, help one another to see our own boasts and biases against Jesus.

We all have them, even Christians.

When we’re by ourselves, it’s often very difficult to see our boasts and biases because we’re so blind to them.

That’s why it’s so important for us to continue to gather and come together.

When we come together, we come before God’s word in humility, the great antidote to boasts and biases.

With God’s help, with the help and support of one another, let’s lay our boasts and biases aside and come to listen clearly to his words.

Today’s passage shows the irony of the Pharisees, who thought that they knew better when they really didn’t.

When someone looks at our life, will they see a life of irony too?

Will they see someone who thinks that we know better but in fact really don’t.

Friends, don’t be fooled by your boasts and biases.

Instead, marvel at Jesus words.

To conclude, remember: Not boasts, nor biases. Instead believe in Jesus.

Come before him in humility as you hear his word.

When you hear it and have an urge to reject it, ask yourself, why am I rejecting God’s word?

What boasts and biases do I have to reject what God is saying?

On what basis do I think that I know better than Jesus?

Friends, not boasts, nor biases. Instead believe in Jesus.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What is one boast against Jesus I’m tempted to make?

What is one unfair bias that I’m tempted to have against Jesus and his church?

When was the last time I marvelled at Jesus’ words?

Friends, may we always marvel at Jesus’ words.

May we come not with boasts nor biases but a firm belief in the Lord Jesu.

Let’s pray,

“Father of mercies, thank you that in your love, you’ve sent Jesus, your one and only Son. Please forgive us for our boasts, thinking that we know better than Jesus. Please forgive us for our biases, looking down on Jesus and his Church, thinking that we’re better. By your mercy, humble us as we come before you and the Lord Jesus. By your Spirit, let us marvel at the words of Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
