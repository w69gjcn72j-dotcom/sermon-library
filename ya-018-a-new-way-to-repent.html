Young Adults (19–30)

A New Way to Repent

Luke 5:27–39 · Sunday 8 November 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah · Sermon by Rev David Yung

Big Idea: Fellow sick sinners, Jesus calls us to a new repentance centred on him, to leave everything, follow him, feast with him and fast in his absence.

Aim: by the end of the study, group members will

see what genuine repentance looks like: Levi stands up and leaves everything to follow the Doctor, and repentance is personal, not transactional;

understand why we feast while the Bridegroom is here, fast in his absence, and why Jesus cannot be a patch on a curated life;

name where they are staying seated or pretending to be healthy, and take one concrete step to serve or invite someone this month.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

1. Connect (10 min)

  • Think of the last public apology you saw (a celebrity, a brand, a friend in the group chat). What made it sound like “sorry, not sorry”? What would a real one need to look like?
  • What is the version of “I’m fine” you give at uni, at work or in the share house when you’re not? Why is it so hard to say the sorry or the “I need help”?

2. Read: Luke 5:27–39 (CSB)

27 After this, he went out and saw a tax collector named Levi sitting at the tax office, and he said to him, “Follow me.”

28 So, leaving everything behind, he got up and began to follow him.

29 Then Levi hosted a grand banquet for him at his house. A large crowd of tax collectors and others were guests with them.

30 But the Pharisees and their scribes were complaining to his disciples, “Why do you eat and drink with tax collectors and sinners?”

31 Jesus replied to them, “It is not those who are healthy who need a doctor, but those who are sick.

32 I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.”

33 Then they said to him, “John’s disciples fast often and say prayers, and those of the Pharisees do the same, but yours eat and drink.”

34 Jesus said to them, “You can’t make the wedding guests fast while the groom is with them, can you?

35 But the time will come when the groom will be taken away from them—then they will fast in those days.”

36 He also told them a parable: “No one tears a patch from a new garment and puts it on an old garment. Otherwise, not only will he tear the new, but also the piece from the new garment will not match the old.

37 And no one puts new wine into old wineskins. Otherwise, the new wine will burst the skins, it will spill, and the skins will be ruined.

38 No, new wine is put into fresh wineskins.

39 And no one, after drinking old wine, wants new, for he says, ‘The old is good.’”

3. Context

Levi was a Jew who collected taxes for the Roman occupiers and probably took a cut. He was the ultimate sell-out. When Jesus says “Follow me” he is still sitting at his booth. The Pharisees, normally the VIP guests at such parties, are left outside to complain. They fast twice a week to show God they are sorry; Jesus’ friends are eating and drinking. Jesus answers with a doctor, a wedding feast, a patch and wineskins.

4. Discuss

  1. (vv.27–28) Levi’s job made him a traitor and a cheat to his own people, and he was still at work when Jesus said, “Follow me.” What does Levi do, and what does that tell us about repentance when Jesus calls?
  2. (vv.27–28) Repentance blossomed from Levi’s life; it wasn’t forced out of him. How is that different from the “sorry” we give under pressure, or when we get caught, or when the call-out comes? Where do we say sorry to God yet stay seated?
  3. (vv.29–32) What is the Pharisees’ complaint, and what does Jesus’ doctor line tell us about who is sick? Why can only those who admit they are sick be helped? What does “pretending to be healthy” look like on a curated feed?
  4. (vv.29–32, application) Levi left his booth but opened his home. Where do we, like the Pharisees, grumble that someone else got more grace than they deserve? What would it look like for your share house or group chat to be a banquet for people who know they need Jesus?
  5. (vv.33–34) Why did the Pharisees think prayer and fasting, not eating and drinking, were the marks of real repentance? What does Jesus mean by “what time is it?”, and what is he claiming by calling himself the groom?
  6. (v.35) When will the disciples fast, and what does that point to? Why should Christians do both, feast and fast? Where are you tempted to feast and forget the broken world, or to despair and forget the joy of Christ? Who needs you to remind them to feast, and who to fast?
  7. (vv.36–38) What do the new patch and the new wine in old wineskins say about adding Jesus to a life that stays the same? Why does the new way of repentance burst the old?
  8. (vv.36–39, application) Where are you keeping Jesus to Sundays, or to the parts of life you choose? What would it look like to let him be Lord of all of it (study, work, relationships, money, your online life) this week?

5. Live It Out

KOG 3·2·1·0 — pray for 3, connect with 2, invite 1, 0 left unloved.

  • Pray for 3 — three friends, flatmates or colleagues who don’t yet know Jesus. Pray for them by name each day.
  • Connect with 2 — a coffee, a meal, a game or a walk with two of them this week.
  • Invite 1 — invite one of your three to our Community Christmas Concert on 13 December (we are praying for 120+), or to your next group. Think too about serving: assistants for KOGKids, KOGMini and KOGYouth, and accompanists for Chinese Parallel.
  • 0 left unloved — look for the person who’s on the edge on Sunday or in your group chat, and welcome them the way Levi’s party welcomed outsiders.

This week’s challenge: Stop pretending to be healthy. This week, tell one person in your group one area where Jesus has been a patch, not Lord, and ask them to pray for you. Then pick one way to serve or to host: a meal at your place, or offering to help with KOGKids, KOGMini, KOGYouth or Chinese Parallel.

Memory verse: “It is not those who are healthy who need a doctor, but those who are sick. I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.” (Luke 5:31–32)

6. Pray

  • Thank the Father for sending Jesus, our great Doctor, to sick sinners like us. Confess where we have stayed seated, curated a healthy image, or grumbled that others got more grace.
  • Ask the Holy Spirit to change our hearts, to help us rejoice in Jesus’ presence and long for his return, and to stretch us as we live for him. Pray for our youths and young adults, and for God’s direction and provision for them.
  • Pray by name for your 3, that they would hear Jesus’ “Follow me” and stand up, and for one to be invited.

7. Leader’s Notes

Framing. Joyfully point to Jesus. Guests and not-yet-Christians may be present; never corner anyone. The good news is not “say sorry harder” but that the Doctor calls the sick and welcomes them to his table. Create a group where “I’m not fine” is safe to say.

Question helps

Q1 — Levi was the classic sell-out, and Jesus invited him anyway. He got up and left everything, including his income. We have no record of what he said; his action spoke. Repentance means recognising we have been wrong and turning back to God, shown in how we respond when Jesus calls.

Q2 — Genuine repentance depends on our relationship with Jesus; it’s personal, not transactional. If we are truly sorry, we will follow Jesus. Compare the forced or PR apology with a life that changes.

Q3 — The Pharisees complained about “tax collectors and sinners”. Everyone is sick; only those who admit it come to the Doctor. The “righteous” don’t say sorry to Jesus and so aren’t forgiven. Jesus heals us so that we repent and turn back, not so that we can sin again.

Q4 — Levi opened his home; those shunned were welcomed and the shunners were shunned. Pray for the Christmas concert on 13 December (120+) as our banquet. Stop pretending to be healthy: repentance starts with admitting you are sick and hearing “Follow me”.

Q5 — The Pharisees fasted 104 days a year; God commanded only one, the Day of Atonement. Jesus asks what time it is: the Bridegroom is here, so celebrate. Sacrifices could not change hearts; God promised a new heart (Ezekiel 36:26), and Jesus, the Heart Surgeon, gives it.

Q6 — The groom “taken away” points to the cross. Now we feast, because we are forgiven and adopted and Jesus is with us always, and we fast, because we long for his return. Repentance is both joy and yearning. Hebrews 10:25: gather, and remind each other. Be there for the friend who needs to feast and the one who needs to fast.

Q7 — New cloth shrinks and tears the old garment; fermenting new wine bursts old, stiff wineskins. The new repentance can’t be patched onto the old. Romans 13:14: put on the Lord Jesus Christ. Levi left his job to organise the feast; all or nothing.

Q8 — If Jesus is Lord, he decides how much of life is his: all of it. Ask: has there been any change, or am I still living the old way? Saying sorry is only the beginning; the heart of repentance is not a Jesus patch but the Person of Jesus.
