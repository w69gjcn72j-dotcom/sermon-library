Good morning, everyone and welcome to the first Sunday in winter at Church!

Is everyone warm?

It’s been a cold week over the past week, hasn’t it?

On Monday night we had winds up to 90km/hr in some parts of Sydney.

The wind was severe not only in Sydney but up north in Queensland as well as down south in Tasmania.

It was dangerous not only to be outside but to be inside as well.

Trees fell on top of homes and on top of cars.

Thankfully, besides property damages, there hasn’t been reports of lives lost due to the wind.

A lot of people were ready for the storm and prepared for it because the weather bureau had warned us through the news that the storm was coming.

It’s a blessing to have services like the weather bureau and the State Emergency Services who can help us through the icy storms.

However, how do we prepare for the other storms we face in life?

How do we prepare for storms of broken families, or the storms of ill health?

What can we do to prepare for the dark storms of life?

That’s what we’re going to see in today’s passage.

In today’s passage, we’ll see the disciples of Jesus in the middle of a ferocious storm,

And we’ll see what Jesus will do about it.

We will see three things:

Journeying in dark waters

The voice over the waters

Invite Jesus on your boat

Before we look into it, let’s pray,

“Father, please open our eyes so that we’ll see the beauty and glory of Jesus. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”

Journeying in dark waters

Look with me at verses 16 to 17, “When evening came, his disciples went down to the sea, 17 got into a boat, and started across the sea to Capernaum. Darkness had already set in, but Jesus had not yet come to them.”

As you read these verses, you get the sense that there’s an increase in darkness.

Firstly, it comes as the evening and then darkness sets in.

Corresponding to this darkness is the absence of Jesus.

Jesus wasn’t with the disciples as they started to sail across the sea.

In fact, the disciples were three to four miles out at sea already.

Has anyone here been to Brighton-Le-Sands? It’s about 5 minutes drive east of us.

From the beach of Brighton-Le-Sands, you can almost see the heads of the bay.

Here’s a picture of it here.

That’s about how far the disciples had rowed from the shore.

They were in the middle of the Sea of Galilee.

While they were in the middle of the Sea, high wind arose and the sea began to churn.

It was as if the sea had woke up in the middle of the darkness.

The sea had suddenly come alive threatened the lives of the disciples!

When you have big wind, you have big waves.

And when you have big waves, the boat can overturn, putting everyone on the boat in danger.

Not only did the disciples face the wind but they were in utter darkness.

They were battered by strong wind and high waves, and they were in the dark.

Their lives were at stake!

Though we might had never sailed through a dark storm like the disciples had, we do face other forms of dark storms in our lives, don’t we?

Talk to the person next to you and list some examples of dark storms that we face in life.

An example might be a health issue, or a national crisis.

What are some dark storms that we face in life?

Talk to the person next to you. You have thirty seconds.

What’s a dark storm in life?

We have a roaming microphone; please speak into it.

There are at least three categories of dark storms that we face in our lives.

A dark storm in life can be communal.

These are dark storms that we experience together as a community.

There are dark storms in many parts of the world today.

The most obvious one has been and continues to be the pandemic.

Many people around the world had been infected by COVID or had been in close contact with someone who had it.

It’s been a dark cloud that’s affected almost everyone in the world over the past two years.

Other dark storms include wars in Ukraine and other places, the oppression in Afghanistan, the unrest in Chile.

These are communal dark storms.

Then there are personal dark storms.

These are storms we face on a personal level.

These can include marriage issues, the tension between different generations, workplace bullying and domestic violence.

These personal storms can cause conflicts between individuals, which lead us to the last dark storm.

It’s the dark storm of the soul; the internal dark storm.

These dark storms can come in the form of depression and anxiety.

Closely related to these are physical illnesses and other health issues.

Many people among us struggle with a health issue or another, often suffering from effects that no one else know about.

And of course, the dark storm of the soul includes those who struggle with spiritual issues.

These spiritual issues include turning ourselves to the bigger, existential questions of life.

Why am I here? Is there a God? Is there life after death? Am I ready to face death?

Over the course of our lives, we will face various forms of these dark storms.

We will face communal dark storms, personal dark storms and internal dark storms.

Christians are not exempt.

There’s a myth going around that says everything will be OK if you become a Christian.

When you become a Christian, you will be prosperous (or at least not poor)

When you become a Christian, you will be healthy (at least not disabled)

When you become a Christian, you will be happy (at least not depressed).

But it’s as myth; the Bible does not teach that you will be free from the worries of this world when you become a Christian.

The Bible never promises that the dark storms of life won’t come.

Christians and non-Christians alike will face the dark storms of life.

Earlier this week, I found out from a pastor friend that his wife was expecting a baby.

However, they soon found out that the baby has a birth defect and will almost certainly have a very short life.

It’s very sad but it the dark clouds of life happen to Christians, even if they are pastors.

How do we face the dark clouds of life when they come?

The first question to ask is not “how”?

Rather, when we face the dark clouds of life, the first question to ask must be “who”.

We must turn to him who speaks over the waters.

The voice over the waters

Look with me at verse 19, “After they had rowed about three or four miles, they saw Jesus walking on the sea. He was coming near the boat, and they were afraid.”

As the disciples were facing their dark storm, Jesus walks over to them, on the sea!

It’s not what people usually do; if we walk on the sea, we’ll sink.

What Jesus was doing was not part of our normal experience.

And so, the disciples responded as you would expect: They were afraid.

What Jesus did was supernatural, nothing short of miraculous.

Now there may be some among us who still only believe in a world where reality is limited to what you can see and touch.

If you still believe only in a world that you can see and touch, you probably won’t believe in miracles.

And you must be pretty confident, based on your knowledge and experience, that you have covered all the bases, that there’s nothing beyond what you can see and touch.

However, most people throughout history and indeed, most people around the world today, believe that there is a reality beyond what we can see and touch.

In fact, most people in the world today believe that there is one God and others believe that there are some sort of higher powers.

Christians believe that there is one God who made the heavens and the earth.

Christians believe that this God is God the Father, God the Son and God the Holy Spirit.

And it’s God the Son himself, Jesus, who had walked on the sea to the disciples.

By walking on water, Jesus showed himself to do what God was said to do in the Old Testament.

For example, in Psalm 77:16 and 19, we read: The water saw you, God. The water saw you; it trembled. Even the depths shook…Your way went through the sea and your path through the vast water, but your footprints were unseen.”

God was said to walk on water and Jesus literally walked on water.

And also in Psalm 29:3-4, “The voice of the Lord is above the waters. The God of glory thunders—the Lord, above the vast water,4 the voice of the Lord in power, the voice of the Lord in splendour.”

Jesus speaks above the waters, just as the voice of the Lord is above the waters.

Jesus does what God does.

Jesus was in control, even in the middle of the storm.

No storm can overcome Jesus for Jesus has made everything, including the storms.

Jesus is the all-powerful God who walks even over waters.

Jesus is also comforting.

Listen to what he said to his disciples, “It is I. Don’t be afraid.”

The disciples were afraid.

They might had been afraid of the storm but they were definitely afraid of Jesus as he walked towards them.

Who walks in water in the middle of the storm?

But Jesus comforted them as he drew near: It is I, don’t be afraid.

They recognised his voice.

“It is I” – it’s a common saying; it’s another way of saying, “Hey, it’s me!”

But the significant of this saying will become more obvious as we continue to read through the Gospel of John.

“It is I”; but who are you?

Who is this “I”?

In the next few chapters of John, we’ll hear Jesus say,

“I am the Bread of Life”, “I am the Light of the World”, “I am the door”, “I am the Good Shepherd”, “I am the Resurrection and the Life”, “I am the Way, the Truth and the Life” and “I am the True vine”.

These are known as the seven I am statements of the Gospel of John.

“It is I”. Why are you? Who is Jesus?

That’s what Jesus going to unpack over the next few chapters of John.

“It is I”. These are extraordinarily comforting words for the disciples in the middle of the dark storm.

It’s because Jesus is who He is that Christians don’t need to be afraid.

That’s the mark of a true Christian: They know Jesus’ voice.

They find comfort in Jesus’ voice; his voice is the welcomed, familiar voice in the middle of the storms of life.

In the dark storms of life, they listen to Jesus’ voice and find comfort and reassurance in it.

My pastor friend preached on God’s word a couple of months before he found out about his baby.

The passage was from Ecclesiastes and there he was reminded that the world was broken.

My friend was encouraged from God’s word that even though the world was broken, God was still in charge and that he was showing them that there was more than the life we have in the here and now.

In the dark storms of life, my friend and his wife found comfort in the words of the Bible.

In the dark storms of life, my friend and his wife found comfort in the words of the one who says, “It is I. Don’t be afraid”.

Jesus’ voice is recorded in the word of God, the Bible.

He is speaking but are we listening?

True disciples recognise the voice of Jesus. We long for it.

That’s why a large part of our service on Sunday mornings is devoted to hearing from God’s word.

We recognise Jesus’s voice, and we want to hear more of it.

What does Jesus want me to do about the situation I’m in?

What does he have to say about the dark storm I found myself swimming in?

Friends, as we come to Church on Sunday mornings, always come with an expectation to hear from God through his word.

Don’t get distracted by the darkness; don’t get distracted by the storms of life.

Don’t get distracted on your phone

Rather, listen, closely to his voice.

When you do listen to Jesus’ voice, you’ll invite him into your life.

For the disciples, after they’ve listened to his voice, they invited Jesus on their boat.

Invite Jesus on your boat

Look with me at the last verse, verse 21, “Then they were willing to take him on board, and at once the boat was at the shore where they were heading.”

When they recognised who Jesus was, they took him on the boat.

Notice that there was no mention of the storm anymore.

There was no record that the storm stopped; the storm could’ve stopped but it wasn’t the main point.

The main point was not the absence of the storm but the presence of Jesus with the disciples.

Over the past few months, you would’ve seen pictures or videos of Volodymyr Zelenskyy, the President of Ukraine.

President Zelenskyy’s country Ukraine had been under attack for more than one hundred days now.

However, rather than leaving for safety, he chose to stay in the country, spending most of his time in the capital Kyiv, to support his people.

Ukraine and her people are going through a very difficult time; they’re going through a dark storm with uncertain days ahead.

President Zelenskyy wants them to know that he’s with them through the storm; he’ll be there until the very end.

As Christians, we will continue to face storms in our life; we will continue to face storms on this side of heaven.

Jesus has never promised that they will be no more storms.

However, we long for Jesus’ presence to be with us through the storms.

He will be with us in our sufferings, in our pains.

He will be with us even in death, for he promises to take those who trust him home.

Notice also what happens as soon as Jesus stepped into the boat.

The disciples miraculously arrived at where they were heading.

They got to their destination as soon as Jesus stepped into the boat.

For those who trusts in him, Jesus has sent His very own Spirit on his believers as a guarantee that he will be with us until the very end.

By his Spirit, Jesus is present with those who believe in him and he promise to take us home with him.

By His Spirit, Jesus promises to take us back to his Home, where he is now.

If you have the Spirit of Jesus, you know that you are heading home.

By His Holy Spirit, Jesus is taking you through the storm and into his home.

That’s the comfort that Christians have.

If we trust in Jesus, no matter how great of a storm we face, no matter how dark the future may be, he will take us home.

Just a few months before the war in Ukraine, a video was circulated around the internet.

It shows a Christian group; maybe it’s a family, maybe a Bible study, or maybe a family Church, I’m not sure.

In the midst of the rumours of an upcoming war, they sang this song.

Take a listen…

Here are the words:

He'll not let my soul be lost His promises shall last Bought by Him at such a cost He will hold me fast

He will hold me fast He will hold me fast For my Saviour loves me so He will hold me fast

Their trust is in their Saviour, Jesus Christ, who will hold them fast.

That’s what Christians do.

As we prepare to face the storms of life, Christians turn to Jesus, we listen for his voice.

For what is life but preparing to go from one storm to another?

Friends, brothers and sisters, listen to what Jesus say to his disciples, “It is I, Don’t be afraid.”

If you have Jesus, there is no need to be afraid.

He’s made the heavens and the earth; he’s made the storms.

Having him in your life means having the assurance of reaching home, his home, where true peace and safety lies.

And so, invite Jesus into your boat! Invite Jesus into your life.

Listen to his voice.

That’s what true disciples do; true disciples listen and obey.

What is Jesus saying to you this morning?

If we listen and obey Jesus’ voice, we don’t need to be afraid of what comes next.

However, if we don’t listen to Jesus’ voice, if we don’t obey, then there’s plenty to fear.

The storms of this life are but a drop in the ocean compare with what come after this life for those who don’t trust in Jesus.

It’s a storm that you can never sail out of and a darkness that you can’t escape from .

That’s what hell will be like.

It doesn’t matter if you are on a cruise if you end up in hell.

I would rather be on a lifeboat that takes us to heaven than a cruise that takes us to hell.

If you’re not sure whether Jesus is in your boat, then please come and see me or Po Ki or Jiawu or Range and we would love to share how you can be sure.

True disciples of Jesus know Jesus’ voice.

No matter how big the storm is, no matter how dark it gets, true disciples of Jesus will listen for Jesus’ voice.

They have Jesus in their boats.

So what does this mean for us?

With Christ in my boat, I can smile at the storm.

Even though my fellow pastor friend is going through a very difficult time, he’s pointing people to Jesus.

He wants people to know that we can have confidence in God’s goodness and love no matter how dark and how hard things get.

If you have Jesus in your boat, you, too, will want to share the hope that you have with other people.

You will want to let them know that you’re not afraid of the storms of life because you have Jesus in your life.

If you do know and trust in Jesus, share this good news with someone this week!

If you have Jesus in your boat, you will want to hear more of Jesus’ voice.

Last week, Range and I got together and we worked out that there are 85% of us in a Growth group of some sort.

It might be a Zoom meeting or an in-person group like KOG Youth.

That’s great!

In Growth groups, we keep pointing people to Jesus.

Make if your priority to attend a Growth Group.

All of us, even the best of us, can fail to hear Jesus’ voice.

And so, let’s help each other here on Sundays as well as in Growth groups to listen to Jesus’ voice.

Remembering that with Christ in our boat we can smile at the storm.

Here are three questions for us to think about this week:

What is one dark storm that you’re facing at the moment?

What has Jesus already said in the Bible about the dark storm you’re facing?

How have you been sharing with other people about the hope you have in Jesus?

Friends, whatever storm you’re facing, whatever storm you’re in, listen to Jesus’ voice.

Invite him in your boat.

With Christ in our boat, we can smile at the storm.

Father, please forgive us when we take our eyes off Jesus. Please keep our eyes focussed firmly on him. Please help us to listen for his voice whenever we’re afraid. Thank you that He is the Lord of heaven and earth and that because he is who he said he is, we don’t need to be afraid. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
