The Mind of Christ

Passage: 1 Corinthians 2:1-16

Big Idea: The apostolic preacher preaches Christ crucified and speaks God’s hidden wisdom to the mature

Nothing but Christ Crucified

No wisdom for the mature without the Spirit

Have the Mind of Christ Crucified

One of the great joys of being a Christians is to be in a Christian community.

As a community, we can share our resources, we can share our lives and we can share our experiences.

There are many times when I face difficulties as a Christian and I have no idea how to proceed.

I would pray about it and try to find God’s answer from the Bible.

Sometimes, I would also go to those who are wiser than me to talk things through.

I thank God for these brothers and sisters who have shared their God-given wisdom with me in a number of situations.

What does it mean to have God-given wisdom?

Does every Christian have God-given wisdom?

Today, we’re going to see what it means to have God-given wisdom.

If you have your Bible there, please open up to 2 Corinthians 2, beginning from verse 1.

We’re going to see three things:

Nothing but Christ Crucified

No wisdom for the mature without the Spirit

Have the Mind of Christ Crucified

Before we look into it, let’s pray,

“Father, by your Spirit, please show us what it means to be truly wise. We pray this in Jesus’ name, Amen.”

Nothing but Christ Crucified

Last week, we read in 1 Corinthians chapter one about how different the wisdom of the world is to the wisdom of God.

We read that the Christians preach Christ crucified, a stumbling block to the Jews and foolishness to the Gentiles.

The Bible continues with the same theme and highlights it even more.

Not only do Christians preach Christ crucified; Christians seek to know nothing except Christ crucified.

Look with me at 1 Corinthians 2:1-2, “When I came to you, brothers and sisters, announcing the mystery of God to you, I did not come with brilliance of speech or wisdom. I decided to know nothing among you except Jesus Christ and him crucified.”

The person who is speaking here is the Apostle Paul.

Paul’s one of the first and definitely one of the most important church planters in the history of Christianity.

He’s planted churches in Ancient Rome, in Philippi and of course, in Corinth.

He’s written thirteen out of the twenty-seven letters in the New Testament.

He had a wonderful impact on countless Christians for the next two thousand years.

But here in 1 Corinthians, he says that when he went to Corinth, he didn’t come as a rock star evangelist.

Unlike many speakers in Corinth at that time, he didn’t come with brilliance of speech or wisdom.

He didn’t use big words.

More than that, he didn’t offer them a wide variety to the Corinthians.

He resorted to know nothing among them except Jesus Christ and him crucified.

That’s all that Paul, the Church planter extraordinaire, talks about: Jesus Christ and him crucified.

That’s what he wants the Christians to know: Jesus Christ and him crucified.

When I was in University, there’s a question that’s often asked as the year approaches the end.

It’s the same question, no matter what class I was in.

Can you guess what the question is?

“Sir, is this in the exam?”

That’s what’s on everybody’s mind as we prepared for exam.

I’m sure there are lots of nice things you can tell us but there’s only one thing on our minds right now: is this in the exam?

That’s what it’s like with Paul.

There could be many things that he can tell the Corinthians, but only one thing mattered: Jesus Christ, and him crucified.

That’s the one thing the wanted the Corinthians, and indeed everyone to know: Jesus Christ, and him crucified.

That’s the benchmark of what a good Christian message is.

Just like students who ask, “is this in the exam?”, wise Christians will ask, “Is this message about Christ crucified?”

As a faithful preacher of God’s word, Paul doesn’t draw attention to himself.

His job is not to tell people how brilliant Paul is but how brilliant Jesus Christ is.

He’s come to announce the mystery of God as revealed in Jesus Christ.

That’s the sum of his ministry.

He’s said that he came to the Corinthians in weakness.

He hasn’t shown himself to be a strong public speaker like the others.

He might have looked unattractive or sounded unimpressive, or a bit of both.

He looked weak in front of the Corinthians.

He also says that he came with fear and trembling.

Did he have a fear of public speaking? Was he afraid of the crowd?

No, he wasn’t afraid of the crowd.

Rather, he was afraid of God.

The two words, fear and trembling, have often been used to describe how people felt in God’s presence.

In Paul’s letters to the Philippians, he tells the Philippians Christians to work out their own salvation with fear and trembling, for it is God who is working in them.

As Paul reveals God’s message to the Corinthians, he knows that he’s doing God’s work and he does it with fear and trembling,

As any good and faithful servants of God would.

And so, Paul appears weak before men and he trembles with fear before God.

Why does Paul keep emphasising his weaknesses?

Look with me at verses 4-5, “My speech and my preaching were not with persuasive words of wisdom but with a demonstration of the Spirit’s power, so that your faith might not be based on human wisdom but on God’s power.”

Paul emphasises how unimpressive he is as a speaker to remind the Corinthians that they shouldn’t base their faith in persuasive words of wisdom.

Rather, they should base their faith on God’s power.

What does God’s power look like?

While there are times when God has demonstrated miraculous works or power in the life of Paul, I don’t think this is what Paul’s referring to here.

Rather, I think he’s here referring to the power of God as shown through the changed lives of people who have believed in the message of the Crucified Christ.

In April 1902, some 120 years ago to this day, a great evangelistic mission was held in Melbourne.

They organised to have every house in Melbourne visited twice.

They had fifty local evangelists preaching in halls and large tents.

A Melbourne doctor, Willian Warren, reported,

“Within a few weeks the Spirit of God laid hold of the Christians and there was a conscious assurance that the city and its suburbs of nearly five hundred thousand population was going to be moved as never before…A policeman averred that since the mission opened in his district, he and his fellow constables had had practically nothing to do…Believers greeted each other with: The Big revival has begun. Glory to God.”

In four weeks, 8642 persons have accepted Jesus Christ as their Lord and Saviour.

As they heard the message of Christ Crucified, they believed and they repented.

God’s power is seen not only when they said that they’ve become Christians.

God’s power is also seen when their behaviour matched their confessions.

That’s what happens when people genuinely believe the gospel of the Crucified Christ.

To believe the gospel is more than just uttering a set of words, as important as these words may be.

To believe the gospel is to surrender ourselves to God and to trust in the Lord Jesus Christ.

To believe the gospel is to give up worldly wisdom for the wisdom of God.

It’s putting our trust not in the brilliance of speech or eloquent speeches.

Rather, it’s to rest in God’s power and God’s wisdom instead.

And at the heart of God’s wisdom is this: Jesus Christ and him crucified.

No wisdom for the mature without the Spirit

And to know this wisdom from God, we need the Holy Spirit.

For there is no wisdom for the mature without the Holy Spirit of God.

Look with me at verses 6-7, “We do, however, speak a wisdom among the mature, but not a wisdom of this age, or of the rulers of this age, who are coming to nothing. On the contrary, we speak God’s hidden wisdom in a mystery, a wisdom God predestined before the ages for our glory.”

For most of the last two chapters, Paul’s been saying that the wisdom of the world is no good at all.

And so, you might think that he’s against wisdom.

He’s against worldly wisdom but there is a wisdom that he wants to focus on.

It’s not a wisdom of this age but a wisdom for the mature.

It’s wisdom from God, a hidden wisdom from God.

It’s a wisdom we can’t get a hold of unless God reveals it to us.

It’s like this.

Before I was a minister, I worked as a pharmacist for seven years.

This was back in the day when most Doctors use pens to write on prescriptions the medicine that they want their patients to take.

Sometimes, we have Doctors with good handwritings.

Other times, we have Doctors with no so good handwritings.

Any doctors or pharmacists here?

Here’s an example of a hand-written prescription.

Can anyone read it? It’s a mystery, isn’t it?

Even to a Doctor, it might look like a mystery.

I haven’t been a Pharmacist for a long time now but there are a few things I do recognise.

The first is Cephalexin 500mg 2 cap twice a day.

The last is Zantac 150mg 1 twice a day.

I can’t tell the second one and so I probably would call the Doctor to confirm.

It’s probably easier now days since most Doctors use computers to print their scripts.

But if you haven’t learnt the medicine or familiar with the shorthand, it can be a mystery.

The Bible tells us that God’s wisdom is a mystery.

It’s hidden and it needs to be revealed to us.

We can’t come to understand it for ourselves.

Even if we have the best of what human wisdom has to offer, we’ll still won’t be able to access it.

However, God has planned this wisdom for our glory, even before the time began.

Unlike human wisdom, which comes to nothing, God’s wisdom leads us to glory.

The Bible says that none of the rulers of this age knew it, for if they did, they wouldn’t have crucified the Lord of glory.

It’s a wisdom that’s hidden from us.

Look with me at verse 9, “But as it is written,

What no eye has seen, no ear has heard,

and no human heart has conceived—

God has prepared these things for those who love him.”

God’s wisdom is not empirical; we can’t see it and we can’t hear it.

You can’t measure God’s wisdom; it’s not a thing that you can get your hands on.

Neither is God’s wisdom limited to our human comprehension.

Human minds don’t conceive or come up with what God’s wisdom got in store.

It’s beyond our imagination; it’s beyond our comprehension.

In his hidden, incomprehensible wisdom, God has prepared something wonderful, something glorious for those who love him.

That’s the key to understanding God’s wisdom.

Human wisdom can’t get to God’s wisdom.

The best of human endeavour can’t get to God’s wisdom.

Rather, God will reveal his glorious wisdom to those who love him.

And to love him, we need to first come to know him.

How do you know this hidden, incomprehensible God?

We come to know him by his Spirit.

Look with me at verse 10-11, “Now God has revealed these things to us by the Spirit, since the Spirit searches everything, even the depths of God. For who knows a person’s thoughts except his spirit within him? In the same way, no one knows the thoughts of God except the Spirit of God.”

How do we know about God? How do we know about his hidden wisdom?

We know God and his wisdom when his Spirit reveals God to us.

Here’s our question for this week.

How many times does Paul refers to God’s Spirit from verses 10-14?

How many times does Paul refer to the Holy Spirit in these five verses?

Talk with the person next to you. Send your answer in by text, WeChat or Whatsapp.

You have twenty seconds.

In these five verses, God’s Spirit is mentioned six times.

The Holy Spirit is vital to knowing God and understanding the mystery of God.

Without the Holy Spirit it’s impossible to know God.

Only the Spirit searches and knows the depths of God.

Paul uses the example of a person to demonstrate his point.

Just as a no one knows a person’s thoughts except for the person himself, no one knows God’s thoughts except for the Spirit of God.

If you have the Spirit of God, you will know God.

Look with me at verse 12, “Now we have not received the spirit of the world, but the Spirit who comes from God, so that we may understand what has been freely given to us by God.”

If you have the Spirit of God, you’ll start to understand what God’s freely given us,

You’ll start to understand God’s wisdom.

If you have the Spirit of God, you will grow in your maturity.

In verse 6, Paul says that “we do, however, speak a wisdom among the mature.”

The mature here refers to Christians who think according to God’s wisdom, whose lives are shaped by God’s wisdom.

To be mature is more than just having a lot of head knowledge.

As we’ll read later on in 1 Corinthians, knowledge, left by itself, puffs up.

To be mature, genuinely mature, is to have a life transformed by knowing and understanding God’s wisdom.

And to know and understand God’s wisdom, we need God himself to reveal himself to us and to change us.

We need God to reveal his thoughts to us and God does reveal his thoughts to us by his Holy Spirit.

In the words of the Swiss theologian, Karl Barth, “God is known through God alone.”

If we want to see changes in our behaviour, we need God’s Spirit to transform us.

If we want to understand God’s hidden wisdom, we need God’s Spirit to reveal himself to us.

There’s no way for us to know and love God, if God doesn’t draw us to him by his Holy Spirit.

Earlier today, I mentioned the great Melbourne Revival of 1902.

However, long before that revival, there was a group of twelve men or so who have been praying for this for years.

John MacNeil, who founded the group, died before the revival took place.

Another person in the group, the Rev Allan Webb, died on the first week of the meetings.

Allan Webb died praying on his knees.

What a way to go, to die praying on your knees!

And God heard their prayers.

The thousands who have come to Christ at the Melbourne Mission didn’t come because the mission was well organized or that the speakers were exceptionally talented, even if these two things were true.

Ultimately, it’s God’s Spirit who moves in our hearts to convince and to convict us of God’s truth.

Without God’s Spirit, it’s impossible for anyone to turn to God.

That’s why I’m so glad that we can pray together at Church on Sundays and also weekly on Tuesday nights.

We also pray together at Growth Groups, Bible studies and KOG groups.

We pray because we long to see God’s Spirit move among us and reveal God’s thoughts to us.

We pray that God’s Spirit will move the hearts of our friends and families so that they, too, will know the wisdom of God.

And we pray for ourselves too, that, we ,too, will understand God’s wisdom and be transformed by Him.

We pray too that all of us will grow in our maturity in Jesus Christ as seek the Spirit’s guidance.

Have the mind of Christ crucified

Paul has a wonderful way to describe what it means to be mature in Jesus Christ.

To be mature in Christ is to have the mind of Christ.

Look with me at verse 13, “We also speak these things, not in words taught by human wisdom, but in those taught by the Spirit, explaining spiritual things to spiritual people.”

Those who are taught by the Spirit will explain spiritual things to spiritual people.

That’s what the teaching of the Bible is.

In the Bible, we see the Spirit explaining spiritual things.

These things are explained by the prophets and the apostles, who have written them down to explain spiritual things to us.

But not everyone understands it.

Look with me at verse 14-15, “But the person without the Spirit does not receive what comes from God’s Spirit, because it is foolishness to him; he is not able to understand it since it is evaluated spiritually. The spiritual person, however, can evaluate everything, and yet he himself cannot be evaluated by anyone.”

The person without the Spirit doesn’t understand the Spirit’s teaching, they’re foolishness to him.

He’s not able to understand them because the teachings of the Spirit are evaluated spiritually.

You need the Spirit to understand spiritual things.

But it’s not just the spiritual things that the Spirit helps us to understand.

The spiritual person, the person who has the Spirit, can evaluate everything.

The spiritual person can evaluate everything, because God’s wisdom has something to say about everything.

It’s a little like this.

Not so long ago, the only screen that a person sees is the screen that is their window.

From the inside of the house, they look through the window to the outside world.

Over the past few decades, many people see the world through electric screens.

When I was growing up, it was mainly through the screen that is the television.

And then, as I grew a little more, it was through the window of the computer.

Now, it’s through the world of the phone.

And of course, for someone like me, I always look at the world through my specialised screen; my glasses.

We might not realise it but our screens, whatever they may be, shape the way we see the world.

For Christians, we, too have a special screen, special glasses as it were.

Rather than the TV, computer or the phone, we see the world through the Spirit of God.

The Spirit of God helps us to see all things from his point of view.

Indeed, the Spirit of God helps us to see all things from God’s point of view, since the Spirit of God knows the thoughts of God.

In this way, the spiritual person evaluates everything.

They want to examine everything from the Spirit’s point of view.

They want to know what the Spirit thought on everything.

In particular, the spiritual person wants to evaluate everything through the message of Christ crucified.

The message of Christ crucified is the mystery that the Spirit of God reveals.

It’s the message that God’s been planning since before the beginning of time.

It’s the message that will lead God’s people to glory.

It’s the message the has implication for all things.

And that’s what the wisdom of the mature is about.

The wisdom of the mature, as revealed by the Spirit, is the message of Christ crucified.

Look with me at the final verse for today, verse 16, “For

who has known the Lord’s mind,

that he may instruct him?

Who has known the Lord’s mind?

Who can claim to have known the Lord’s mind?

It sounds like a rhetorical question at first because the answer surely must be nobody.

Nobody can know God’s mind.

But Paul has a surprising answer.

“But we have the mind of Christ.”

If you have the mind of Christ, if you have put your trust in the Lord Jesus, if you believe in Christ crucified,

Then you’ll have the Spirit of God.

If you have the Spirit of God, you’ll know the Lord’s mind.

A few days ago, I was talking with a brother in Christ who spoke with someone who was about to go into full time ministry.

When the person makes a comment, my friend would say, “What does the Bible say about that?”

And they would both have a small chuckle.

It’s a simple question but it’s so helpful!

As spiritual people, as people who have the Spirit of God, as people who have the mind of Christ, we want to evaluate all things spiritually.

We want to ask, “What does the Bible have to say about that? What does the Crucified Christ have to say about that?”

For at the end, it’s only what God thinks that matters.

As Christians, we want the thought of Christ crucified to be what helps us evaluate the world.

We want to see things as spiritual people, people filled with the Holy Spirit.

We want to see things from God’s perspective.

As Christians, we want the message of Christ crucified to fill our lives so that we’ll let it speak to our life situations, whatever they may be.

We want to grow and mature in Christ as we grow in God’s wisdom.

What does this mean for us?

Be wise with the mind of Christ

Be wise with the right sort of wisdom.

I remember that a long time ago, I brought a Do-it-yourself garage shelves from Bunnings.

It was fairly simple to set up and so I started to assemble it in record time.

However, as I was approaching the end, I realised that the shelves don’t fit.

That’s when I realized that I had done the whole thing the wrong way and I had to start again.

I got things done quickly but it was a waste of time because it was wrong all along.

Someone once said success is hearing ‘well done’ from the only lips that matter.

Failure is succeeding at things that don’t finally matter at all.

What’s the most successful thing that you’re doing right now?

From God’s point of view, is it a success or a failure?

If I’ve been working to make myself look good, then I’ve been a failure.

If I’ve been working to make Christ look good, as God would have us, then we know that it’s success.

Think according to the mind of Christ.

Use the mind of Christ to evaluate everything, from how you spend your time, to how you treat people.

What does the Bible say about what you’re doing?

Friends, be wise with the mind of Christ.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Do I think with the mind of Christ?

Have I matured in growing in God’s wisdom?

What do I do to make Jesus look good?

Friends, don’t see the world as the world sees itself.

Be wise with the mind of Christ.

Let’s pray,

“May the mind of Christ, my Saviour, Live in me from day to day, By His love and power controlling All I do and say.

Father, please forgive us when we treasure the world’s wisdom more than your wisdom. Forgive us when we don’t tremble in fear before you. By your Spirit, please transform so that we may be mature, living lives transformed by the gospel of Christ crucified. By your Spirit, reveal your wisdom to us, show us the Lord Jesus Christ, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
