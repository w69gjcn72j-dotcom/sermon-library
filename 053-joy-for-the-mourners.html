Joy for the Mourners

Passage: Isaiah 61:1-7

God’s Anointed One brings good news of joy to disgraced mourners

1. Good news of the LORD’s favour

2. Good news to disgraced mourners

3. Good news in the Lord’s Anointed (Jesus!)

Everyone, hold out your phone.

Yes, even you at home on Zoom or Youtube, take it out.

Now take a photo around you that captures any form of happiness.

It might be a smile from the person next to you, maybe it’s a nice view, maybe it’s a piece of furniture.

If you don’t have your phone, try to remember the view, and maybe draw it later

Ready, take the picture.

If you can, send it to the Church’s Wechat or whatsapp page.

If you’re drawing, bring it in next week!

We all want to capture our moments of joy because we know they’re so brief.

We want to hold on to them because we know they don’t last long.

But what if joy doesn’t have to be brief?

What if there’s a joy that’s permanent?

In today’s passage, we hear these words, “And eternal joy will be theirs”.

According to the Bible, there is such a thing called eternal joy, a joy that lasts forever.

And it’s God who gives this eternal joy.

How do I get to have this eternal joy from God?

If you have your Bible there, please open up to Isaiah 61:1

In today’s passage, we’ll see three things. We’ll see…

Good news of the Lord’s Favour

Good news to disgraced mourners

Good news in the Lord’s Anointed (Jesus!)

Good news of the Lord’s Favour

Please open up with me to Isaiah 61, beginning from verse 1,

“The Spirit of the Lord God is on me, because the Lord has anointed me to bring good news to the poor. He has sent me to heal the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives and freedom to the prisoners; 2 to proclaim the year of the Lord’s favor, and the day of our God’s vengeance; to comfort all who mourn,”

God has good news that he’s about to announce through his Messenger.

It’s great to receive good news isn’t it?

Earlier this week, I received good news from the Archbishop that he in turn received from the Government.

As a congregation, we’re allowed to sing again!

For almost nine months, we weren’t allowed to sing inside Churches as a congregation.

And we thank God for the musicians who have been able to sing on our behalf over the past nine months.

But now, we’re allowed to sing.

Face masks are recommended, though not mandatory, for those of us who are over 12.

But either way, we can sing praises to God, and that is good news.

But even better than singing again at Church is the good news in this passage.

There are at least seven facets about this Good News that God wants to announce.

God has anointed a Messenger to bring the good news (verse 1)

God had chosen a special messenger to bring his good news.

This is a servant on whom God’s put his own Spirit.

He has the Spirit of the Living God, bringing the Word of the Living God to the people of the Living God.

What he says, he says with the authority of God.

What has he come to say?

He’s come to proclaim the year of the Lord’s Favour and the day of our God’s vengeance (verse 2)

He’s saying that the time has come when God will show favour to his people.

The language used here is very similar to the Year of Jubilee as written in the law of Moses.

According to Leviticus 25:8, every fiftieth year was to be a Year of release.

Debts would be cancelled, slave were freed, people who were forced to sell their homes because of poverty will receive their homes again.

It was a year of freedom! It was a year of celebration.

Here in Isaiah 61, the Year of Freedom has come.

This is not just another Year of Jubilee but the Year of Jubilee, the time that all celebration of Jubilee point to.

It’s what the Year of Jubilee is really about.

It’s “The” Year of Jubilee because there will be true liberty to the captives and true freedom for the prisoners.

It’ll also be the day of God’s vengeance on his enemies.

Isaiah will explore that idea in later chapters, from chapter 63 onwards.

But here, Isaiah wants us to focus on the Lord’s favour, the comfort that the Good News will bring.

What comfort will this Good News bring?

Exchange of crown for ashes

Look with me at verse 3, “to provide for those who mourn in Zion; to give them a crown of beauty instead of ashes, festive oil instead of mourning, and splendid clothes instead of despair. And they will be called righteous trees, planted by the Lord to glorify him.”

God will give them a crown of beauty instead of ashes.

Ashes were used by ancient Israelites to mourn the death of a loved one.

They would put ashes on their head so that other people will see that they’re mourning.

Instead of ashes for mourning, God will give them a crown of beauty.

They would have a clear sign on their head that they would be honoured and celebrated.

There would have oil of gladness rather than mourning.

Rather than being crumbling ashes, they would be called righteous trees, long-lasting, sturdy and not easily toppled in the winds of trials.

Rebuild ancient ruins

Verse 4, “They will rebuild the ancient ruins; they will restore the former devastations; they will renew the ruined cities, the devastations of many generations.”

For the first hearers of Isaiah, they had the massive job of rebuilding a city ahead of them.

Because of their persistent disobedience to God, God had allowed the Babylonians to destroy them.

But now, God has announced that their ancient ruins would be rebuilt.

Their cities would be renewed.

Though their disobedience had led to the destruction of the city, the time for rebuilding has come.

Implicitly, God is saying that he’s no longer punishing them for their sin; it’s been dealt with!

Strangers will be attracted to them

Verse 5, “Strangers will stand and feed your flocks, and foreigners will be your plowmen and vinedressers.

This is not about them treating other people as second-class citizens.

Rather, it’s a recognition from other people that God is blessing his people and they want to join God’s people.

Called the Lord’s priests

Verse 6, “But you will be called the Lord’s priests; they will speak of you as ministers of our God; you will eat the wealth of the nations, and you will boast in their riches.”

When the Israelites came out of Egypt, God said that the Israelites will be a kingdom of priests and God’s holy nation. (Exodus 19:6)

The time has finally come for this to be fulfilled!

Their nation as a whole will be a nations of priests.

People from other nations will come to God through God’s people.

Through them, God will bring his blessings, his joy to people of other nations.

Eternal Joy

Verse 7, “In place of your shame, you will have a double portion; in place of disgrace, they will rejoice over their share. So they will possess double in their land, and eternal joy will be theirs.”

Eternal joy, ever-lasting, never ceasing joy, will be theirs.

They will have a joy that lasts forever.

This joy won’t be snapshot that you take on your holidays that enjoy for a brief moment.

Rather, it’s like moving to a new home, and watching the view in real time, for years and years to come.

It’s real, everlasting joy.

That’s the good news that God’s sending his Messenger to bring.

I don’t know about you, but I get a lot of notifications on my phone.

There are news updates, there are status updates, there are a lot of messages.

When we’re in Church, I need to remember to turn the updates off.

Sometimes the new updates may be true, other times false.

Sometimes the news is good, other times bad.

Sometimes it can have long-lasting impact, but mostly they short-lived.

As we’re bombarded with all sorts of news, let’s not lose the most important news of all.

God has Good News for us, it’s the Year of the Lord’s Favour, it’s good news that leads to everlasting joy.

It’s Good News that will have an eternal impact.

Whatever news you’ll hear today, whatever news you’re going to ever hear, won’t be as important or as long-lasting as God’s good news.

Whatever news you’ll hear today, unlike God’s good news, won’t give you eternal joy.

Don’t miss out on God’s good news.

Have you got God’s good news yet?

You may not, because God’s good news is directed to a specific group of people in today’s passage.

God’s the sender of the Good News.

are the recipients?

Good news for Disgraced mourners

God’s good news is for disgraced mourners.

A few days ago, Caitlyn received a huge package.

It’s from her friend Penny from her old school.

It’s a Christmas present that Caitlyn will open on Christmas Day.

It’s not for me, it’s not for Jenny, nor any other members of our family.

It’s a gift specifically for Caitlyn.

In the same way, God’s Good News is specifically for a group of people, the disgraced mourners.

In verse 1, we see that it’s good news for the poor, the broken-hearted, the captives and the prisoners.

Between verses 2-3, we see that the good news is sent to comfort all who mourn and to provide for those who mourn in Zion.

Festive oil is given to those who were mourning.

Those who were mourning had ashes on their head.

These were not happy people!

Their cities were in ruins; their homes were destroyed.

And they had shame and disgrace.

Here, their shame and disgrace were more than just being embarrassed about their status in life.

They were shamed and they were disgraced because they were people who have recognised their sins.

When someone is disgraced it’s usually because they were exposed as frauds.

These were people who were deeply unhappy, crying, mourning even, because they were sinful.

These are the recipients of God’s good news.

God’s good news is for them.

God’s good news about how to receive God’s own eternal joy is for these unhappy, mournful people.

That’s so counter-intuitive, isn’t it?

It’s so different to how we go about getting our happiness.

We think we’ll get our happiness when we do what we want, we buy what we want and when we get what we want.

And we don’t want any delays.

We want instant gratification.

To stay happy, we don’t want to hear any bad or negative news about ourselves or the people around us.

We only want to hear happy news.

The problem with this is that it’s a shallow happiness.

Someone who has shallow happiness may smile and laugh on the outside.

But if you give them a little push, you’ll see that they are in fact miserable, sad and even angry on the inside.

Other people, of course, are more realistic about life and they can get very depressed about how bad things are.

They see the sadness of live and they give up on joy.

They see the ruins of society; they’re distressed at the devastations of their nations and they sink in depression.

Bible doesn’t tell us to hide or deny our failures nor does it tell us to sink in our depression either.

Rather, it tells us to be honest and face up to our failings.

The Bible tells us to see how we have failed God and our fellow men and women.

And it tells us to mourn.

The Bible tells us to look at our sin and be rightly disgusted at what we’ve done.

When we look at our sins, we need to get ready to be hurt by what we see.

We need to be ready to cry and even mourn for our sins.

Because it’s only as we see how deep we’ve fallen that we see how good, God’s good news really is.

That’s the paradox for eternal joy: To have God’s everlasting joy, we must first be mourners.

Until we mourn at our disgrace, we can’t have God’s everlasting joy.

For God’s good news of eternal joy is for those who have mourned over their sins.

Mark Hancock was the chief executive of several companies and before that he was a general manager of the NSW Legal Aid Commission.

He was blessed with good health, a loving wife, successful children and staggeringly beautiful grandchildren.

And for a short time, from the age of 63, he was an ice addict.

Ice is the nickname of a drug called Crystal methamphetamine.

It is a stimulant drug that gives its users feelings of happiness and confidence.

High doses of ice can cause a psychological condition known as ‘ice psychosis’, which is characterised by paranoid delusions, hallucinations and bizarre, aggressive or violent behaviour.

Many young and old people had been injured and even killed by people who had ice psychosis.

Mark Hancock wasn’t a drug user but was invited to try some by his work colleagues.

He was quickly addicted and tried to hide it from his friends and families.

But soon, they found out about his addiction.

But rather than abandoning him, they came to his rescue.

For Mark Hancock, that day was a day of deepest shame and also the greatest pride.

It was a great day of shame because he had to admit he was a drug addict.

It was a great day of joy because his family came to his rescue.

In a similar way, before we can receive God’s good news, we need to recognise that, we too, have an addiction that we need to be saved from.

It’s our addiction to sin.

We can’t help but sin.

We need to come to a recognition, often a sorrowful recognition, that we have wronged both God and men.

But the Good News is that God has come to save us from our addition; God has come to save us from our sin.

God’s good news has come in his Anointed Messenger, in the Person of Jesus.

Good news in the Lord’s Anointed (Jesus!)

The Messenger that God anoints in Isaiah 61 is none other than Jesus.

Here’s the account of the first time Jesus read from the Old Testament, as recorded in the Gospel of Luke 4:, beginning form verse 17:

“The scroll of the prophet Isaiah was given to him, and unrolling the scroll, he found the place where it was written:

18 The Spirit of the Lord is on me, because he has anointed me to preach good news to the poor. He has sent me to proclaim release to the captives and recovery of sight to the blind, to set free the oppressed, 19 to proclaim the year of the Lord’s favour.

20 He then rolled up the scroll, gave it back to the attendant, and sat down. And the eyes of everyone in the synagogue were fixed on him. 21 He began by saying to them, “Today as you listen, this Scripture has been fulfilled.”

Jesus says that he’s the fulfilment of Isaiah 61.

Jesus is the Spirit filled Messenger from God, anointed by God to tell the Good News of God.

Jesus is here to heal the broken-hearted.

Jesus’ message is Good News for those who mourn.

In fact, Jesus himself had taught, “Blessed are those who mourn for they will be comforted.” (Matthew 5:4)

Jesus wants us to mourn over our sins, but he doesn’t want us to stay in our mourning.

He wants us to mourn because it’s only as we mourn over our sins that we’ll be comforted by God.

It’s after we mourn over our sins that we’ll have God’s eternal joy.

Jesus has said in John 15:11, “I have told you these things so that my joy may be in you and your joy may be complete.”

Jesus tells us about our sins, but he also tells us about the joy that comes about because of the forgiveness of our sins.

Jesus can offer us forgiveness of our sins because he has come to take away the disgrace of our sins.

The Good news that God has for us is that he’ll give us a crown of beauty for ashes.

We can have a crown of beauty because Jesus took the crown of thorns on our behalf.

Jesus gave up his crown of beauty for our crown of thorns.

To give us the hope of eternal life, the Lord of Life died on our behalf.

But being the Lord of Life, he triumphed over death.

His resurrection shows us that in him, we can indeed have eternal joy.

The Great reformer Martin Luther describes our relationship to Jesus like that of a married couple.

Jesus is the Husband and we’re the Wife.

Just as a Husband shares all he has with His Wife, and the Wife with her Husband,

Christ shares all his goodness and his life with us and we share all our sin and death with him.

Luther says, “Christ is full of grace, life and salvation. The soul is full of sins, death and damnation. Now let faith come between them, and sins, death and damnation will be Christ's, while grace, life and salvation will be the soul's... By the wedding ring of faith he shares in the sins, death and pains of hell, which are his bride's.... Her sins cannot now destroy her... and she has that righteousness of Christ, her husband.”

Luther calls this the sweet exchange.

And it is a sweet exchange.

For those who belong to Jesus, they have grace, life and salvation because Jesus took their sin, death and damnation.

That’s Good News for those who mourn for their sins.

Because Jesus has died for them, they can have eternal joy!

And so, God’s good news of joy has come to the mourners and this good news of joy has come in the Person of Jesus.

What does this mean for us?

We need to remember that Jesus heals the broken-hearted.

I love that imagery of Jesus holding a broken heart and healing it, making it whole.

When I was growing up in Sunday School, we learnt this song ‘He’s able’.

It has actions as well.

He’s able, he’s able, I know He’s able

I know my Lord is able to carry me through.

He mends the broken-hearted, he set the captives free, he makes the lame to walk again and cause the blind to see.

Our heart is like a torn piece of cloth, no good for anything.

It’s spiritually broken and it’s emotionally broken.

But Jesus mends it, repairs it, and gives us a new heart.

At the cross, Jesus broke his heart to give us a new one.

Over the years, I’ve seen young men and women whom we love in the Lord Jesus, but who had to hear that they have sinned against God.

Often it broke their hearts.

And indeed, not just their hearts but ours as well because it’s hard to see their hearts broken.

But it’s a joy to see that when they turn back to Christ, their hearts are healed, mended by Christ himself.

It’s a joy to see that they now have everlasting joy in Christ.

This is the good news for the mourners: Jesus heals the broken-hearted.

Here are the three questions for this week:

When was the last time our heart broken over sin?

Has my heart been healed by Jesus?

What’s the source of my joy?

Come before Jesus, mourn for your sins and find joy that lasts.

Let’s pray

“Father, thank you for the joy that is ours in Christ. We thank you that despite our sinfulness, you’ve sent your only one and only Son to save us. Forgive us for our pride. Please help us see our brokenness before you and come before you with sorrow for our sins. Please heal our broken hearts in Christ. Let us find eternal joy in him. For His sake we pray, Amen.”
