Growth Groups (adults)

Hope Against Hope

Romans 4:1-25 · Sunday 9 August 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah · For adult home groups

Big Idea: Be fully and joyfully convinced, hoping against hope, in the God who credits righteousness to those who trust in him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead.

Aim: (1) See that righteousness is credited as a gift, never earned as wages; (2) walk in Abraham’s footsteps by trusting God’s promise when the situation looks dead; (3) name one household to pray for and invite.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60-75 minutes.

1. Connect (10 min)

  • Films used to run the credits at the start; now we skip them entirely. Who in your life does real work that nobody credits — at home, at work, at church?
  • Nobody writes a thank-you card to their employer for their salary, and nobody invoices you for a birthday gift. Where do you catch yourself treating God’s kindness as wages you have earned?

2. Read — Romans 4:1-25 (CSB)

1 What then will we say that Abraham, our forefather according to the flesh, has found? 2 If Abraham was justified by works, he has something to boast about — but not before God. 3 For what does the Scripture say? Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him for righteousness. 4 Now to the one who works, pay is not credited as a gift, but as something owed. 5 But to the one who does not work, but believes on him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is credited for righteousness.

6 Likewise, David also speaks of the blessing of the person to whom God credits righteousness apart from works: 7 Blessed are those whose lawless acts are forgiven and whose sins are covered. 8 Blessed is the person the Lord will never charge with sin.

[vv.9-15, read aloud from your Bibles: Abraham was credited righteous while still uncircumcised, so circumcision was a seal on a righteousness already received — making him the father of all who believe, circumcised or not. The promise that he would inherit the world came not through the law but through the righteousness that comes by faith; if law-keepers are the heirs, faith is made empty and the promise nullified, because the law produces wrath.]

16 This is why the promise is by faith, so that it may be according to grace, to guarantee it to all the descendants — not only to the one who is of the law but also to the one who is of Abraham’s faith. He is the father of us all. 17 As it is written: I have made you the father of many nations — in the presence of the God in whom he believed, the one who gives life to the dead and calls things into existence that do not exist.

18 He believed, hoping against hope, so that he became the father of many nations according to what had been spoken: So will your descendants be. 19 He did not weaken in faith when he considered his own body to be already dead (since he was about a hundred years old) and also considered the deadness of Sarah’s womb. 20 He did not waver in unbelief at God’s promise but was strengthened in his faith and gave glory to God, 21 because he was fully convinced that what God had promised, he was also able to do. 22 Therefore, it was credited to him for righteousness.

23 Now it was credited to him was not written for Abraham alone, but also for us. 24 It will be credited to us who believe in him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead. 25 He was delivered up for our trespasses and raised for our justification.

3. Context

Paul has just said all have sinned and that God justifies sinners freely through Jesus (3:21-31). The obvious objection: what about Abraham? Jewish teachers held him up as the man who kept the law before it was given. Paul opens the file and shows Abraham too was declared righteous by trusting God — years before circumcision, centuries before the law — then points us to the same God, who raised Jesus.

4. Discuss

  1. Verses 1-3: what did Abraham “find”? Why Abraham, of all people?
  2. Verses 4-5 contrast wages with a gift. What is the difference, and why is “justifies the ungodly” so startling?
  3. Verses 6-8 quote Psalm 32. How does David describe the blessed person? Is that how we use the word “blessed”?
  4. Verses 9-12 press hard on the timing of Abraham’s circumcision. What is Paul protecting by insisting on that order?
  5. Verse 15 says “the law produces wrath.” What does the law do well, and what can it never do for us?
  6. Verses 18-21: Abraham did not waver but was strengthened and gave God glory. What made his faith strong — his feelings, or something outside himself?
  7. How does Paul move from Abraham to us in verses 23-25? What are we asked to believe, and what is already done?
  8. Are you asking “what have I achieved for God?” or “what has God credited to me in Christ?” Where are you hoping against hope — a child, a marriage, a diagnosis, a workplace? What would giving God the glory there look like this week?

5. Live It Out

KOG 3 · 2 · 1 · 0

  • Pray for 3: three names — a neighbour, a colleague, someone at the school gate. Daily, by name, this week.
  • Connect with 2: two get real contact — a meal, a coffee, a hand with something practical.
  • Invite 1: invite one to church, to this group, to Father’s Day on 6 September, ALIVE South Sydney on 12 September, or our 157th Anniversary on 20 September.
  • 0 left unloved: who in this group is hoping against hope right now? Ring them this week — do not just think about it.

This week’s challenge: Each morning, before anything else, say out loud one thing God has credited to you in Christ. Then tell one person about the God who gives life to the dead.

Memory verse: “He did not waver in unbelief at God’s promise but was strengthened in his faith and gave glory to God, because he was fully convinced that what God had promised, he was also able to do.” (Romans 4:20-21)

6. Pray

  • Thank God that he credits righteousness to the ungodly who trust him — our standing rests on Christ, not our record.
  • Pray by name for your three people, and for courage to speak plainly about Jesus with them.
  • Bring God the situation in your household that looks dead, and ask him to strengthen your faith so he gets the glory.

7. Leader’s Notes

Point joyfully to Jesus tonight; do not audit anyone’s faith. Assume there are guests, not-yet-Christians, and people quietly grieving a hope that has not come. Never corner anyone — answer the harder questions in the first person first.

Brief answers to key questions

  • Q1: Abraham found he had nothing to boast about before God — an idol worshipper for most of his first 75 years, who lied to save himself. Paul picks the strongest test case: if even Abraham is credited rather than paid, nobody is paid.
  • Q2: Wages are owed as a debt; a gift is free. Exodus 23:7 says God will not justify the guilty — and he does not. Romans 3:25 showed God himself paid the price in Jesus, so this is not injustice but costly grace.
  • Q4: Paul protects both the freeness of the promise and its reach. Abraham was credited righteous while uncircumcised, so he fathers all who believe; circumcision sealed what was already received.
  • Q6: Not feelings. Abraham weighed his hundred-year-old body and Sarah’s womb honestly, and was still fully convinced God was able. Faith is not pretending the facts are different; it is being convinced God is greater than the facts.
  • Q7: The God who gives life to the dead and calls into existence what does not exist is the God who raised Jesus. Verse 25 sums it up: delivered up for our trespasses, raised for our justification.
