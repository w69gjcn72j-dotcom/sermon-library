The God-forsaken Cross of Christ

Mark 15:33-47

Big Idea: On the God-forsaken cross of Christ, the faithful see a glimpse of hell that they’ll never experience

The God-forsaken Cross

God abandons Jesus

God abandons his temple

An awe-inspired executioner

The climactic confession

Christ became sin for us (2 Cor 5:21)

A courageous step in faith

Courage to love a crucified Christ

Faith of never being abandoned

Isn’t it good to come back to Church on Good Friday?

It’s now been two years since we’ve had a physical in-person Good Friday service at Church.

It’s so good to see you here.

And for those watching at home, we hope to see you in person again real soon.

For many people around the world, Good Friday is just another holiday in our calendar.

Maybe a day to go to the beach or to the Easter Show.

What’s the significance of Good Friday?

Why do we celebrate it?

What does it have to do with me?

In today’s passage, we’ll see what happened on the very first Good Friday many years ago.

We’ll see three things:

The God-forsaken Cross

An awe-inspired executioner

A courageous step in faith

We’ll see that what happened to Jesus on the cross had a profound impact on the world for the past two thousand years and impacts everyone today, whether you’re here at Church or watching us online.

The God-forsaken Cross

If you have your Bible there, please open up with me to Mark 15, beginning from verse 33.

Here, we see Jesus on a God-forsaken Cross.

“When it was noon, darkness came over the whole land until three in the afternoon., 34 And at three Jesus cried out with a loud voice, “Eloi, Eloi, lemá sabachtháni?” which is translated, “My God, my God, why have you abandoned me?”

Here, God is abandons Jesus.

It’s a horrible feeling to be abandoned, to be left behind.

Sadly, many people in our community have experienced being abandoned.

I remember a scene from the TV series Fresh Prince of Bel Air that did a wonderful job of showing what being abandoned felt like.

In Fresh Prince of Bel Air, a young boy named Will was taken to his Uncle and Aunt to live because his mother didn’t want to raise him up in a rough neighbourhood.

Where was Will’s Dad? He left Will and his mother many years ago.

In this episode, Will’s Dad turned up and they got on really well together.

However, after a while, Will’s Dad decided to leave Will again.

Will was devastated.

While his uncle hugged him to comfort him, Will kept saying, “How come he don’t want me, man? How come he don’t want me?”

On the first Good Friday, it got unnaturally dark at noon.

In the middle of that darkness, at around 3pm, after Jesus had been on the cross for six hours, Jesus cries out, “Eloi, Eloi, lema sabachthani?”

“My God, my God, why have you abandoned me?”

“How come you don’t want me, God?”

It was an utterly devastating moment.

By this time, all of Jesus’ disciples have left Jesus, the religious leaders of the days have condemned Jesus, the Roman governor had sentenced Jesus to death on the cross.

Jesus was socially abandoned and emotionally abandoned.

But now, he’s spiritually abandoned.

On the cross, when Jesus needed him the most, God the Father abandons Jesus.

What’s so devastating is that God the Father had known Jesus for all eternity.

God had known Jesus before he created the world; he’s known Jesus better than anyone, before there’s anyone else.

God the Father and God the Son had a loving, perfect relationship.

When Jesus prays, he calls God, Abba, Father or Daddy or Dadda.

They had a close and perfect relationship.

God’s face has always been towards Jesus; he always turns his face to Jesus.

But on that day, on that very first Good Friday, God turned his face away.

He turned his back in Jesus, even as Jesus faces the most horrific day in his life, the most horrific day he’ll ever face in eternity.

While Jesus suffered physically and mentally, it’s the spiritual pain that causes Jesus the most suffering.

On the cross, Jesus suffered God’s fury against our sin.

On the cross, we see a glimpse of what hell is like.

I remember some non-Christians used to joke that they look forward to hell because they’ll spend the rest of eternity with their friends.

But hell won’t be like that.

They’ll be no friendships in hell; there will be no love, only hate.

No hope, only abandonment.

It will be a place that God turns his back on.

It will be a place that will only know the back of God’s fury and never know of the face of God’s love or any love for that matter.

And that’s where Jesus went on the cross.

We read in the Apostle’s Creed that Jesus descended into the dead.

That’s where he went spiritually on the cross; Jesus went through hell on the cross.

That’s what Jesus suffered on the cross.

There’s an old Christian saying, “The sufferings of his soul were the soul of his suffering”.

The heart of Jesus’ suffering, the heart of his suffering, is his spiritual suffering, what he went through in his spirit.

What makes it so hard is that even during this intense time on the cross, God the Father still loves the Son as he’s always been and God the Son still loves God the Father.

If it was pure hatred, it would’ve been a lot easier, but it wasn’t.

The Father still loves Jesus, even as he abandons him.

The Son still loves the Father, even as he was abandoned.

What we see on the cross is only a glimpse of what Jesus was going through between God the Father and God the Son.

We can only get a hint of the magnitude of what Jesus has done on the cross.

Jesus’ suffering on the cross came to an end at 3pm, when Jesus gave a loud cry and breathed his last.

In the Gospel of John, it’s said that Jesus’ last words were “It is finished” before he gave up his spirit.

After Jesus gave up his spirit, the curtain in the temple was torn in two.

The temple was the meeting place between God and his people.

In the temple was a place known as the Holy of holies.

This is the place that symbolised God’s presence and God’s holiness.

It’s the most distinctive part of the temple.

When Jesus died, the curtain that separated God’s holiness and man’s sinfulness was torn in two.

It was ripped apart from top to bottom.

The curtain of the temple was very thick, up to 9cm, or the breadth of an average hand.

It wasn’t an accident.

Just like the unnatural darkness that covered the land from noon, the tearing of the curtain temple was God’s doing.

After the death of Jesus, the temple in Jerusalem will no longer be the meeting place between God and his people.

God’s abandoned the temple when he abandoned Jesus on the cross.

Now, someone might ask, isn’t a good thing that the curtains are torn?

Isn’t good that we can have access to God now?

While there is another part of the Bible that talks about us having access through Jesus, the new curtains, I don’t think the tearing of the curtain here refers to an opening up of access to God.

The curtains are violently ripped apart, not opened.

It’s like this.

One of the most distinctive part of our Church are the stained-glass windows.

They are exquisite.

Now imagined that God decides to deliberately smash them to smithereens.

What would that tell you?

It’ll tell you that God’s unhappy, God’s displeasure, with something.

When God torn the curtains of his temple, he’s showing his displeasure with his temple, as well his displeasure with his people.

The old way of coming to God must end; a new way must begin.

For that to happen, Jesus had to go through a very Bad Friday.

On that Bad Friday, God abandoned Jesus at the cross and he abandoned his temple as the meeting place with his people.

An awe-inspired executioner

Almost as soon as Jesus breathed his last breath on the Bad Friday, goodness starts flowing across the land.

And it began at the very foot of the cross.

At the foot of the cross, we hear from an awe-inspired executioner.

Look with me at verse 39, “When the centurion, who was standing opposite him, saw the way he breathed his last, he said, “Truly this man was the Son of God!”

The Centurion, the Roman executioner confesses that Jesus is the Son of God.

It’s a twist that no one saw coming!

A twist in a story or a movie is a surprise that you didn’t expect coming.

Here’s an example of a twist.

And spoiler warning: if you haven’t watched the movie The Empire Strikes Back, you may want to block your ears or mute your screen for the next 10 seconds.

The Empire Strikes Back is a great movie.

It’s a story about a young man named Luke who prepares to fight against the evil Darth Vader.

But at the end of the movie, at the most important moment, we hear Darth Vade says to Luke, “No, I am your Father”

What a major twist!

It’s a twist that the audience see coming.

That’s what’s happened in the Gospel of Mark.

Throughout the Gospel of Mark, we the readers get to discover who Jesus is through the lens of the disciples, the priests and the ordinary people in Jesus’ life.

Some say he is a teacher; others say that he’s a prophet.

And as more is revealed about Jesus, we expect the disciples, or a wise teacher to finally work out who Jesus is.

But, no, it’s not his followers or esteemed teachers but a Roman executioner who utters the climactic words at the cross, “Truly, this Man was the Son of God!”

This Centurion would’ve witnessed many deaths, including many executions by crucifixion.

But this wasn’t like any other crucifixions he’s seen.

This Roman centurion, this executioner, was convinced that he’s witnessed an extraordinary death.

He’s convinced that he just witnessed the death of the Son of God.

And he’s right!

He has witnessed the most significant and most influential death in history.

He’s seen Jesus abandoned by God; he’s witnessed the death of the Son of God.

Why was the death of the Son of God such a significant moment in history?

There are so many reasons that we see in the Bible.

We won’t have time to go through them all; I encourage you to read the Bible or to join our Bible studies to find out more.

There is one aspect that I want to focus on.

The Apostle Paul states here very succinctly in 2 Corinthians 5:21, “21 He made the one who did not know sin to be sin for us, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God.”

What the Centurion witnessed was significant because on the cross, Jesus became sin for us.

He’s numbered with the sinners.

He’s counted as one of us.

In America right now, there are around 2500 prisoners on the death row.

These are people who have been convicted of a horrible crime and the court decided that they should be executed.

You and I, as sinners against God, are on the death row.

When we’ve turned our backs toward God, when we don’t worship God as God, we’ve been condemned to be on the death row.

As we’ve turned our backs on God, we deserve to have God’s back turn towards us.

We deserve the hell that Jesus endured on the cross.

But on the cross, it’s as if Jesus put his hand up and said, “I’ll take her place instead; I’ll die for him instead”.

On the cross, Jesus exchanged his life for our death.

This is what the ancient Christians called the Sweet Exchange.

That’s the sweet exchange for all those who would put their trust in the Lord Jesus.

For those who trust in Jesus, Jesus took on the death row for us.

That’s why this Friday is called the Good Friday.

It was a Bad Friday for Jesus so that it’ll be a Good Friday for us.

It’s a Sweet Friday because on the day, Jesus became sin for us, to save us from God’s fury.

A courageous step of faith

As Good as Good Friday is, it’s a goodness that’s available but not experienced by everybody.

To experience it, we need to take a courageous step of faith.

Look with me at verses 42-43, “When it was already evening, because it was the day of preparation (that is, the day before the Sabbath), 43 Joseph of Arimathea, a prominent member of the Sanhedrin who was himself looking forward to the kingdom of God, came and boldly went to Pilate and asked for Jesus’s body.”

Joseph of Arimathea boldly, courageously, went to ask for Jesus’ body.

Joseph had been looking forward to the Kingdom of God to come and he must have heard Jesus speaking about it.

In the Gospel of Matthew, it’s recorded that Joseph was a disciple of Jesus.

Now, Joseph was a prominent member of the Sanhedrin, and so he would’ve been there when his fellow Sanhedrin Council members mocked and condemned Jesus to death.

We don’t know why he didn’t stand up for Jesus then.

Maybe he was afraid; maybe he didn’t want to risk his reputation.

But now, Joseph decides to pluck up courage and boldly identify himself as someone who cares for Jesus.

It takes courage because Jesus has just shown to be a loser, crucified shamefully among criminals.

Joseph knows that according to God’s word, a dead body, even a convicted criminal, need to be buried before sunset.

And so, with the disciples nowhere to be seen, and possibly with his peers glaring mercilessly at him, he picks up Jesus’ body, wraps Jesus with linen and place it in a tomb.

It takes courage to stand up for the Crucified Christ.

That’s what faith is.

Faith is not for the wimps.

Faith takes courage; it requires courageous faith to trust in the Crucified Christ.

Today, there are many Easter services here in Sydney and all around the world.

We’re thankful that we can come together to celebrate Easter.

But there are many countries around the world where it’s dangerous to come to Church for Easter.

Even last Sunday, when Christians come together to celebrate Palm Sunday in a Church in Makassar, Indonesia,

two suspected bombers came and tried to get into the Church.

The bomb went off and 14 people were injured.

It was horrible.

And it’s just one of many instances when Christians were just doing Christian things like coming to Church on Easter and they were attacked.

For many countries around the world, it takes courage to be a Christian.

It’s courage inspired by faith.

It’s their faith, their trust in Jesus, that gives them the courage to do what they would otherwise be afraid to do.

We might see glimpses of faith in singing, in religious buildings among religious people.

Rather, true faith is often seen when there are risks, when there are costs involved, and sometimes, when safety may not be guaranteed.

In the case of Joseph of Arimathea, he might lose his reputation in the Sanhedrin council, he might become less prominent because he’s now associated with a crucified Jesus.

What are some costs today, in our community, that comes with being a Christian?

Take a moment to think about it.

If you’re at home, please send me a text on Whatsapp, Zoom or Wechat.

What are the costs to being a Christian in our world today?

It takes courage to be a Christian; it takes guts to be a Christian.

It’s courage inspired by faith and it’s a faith that will be rewarded.

For those who trust in Jesus, we can be absolutely sure that he’s saved us.

Because Jesus was abandoned by God, those of us who trust in him will know that we’ll never be abandoned by God.

We will never see hell.

We see a glimpse of hell here, in the cross of Christ, and we’ll see smaller glimpses of hell here, in our community,

But we’ll never experience hell.

Because Jesus experienced hell, we won’t experience it.

This is as close to hell as we’ll ever be.

This is the sweet exchange.

Because of what happened to Jesus on the cross, because Jesus took out place, God is with us forever more.

He will never leave us; he will never turn his back towards us.

And so, how should we respond?

Take courage and confess that Jesus is the Son of God.

It’s like the story of a Sheikh, a Muslim teacher, in Uganda.

To protect his identity, let’s call him Hussein.

Hussein studied Islam Apologetics in Saudi Arabia.

Eventually, he became a Sheikh in Uganda.

Even though he was a teacher in Islam, he said that he lived with a restless heart.

One day, a Christian pastor shared the gospel with Hussein, explaining to him the gospel about the Lord Jesus and his relation to God the Father in the Bible.

When Hussein heard about it, he was filled with joy and eventually gave his life to Jesus.

When he formally declared that he was a Christian, people tried to kidnap him in order to kill him.

This only happened a few years ago, back in 2017.

As a result, he sent his wife and children away and he himself fled to a refugee camp.

This is what Hussein says, “Although my life may be in danger, I know I am secure in God’s hands. Although I am old and have lost everything after clan leaders took my land, I have hope. I also know that the best gift I will ever give my Muslim family and friends is the Bible.”

Like many Christians before him and like many Christians around him, it takes courage not only to be a Christian but to remain a Christian.

But it’s courage inspired by faith in the Lord Jesus Christ.

Hussein and Christians like him believe that Jesus has truly died on the cross for their sins.

It’s a truth that Christians will proclaim boldly because it’s a truth that we hold dear to our hearts.

What Jesus did on the cross has had a deep, wonderful and personal impact on each Christian’s life,

And we want to share it.

And so, when your friends asked you what you’ve done during Easter, tell them that you went to Church,

Tell them that you’ve celebrated Easter.

Tell them that Jesus has died to save people like you and me.

Just as the life of Hussein has changed, tell them what’s changed in your life since you’ve trusted Jesus.

If you want to find out more about Christianity and why Jesus is worth following,

Come back on Sunday and find out more.

Also, come and join our Christianity Explored classes on the following Sunday.

We love nothing more than to share the good news of Jesus Christ.

And so, this Easter, take courage and confess Jesus as the Son of God.

As we go, here are three questions for us to consider:

How have I thanked Jesus for what he’s done at the cross?

Have I received what he’s won for me at the cross?

Is God’s back still turn towards me or is his face turn towards me because of the sweet exchange that Jesus has made at the cross?

Friends, this Easter, thank God for Jesus, who was suffered on the God forsaken cross to save you from hell and to bring you to God.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for the love that you have for us in Jesus. We thank you for the sweet exchange that Jesus has won for us there. Please forgive us for not loving Jesus as much as we should. Please save us. Turn your face towards us. And by your Spirit, give us the courage to put all our trust in Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
