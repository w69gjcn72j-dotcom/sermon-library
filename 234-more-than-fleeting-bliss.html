Welcome to the second Advent Sunday.

Advent is a term used to describe this period of time before Christmas.

Christmas is almost here! How many more days have we got to go?

10 days! We have 10 days until Christmas!

Many families and many churches are busy preparing for Christmas.

Our Church, of course, has our Christmas concert and our Christmas services coming up.

Start inviting your friends if you haven’t done so already!

Christmas is good and all, but you know what?

As well as Christmas, I also look at the time after Christmas.

Since I’m a minister, my family is usually quite busy serving with me and we don’t get a break until Christmas is over.

I know of a minister working in Hong Kong who’s Australian.

He loves his church, and he loves Hong Kong but as soon as he finishes the Christmas service he would catch a plane to Australia to celebrate Christmas with his family.

Do you look forward to the time after Christmas as well?

You’re not the only one, for Jesus looks forward to a time beyond Christmas too.

What does Jesus look forward to beyond Christmas?

That’s what we’re going to find out in today’s passage.

If you have your Bible with you, please open up to Revelation chapter 19, beginning from verse 1.

We’ll see three things in today’s passage:

1. The triumphant hallelujahs of heaven

2. The joyful preparation of a radiant bride

3. True worship accords with Jesus’ testimony

But before we begin, let’s pray, “Father, we thank you for bringing us into your presence as we hear from your words. Please take all worries, all temptations, all distractions away from our hearts as we hear from you. Help us to see Jesus more clearly. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”

1. The triumphant hallelujahs of heaven

The passage begins with the triumphant hallelujahs of the heavens.

Just prior to this chapter, Revelation talks about the destruction of the great city of Babylon.

Babylon was at one time a powerful city in the ancient Middle East.

It was a major world power that covered a massive area.

It was also an enemy and an oppressor of God’s people, the Israelites.

By New Testament times Babylon was no longer in power; Rome was the superpower of their time.

Throughout Revelation, Babylon is presented as the symbol of a society that rebels against God.

To the first Christians, Babylon was represented by Rome, but today it can represent any great world power that turns away from God.

Whether it’s the superpower in the West or the superpower in the East, whether it’s political or financial power, we can still see glimpses of Babylon, the same power that rebelled against God many centuries ago.

The Bible tells us that one day, Babylon, Rome, and any world power that rebels against God, will be destroyed.

Look with me at Revelation chapter 18 verse 21, “Then a mighty angel picked up a stone like a large millstone and threw it into the sea, saying,

“In this way, Babylon the great city will be thrown down violently and never be found again.”

How should we respond to the destruction of Babylon?

Look with me at Revelation chapter 19 beginning from verse 1:

“After this I heard something like the loud voice of a vast multitude in heaven, saying, Hallelujah! Salvation, glory, and power belong to our God.”

When the Bible was read earlier, did you notice how many times the word Hallelujah was used?

Four times!

And do you know how many times the word Hallelujah appears in the New Testament?

Four times! They are all here!

The word Hallelujah means, “Praise Yahweh”

Hallelujah! Praise Yahweh!

More than that, what we just read in Revelation is a song.

To be more exact, it’s an antiphonal song.

That is, it’s a song where someone calls and another person responds.

We do this quite often in Church.

When I say, “The Lord be with you / 主與你同在“ you say, “And also with you / 也與你同在“

The same thing is happening here.

Someone says Hallelujah and someone else replies Hallelujah.

Let’s try it: Hallelujah, Hallelujah, Hallelujah, hallelujah.

Why did they sing Hallelujah? Why praise God?

Look with me at verse 2, “because his judgments are true and righteous, because he has judged the notorious prostitute who corrupted the earth with her sexual immorality; and he has avenged the blood of his servants that was on her hands.”

God’s judgement against Babylon and every world power that rebels against him is righteous and true.

This includes every person who is part of that world power; it includes every rebel.

What crime did Babylon and its inhabitants commit?

There are many, but here they are summed up as Babylon being a notorious prostitute and a violent murderer.

Throughout Revelation, Babylon is compared to a prostitute.

A prostitute is someone who lures people into sin, who promises happiness and satisfaction for a fee.

However, the cost is far greater than the fee the prostitute demands.

Going to prostitutes can lead to family breakdown, an even deeper sense of loneliness, fleeting happiness, and ultimately, it’s sin against God.

And that’s what the world powers have been doing.

Instead of living lives to please God, the world powers tell you that there’s an alternative to God’s way.

It promises happiness and satisfaction, comfort and a sense of achievement.

What’s the fee? Don’t go to Church on Sundays, don’t follow Jesus, obey God only as an option.

Just like the prostitute’s fee, the cost is far greater.

Not only does following the world’s way lead to greed, selfishness and hatred.

Ultimately, it will lead to God’s condemnation.

As well as being a notorious prostitute, Babylon and all wordly powers are described to be like a violent murderer.

They hate God and they hate God’s people.

Since they can’t kill God, they kill God’s people.

The blood of countless Christians are on the hands of Babylon and all those who follow her ways.

In a report given to the House of Commons in England, it’s been noted that religious persecution has gone up.

It was reported that 365 million Christians around the world are persecuted for their faith.

That’s one in every seven Christians around the world.

Of these, 4998 Christians were killed because they’re Christians.

God has promised that he will avenge their blood.

All worldly powers, every individual who contributed to the death of Christians will face God’s wrath for killing God’s family.

Look with me at verse three: A second time they said, Hallelujah! Her smoke ascends forever and ever!

They will utterly be destroyed; no matter when you look for them in the future, be it 4000 or 4 billion years from now, all you will see there is smoke.

That was the call.

The response came firstly from heaven, in verse four, “Then the twenty-four elders and the four living creatures fell down and worshiped God, who is seated on the throne, saying, Amen! Hallelujah!”

And the earth is also called to respond, “A voice came from the throne, saying, Praise our God, all his servants, and the ones who fear him, both small and great!”

Earth echoes the hallelujahs of heaven, because salvation, glory and power indeed belongs to our God.

And God will triumph over every prostituting and murderous power.

Last Sunday, as we met here on Sunday, Islamic rebel forces overtook Damascus, capital of Syria and the Syrian President Bashar al-Assad fled with his family.

Many prisoners, including many women, were freed.

Since then, there’s been a lot of celebration on the streets.

However, many Christians are nervous because while Al-Assad was bad news for many Christians, they’re now under the rule of an Islamic group that was known to persecute Christians.

It’s going to be difficult for Christians, especially new ones.

Would you still be faithful and go to Church if you’re in Syria?

Would you work as a minister of the gospel, knowing that you’ll be a potential target?

Revelation chapter 19 was written with Christians like these in mind.

The political powers may be great, but God is greater, and he will win in the end.

How about us? Will we be able to stand firm in our faith when persecutions come?

Will we trust that God is in charge, even when our lives are in danger?

Don’t wait until that day to find out whether you trust God.

Start trusting in God now!

Start with trusting him in the small things before trusting him with the big things.

Don’t be tempted by the Babylon, the great prostitute; don’t give in to the world’s temptation to lure you away from Jesus.

For salvation, glory and power belongs to our God and he will triumph over every rebel power.

2. The joyful preparation of a radiant bride

So far, we had three hallelujahs.

Here’s the fourth one in verse six,

“Then I heard something like the voice of a vast multitude, like the sound of cascading waters, and like the rumbling of loud thunder, saying, Hallelujah, because our Lord God, the Almighty, reigns! Let us be glad, rejoice, and give him glory, because the marriage of the Lamb has come, and his bride has prepared herself.”

The first three hallelujahs rejoice in the power of God for destroying Babylon.

The fourth hallelujah rejoices in the coming marriage of the Lamb.

As many of you know, my daughter is Zoe getting married.

And she’s doing a lot of preparing.

She’s organising the Church that she’s getting married in.

She’s organising the invitations

And she’s been looking for her dress!

She’s gone to different bridal shops and tried out different dresses.

She wants to find the perfect dress for the day.

She wants to find beautiful white dress, and she will look radiant on the day.

On that day, she will stand out because she will be the only one wearing the beautiful white dress at the wedding.

As a minister, and as a father, here’s a community notice to our ladies: do not wear a white dress to weddings in general.

Only the bride is supposed to wear white dress for the focus on that day will be on her.

I’m looking forward to Zoe’s wedding to Rohan, but that’s not the one I’m looking forward to the most.

It shouldn’t be the one that they look forward to most either.

The one that all Christians look forward to is the marriage of the Lamb and his bride.

Throughout Revelation, we see Jesus as the Lamb who was slain.

And who’s Lamb’s bride? The Church.

Look with me at Ephesians 5:25-27, “Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ loved the church and gave himself for her 26 to make her holy, cleansing her with the washing of water by the word. 27 He did this to present the church to himself in splendor, without spot or wrinkle or anything like that, but holy and blameless.”

Jesus has died for us, the Church, to make us clean in God’s sight.

As a people saved by him, we follow him as best we can but we still fail because we’re still sinful.

But one day, on the day that he comes back, he will take us to be with him forever.

On that day, we won’t struggle with sin anymore; we’ll be perfect.

But until that day, we must remain faithful and loyal to him, just as someone who is engaged is faithful and loyal to his or her future spouse.

An engaged couple is committed to one another from the day they’re engaged until death separates them.

But Christians are committed to their Lord from the day they believe until, well, never.

When Christians make their commitment to Jesus, they belong to Jesus and Jesus is theirs forever.

Even so, just as the engaged couple look forward to the day of their wedding, when their marriage is celebrated by all their families and friends,

Christians look forward to the day when Jesus comes back for them.

That’s what Jesus looks forward to beyond Christmas! He’s looking forward to the day when he takes us to be his forever.

Until that day, we must prepare ourselves.

Just as a bride prepares for her wedding, we must prepare for our meeting with Jesus.

How? Verse 8, “She was given fine linen to wear, bright and pure. For the fine linen represents the righteous acts of the saints.”

Waiting doesn’t mean doing nothing.

Waiting means preparing and we prepare by living righteously.

We don’t live self-righteously, by looking down on other people.

We live righteously, to do what’s right in God’s eyes.

Jesus is looking forward to that great day of celebration.

Jesus has died for us to make us ready.

Are we looking forward to that day as well?

3. True worship accords with Jesus’ testimony

To know how to live righteously before God, we need to know what true worship of God is.

And to know what it means to worship God truly, we need to know the testimony of Jesus.

Look with me at verse 9, “then he said to me, “Write: Blessed are those invited to the marriage feast of the Lamb!” He also said to me, “These words of God are true.”

Do you have a pen in front of you?

You should be able to find a small, blank invitation card in front of you.

Let’s do what verse 9 says, write this down: “Blessed are those invited to the marriage feast of the Lamb!”

It is a blessing to be invited to the marriage feast of the Lamb!

What an honour it is to be invited to the greatest wedding ever!

What a blessing it is to finally be with Jesus, the very thing that you prepared your whole life for since you became a Christian!

The author of Revelation, the Apostle John, bows down to worship the person who told him to write these things down.

But the person responds by saying (in verse 10, “ Don’t do that!a I am a fellow servant with you and your brothers and sisters who hold firmly to the testimony of Jesus. Worship God, because the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy.”

He, too is but a fellow servant with John and his brothers and sisters, with us, who holds firmly to the testimony of Jesus.

The testimony of Jesus is what he says about himself.

For example Jesus says, “For God loved the world in this way: He gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who believes in him (that is Jesus) will not perish but have eternal life”

We’re to hold on to testimonies like these.

For our worship of God is now based on the testimony of Jesus.

We are to worship God according to what Jesus has said about God and himself.

Why? Because the testimony is the spirit of prophecy.

God the Holy Spirit has been pointing us to Jesus throughout the Bible, in both the Old and the New Testament.

All the prophecies that the Holy Spirit gives through his prophets find their fulfilment in Jesus.

More than that, what the Holy Spirit of God says is now said through the lips of Jesus.

And all the words of the apostles, as written in the Bible, are echoes of Jesus’ own words, for it’s the Holy Spirit who guides the apostles to write the words of the New Testament.

That’s what true worship of God is; it’s worshipping God according to the testimony of Jesus.

Do you know the testimony of Jesus?

Have you heard what Jesus’ said about himself?

Have you read what the Holy Spirit has said about him through the Bible?

Read the Bible in your Growth group, here at Church and privately in our own quiet time and find out for yourself.

If you don’t know the testimony of Jesus, you might have been worshipping God falsely.

What does Jesus look forward to after Christmas?

The great day when he comes back for us!

During Christmas, we thank God for the first coming of Jesus for Jesus came to earth to save us!

During Christmas, we also thank God as we look forward to the second coming of Jesus, for Jesus will come back for us!

Joy: More Than Fleeting Happiness

Revelation 19:1-10

Good morning, everyone. Today, we gather on this second Advent Sunday to delve into the depths of joy, a theme that transcends the fleeting happiness we often chase in our daily lives. We'll be focusing on Revelation 19:1-10, exploring the triumphant hallelujahs of heaven, the joyful preparation of a radiant bride, and the essence of true worship according to Jesus' testimony.

The Triumphant Hallelujahs of Heaven

Let's begin by examining the hallelujahs that resound in heaven. Revelation 19 opens with a chorus of voices proclaiming, "Hallelujah! Salvation, glory, and power belong to our God." These triumphant exclamations follow the destruction of Babylon, a symbol of a world power that defies God. Babylon, once a mighty city, represents all worldly powers that rebel against divine authority.

In Revelation 18:21, we read about the ultimate fate of Babylon: "Then a mighty angel picked up a stone like a large millstone and threw it into the sea, saying, ’In this way, Babylon the great city will be thrown down violently and never be found again.’" The demise of Babylon is celebrated in heaven because it signifies the victory of God's righteous judgment over corruption and oppression.

The hallelujahs in this passage are more than just words; they are antiphonal songs, where a call is met with a resounding response. These songs of praise echo through heaven and earth, reminding us that God’s judgments are true and righteous, and that He avenges the blood of His servants.

The Joyful Preparation of a Radiant Bride

As we continue, we encounter the fourth hallelujah, celebrating the forthcoming marriage of the Lamb. This imagery depicts Jesus as the Lamb and the Church as His bride, who has prepared herself for this divine union. The radiant bride is adorned in fine linen, representing the righteous deeds of the saints.

Just as a bride meticulously prepares for her wedding day, we, as the Church, must prepare for our eternal union with Christ. This preparation involves living righteously and faithfully, embodying the values and teachings of Jesus in our daily lives.

True Worship According to Jesus' Testimony

To worship God truly, we must understand and adhere to the testimony of Jesus. Revelation 19:9-10 underscores the importance of holding firmly to Jesus' words and prophecies: "Blessed are those invited to the marriage feast of the Lamb! These words of God are true."

True worship is not merely about rituals or ceremonies; it’s about aligning our hearts and actions with the teachings of Jesus. The testimony of Jesus, guided by the Holy Spirit, is the foundation of our faith and worship. As we read and reflect on the Bible, we deepen our understanding of this testimony and strengthen our relationship with God.

Conclusion

As we approach Christmas, let us not only celebrate the birth of Jesus but also look forward to His second coming. We are reminded that God will triumph over all worldly powers, and we are called to echo heaven’s praises, prepare ourselves as a radiant bride, and worship God according to Jesus' testimony.

May our lives be filled with the joy that comes from knowing and serving our Lord, and may we always be ready to proclaim, "Hallelujah! Salvation, glory, and power belong to our God."

Let us pray: "Father, we thank You for bringing us into Your presence as we hear from Your words. Please take all worries, all temptations, all distractions away from our hearts as we hear from You. Help us to see Jesus more clearly and to live in a way that glorifies You. In Jesus' name, we pray, Amen."

Three questions for reflection this week:

How does the world tempt you away from God in your daily life?

How do you echo the "Hallelujahs of heaven" in your actions and words?

What do the "righteous deeds of the saints" look like in your life and in the lives of those around you?

Friends, let us joyfully anticipate the day when we will be united with Christ, living righteously and worshipping truly, for the glory of God. Hallelujah! Amen.
