The Gift That Keeps Giving

Big Idea: Beloved, give the gift of God’s forgiving and life-giving love, as revealed in the Son’s mission of atonement.

Additional passages: Romans 13:8

1. God’s beloved loves because God is love

2. Love is seen in the Son’s mission of atonement

3. Give the gift of God’s forgiving and life-giving love

Who loves chocolate? If you’re a chocolate lover, have you heard of Godiva?

Godiva is a Belgian chocolate company that made beautiful chocolates.

A few years back, they had this hilarious Christmas commercial called the “box that keeps giving.”

Check it out here: https://youtu.be/Q-2X8g1xK-g?feature=shared

Here’s the clever part: when you open the box, you have two boxes.

One says “Keep,” and the other says “Give.”

After you finish the “Keep” box, you give away the “Give” box as a gift.

The next person gets the same thing, but with half the chocolates.

This keeps going until there’s only two pieces left.

It’s like a gift that keeps giving, until the chocolate runs out.

At Christmas, we often think about getting good gifts for the people we love. Wouldn’t it be amazing to give someone a gift that keeps on giving?

That’s what we’re going to explore in today’s passage.

If you have your Bible with you, let’s open it up to 1 John 4:7-11 together.

In this passage, we’ll discover three things:

1. God’s beloved loves because God is love.

2. Love is seen in the Son’s mission of atonement.

3. Give the gift of God’s forgiving and life-giving love.

Before we dive in, let’s pray, “Father, Giver of all good and perfect gifts, please open our hearts to your words so we can receive the gift you long to give us. In Jesus’ name, we pray, Amen.”

1. God’s beloved loves because God is love.

The first thing we see is that God’s beloved loves because God is love.

Look with me at 1 John chapter 4 verse 7, “Dear friends, let us love one another, because love is from God, and everyone who loves has been born of God and knows God.”

The phrase translated ‘My friends’ is literally the word ‘beloved’.

We don’t use the word beloved much anymore.

The word just means, my loved ones.

Here, the author John the Apostle is referring to a group of people that he loved; they were his beloved.

But more importantly, these people were loved by God; they were God’s beloved.

I don’t know about you, but I don’t usually call my friends ‘beloved’.

Some of us might grow up in cultures where we don’t refer to families and friends as our beloved, or people whom we love.

Having said that, I’ve heard many Chinese men refer to their wives as ‘Lover’, or ‘darling’, or ‘heart’.

My Chinese brothers, you have my respect!

We might call our spouse or close family members with sweet and intimate names, but we probably won’t use these terms on our friends.

But here, John calls his first readers his dear friends, his beloved.

He loved them, and he publicly acknowledged it.

Maybe we need to do that more often.

And so, beloved, what did John tell them to do?

They are to love one another.

What does that mean? For one thing, we know that love often mean different things to different people.

ii) A few days ago, I heard a radio show where the radio hosts called a man on behalf of a woman.

Apparently, the woman was upset because the man hasn’t called the girl back after their romantic date.

She thought they connected and just wanted to know why he didn’t call her.

She thought this was love; she said she was absolutely sure that this was love.

However, when the host called the man, he didn’t think it was a date at all.

He met the girl on the street and casually told her where he’s going with his friends for dinner afterwards

He didn’t invite the girl, but the girl appeared at his restaurant.

They had a few conversations about her but that was it; he didn’t even know her last name.

He didn’t think it was love at all.

They shared the same evening, but they had very different opinion of whether it was love or not.

What does the Bible mean when it tells us to love one another?

Whatever we think about what love is, we must begin with God, for love is from God.

To understand love, we must begin by knowing who God is.

While there are different interpretations of love, most people agree that it’s a good and beautiful thing.

The Bible goes one step further and tells us why love is good and beautiful.

Love is good and beautiful because it comes from God.

More than that, love is holy.

Now notice that this love I’m talking about is God’s love.

It is God’s love that’s holy and pure.

This love of God is different from the love that we choose to define by ourselves.

Real love, genuine love is pure because it comes from God and it’s holy.

This is a love that comes from God and it is experienced by those who are born of God and know Him.

Listen to how this love is described in 1 John chapter 5 verse 1, ‘Everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ has been born of God, and everyone who loves the Father also loves the one born of him.

Everyone who believes in Jesus, the Son of God, will love with the love of Jesus and the love of the Father.

They will love God and His people because that’s what God’s love in them urges them to do.

On the other hand, those who don’t love don’t know God.

It doesn’t matter what they do or how much they know about God; if they don’t love God and His people, they haven’t truly known Him.

This is not talking about knowing God intellectually, but personally.

They can say the facts about God, they might even teach it, but if they don’t love, they never truly knew God.

Those who know God, who have been born again, will love God and His people because God is love.

That doesn’t mean that love a god-like being.

Love is not God, but God is love.

If you truly know God, love Him, and are born of God, if you are beloved of God, you will love with the love of God.

Here at St Paul’s we have something quite unique in our days:

We have an intergenerational service.

This is a wonderful way to learn about what it means to love God and to love his people.

For example, when children see their parents loving and serving, they naturally follow suit.

When they see uncles and aunties, older brothers and sisters loving and serving, they learn to love and serve too.

But if they see us not loving and not serving, they’ll follow that example as well.

In the same way, those who have experienced God’s love will love God’s people the way God loves people.

They see the way God loves, and they follow suit.

The depth of our love for God shows in the way we love others.

If we don’t love, it means we don’t truly know God.

If we love one another God’s way, it shows that we do know God.

Our relationships with others are important to God, because he wants us to love them with his love.

God is love, so we should love one another as he loves us.

2. Love is seen in the Son’s mission of atonement

How does God love us?

The way he loves us is how the Bible defines love.

Look with me at verse 9, “9 God’s love was revealed among us in this way: God sent his one and only Son into the world so that we might live through him.”

God’s love is seen in Jesus, God the Son’s mission of atonement.

This is how God demonstrates his love to the world: God’s sent Jesus into the world so that we might live through him.

That’s why we celebrate Christmas as Christians; we celebrate the fact that God’s sent His Son into the world. On Christmas Day, many families will get together for lunch or for dinner.

My family is organising with the wider family to come together.

In fact, my brother in law has just flew back from Hong Kong and he’s outside our Church to pick up his car, right about…now (let me check my security camera )

The first Christmas didn’t start with families coming together.

It started with God sending his Son out of heaven, out of his home, to a tiny manger in a tiny town on a tiny planet.

It wasn’t because Jesus had done anything wrong, but because he was on a mission from God.

He was sent to us so that those who believe in him can live through him.

The first part of God’s definition of love: God’s sent his Son on a mission so we might live through him.

The second part of God’s definition echoes the first.

Look with me at verse 10, “Love consists in this: not that we loved God, but that he loved us and sent his Son to be the atoning sacrifice for our sins.”

Whatever we think our love for God is, whatever service we give, whatever song we sing, or whatever sacrifices we make, these are not definitive acts of love, as good as these acts may be.

True love is God’s love for us.

True love is God sending his Son to us so that those who believe in him can live through him.

True love is God sending His Son to be the atoning sacrifice for our sins.

The idea of atonement is a concept rooted deep in the Bible and in ancient civilizations where sacrifices were performed to appease the gods and alleviate their wrath.

To atone is to make amends for one’s wrong.

The problem is no matter how hard we try, we can never make amends for our wrong against God.

We are far too sinful, far too weak, to make amend for any sin we committed against God, let alone a life time of sin.

Because at the core of all this is our lack of love for God; we don’t love God as much as we should.

We’ve all fallen short of God’s expects of us, and we have no way to make amends for our sins.

Our lack of love for God and for one another rightly makes God’s angry at us.

But God, out of his love for us, God sent his Son Jesus Christ to be the ultimate atoning sacrifice.

It’s because Jesus had faced God’s anger, died on the cross and rose from the dead, that we can have life.

Jesus was the sacrifice that God made to take away his wrath against us.

And so, what is God’s definition of love?

Here are five things that we see in today’s passage:

Love is sacrificial

Love as God defines is not just a feeling; love is God’s profound act of sacrifice.

Love is familial

Only those born of God, that is, those born again in Jesus Christ can love God and God’s people with God’s love.

The love we have for God and one another is a family trait of being in God’s family.

Love is missional

God sent his own Son, Jesus, on a mission to take on the his own wrath against us.

Jesus accomplished his mission by dying for God’s people on the cross.

Love is life-giving

Love is not about personal satisfaction and fulfilment.

Love is life-giving; it gives life to other people, even when it’s at great cost to our own life.

That’s what we see in God’s love for us in Jesus.

Through Jesus’ death and resurrection, we can have life, everlasting life.

The sacrificial love of Christ is life-giving.

Love is forgiving

Forgiveness is the process of addressing the sin of others, not about condoning it.

We are all sinners, every one of us.

But rather than condemning us, God wants to forgive us.

And so, out of his love for us, God destroys sin, the barrier between us and him, for us to be restored to him.

God’s love is the only way to reconcile with him.

Sin is wrong, and God demands a price from the sinner.

Just as a car accident can have serious consequences, our sins have a profound impact on our lives and the lives of others.

When we pray the Lord’s prayer, when we say forgive us our sins, as we forgive those who sin against us,

The word there translates the word debt.

Our Chinese brothers and sister already know this because that’s the word that’s commonly used in the Lord’s prayer.

In fact, it was used in the older form of the Lord’s prayer: Forgive us our debts, as we have forgiven our debtors.

When we ask God to forgive us our debts, we’re asking him to cancel the debt, the price, that we owe him.

Out of his love for us, God cancels our debts to him in Jesus.

When we believe in Jesus, our sins are paid for! We don’t owe God for our sins anymore because of what Jesus has done!

But God doesn’t just want to cancel our debts; he also wants us to cancel the debt that others owe us.

Sin can do horrible things to relationship and forgiveness of wrongs can be very hard.

Forgiveness is not something to be taken for granted.

Forgiveness is a beautiful to see in a relationship but it’s also costly for the person who forgives.

But we forgive because God forgave us; we love because God loved us.

Forgiveness is how God shows us his love, and so, forgiveness is how we should show our love to each other.

Gentry and Hadley were a Christian couple traveling back from their sister’s wedding in Forest Hills, Charlotte on May 23, 2015, when his car was hit by a truck driver at the traffic lights.

His car then hit his wife’s car, killing their two-year-old son Dobbs.

Hadley was eight months pregnant at the time.

Their son, Reed, was born, but he also died three days later.

This is what he said at their children’s funeral, “We have, in our hearts, forgiven the man who did this. It wasn’t easy, but in some ways it was because we know – Hadley and I – that Jesus Christ has forgiven us our debt… So in some ways, it was very easy to forgive a man who made an accident.”

Has someone wronged you? Does someone owe you a debt?

What will loving that person look like?

What will forgiving that person look like?

3. Give the gift of God’s forgiving and life-giving love

This is the Bible definition of love: Love is both forgiving and sacrificial.

This is he love that God has shown us in Jesus.

How do we respond to God’s love?

Look with me at verse 11, “Dear friends, if God loved us in this way, we also must love one another.”

IF you know and love God, God’s gifted you with his love.

Now go and give the gift of God’s forgiving and life-giving love to someone else.

John chapter 3 verse 16 says that, ‘For God loved the world in this way: he gave his one and only son.

That’s God’s way of loving the world; now go and love your world with the same kind of love, with God’s love.

The word translated ‘must’ at the end of verse 11 can also be translated as owe.

You owe God’s love to them.

For Christians, there are two debts that we have between one another.

Firstly, as sinners, we have a debt of sin that we owe to others and others owe us.

We need to forgive them, cancel their debts, just as God forgives you.

Second, as followers of Jesus, we all have a debt of love that we owe to God and his people.

We will always owe them our love.

It’s a little bit like this:

Who likes to go to Yum Cha?

For those who haven’t been, Yum Cha is a delicious meal with a variety of dishes.

We order the dishes and then we pay when we’re ready to go.

On thing that I’ve seen at many meals is the scene of Asian mothers going through the list to make sure the restaurant didn’t add any extra dishes.

That’s what the bills is for; the bill tells you how much you need to pay.

It’s a small debt we owe to the restaurants, but we can’t leave until we pay it.

If you are a Christian, you are beloved of God!

Being a beloved of God means we have eternal life, love, the assurance of knowing that we’re saved and forgiven by God in Jesus and we can even call God our Father.

It’s like a spiritual feast where we receive every spiritual blessing in Christ.

If we have these things, we know the right response is to love with God’s love.

We owe love to God and to God’s people.

That’s how we will respond if we truly understand the significance of the gift God has given us in Jesus.

Romans chapter 13 verse 8 tells us: Do not owe anyone anything, except to love one another, for the one who loves another has fulfilled the law.

This is the debt that all Christians owe to each other.

We owe one another God’s love, God’s sacrificial, forgiving love.

This is a love that mustn’t stop with us; it’s a love that we must pass on to others.

What does loving one another with God’s love look like in practice?

It’s a love that keeps forgiving.

God’s love is giving and forgiving and so our love must also be giving and forgiving.

This is what the Church should be.

We ought to be a place where we give and forgive.

But that’s not always the case.

We should be a place of love and respect, but we fail miserably because we’re sinners.

What often happens is that when people disagree at church, they leave.

That’s how people do with shopping centres and hotels: if you don’t like it, and you leave.

Sadly, many people who claim to be Christians treat churches like shopping centres and hotels, leaving when they’re confronted with their own sin or they confront the sin of others.

Instead of forgiving or asking for forgiveness they just leave.

It’s a sad reality that’s been happening in churches since the beginning.

However, imagine being a Church that loves one another with God’s love.

Imagine being a church that’s not known for its impressive building, clever strategies, or even its engaging preachers.

Instead, imagine a church that’s known as a church that loves according to God’s love, a church that gives and forgives.

I’m not talking about hiding your sins or shortcomings, but being able to confront them and forgive them in the name of Jesus.

You can keep your impressive buildings and strategies, but give me a church that loves live God’s love.

That’s a church that has received the gift that keeps giving.

That’s the gift to give this Christmas - God’s sacrificial, life-giving, and forgiving love.

It’s a gift that gives life! It’s a gift that recognizes God’s infinite love for us.

It’s a gift that urges us to keep loving and to keep forgiving one another.

Is there anything more lasting and more fitting than the gift of God’s love for us in Christ Jesus?

Do you have the gift that keeps giving?

Give the gift of the gospel.

And forgive those who sinned against you, just as God’s forgiven your sins in Jesus.

Beloved, my dear brothers and sisters, give the gift of God’s forgiving and life-giving love, as revealed in the Son’s mission of atonement.

Remember the Godiva commercial?

The chocolate box is designed as a gift for you and as a gift for you to pass on.

That’s a what knowing the gospel of Jesus Christ is like.

It’s like a box of Godiva chocolate.

The gospel, the love of God, was never meant for you to enjoy alone.

It was never meant to stop with you.

It was made for you to share it.

Who will you share the gospel with this Christmas?

Beloved, give the gift of God’s forgiving and life-giving love, as revealed in the Son’s mission of atonement.

How does the Bible define what love is?

Is love as described by the Bible reactive or proactive? Is it both or is it neither?

How are you ‘repaying’ the debt of love you owe God (and God’s people)?

Friends, give the gift of God’s forgiving and life-giving love.

Love as God has revealed his love for us in the son’s mission of atonement.
