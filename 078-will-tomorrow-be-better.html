1. Predicting the end of the temple

Let's open the Bible and read Mark 13:1

As he was going out of the temple, one of his disciples said to him, “Teacher, look! What massive stones! What impressive buildings!”

The temple that this disciple saw at that time was really a magnificent building.

This temple was not the one built by King Solomon.

What the disciples saw was the temple built by King Herod.

He started to build the temple in 20 BC.

When Jesus and his disciples saw this temple, it was about 33 AD.

Although fifty years have passed, the construction of the temple has not yet been completed.

Herod’s temple took a total of 84 years to complete, finally finishing in 64 AD.

Why did it take so long to build this temple?

It was because this temple was massive.

Its area was more than 162,000 square metres.

That’s equivalent to 130 Olympic swimming pools.

There was a sanctuary in this temple.

The pillars of this sanctuary were as high as fifty metres.

When Jesus' disciples saw this magnificent temple, it’s little wonder that they respond by saying, "what impressive buildings!".

For the disciples and Jews at the time, the temple was not only an impressive building.

The temple was a symbol of God’s presence.

When King Solomon completed the first temple, he prayed to God: When your people Israel are defeated before an enemy, because they have sinned against you, and they return to you and praise your name, and they pray and plead with you for mercy in this temple, 34 may you hear in heaven and forgive the sin of your people Israel. May you restore them to the land you gave their ancestors.

When they pray to God in this temple, God will listen to them.

When a devout Jew looked the temple, it wasn’t just a building.

He would think of God's promise, and he would see that God is with his people.

However, from the day Jesus entered Jerusalem right up to the moment his disciples were praising the temple,

Jesus had repeatedly shown that he was not happy with the temple in its current state.

Once, Jesus entered the temple and drove out those who were buying and selling in the temple.

When the chief priest who was in charge of the temple asked Jesus who gave him the authority to do these things,

Jesus used a parable to say that God would punish those who were in charge of the temple for failing in their responsibility to look after the temple.

Up to this point, Jesus has not been dealing the temple, per se.

Jesus was dealing with the people who were in charge of the temple at that time.

But in our passage today, he said to his disciples: “Do you see these great buildings? Not one stone will be left upon another—all will be thrown down.”

Here, Jesus is saying that it’s not only those who are in charge of the temple who will be punished by God.

The entire temple will be demolished.

Jesus said that this magnificent temple is destined to be destroyed.

When Jesus said this, it was about 30 AD.

In 70 AD, the Roman Empire sent their troops to attack Jerusalem.

In the process, they razed the temple to the ground.

The Jews could no longer pray in the direction of the temple.

Their temple was destroyed.

The temple that symbolised the presence of God has gone.

For the Jews, this was a very big blow.

Almost twenty years ago, on September 11, 2001, when terrorists attacked the Twin Towers in New York, the Americans were dealt with a massive blow.

The twin towers at that time symbolised Americans' economic achievements.

But when the terrorists razed the twin towers to the ground, the Americans' confidence in themselves took a very big hit.

How about us?

What’s one big event in our lives that have shaken us to the core?

Talk with the person next to you and ask what’s one big event and that has shaken us?

If you’re at home, you can message us via Zoom, Whatsapp or Wechat

When the Roman Empire destroyed the temple, the faith of the Jews at that time also took a very big hit.

Was God really with them?

Are they still the people of God?

If God allows his temple to be destroyed, is the relationship between the Jews and God over too?

If the relationship between God's people and God is over, is the relationship between mankind and God over too?

Jesus had four disciples, Peter, James, John, and Andrew.

They asked Jesus privately: 4 “Tell us, when will these things happen? And what will be the sign when all these things are about to be accomplished?”

They believed what Jesus said; they believed that one day the temple would be destroyed.

They wanted to know how they can prepare for that day.

They want to know what signs they have to pay attention to.

Jesus told them not only how to prepare for the destruction of the temple.

Jesus told them how to prepare for the end of the world.

2. Preparing for the end of the world

First, they must guard against false Christs.

Let's look at verse 5: Jesus told them, “Watch out that no one deceives you. 6 Many will come in my name, saying, ‘I am he,’ and they will deceive many.

There are many people who will call themselves Christ; they will say that they are Saviours.

They will teach their own things, and not the truth Jesus teaches.

They’ll probably be eloquent and articulate, able to convince many people.

In fact, Jesus say that many will be deceived by these false Christs and believe in them.

But the disciples of Jesus must be careful not to be deceived.

Second, the disciples of Jesus need to be careful about sign-like events.

Let’s look at verses 7-8: 7 When you hear of wars and rumors of wars, don’t be alarmed; these things must take place, but it is not yet the end. 8 For nation will rise up against nation, and kingdom against kingdom. There will be earthquakes in various places, and famines. These are the beginning of birth pains.

Wars are very tragic events.

There are many places in the world where there are wars and unrest right now.

On the battlefield, soldiers and civilians will experience pain and death.

When they encounter the suffering caused by the war, they may ask: "Wil today be the end of the world?"

When we see countries fighting, when we hear people attacking other people, even their own people, are these the signs of the end of the world?

As well as man-made disasters, we also have many natural disasters in our world.

There are many places in the world that experience earthquakes and famines.

Often, when a place experiences a major earthquake, many homes will be destroyed.

When we encounter more of these natural and man-made disasters, are we about to see the end of the world?

Are these the signs of the end of the world?

Jesus said: "These things must happen, but this is not yet the end."

Although these things bring a lot of pain, this is not yet the end.

Even if Jerusalem was attacked by the Roman Empire, even if they razed Jerusalem to the ground, this would not be the end.

Even if the temple is destroyed in the Roman Empire attack, this would not the end, this would not be the end of the world.

Jesus tells his disciples: "Don't panic; these things must happen, but this is not yet the end."

In the Chinese Bible, these are described as "the beginning of disasters."

In the Chinese bible, we see this explanation: "The literal translation of the original "the beginning of birth pains"

These natural and man-made disasters are "birth pains."

When a pregnant mother is about to give birth, she feels a lot of pain.

Some will hurt for several hours; others will hurt for more than ten hours.

I even heard that some go in labour for tens of hours!

But after a mother has experienced these pains, she will meet a new life.

She will meet her baby.

Jesus said that the world is now experiencing "birth pains."

Both Christians and non-Christians will experience these labour pains; both Christians and non-Christians will experience natural and man-made disasters.

We will experience wars, earthquakes, and famines together.

These sufferings remind us that this world is not eternal.

One day, this world will be destroyed.

One day, a new world will come.

The question we have to ask ourselves is whether we live for this world, that will be one day be destroyed

or whether we live for the new heavens and new earth that will come, that will last forever.

These ominous signs, these tragic events, will happen, because this world will pass away one day.

But these things are not the end, these are not the signs of the end of the world.

In addition to these things, the followers of Jesus must face the hardships; they must endure suffering.

In addition to the natural and man-made disasters that the world encounters, Jesus said that those who believe in him will encounter additional sufferings.

Let's look at verse 9:

“But you, be on your guard! They will hand you over to local courts, and you will be flogged in the synagogues. You will stand before governors and kings because of me, as a witness to them.”

Believers will be punished and beaten for the sake of the Lord Jesus.

Believers will bear witness to the Lord Jesus before rulers and kings.

If and when these kings do not accept their words, the believers will go to jail for the Lord Jesus' sake.

But the Lord Jesus said that believers that they are not alone when they do all these things for Jesus.

The Holy Spirit will be with the believers.

When they are on trial, they don't need to worry, because the Holy Spirit will give them the words to say.

When we look at the Book of Acts in the Bible, we see these very things happening to believers.

They were willing to be punished and beaten for the sake of the Lord Jesus.

They bore witness to the Lord Jesus in the presence of rulers and kings.

In the book of Acts, we often see that when believers are filled with the Holy Spirit, they testify about the Lord Jesus and they share the gospel.

Even so, the road they travelled on was not an easy one.

Their relatives would betray them and would even them.

Believers, both past and present, will be hated by everyone, for the sake of the name of Jesus.

In verse 13 Jesus said this:

" You will be hated by everyone because of my name, but the one who endures to the end will be saved."

Although believers will encounter a lot of suffering, they must persevere to the end.

Only those who endure to the end will be saved.

We who follow the Lord Jesus must never give up half way.

Jesus wants us to preach the gospel.

He wants us to preach the gospel to all peoples.

Even if we encounter punishment, even if we encounter hatred, or even death, we cannot give up.

We cannot give up because only those believers who endure to the end will be saved.

Who are the believers who are truly saved?

It’s the believers who endure to the end.

They will be saved.

The believers of the first century persevered to the end and faithfully preached the gospel to all people.

In the past two thousand years, believers have remained faithful and went to different parts of the world to preach the gospel.

For Jesus' sake, countless believers have experienced a lot of pain.

But they still endure to the end and preach the gospel.

Perhaps there are those among us who are Christians, who have suffered a lot for the sake of our Lord Jesus Christ.

I am very grateful to the Lord for you, that you are enduring to the end.

When I was a young Christian, I have often asked myself, if I encounter suffering, real suffering for the Lord Jesus' sake, would I endure to the end?

When I am in distress, will I endure, or will I give up?

In this passage, we see that even if we are suffering, we are not alone.

The Holy Spirit of God will be with us.

He will lead us through the sufferings we will encounter.

He will also teach us in our lives, enable us to learn the lesson of faith, and prepare us so that we’ll be ready on the day we face the test of faith.

For a new believer, he might give him some basic lessons in faith.

For example, will this new believer faithfully come to church, in person or online, every Sunday?

Would this believer not come to church some trivial things?

Will he not come to church because of work, because of the children, because he’s not in the mood?

How will he face this basic, basic lesson of faith?

If he passes this faith lesson, the Holy Spirit will teach him the next faith lesson.

The Holy Spirit will teach believers who belong to the Lord to grow our faith so that we can endure to the very end.

From the verse five to thirteen, Jesus said that the world and believers will encounter the labour pains, and that the true believers will endure to the end for the Lord Jesus’ sake

Starting from verse fourteen, Jesus describes a catastrophe.

His command to the disciples here is not to stand staunchly.

Here, he tells the believers to flee to the mountain.

One day, an unparalleled distress will come

3. An unparalleled distress

Let's look at verses 14-16,

“When you see the abomination of desolation standing where it should not be” (let the reader understand), “then those in Judea must flee to the mountains. 15 A man on the housetop must not come down or go in to get anything out of his house, 16 and a man in the field must not go back to get his coat.

Jesus says that when believers see the abomination of desolation standing where they should not be, the believers should flee.

In the Book of Daniel in the Old Testament, the Bible mentions a person who’s described as the "abomination of desolation "

In Daniel's prophecy, he said that the "abomination of desolation " would defile the temple

After a period of time, a general named Antiochus IV entered the temple in 167 BC and sacrificed there.

He wasn’t s there to offer sacrifices to God; he was offering sacrifices to Greek idols.

Not only that, he also sacrificed pigs, animals that the Jews considered unclean.

He deliberately defiled, vandalised the temple of God.

Many people agree that General Antiochus IV is the whom Daniel described as a the "abomination of desolation ".

In today's passage, Jesus once again referred to another "abomination of desolation ".

When this "abomination of desolation " stands in the place where it should not be, the believers must flee.

If they are on the roof, they won't have time to come down and get the things inside the house.

They must leave as soon as possible.

Jesus said that those who are pregnant and nursing children will suffer because they fled with the babies inside of them and or carrying babies who do not know how to walk yet.

What Jesus describes here is a very urgent matter.

Here, Jesus doesn’t use the phrase "the pain of birth" to describe this period.

Jesus is not describing an extended period of time.

He is here to describing a unique moment.

In 70 AD, Jerusalem and the temple inside of it were attacked by the Roman Empire.

The temple at that time was razed to the ground.

At that time, about one million Jews were killed.

For the Jews at the time, this was a very tragic event.

Was the Roman Empire the ""abomination of desolation" described by Jesus?

Open up with me to verse 19, "For those will be days of tribulation, the kind that hasn’t been from the beginning of creation until now and never will be again.”

For the Jews at that time, they did encounter a catastrophe.

But Jesus said that the disaster he’s describing is unparalleled: since God created all things until now, there has never been such a disaster.

In human history, human beings have never experienced the distress described by Jesus.

Not only that, Jesus said that mankind will never experience this distress again in the future.

Although what the Jews have experienced at that time was very tragic, other people have experienced similar disasters in different places in the world before and after them.

Some people have even experienced events more miserable than what the Jews at that time.

Therefore, the distress that Jesus mentioned here has probably not happened yet.

In verse twenty, Jesus said, "If the Lord had not cut those days short, no one would be saved. But he cut those days short for the sake of the elect, whom he chose.”

Here, it is not just the Jews who live in Jerusalem Jews who are affected.

Those who are affected are everyone, all human beings.

We thank God because He will reduce those days, otherwise none of us can be saved, and all humanity will perish.

When the unparalleled distress comes, when the "abomination of desolation" comes to stand in the wrong place, what should we do?

Jesus once again tells us to watch with wisdom and caution.

4. Watch with patience and wisdom!

Let's look at verse 21, “Then if anyone tells you, ‘See, here is the Messiah! See, there!’ do not believe it. 22 For false messiahs and false prophets will arise and will perform signs and wonders to lead astray, if possible, the elect.”

From the day of birth pains to the day of catastrophe, false Christs will continue to deceive people and make people follow them.

They will use signs and wonders to confuse people, even including believers, if possible.

That’s why Jesus said to believers that in verse 23: " And you must watch! I have told you everything in advance.“

Jesus has told us in advance that false christs will come.

We must be wise and cautious.

How can we learn to be wise and prudent?

First, we must learn to read the Bible.

We believe that the Bible is inspired by God and is given to us by God.

If someone says that God has revealed himself to him, we have to see if what he said matches the teaching of the Bible.

God inspires the writing of the Bible, through his Holy Spirit.

God the Holy Spirit teaches us through the Bible, and God does not contradict himself.

If someone says that God has revealed himself to him, even if he says that the Holy Spirit has revealed God to him,

But if what he says is not in line with the Bible, you know that his teaching does not come from God.

Therefore, if we are to be wise and cautious, we need to study and read the Bible.

Will tomorrow be better?

Brothers and sisters, in today's passage, we see that the world we’re living in is going through birth pains.

The world has gone through pain before and will go through pain again tomorrow.

And we know that one day, an unparalleled distress will come, a disaster that hasn’t been seen from the beginning and will never happen again.

That tomorrow will not be better but will be the worst.

But Christians are told by Jesus how to prepare for that day.

Jesus told us to persevere and to endure; we must watch with patience and wisdom.

Before an unparalleled distress strikes, we must preach the gospel to all people.

We must tell them that there’s hope for tomorrow, even a disastrous tomorrow, because there’s salvation in the Lord Jesus Christ.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Do I face tomorrow with Jesus or without Jesus?

What may tempt me to stop enduring suffering for Jesus’ sake?

What is God doing in my life to strengthen my faith in Jesus?

Please strengthen our faith so that we can preach your gospel. We pray in the name of the Lord Jesus, Amen.”
