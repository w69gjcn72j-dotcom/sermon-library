Hope for the Broken

Passage: Isaiah 40:1-11

Big Idea: God shows his glorious might and loving comfort to his broken people in Jesus, the Good Shepherd.

Question: How does God deal with my brokenness?

God’s people are broken

God’s glory is coming

God’s glory has come in the Good Shepherd

As you come into Church today, you would notice a lot of rubbish on the streets.

That’s because it’s time for the Council to collect rubbish from our streets again.

As I walk along Princes Highway, here are some of the things I’ve seen on the streets:

Broken fans, broken furniture, broken toys.

Someone’s even put a broken basin in front of our Church and it’s not even ours!

That’s how some people see 2020: it’s a broken year!

If we could, we’ll throw the year 2020 away along with the broken basin!

If 2020 has taught us anything, it’s this: human beings are very fragile and can easily be broken.

No matter how far we might think we’ve come, no matter how mighty we think we are, an invisible virus can wreak havoc on our lives.

Even as we speak, the United States are going through a terrible time with COVID-19.

On Thursday, one American life was lost every 30 seconds to the virus, with 200,000 new cases.

Please continue to pray for the Americans and many people all around the world who are still suffering from COVID-19.

Human lives are fragile.

More than that, human lives are not just fragile, they are broken.

Not only are we vulnerable to viruses; we’re vulnerable to our own failings.

If we’re honest with ourselves, it doesn’t take long before we see the failings in our own lives.

It doesn’t take long before we see how we could’ve loved our families more, worked harder in our studies, be a better person than we are.

Only the proudest, blindest person doesn’t see the brokenness in their lives.

If anything, the hardship that we face in a difficult year like 2020 doesn’t break us; rather it reveals the brokenness that’s already inside of us.

When we’re under pressure, it shows who we really are on the inside.

And often, it’s not pretty.

God, of course, already knows who we are on the inside, long before we see it ourselves.

How does God deal with what he sees inside us?

How does God deal with our brokenness?

This is what we’ll see in today’s passage, in Isaiah 40:1-11

In today’s talk, I’m going to make three points:

God’s people are broken

God’s glory is coming

God’s glory has come in the Good Shepherd

God’s people are broken

Open up your Bible to Isaiah 40.

Isaiah is a long book in the Old Testament, with 66 chapters.

For many of the first 39 chapters, God has told his people that they have failed God miserably.

Though as God’s people, they should’ve been obeying God, they were rebelling against him, time and time again.

God’s people were a broken people.

And so, God said he would send foreign forces to punish Israel.

There were chapters after chapters of judgement pronounced in these 39 chapters.

However, by the time we come to chapter 40, there’s a change in tone.

Look with me at Isaiah 40:1

“Comfort, comfort my people,” says your God.

“Speak tenderly to Jerusalem, and announce to her that her time of hard service is over, her iniquity has been pardoned, and she has received from the Lord’s hand double for all her sins.”

A time of comfort has come for God’s broken people.

They had been suffering hard services for their sin.

In the context of Isaiah, we know that God’s people have been suffering under a foreign power, a power that God himself had sent.

It’s been a difficult time for God’s people.

It’s a time when they remember how fragile and how broken they are.

Look with me at verse 6-8.

“A voice was saying, “Cry out!” Another said, “What should I cry out?” “All humanity is grass, and all its goodness is like the flower of the field. 7 The grass withers, the flowers fade when the breath of the Lord blows on them; indeed, the people are grass. 8 The grass withers, the flowers fade, but the word of our God remains forever.”

God says all people, including his own, are as short-lived as the flowers of the field.

On Friday, I picked up these flowers from our front yard.

They were all bright yellow when I picked them.

Now, they’re all withered and lifeless.

In a little while, you won’t see any traces of them.

That’s what all humanity, all people are like.

We are not invincible people with long lasting legacies.

Human beings, like flowers of the field, will fade away.

Here in verse 6, the word translated as goodness is the Hebrew word ‘Hesed’.

The word Hesed is seen throughout the Old Testament and is usually translated as God’s faithful, loving kindness.

In response to God’s love for us, we ought to faithful to God.

We ought to be showing our Hesed to God.

To show God Hesed, to be faithful to God, would be a good and beautiful thing for us to do.

It would be the best thing for us to do.

That would be what’s expected from the very best of humanity.

However, even the very best of what we offer to God can and does fade away.

No matter how strong we think we are, no matter how good we think we are, we will fade away.

A few years ago, many Churches had set vision statements for the Year 2020.

Many have set goals of what they want to achieve.

Many have dream dreams of what they would like to see happen in 2020.

In fact, our diocese had a Mission 2020 template.

It was done using data from a survey in 2011 and was sent to Churches a few years ago to anticipate goals we can aim for in 2020.

As with many other goals and visions, these dreams weren’t realised in 2020.

There were many broken dreams in 2020.

2020 is a reminder of what God has said in Isaiah all those years ago.

“All humanity is grass.”

Even the very best of humanity fades away.

But that’s not all that God tells us in Isaiah.

God tells us that a change is coming.

Thank God that a change is coming!

In fact, God tells us that his glory is coming.

God’s glory is coming

Look with me at verses 3-5,

“ A voice of one crying out:

Prepare the way of the Lord in the wilderness; make a straight highway for our God in the desert. 4 Every valley will be lifted up, and every mountain and hill will be levelled; the uneven ground will become smooth and the rough places, a plain. 5 And the glory of the Lord will appear, and all humanity together will see it, for the mouth of the Lord has spoken.”

Yes, people are broken but God won’t leave us in our brokenness.

He’s coming to do something about it.

God’s coming and he’s telling people to get ready to meet him.

He’s telling people to fill the valleys, level the mountains and the hills, smooth the rough grounds.

Make a highway in the desert if you have to.

Get ready to see God’s glory!

For those of us living around in Kogarah, you might have noticed signs that say there’s a new Motorway being built in Kogarah.

It’s the M6 Stage 1.

There will be tunnels built linking President Avenue to the M8 Motorway at Arncliffe.

What does it mean for us?

It’ll only take us 15 minutes to drive to Anzac Bridge and, wait for this, only 8 minutes to drive to the city.

Eight Minutes!

Tonnes of dirt will be removed, kilometres of tunnels will be built, roads will be paved

So that if people from the city want to come to Kogarah, it’ll only take 8 minutes!

While I’m sure that over the coming years, there will be many great things in Kogarah for people to come to,

But nothing will ever compare with God’s glory.

If we’re ready to remove obstacles and build highways to Kogarah, how much more should we remove obstacles and build highways to see God’s glory.

Of course, that doesn’t necessarily mean that we all need to physically build a highway, but it does mean that we need to remove whatever it is in our lives that stops us from seeing God’s glory.

What can stop us from seeing God’s glory?

It might be our gaze on wealth that stops us from seeing God’s glory.

We might be more interested in what money can buy than to see God’s glory.

Other times, we might stare at the over-achievers, the rich, the powerful, the beautiful, the clever, instead of seeing God’s glory.

We rely on humans to lead us and rescue us rather than turning our eyes to God.

When we turn our gaze on anything or anyone else other than God’s glory, we miss out on what He has for us.

What does God’s glory bring?

In our hardship, often from our own doing, God’s glory brings us comfort.

At the beginning of the passage, God tells his prophet to speak tenderly to Jerusalem, that “her iniquity has been pardoned.”

At the end of today’s passage, the idea of comfort comes again.

This time, God’s comfort is illustrated with the idea of his arm.

Look with me at verses 10,

“See, the Lord God comes with strength, and his power establishes his rule. His wages are with him, and his reward accompanies him.”

The word translated power here is the same word as arm.

It’s God’s arm that establishes his rule.

God’s arm is powerful and it’s by his arm that he wins over his enemies.

Nothing can defeat God’s arm.

As God comes in his glory, he carries his wages and his reward with him, with his arm, so to speak.

What’s in his arm?

Verse 11 11 He protects his flock like a shepherd; he gathers the lambs in his arm and carries them in the fold of his garment. He gently leads those that are nursing.”

God uses his mighty, powerful arm to carry his people like a shepherd would his lambs.

God’s arm is both powerful and gentle, mighty and tender.

That’s what you see when you see God in his glory.

When God comes in his glory, you’ll see the mighty and tender arm of God

A phenomenon that many people have seen among fathers is something called the Dad reflex.

It’s when Dads tend to have these superhuman reflexes to save their kids when they get into all sorts of trouble.

Researches have shown that when a father look at their kids, there’s an adrenaline spike so that they are even more ready than usual to respond in a fight or flight scenario.

These Dad reflexes have saved many young children from getting seriously hurt.

When God looks at his children, he has a Heavenly Father reflex that saves them from spiritual dangers.

That doesn’t mean that they would never experience any pain because we still live in an imperfect world.

But it does mean that even in those times of pain, we’re still safe in his mighty, tender arm.

That’s the comfort that God’s people can have as they prepare for the arrival of God’s glory.

And so, God tells his people to keep an eye out for God’s glory; they’re to look out for God’s glory.

God’s people waited many years for God to show his glory.

It wasn’t until some seven hundred years afterwards that God had finally shown his glory.

In the coming of Jesus, the Good Shepherd, God’s glory has finally come.

3. God’s glory has come in the Good shepherd

Listen to the beginning of the Gospel of Mark:

“The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God. 2 As it is written in Isaiah the prophet:

See, I am sending my messenger ahead of you; he will prepare your way. 3 A voice of one crying out in the wilderness: Prepare the way for the Lord; make his paths straight!

It’s a direct quote from Isaiah’s message!

With the coming of Jesus, God’s glory has come!

It’s like seeing the sunrise after waiting in the darkness for a very long time.

With the coming of Jesus, John the Baptist declares, “Behold, Look, the Lamb of God that takes away the sin of the world!”

With the coming of Jesus God has shown his glory to the world.

With the coming of Jesus, God gives his people comfort.

God has said that his people’s iniquity has been pardoned and they have received from the Lord’s hand double for all their sins.

That’s because Jesus Christ, by dying on the cross, has paid for all their sins.

With his outstretched arm on the cross, Jesus has paid for the sins of those who belong to God.

It’s by his powerful arms, the very arms of God, that Jesus has saved his people.

It’s also by the same arms that Jesus carries his people.

Jesus is the Good Shepherd who carries his lambs in his arms.

Jesus describes himself as the Good Shepherd who lays down his life for his sheep.

That’s the comfort that Christians have in the Lord Jesus.

We know that in Jesus, they have the glory of God.

We know that in the Lord Jesus, they have a Good Shepherd who had saved us by his mighty power and who carries us safely in his arm.

We know that despite our fragility, our brokenness, our failures, we can come safely to Jesus.

Our strengths might be spent, our status might be poor, our lives may fade away, but we have a Saviour whose glory will never fade, whose words will always remain.

Though we are broken, we have Jesus, God’s glory.

How does God deal with our brokenness? In the Book of Isaiah of the Old Testament, God promised to come in his glory to deal with our brokenness

In the Gospel accounts in the New Testament, we see that God’s glory has come in the Person of Jesus, the Good Shepherd,

Who saved us by his mighty arm.

How should we respond?

Shout out Jesus’ glory.

In English, to shout out somebody is to acknowledge somebody who’s made a big influence on you, especially when you’re placed in the limelight.

For example, someone on the radio might say, “A shout out to my Music teacher, Mrs Melody, who taught me the love of music.”

As Christians, we’re to shout our Jesus’ glory whenever we get the chance.

We shout out Jesus’ glory in praise when we see his glory, when we see what he’s done to save us.

We shout in amazement at who he is and what he’s done.

We might do that in the privacy of our own daily Bible study or when we pray.

We also shout out Jesus’ glory in public and tell others about how good Jesus really is.

In our passage today, God’s message was sent to Jerusalem but it wasn’t a message just for Jerusalem alone.

God’s glory will appear, and all humanity together will see it (Verse 5).

Verse 9, “Zion, herald of good news, go up on a high mountain. Jerusalem, herald of good news, raise your voice loudly. Raise it, do not be afraid! Say to the cities of Judah, “Here is your God!”

God’s glory is not limited to within the walls of the Church.

It’s to be proclaimed to all the cities, to all people, to all the world.

We won’t let anything stop us from proclaiming God’s glory to all the world.

One person who hasn’t let her trials stop her from proclaiming Jesus is ​JONI EARECKSON TADA

Joni was a young 17 year old girl enjoying life.

She enjoyed horse-riding and swimming as well as many other activities.

One day, she was next to a lake and decided to jump in.

However, she misjudged the depth of the lake and jumped headfirst into the creek bank.

As a result, she broke her neck and became a quadriplegic.

She was a young Christian but she hated life; she didn’t want to live.

However, as she was struggling with her condition, someone told her, ‘Joni, Jesus understands what you’re going through.

There was a time when he, too, couldn’t move his hands and his feet.

It’s because they were nailed to the cross.”

In time, Joni understood that Jesus suffered and died on the cross for broken people like Joni and indeed, broken people everywhere.

Since then, Joni has been shouting Jesus’ glory whenever she gets the chance.

She wants the whole world, all flesh to know, that God’s glory has come in the Person of Jesus.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Have you admitted your brokenness before God?

Do you know the joy that comes from knowing Jesus, God’s glory?

When was the last time you shouted out Jesus’ glory to someone else?

As Christmas approaches, it’s a reminder that God’s glory has come.

Clear all obstacles, go to the high mountains, tell your friends, sing it out loud, “Behold, here is your God, here is God’s glory, here is Jesus!”

This Christmas, shout our Jesus’ glory!

Let’s pray,

“Our heavenly Father, thank you for showing us your glory in the Person of Jesus Christ. Father, thank you for loving us and saving us even though we’re broken and fickle. Father, by your Holy Spirit, breathe over us, let us hear your glorious word, that we might live for the glory of Jesus. Please use all of our lives to shout out his glory. For your glory and for Jesus’ glory we pray, Amen.”
