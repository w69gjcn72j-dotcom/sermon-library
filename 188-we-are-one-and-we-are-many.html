We Are One and We Are Many

1 Corinthians 12:12-31 · 2021
Big IdeaBe the one united body of Christ God has arranged us to be.

We are one and we are many

1 Cor 12:12-31

Big Idea: Be the one united body of Christ God has arranged us to be

What God has arranged together

We all need each other

Though gifted differently, together we desire the greater gifts

Back when I was in first year University, I joined Bridge Club.

Bridge was a card game that my Father taught me and I though I’ll give it a go at the Bridge Club.

Besides the Bridge Club, there were rock-climbing clubs, pretty sure there would’ve been a chess club.

There was even a Shanghainese club and a Hong Kong Club.

Of course, there were also many Christian clubs to join.

I joined three in my first year.

Some students, I’m sure, would’ve thought the Christian clubs were just one of the many social clubs on offer at the University.

But is the Church like a social club?

Do I treat the Church like a social club?

Do I come and go to Church as I please, just like a social club?

How’s the Church different from a social club whose specialty is religion?

Though we might expect some people who have never been to Church to think of the Church as a social club,

Sadly, some people who come to Church all their lives think the same.

Though we might not put it quite as crass as that, by their actions, some people at Church also treat Church like a social club.

Why is it wrong, sinful even, to think of the Church like a social club?

Please open your Bible with me to 1 Corinthians 12, beginning from verse 12.

In today’s passage, we’ll see three things:

What God has arranged together

We all need each other

Though gifted differently, together we desire the greater gifts.

Before we look into God’s Word, let’s pray,

“Father, by your Spirit, please open our hearts and our minds to understand it, and please move our hands and feet so that we may obey it. In Jesus’ name, Amen.”

What God has arranged together

Firstly, what God has arranged together, let not the Church separate!

Look with me at 1 Corinthians 12:12-13

“For just as the body is one and has many parts, and all the parts of that body, though many, are one body—so also is Christ. 13 For we were all baptized by one Spirit into one body—whether Jews or Greeks, whether slaves or free—and we were all given one Spirit to drink.”

Christians believe in and belong to one Lord, Jesus Christ.

We all believe in the one God.

But though the God we believe in is the same God, there’s a lot of differences between Christians.

Even so, it’s God himself who brings all these different people, from different backgrounds, different language groups, to become one, beautiful, body He calls the Church.

It’s like this:

Last Christmas, my daughter Zoe had a puzzle as her present.

It’s a beautiful puzzle of butterflies; it had 1000 pieces.

And so, just after Christmas, she started putting it together.

She looked at the cover to see where different butterflies are in the picture.

The rest of the family gave her a hand whenever we could.

After about a week, we’ve completed the puzzle.

If all we had were the individual pieces and if we didn’t know that they were pieces of a puzzle, we’d probably disregard it and throw it away.

But knowing they were pieces of a bigger picture, and knowing what the big picture should look like,

we were able to slowly and patiently put the puzzle together, knowing that it would be a magnificent picture at the end.

That’s a little like what the Church is.

The Bible describes the Church as different parts of the body.

Every Church member is a different body part, but we come together as a magnificent picture of the body of Christ.

If we believe in the same Lord, we’re all baptised into the one Spirit.

That is, we all have the Holy Spirit inside us and we live by the Holy Spirit.

We’re baptised, we’re completely immersed, in God the Holy Spirit.

And yet, we’re all unique; we’re all different.

The Spirit doesn’t make us all the same when we become Christians.

God the Holy Spirit is far more creative than that!

When we become a Christian, he gives us a new life, with new gifts and places us in the Church, described here as the Body of Jesus Christ.

What he wants to see in the Church is not uniformity, that everyone must be exactly the same.

Rather, he wants to see unity in us: different and yet working together.

Look with me at verse 14-16,

“14 Indeed, the body is not one part but many. 15 If the foot should say, “Because I’m not a hand, I don’t belong to the body,” it is not for that reason any less a part of the body. 16 And if the ear should say, “Because I’m not an eye, I don’t belong to the body,” it is not for that reason any less a part of the body.”

Just because you’re from a particular background is not an excuse for you to leave the body, the Church.

Just because you speak a particular language is not an excuse for you to leave the Church.

Just because you dress worse than everyone else at Church, because you don’t earn as much as them, is not an excuse for you to leave the Church.

You come to Church because you are a body part, a member, of the Body of Christ.

Verses 17-18, “17 If the whole body were an eye, where would the hearing be? If the whole body were an ear, where would the sense of smell be? 18 But as it is, God has arranged each one of the parts in the body just as he wanted.”

The body is not represented by just one body part.

The body consists of the whole body; the whole body includes every body part.

There is One Spirit of God; there is One Body of Christ.

And there is one God who has arranged the body parts together.

Look with me at verses 19-20 “19 And if they were all the same part, where would the body be? 20 As it is, there are many parts, but one body.”

The Church is God’s design; he’s arranged the different parts together.

He’s arranged the many parts together.

God’s put them together.

Different parts; one body.

Can you think of another part of the Bible where God forms a body from different parts?

Let me give you a clue: the two will become one flesh.

That’s right! It’s marriage!

The oneness we have when we’re united to Christ by the One Spirit that sounds a lot like a marriage between a man and a woman.

Of course, the Bible is not saying that we are married to one another when we come to Church,

But often the Church is described as the Bride of Christ.

It’s a description of a very close, very intimate relationship.

We are bound to Christ and to one another by something that makes us one, as if we’re now one flesh.

Better still, we are bound to Christ and to one another not by something but Someone;

and that Someone is none other than the Holy Spirit.

Though we are unique and different, we’re bound by One Spirit to one Lord, forming one Body.

We are still distinctive individuals, but God has arranged us so that we are bound together by His Spirit to the Church, the Body of Christ.

What separates us in the world does not separate us in the Body of Christ.

When I was in University, I joined a Bridge Club.

It’s a club where we played the card game Bridge.

I attended a few times but because I didn’t think I was good enough, I left.

And nobody noticed (not that I know of anyway).

However, it’s very different when the time came for me to leave different Churches.

I love the Churches where I’ve attended, and I’ve only left them when I moved to a different country or when God’s called me and my family to start a new ministry.

It’s a painful experience each time, not because things went badly but because I love the Churches that I’ve been a part of.

These were my brothers and sisters in Christ, people with whom I have shared my life.

If we do ever leave a Church, it’s always done after much thought and prayers.

It’s never taken lightly.

There will be times in our lives where we will have legitimate reasons to think about leaving a Church.

It might be because you’ve moved countries; it might be because God’s called you into a new season of ministry.

Those times will be painful; it will be a time of loss.

And so, don’t treat leaving the Church like leaving a Bridge Club.

God’s arranged each one of us here at Church for his special reason, according to his own pleasure and will.

Don’t separate what God has put together; don’t leave the Church for human reasons.

Why shouldn’t we treat the Church like a social club? Because the Church is what God has arranged together.

Also, it’s because we need each other.

We all need each other

Look with me at verses 21-22, “21 The eye cannot say to the hand, “I don’t need you!” Or again, the head can’t say to the feet, “I don’t need you!” 22 On the contrary, those parts of the body that are weaker are indispensable.

We need each other!

We don’t just need the obvious, important parts of the body.

We need all parts of the body, including those parts which seem weaker and not as important.

To demonstrate what that looks like, I’m going to need your help at home.

In fact, if there are fathers out there, can you come and give your child / teenager a hand?

This will involve three people: One person to show their balancing skills (and so preferably a younger person, 40 and under)

One person to be on standby, just in case the person falls.

One person to take the video and send it into us.

If you’re the only one at home or if it’s not advisable for you to fall, then please don’t do this.

However, if you would like to try, make sure you have plenty of room around you.

Camera person, are you ready?

Balance person, see if you can do this:

Stand and put one leg on the other, like so.

Support person, be on standby.

Let’s see how long you can do that for. Go!

How many seconds can you hold for? Send in your answers by wechat, whatsapp or zoom.

Now, do the same thing, but with your eyes closed.

Ready? Go!

How long did you last for? Send in your answers by wechat, whatsapp or zoom.

Send in your videos as well! We’ll post them up for people to see.

What does this tell us?

Our eyes are important to help with balance.

Also, it’ll be good to have both our legs too, and our arms to help.

That’s a little like what the Church is like.

We need each other to stand firm and grow in Christ.

The head people, those who are blessed with head knowledge can’t say that they don’t need people who are blessed with skilful hands.

You can’t have an elite group in the Church who look down on other groups.

In fact, this passage tells us that the weaker parts are indispensable, and those we consider less honourable we clothe with greater honour and our less respectable parts are treated with greater respect.

We need the weaker parts of the Church, just like we need the weaker parts of the body.

We honour those in the Church who might not be honoured according to the world’s standards.

The more respectable parts of the body we don’t need to give additional respect.

Why? It’s so that there’s no division in the Church.

God has arranged the Church so that there’s unity in the Church.

Though we might be isolated from one another physically at the moment, we must not isolate ourselves spiritually from one another.

Look with me at verse 26, “26 So if one member suffers, all the members suffer with it; if one member is honoured, all the members rejoice with it.”

If one part of the body suffers, all parts suffer.

No one suffers alone.

If one part of the body is honoured, all parts rejoice.

No one rejoices alone.

We thank God that over the past few years, we’ve welcomed new babies into our Church.

And I hope to see many babies back in Church when we do come back together.

I love hearing the noise that babies make at Church.

It’s a reminder that there’s life and hope in the Church.

Though they might look insignificant, though they are weak, by their very presence, they play an important part in the life of the Church.

If I were to ask you to think who’s the weakest person in our Church, who comes to mind?

Is it a baby? Is it an older person?

Perhaps a less educated person? A poorer person?

Can you see that person in your mind?

That person is a vital member of the Church.

That’s a person we need to honour.

We need that person, and that person needs you.

They are indispensable to the Church; they are indispensable to you.

God loves the person and had arranged that brother or sister in his Church.

God loves that person enough to send his Son to die for that person, just as He’s done for you.

Do you love that person?

Do you rejoice when that person rejoices?

Do you suffer when that person suffers?

Do you even know whether that person is suffering?

God has arranged each one of us in the Church just as he wants.

That person isn’t indispensable; we need each other.

And though we might be different and are gifted differently, we work together and grow together.

And we desire the greater gifts together.

Though gifted differently, together we desire the greater gifts

Look with me at verses 27-28, “Now you are the body of Christ, and individual members of it. 28 And God has appointed these in the church: first apostles, second prophets, third teachers, next miracles, then gifts of healing, helping, leading, various kinds of tongues.”

Each member of the Church is given a different gift and they function differently for the good of the Church.

When I was studying biology in University, I learnt that the body has different systems.

For example, there’s the skeletal system, the circulatory system, and the nervous system, as well as a number of others.

We would spend a few weeks learning the details of each system, which is simply not enough time!

The incredible thing is that even though these systems are complex, they work together to keep a person going.

The Church, too, is made up of many very different people, but we all do our part to grow in the Lord Jesus.

Last week, we’ve gone through some of these gifts, particularly in terms of qualities that a person may have, like wisdom and knowledge.

This week, I’ll just focus on two in particular: Apostles and prophets.

Apostles are people in the New Testament times who were sent personally by Jesus to be missionaries and to found Churches.

Many of them were eyewitnesses of Jesus, such as the disciples who followed Jesus.

Paul was an extraordinary apostle in that Jesus appeared to Paul and made Paul an apostle after his resurrection.

There is no New Testament evidence to suggest that there were apostles appointed by Jesus beyond the New Testament times.

The apostles were a selected and limited group of people in the New Testament.

We read about prophecies over the past few chapters in 1 Corinthians.

The prophets are generally seen as people who understood the gospel and were gifted to proclaim a particular gospel truth to a particularly timely situation.

The reformed theologian John Calvin says that those who are considered as prophets “were blessed with the unique gift of dealing with Scripture, not only by interpreting it, but also by the wisdom they showed in making it meet the needs of the hour.”

The prophets speak a timely gospel truth that needs to be said in an hour of need.

They provide a point of clarity from God’s word when God’s people are confused about a particular topic.

As well as these two gifts, there are a number of others including teachers, miracles, even helping.

What a great gift the gift of helping is!

I’d love to have more people to help because I need help!

The gifts that Paul lists here is a great list of gifts for the Church but God’s given even more gifts that these to the Church.

Discover God’s gifts for you.

Look for them and find them.

He’s given at least one gift to each of us; what has he given you?

Interestingly, after he lists the gifts and emphasised throughout the whole passage that we need to make sure we don’t look down on other people’s gifts,

He ends the passage with these words, “ 31 But desire the greater gifts. And I will show you an even better way.”

Desire the greater gifts!

I don’t think Paul is here suddenly telling people that you ought to envy the more spectacular gifts, the very thing he’s telling people not to do throughout the past passage.

But I think that when he’s telling us to want the greater gifts,

he’s asking us to think about what’s the best thing that God’s given them for the Church.

How does God want them to improve their gifts for the good of God’s people?

Does God have other gifts that God wants to give them for the good of God’s people?

They want these not so that they can boast over other people.

They do it so that they can show more love to one another.

It’s the even better way that Paul will expand on in the next chapter.

Can you imagine coming home from shopping and holding your shopping bag by your elbow because your hand is in your pocket?

It’s possible, of course, but it’s awkward.

Wouldn’t it have been a lot easier to take the hands out of our pockets and use them to carry the bags?

Of course it is!

But some people at Church are just like the hands who hide themselves in the pockets.

They don’t use their gifts from God for the Church.

Can the Church grow?

Yes, but it would’ve been so much better for the whole Church if they use their gifts.

The Church would grow a lot more quickly.

And so, if you have a gift from God, don’t hide your gift from the Church.

Use your gifts for God’s glory.

And don’t just be happy offering your bare minimal for God.

Those who see themselves as parts of the body won’t be satisfied with offering just their best to God.

They will always seek to beat their PB, their Personal Best, for God’s people and for God’s glory.

They want to improve their gifts; they want to learn additional gifts.

They want to level up.

That’s why the Giftedness team at our Church want to provide as much training for our church members as possible.

We want our church members to discover their gifts and to keep using them for God’s glory.

We want to keep improving on what God’s given us.

We want to beat our PB; we want to keep getting better.

So, is the Church a social club?

Absolutely not.

The Church is Christ’s body, filled by the Spirit of God.

And so, don’t treat it like a social club.

If you believe in Jesus, you belong to the Church.

Each member of the Church, even the weakest one, is precious to you.

As a Church, we need to be the one united body of Christ that God has arranged us to be.

Be a body, not a social club.

Be a Church where if one part suffers, all parts suffer.

Some of us would’ve received the sad news about Oakhurst Anglican Church.

15 families there had contracted COVID.

One of those families is the family of an assistant pastor, John Deng.

His wife Arop, sadly, died from COVID on Friday afternoon.

She had left her husband and nine children behind.

As a Church, we’ll be giving them a gift to support them and the others in the Church in their time of need.

One part suffers, we all suffer.

If you are suffering, please let one of us at Church know and we’ll go through your suffering with you.

If you have something to praise God about, let us know so that we can rejoice with you.

Together, let’s be the Church, the Body of Christ that God has made us to be.

Let’s be a body and not a social club.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What am I doing to get to know and love other members of our Church better?

In what way do I need the weakest member of our Church?

What greater gifts am I desiring for the glory of God and the good of God’s people?

Friends, let’s be a body and not a social club.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for giving us the Church. We thank you that even though we might not be impressive according to the world’s standards, we’re precious in your eyes. Please forgive us when we treat it like a social club. Please move our hearts so that we will see the Church as the body of Christ. By your Spirit, bind us to Christ and bind us to each other. Unite us by Your Spirit, so that together we will glorify you in all that we do, for Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
