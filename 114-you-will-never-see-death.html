You will never see death

Passage: John 8:48-59

Big Idea: The Father glorifies the Son, so honour the Son by keeping his word and you will never see death

The Father glorifies the Son, the Son Honours the Father

Keep the Son’s word and not see death

“Nothing in life is certain except…. for death and taxes.”

It’s a common English phrase that gets repeated in various forms over the past four hundred years.

There might be a lot of things that might change in life, but no matter who you are, no matter which country you’re from,

you’re going to see death and taxes.

Of course, that doesn’t stop people from trying to avoid them.

Many multinational companies, millionaires, actors, and singers hide their money in foreign accounts to avoid paying taxes.

Their success rate varies.

Some are more successful than others in evading taxes.

But for most of us law-abiding citizens, we pay taxes to our government, as we should.

How about death?

Can we evade death?

Of course, many people have tried over the centuries.

In different cultures, people have tried to avoid death by looking for the elixir that gives immortality, or the fountain of youth that stops you from dying.

Unlike tax evasion, there is far less success.

Of course, we have tried and succeeded in delaying death.

We thank God for doctors and nurses who have kept many of us going for a little longer.

But death remains something that we can’t avoid.

Or can we?

In today’s passage, Jesus says, “Truly I tell you, if anyone keeps my word, he will never see death.”

He will never see death!

Is it really possible that we can avoid death?

What if death isn’t inevitable?

What if I don’t have to die?

That’s what we’re going to see in today’s passage.

So please open up to John 8, beginning from verse 48.

We’re going to see two things:

The Father glorifies the Son, the Son Honours the Father

Keep the Son’s word and not see death

Before we do, let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for your word. By your Spirit, please open our hearts and our minds so that we can understand it. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

The Father glorifies the Son, the Son Honours the Father

Our passage comes straight after a debate between Jesus and the Pharisees about the identity of Jesus.

Jesus says that he’s from God and has the authority to set people free.

The Jews tried to argue against Jesus but they were outmatched and outclassed.

And so, rather than accept that Jesus was right and they were wrong, they resort to call Jesus names.

They said, “Aren’t we right that you’re a Samaritan and have a demon?”

The Jews looked down the Samaritans because of their mixed heritage.

By calling Jesus a Samaritan, they were casting doubt on whether Jesus had any right to talk about the Jewish religion.

And of course, by calling Jesus a demon, they were saying that Jesus was from the devil, and so not very trustworthy at all!

They publicly dishonoured Jesus.

Jesus denied their claims: He does not have a demon.

On the contrary, even as Jesus was honouring his Father, they were dishonouring him.

They were dishonouring the One who was honouring the Father.

However, at the end, it’s not whether they honour or dishonour Jesus that counts; it’s what God the Father things that counts.

Look with me at verse 50, ““I do not seek my own glory; there is one who seeks it and judges.”

‬‬Though they dishonour him the Father seeks his Son’s glory.

One of the most outstanding Australian athlete in recent times is a young man named Peter Bol.

Peter was born in Sudan but his family escaped the civil war there to come to Australia when he was eight.

He trained as a runner and represented Australia in the 2020 Tokyo Olympics in the 800m race, where he came fourth by 0.53 of a second.

His Mum called him after the race and said in Arabic, ‘you’ve lifted the community’s heads up,

But his dad said ‘no, I think you’ve lifted the nation’s head up.’

Both of Peter Bol’s parents were proud of their son.

They were happy to see their son’s glory.

God the Father seeks the glory of God the Son.

God the Father points the world to Jesus and says, “Look, here is my Son.”

God the Father seeks the glory of what God the Son would accomplish and achieve.

For the Father seeks the glory of Jesus.

We see this again in verse 54, ““If I glorify myself,” Jesus answered, “my glory is nothing. My Father—about whom you say, ‘He is our God’—he is the one who glorifies me.”

God glorifies Jesus. Do we?

As God the Son, Jesus honours his Father.

Jesus obeys God in all that he says and does.

Look with me at verse 55, “You do not know him, but I know him. If I were to say I don’t know him, I would be a liar like you. But I do know him, and I keep his word.”

Jesus knows the Father and keeps his word.

That’s how God the Son honours God the Father.

God the Son obeys God the Father.

And so we have this dynamic at work: The Father glorifies the Son and the Son honours the Father.

There’s glory and honour flowing between God the Father and God the Son.

This dynamic is foundational to Christian thinking.

A lot of times, when we come to Church or come to the Bible, we immediately think about how it benefits me, how it’s going to apply to me.

How do I have a better life? How do I have a better family? How do I become happy?

But it’s vital to remember that when we think about what the Bible teaches, that it doesn’t start with us; we must start with God.

We must begin by thinking who he is.

God’s the Centre of the universe, not us.

We need to seek to understand who he is before we can understand who we are.

That doesn’t mean we need to understand everything about God.

That would be impossible!

However, our understanding of God must form the foundation of all our thoughts.

When we come to Church or come to the Bible, we must begin with him.

What have learnt about God so far?

God the Father glorifies God the Son, God the Son honours the God the Father.

Where do we come in in our understanding of God?

Keep the Son’s word and not see death

Those who believe in the Son’s word will not see death.

Look with me again at verse 51, “Truly I tell you, if anyone keeps my word, he will never see death.”

God the Son honours God the Father by keeping the Father’s word.

Believers honour God the Son by keeping the Son’s word.

Believers obey Jesus’ word; that’s how we honour Jesus.

As believers, we keep Jesus’ word, just as he’s kept the Father’s word.

What does it mean to keep Jesus’ words?

We obey it.

When Jesus says jump, we say, “How high?”

If Jesus has said it, we’ll do it.

That’s what it means to keep his word; that’s what it means to honour Jesus.

When we keep his word, something incredible happens.

Actually, to be more accurate, when we keep his word, something incredible will not happen!

We will not see death!

When we keep Jesus’ word, Jesus says, we will never see death.

When the Jews heard Jesus, they said that a demon’s possessed him.

How can Jesus say that anyone who keeps his word will never taste death?

Abraham, the great man of faith, who trusted in God’s word, died.

The prophets, who transmitted God’s word, died.

Look with me at verse 53, “Are you greater than our father Abraham who died? And the prophets died. Who do you claim to be?”

Listen again to what Jesus says, “Truly I tell you, if anyone keeps my word, he will never see death.”

Let that sink in for a moment.

Jesus is saying that death is not inevitable.

If anyone keeps his word, they don’t have to die, in fact, they will never see death.

Does that sound similar?

Can you remember a time in the Bible when someone promises someone else that they will not die?

Before things went bad for the world, while it was all still perfect, the devil came into the Garden of Eden, a place of perfect beauty.

There, he tempted the first humans to turn away from God.

Though God said that the man and the woman was not to eat from the fruit of the knowledge of good and evil and if they would eat it, they would surely die,

The devil said, “You will not surely die!”

In effect, the devil tells the man and the woman, “you can disobey God and keep living! Death is not inevitable!”

However, as the man and the woman would soon find out, it was a lie.

When you sin against God, when you choose to disobey God, you will face death, you will die.

That’s God’s inevitable judgement against sin.

When you sin against God, you will die.

You will die physically, but worse, you’ll also die spiritually.

That is, you will be banished from God, the source of all life.

However, in contrast with the devil’s lies, Jesus says that if you keep His word, if you keep the word of God the Son, you will never see death.

Jesus is here not talking about physical death.

Christians and non-Christians alike will see physical death.

However, the Christians, those who keep Jesus’ word, will never, ever face spiritual death.

They will never be separated from God the Father and God the Son, the very source of life.

They will have everlasting life, eternal life.

They will have a life that will not die.

They will have a life that will never die, for physical death can never extinguish spiritual life.

Physical death has no power over eternal, spiritual life.

The Bible commentator, George Beasley-Murray once said, “The believer receives eternal life, i.e. the life of the kingdom of God, over which death has no power and which is destined for resurrection”

The physical death that we die is only a small moment compared with the eternal life that is ours in Jesus, God the Son.

If you keep his word, you will not see death.

Over the past week, I’ve seen more deaths than usual.

Two famous Australian singers, Judith Durham of the Seekers and Olivia Newton-John, both died.

A famous Australian football coach, Paul Green, died.

A friend of someone I know died.

I visited someone in hospital last week who was about to die.

Sadly, I can’t offer hope to families of those who were dying, if their loved one had not known Jesus.

I can’t offer them comfort, if their loved one had dishonoured Jesus all their lives.

I know that if someone had wrongly dishonoured any one from my family, I would be deeply upset.

Can you imagine what God the Father would do to anyone who dishonours his Son?

Anyone who had shown contempt for Jesus, knowingly disobeying him, intentionally rejecting his word, can expect nothing less than the full wrath and anger of God the Father, for they had dishonoured His Son.

However, for those who have kept Jesus’ word, there is an assurance that they will not see death, they will not taste death.

Again, Jesus is not referring to physical death.

In many cases, those who keep Jesus’ word might be more likely to die physical deaths because they were keeping Jesus’ word.

There had been many Christians who have died physically throughout history because they kept Jesus’ word.

However, they will never, ever see the horror of the judgement of God.

Instead, they will have eternal life, an everlasting life, not because they’re smarter, more clever, or even more loving to their friends and families.

No, they will not see spiritual death because they have kept Jesus’ word.

They honour Jesus. They believe in Jesus. They obey Jesus.

When Jesus says “jump”, they say “how high?”

And so, keep the Son’s word.

This is not optional to living.

Obedience to Jesus is a pre-requisite to living.

Honour Jesus by keeping, believing and obeying his word!

Now someone might ask, “Doesn’t this mean that it’s salvation by works?

Doesn’t it mean that I earn my salvation by obeying Jesus?”

No, absolutely not!

To believe in Jesus means that we believe that he died for our sins on the cross and that he was raised from the dead.

We believe that Jesus took upon himself the judgement and wrath God has against us.

We believe that Jesus died in our place.

And we believe that on the cross, he bought our lives with his life.

We now belong to him.

As Paul says in Galatians 2:20, “ I have been crucified with Christ, and I no longer live, but Christ lives in me. The life I now live in the body, I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me.”

On the cross we have been crucified with Christ.

We now have a new life in Christ; our life is not ours anymore but his.

If we truly believe in him, we will gladly live our new life for him.

If we truly believe in him, we will gladly keep his word, for his word is life.

We will keep his word, we will rely on his promise, even when life gets hard, even when we’re dishonoured.

Right now, all around the world, many people are dishonoured because they keep Jesus’ word.

Last year, in Nigeria, a group of militants killed the husband of a Christian woman called Rose.

Rose’s husband was a Christian pastor.

Rose is now a widow with two children and another baby on the way.

After giving birth, the baby passed away.

Listen to what Rose says about her ordeal,

“When I lost my husband, my in-laws never cared about me, they didn't come to check on me or the children, so when Patience (her baby) died, I thought they would rally around me, but nobody came.

"I felt so pained in my heart... But I decided to let go of my bitterness and let God heal me."

"When people see my children in the community, they are forced to believe that God is real, because we don't look like what we have been through.

No matter what storms life may bring, I will smile, because God is in the boat with me, and He is the one who is in control of the storm.”

Despite the death of her husband, despite the rejection of her in-laws, despite the death of her daughter, Rose kept Jesus’ word.

She knows that God is in the boat with her.

Since God is in control of the storm, she can smile at the storm.

She knows the despite the danger she faces, she’s secure in Christ.

She looks forward to seeing Jesus one day.

On that day, Jesus will be glorious.

God the Father looks forward to that day.

Believers of Jesus will honour him on that day.

On the last day, believers from every century, will rise to new life, praising Jesus, testifying that what Jesus’ said is true.

Indeed, our life, our eternal, spiritual life, at its core, is not about us.

At its core, our eternal life is about Jesus.

We live because he said we would.

Our eternal life is a fulfilment of his promises that he’s made to all those who keep his word.

He’s said that those who keep his word will never see death.

And so, our eternal life will be glorious, because it brings glory to Jesus, the very thing that God the Father looks forward to.

Can death be evaded? Is death no longer inevitable?

In Jesus Christ, it’s a resounding Yes!

Those who trust in him, who believe in him, who keep his word, will have eternal life.

Those who keep his word, will never, ever taste death.

Do you know with absolute certainty that you have eternal life?

If not, please make this your top priority today.

There’s nothing more urgent than being absolutely sure that you have eternal life.

The person I visited at the hospital was alive and well in the morning but was on her death bed at night.

I thank God that she and her family knows Jesus.

Friends, the default position for everyone who had sinned against God, who had dishonoured Jesus, is death.

Come to either me or Hien or Jiawu or Po Ki afterwards.

Find out how you can avoid eternal and spiritual death.

Jesus has come to give you his word; Keep it and have eternal life.

The Father glorifies the Son, and so honour the Son by keeping his word and you will never see death

And so, friends, remember what Jesus has said, “Truly I tell you, if anyone keeps my word, he will never see death”

Why not start with this verse?

Use it in your conversations, use it in your decisions.

Remember that death, spiritual death, is not inevitable.

“Truly I tell you, if anyone keeps my word, he will never see death”

How does Jesus’ honour of God the Father affect your view of God?

How am I honouring Jesus in my words and deeds?
