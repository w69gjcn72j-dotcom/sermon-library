While I was studying in Moore College, my family lived in Newtown.

Anyone’s been to Newtown?

If you’ve been to Newtown, you know that it’s an interesting suburb where you’ll meet interesting people.

There will be people who dress differently and who may have very different opinions from us about sexual matters.

When our children were small, we would often go shopping somewhere else as there will be times when the behaviours of some of those walking by may be difficult to explain.

Not impossible, just difficult.

We live in a world where you many people talk openly, probably too openly, about sexual matters.

You hear it conversations, talk-shows, reality TV shows.

In the last few years, you even hear about it in parliament where people debate on sexual matters.

You might remember that a few years ago, there was a debate on people of the same sex marrying each other.

Everyone seems to be giving their opinion on sexual matters and everyone is welcome to share.

Everyone that is, except for the Christian Church.

People want to hear what the homosexual group think, how the transgender group feel, but they don’t want to hear what the Church thinks.

Why does my private sexual behaviour matter to Jesus Christ and the Church?

Sexual behaviour is not a new thing for the Church.

We’ve been talking about it for the past two thousand years.

If you have your Bible there, please open up to 1 Corinthians chapter 6, beginning from verse 11.

Here, we’ll see what the Bible says about why your sexual behaviour matters.

Your sexual behaviour matters because your body matters.

We’re going to see three things in today’s passage:

My rights over my body

Your body matters to God the Father, God the Son and God the Spirit

Flee sexual immorality, glorify God with your body

There are some parts in today’s passage that might be confronting for some of our younger members.

For some of our parents, if you would to take your children to the hall for the talk and come back to listen to the talk later on, now’s the time to take them.

My rights over my body

Amnesty International has a campaign called My Body My Rights.

It’s a campaign to stop the control and criminalisation of sexuality and reproduction.

The tagline is: “It’s your body. Know your rights.”

I am glad that Amnesty is working hard to protect the vulnerable in our society.

However, what does it mean to have your rights, especially in sexual matters?

Is there any limit to our sexual rights?

Increasingly in many parts of the world, the answer is no.

In many parts of the world, especially in the western society, there are no limits to your sexual rights.

It’s argued that it’s your body and it’s your choice to do whatever you want with your body.

It’s not just your right; it’s your power and it’s your privilege to do whatever you want with your body, especially when it comes to sexual matters.

There is a general acceptance in our society for people to be homosexuals.

It’s their body, it’s their choice, it’s their right!

As the homosexual group becomes more mainstream, other sexual groups want to become accepted too.

There’s even a group who identify themselves as pansexual, who are open to have sexual relationship with anything (that’s what pan means).

One of the more vocal groups over the past few years are those who identify themselves as transgender.

They’re a woman or a man, who’s changed their gender to the other gender.

One of the more famous ones is someone called Ellen Page.

She was a wonderful actor and had starred in some of my favourite movies.

She was a homosexual then but now she’s become a transgender and identifies herself as Elliot Page.

According to Amnesty, it’s Elliot Page’s right; it’s in her power to be a boy or a girl.

That should settle it, right?

Elliot or Ellen should not be limited or restricted; she should have the power to choose.

But what if we should the boundary of someone’s power and right a bit further.

What about the right, the power and the wealth to have sexual relationship with whoever you choose?

Where are the limits then?

Sadly, there are those with power who think they have the right to have sexual relationship with whoever they want, as long as they can afford it.

It’s this power and desire for control that leads to the practice of prostitution.

Prostitution is basically the industry of buying and selling sex outside of marriage.

It’s been around in many cultures for thousands of years.

According to ancient texts, it was often expected that after a banquet, even after a religious banquet around temples, prostitutes would be brought in for entertainment.

These prostitutes were dehumanised as objects of entertainment for those with money and power.

But it’s not just back then that people who worked as prostitutes were dehumanised.

It is estimated that 4.5 million people in the world right now are victims of sex trafficking.

That is, 4.5 million souls are sold as objects to people who believe they have the right and the power to have sexual relationship with whoever they choose.

Where are the limits to their power?

Some might say the limit is consent of those selling their bodies.

But is it really consent when their families are in poverty, or worse, in danger, if they don’t give their consent?

Does consent from the powerless make it right?

Our passage in the Bible begins to address the issue of prostitution and sexual immorality in general with these profound words, in 1 Corinthians 6:12,

“Everything is permissible for me,” but not everything is beneficial. “Everything is permissible for me,” but I will not be mastered by anything.”

The Corinthian Christians had a slogan that Amnesty would probably be happy to use.

My body, my right. Everything is permissible for me.

I can do whatever I want.

But the Bible follows that up with the phrase, not everything is beneficial.

Yes, you might claim to have the right to do whatever you want with your body, but is it beneficial?

Does it benefit anyone else rather than yourself?

Does your right to freedom infringe on the freedom of others?

The Bible Commentator Thiselton said that Paul might had been thinking of the “well-known paradox that if everyone claims unqualified autonomy, no one can be free, for everyone is threatened by the freedoms of the other.”

When we think about our rights, especially our sexual rights, we need to think about it not only in terms of our rights,

But also whether it’s beneficial to others.

More than that, when we think about our rights, even our sexual rights, we need to think about it not as a mere transaction,

But whether we’ll be mastered by it.

When it comes to sexual matters, those who think they have control over it often don’t realise that they’re slaves.

One example is pornography.

Though not strictly prostitution, like prostitution, those involved in pornography sell their bodies for a price in order to make pornography.

Research have shown that when a person watches pornography, there is a release of brain neurochemicals like dopamine that mimic the drug cocaine.

A person feels happy, like he’s on drugs, when they watch pornography.

More than that, there’s also data to show that the brain neural pathways rewire, or reconfigure, after watching porn.

Over time, the person watching porn becomes addicted to porn and becomes a slave to porn.

Someone who is addicted to porn or engage in prostitution may give many reasons and excuses that justify their actions.

They will argue that what they’re doing is permissible.

But is what they’re doing beneficial?

Are they being enslaved by their actions?

Not only is prostitution (and by extension, pornography) not beneficial enslaving.

It has absolutely no place in the life of a believer because it seriously corrupts our relationship with God and His Church.

Your body matters to God.

Here, the apostle Paul shows us that our body matters to God the Father, God the Son and God the Holy Spirit.

Your body matters to God the Father, God the Son and God the Spirit

Look with me at verse 13, “Food is for the stomach and the stomach for food,” and God will do away with both of them. However, the body is not for sexual immorality but for the Lord, and the Lord for the body.”

The Corinthians might have argued that just as food is for the stomach and the stomach for food,

So the body is made for sexual immorality and sexual immorality is for the body.

Hunger is satisfied by food; the body is satisfied by sexual immorality.

There’s a physical need and a physical satisfaction; one exists for the other.

But Paul says there’s a difference.

Paul acknowledges the physical need of hunger and the physical satisfaction that food brings, and he says that God will do away with both one day.

But that’s not the same relationship between body and sexual immorality.

The body is not for sexual immorality but for the Lord and the Lord for the body.

Here, the Lord is the Lord Jesus.

There’s a relationship with between your body and the Lord that is deeper than being merely physical, like food and stomach.

God will do away with food and stomach, but he won’t be doing away with your body in the same way, just as he didn’t do away with the body of Jesus in the same way.

Look with me at verse 14, “God raised up the Lord and will also raise us up by his power.”

There’s a common misunderstanding that says Christianity is only a matter for the spirit, a matter for the heart and that the body doesn’t matter.

But the body does matter.

God affirms the importance of our body when he raised Jesus from the dead.

God could’ve just made Jesus go spiritually without the body into heaven.

But he didn’t.

God raised Jesus from the dead and gave him a new, resurrected body that will never die again.

Jesus is now in heaven, with a new body and he’ll come back in his new body.

The Lord Jesus is for the body; he’s pro-body.

The body is a good thing.

God raised him in a body and God will raise those who believe in Jesus with a new body as well.

Our bodies are important to God.

In fact, those who believe in Jesus are often called the body of Christ.

Paul uses the idea that we’re the body of Christ to emphasise the importance of what we do with our physical body.

Look with me at verse 15, “Don’t you know that your bodies are a part of Christ’s body? So should I take a part of Christ’s body and make it part of a prostitute? Absolutely not!”

Your bodies are parts (members) of Christ’s body.

Another way to see this is that your bodies are organs and limbs of Christ’s body.

Just as the body have many parts, Christ’s Church, Christ’s body, have many parts.

Here’s a question for us to think about:

Think about the different ministries we have at Church, which organ of the body would you use to describe the different people of Christ’s body?

Maybe it’s the welcomers are the hands waving hello, or the teachers as the mouth, teaching God’s word.

Which organ of the body would you use to describe the different people in Christ’s body?

You can send your answers in by whatsapp, wechat or zoom.

You have twenty seconds.

We are the body of Christ, we are the organ and the limbs of Christ.

Paul asks us rhetorically, should I take a part of Christ’s body, should I rip out a limb or an organ of Christ, and unite it to a prostitute?

Never!

Why does Paul use such strong language?

Because when you have sexual union with somebody, you form a strong bond with the person.

Look with me at verse 16, “Don’t you know that anyone joined to a prostitute is one body with her? For Scripture says, The two will become one flesh.”

In Genesis 2:24, when the Bible describes marriage, it says that the man and his wife become one flesh.

It’s a deep union between the man and the wife.

They form a new unit, one flesh, almost like a new human.

There is not just a sexual relationship but a sexual union between the man and the wife.

They become one; it’s as if they’re glued and stuck together.

To tear them apart is like tearing limbs apart.

When God made us, he’s made us to be either single or to be sexually committed to someone of the opposite sex.

God has not made us to be sexually immoral.

God has not made us to have sex with multiple partners outside of marriage or to have sex with whoever and whatever you want.

Never!

When you are married to a person and have sexual relationship with your husband or your wife, you are joined with them, you form a deep, sexual, emotional bond with them,

You have a new identity; you’re one flesh.

Amazingly, Paul goes even further when he describes our relationship with Jesus.

Look with me at verse 17,

“But anyone joined to the Lord is one spirit with him.”

When you become a Christian, you are joined to the Lord.

You have a new identity in him.

It’s an identity that goes even deeper that your relationship with your spouse.

When you believe in Jesus, you belong to him; you’re one with him in spirit.

And so, God the Father values your body because he will raise you up just as he’s raised Jesus up.

God the Son values your body because your bodies are part of Christ’s body, his organs and limbs and you’re one with him in spirit.

God the Spirit also values your body because you are his temple, his home.

Look with me at verse 19, “Don’t you know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you, whom you have from God?”

If you trust in Jesus, your body is the home of the Holy Spirit.

God the Holy Spirit, the Spirit from God, lives in you, your body.

As the temple of the Holy Spirit, don’t blemish your body.

Keep your body, the temple of the Holy Spirit, holy.

I’ve been working in ministry for fourteen years and by far, one of the most overwhelming pastoral issues that I face often have something to do with sexual unfaithfulness.

Often, they start as something subtle, like secret sharing or secret meetings.

There are secrets that you would share with the other person rather than your spouse.

These are often the beginning of emotional unfaithfulness.

After a while, these acts of emotional unfaithfulness led to something more.

Sadly, there are times when they do end up in sexual unfaithfulness.

And these acts of sexual unfaithfulness will cause painful, ripple effects.

They are painful to the spouse or spouses involved, they are painful to the children, to the parents, and to the Church.

There are different reasons given but they often come back to the words along these lines: don’t I have a right to be happy?

Don’t I have a right to do what I want?

These are just variations of what the Corinthians had been saying, “Everything is permissible for me.”

What should we do? How do we keep our body holy?

Flee sexual immorality, glorify God with your body

Flee sexual immorality, glorify God with your body

Look with me at verse 18, “Flee sexual immorality! Every other sin a person commits is outside the body, but the person who is sexually immoral sins against his own body.”

Flee sexual immorality. Run away!

As someone who lives in Australia, we know that we have many dangerous animals all around us.

When we swim in the sea, we have to watch out for sharks.

When we swim in rivers, especially in the northern parts of Australia, we have to watch out for crocodiles.

When we put on shoes, especially boots left outside, we need to check whether spiders in them.

This week, when I was about to go out, I looked at my shoes and found one of these in one of my shoes.

What do we do when we see dangerous animals, or for some of us, even fairly harmless ones like cockroaches?

We run away!

That’s what the Bible tells us to do with sexual immorality.

Run away.

Here, sexual immorality is not just talking about prostitution but every sexual activity that’s outside of the sexual union between a man and a woman.

That includes but not limited to sex outside of marriage, that includes pursuing an active homosexual lifestyle, that includes pornography, that includes prostitution.

Sexual immorality is joining and becoming one flesh with someone who’s not your spouse.

Don’t do it!

Instead, flee, run away from the temptation.

Don’t play with fire!

Remember Joseph, son of Jacob, in the Old Testament?

He was a young man, probably late teen, when he was tempted by his boss’ wife.

What did he do?

He bolted out the door! He fled.

When we’re tempted with sexual sins, flee.

That’s the negative action: flee sexual immorality.

The positive action is to glorify God with your body.

Look with me at verse 20, “You are not your own, for you were bought at a price. So glorify God with your body.”

Your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit; that’s who you are.

More than that, you were bought at a price.

The prices is the death of Jesus.

Jesus has paid for you, all of you, body included, with his blood.

Jesus has saved you from all your sins, including your sexual sins.

You are his.

Don’t fall into sexual sins again.

Instead glorify God with your body and you glorify God with your body by keeping your body sexually pure.

To keep your body sexually pure is in itself a marvellous testimony of God’s glory.

In Denver Colorado, there’s an old Episcopal Church.

It was founded in 1889, twenty years after our Church St Paul’s Kogarah was founded.

It was a Church for 86 years.

However, it was sold and was turned to a nightclub.

It’s now actually called the Church Nightclub.

Here they serve vodka sodas and play techno music.

I don’t know if the club is any good, but I’m sure that those who actually built the Church would’ve been disappointed that their Church, once dedicated to God, had turned into a nightclub.

Friends, you are the temple of God; don’t turn your body into a nightclub.

Jesus has bought you with his own blood; don’t cheapen who you are by giving in to sexual immorality.

Be sexually faithful.

If you are married, stay faithful, sexually and emotionally, to your spouse. Be sexually faithful.

If you are single, be sexually faithful and be celibate

If you’re in relationship, remain sexually faithful by being celibate, refraining from any sexual temptations, until you publicly make a commitment to each other before God and the Church in marriage.

Remember that God treasure you and your body and he wants what’s best for you.

Your body is the holy temple of the Holy Spirt.

Glorify God with your body, with his glorious temple.
