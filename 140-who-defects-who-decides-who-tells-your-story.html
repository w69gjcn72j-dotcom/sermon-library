Who Defects, Who Decides, Who Tells Your Story

Acts 1:15-26 · 2024
Big IdeaGod's word must be fulfilled, be they hell-bound defections or Christ-honouring decisions.

Who defects, who decides, who tells your story

Passage: Acts chapter 1 verses 15 to 26

Additional passages: Psalm 69: 25, Psalm 109:8, Acts 1:6-7

Big Idea: God’s word must be fulfilled, be they hell-bound defections or Christ-honouring decisions

First point: God’s word is fulfilled in hell bound defections

Second point: The defector’s position must be filled

Third point: God’s word must be fulfilled in Christ-honouring decisions

Imagine you have a special book, a magic book, if you will.

What’s so special about the book?

If you write things in there, even if it’s in the future, it’s going to happen.

And you know what? Some of you already have it.

Some of the parents here might know that book, but some of the younger ones might not even have heard of it.

Of course, that magic book is the diary.

You write in your diary that you’re going to see the dentist on Friday and, guess what, you will see the dentist on Friday.

Incredible, right?

You plan it, you write it down, and you make it happen.

Anyone else here use a diary? On the phone?

I know a lot of people don’t plan and just let things happen spontaneously, but life often doesn’t work like that.

If you don’t plan it, if you don’t write it down, if you don’t make effort to make it happen, it’s not going to happen.

Of course, what we can do is often very limited.

We can plan simple things like going to visit the Doctor’s, or even planning to go on a holiday.

But there are things that we can’t plan, things that we can’t put in our diary.

We don’t know what kind of global events will shape our lives in the next few days, let alone the next few years.

We don’t know when the market will go up or when it will go bust.

We don’t even know how many days we have left in our lives.

We might write our plans, and hopes, and appointments in the pages of our diaries, but we can’t be sure that at the end of our lives,

We can’t be sure that our life story is exactly as we have written it in our diary.

If that’s the case, should we still plan, should we still write in the pages of our diaries?

Even if we choose not to do anything, our lives still tell a story.

If at the end of your life, you haven’t done anything, that in itself, is still a story.

And so, who writes the diary of my lives?

Who writes the story of our lives?

That’s what we’re going to see in today’s passage.

And so, please open up with me to Acts chapter one, beginning from verse 15.

We’re going to see three things:

Firstly, God’s word is fulfilled in hell bound defections

Second, the defector’s position must be filled

And finally, God’s word must be fulfilled in Christ-honouring decisions

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Father, please help us to understand your word clearly, so that we might know your will for us in the Lord Jesus Christ, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

God’s word is fulfilled in hell bound defections

Please open up your Bible with me to Acts chapter one beginning from verse 15.

Let’s read this together, “In those days Peter stood up among the brothers and sisters—the number of people who were together was about a hundred twenty.”

The 120 people there included the Eleven apostles, Jesus’ mother Mary and Jesus’ brothers.

Jesus ascended back to heaven a few days ago (no more than ten days ago) and they’ve been praying together since.

In English, there’s a phrase called the ‘Big elephant in the room’, which means there’s a difficult situation that people don’t want to talk about.

Peter got up to address the big elephant in the room.

There were only Eleven apostles there; One person was missing.

His name was Judas.

We all know Judas as a traitor but the Eleven and many there there knew him as a friend.

They ate together, they served Jesus together, and they probably even did miracles together.

How could he have done it? How could he have been a traitor?

He was one of the closest ones (he was one of the Twelve!) and no one suspected him.

Could there be more traitors in their group?

And why did Jesus pick a traitor to be one of the Twelve? Did Jesus make a mistake?

I still remember going to a youth conference many years ago with many keen, excited youth leaders.

And then the presenter at the conference said something shocking.

I can’t remember exactly how many, but a large percentage of these youth leaders will not be a Christian in a few years.

We all then started to look at the person next to us.

We were thinking: will it be you? Or will it be me?

The disciples were shocked that one of their own betrayed Jesus.

Peter had thought hard about this and applied what Jesus has taught him since the resurrection.

Jesus said that the Old Testament Scripture finds its fulfilment in him.

This includes what the Bible has said about the suffering of the Messiah, the Anointed One.

And so, Peter, understood the psalms aren’t just songs written by King David for the people of his time.

The Holy Spirit inspired King David to write psalms that foretold how Jesus the Messiah would be betrayed by Judas.

What Judas did, while bad and wicked, wasn’t due to a mistake by Jesus.

It was all part of God’s plan.

The Holy Spirit had revealed a glimpse of God’s plan in the psalms of David, written one thousand years before Judas’ betrayal.

And so, Peter can say with confidence, in verses 16-17, “Brothers and sisters, it was necessary that the Scripture be fulfilled that the Holy Spirit through the mouth of David foretold about Judas, who became a guide to those who arrested Jesus. For he was one of our number and shared in this ministry.”

What the Holy Spirit has revealed in the Scripture must be fulfilled.

It had to be one of their number, one of the Twelve, who betrays Jesus, for only a friend can betray a friend.

After Judas’ betrayal, he bought a field using the unrighteous money that he earned from betraying Jesus.

In the Gospel of Matthew, we read that it was the chief priests bought it with Judas thirty pieces of silver.

They couldn’t take the money to the temple because it was blood money.

It was blood money because the cost of it was Jesus’ blood; innocent blood was spilt for thirty pieces of silver.

It was also blood money because Judas killed himself and his blood was spilt over the field where he died.

Guilty blood as also spilt for those thirty pieces of silver.

Judas’ died a bad death; he did a wicked thing before God and was judged by God for it.

The Bible emphasized here that it was Judas’ own wickedness that led him to judgement.

God’s judgement on Judas was confirmed when Peter said this at the end of verse “Judas left to go where he belongs”

The word ‘left’ has the idea of ‘defected’ in the original language.

Judas defected to go where he belongs.

Judas was originally one of the Twelve, the very group who would be the central to how Jesus establishes his new Kingdom.

They were to be a new beginning for God’s people!

But Judas defected from the Kingdom of God to the place of darkness, a place of judgement, a place of eternal torment for 30 pieces of silver!

He defected from Jesus and will now face an eternity in punishment.

Friends, defection from Jesus is a very serious sin.

One of my fears is seeing many people who came to Jesus, who came to Church but had now defected from Jesus.

I have close friends, close relatives, who defected from Jesus for an extra few dollar of work on Sundays.

Others defected from Jesus because of their boyfriend or girlfriend.

Still others defected from Jesus because they want to have more fun.

Whatever it is, it’s their version of thirty pieces of silver.

They used to be in fellowship, they used to follow Jesus but they had now defected.

It’s a frightening trajectory because if they keep going, they will go to the place where they belong.

Christians belong with Jesus Christ, who is in heaven; and people who aren’t with Jesus Christ belongs to the place where Judas is right now.

There will be no friendship in that place; no love, no second chances.

Only a constant running away from God’s anger.

Does that mean all hope is lost if someone has defected from Jesus?

No, at least not yet!

There are many stories of people who left Jesus when they were younger but returned to him as an adult.

I’ve heard a few stories of people at our Church who heard about Jesus in Scripture classes at school and who might have even been to Church a few times,

But only became a Christian later on in life.

I thank God for them!

If you know someone who has defected from Christianity, keep praying for them to turn back to Jesus Christ.

There’s a chance as long as they’re still alive!

But don’t let them delay, because neither you nor they know how long they still have.

We might write pages after pages in our diary of what will happen to our lives, but God might have other plans.

If you are tempted to defect from Jesus, or if you have defected, turn back to Jesus now! Don’t delay!

Don’t defect Jesus for your 30 pieces of silver; whatever it is, it’s not worth it!

Nothing is worth going to the place where Judas and other defectors belong.

The defector’s position must be filled

Though it was shocking to the Eleven disciples, the defection of Judas was not a shock to God.

Even Judas’ defection was part of God’s plan.

Of course, the disciples didn’t know that Judas would betray Jesus.

But looking back, as they read the Old Testament once again in light of who Jesus is and what he went through, they understood that God’s word had to be fulfilled.

Things will happen according to the written word of God.

And now as they read the Scripture and think through what Jesus’ said, they start to think about what God’s plan for them is.

What does God want them to do?

God wants them to fil in the defector’s position.

Look with me at verse 20, ““For it is written in the Book of Psalms:

Let his dwelling become desolate;

let no one live in it; and

Let someone else take his position.”

Judas’ place was desolate, but now someone else must take up his position.

Why? Because the Eleven must become the Twelve once more.

Having eleven people on the team might work if you’re playing soccer.

When a soccer game starts, you have 11 people on the field.

You have one goal keeper, and then you have the other ten in some sort of configuration.

You can have 4-3-3, 4-4-2, and probably Yi’s favourite, 4-2-3-1, because that was Chelsea’s formation.

Sometimes a player might be sent out for bad behaviour, and they can keep playing.

But once you get down to six players, your team is forfeit.

You don’t have enough players to play a proper game of soccer.

Having eleven on the team is great and having seven members are absolute minimal.

Similarly, having Twelve Apostles was essential for the first believers; Having Eleven Apostles will be like having a forfeit.

It would be wrong to have Eleven instead of Twelve.

Of course, the number twelve is important for the Jewish believers because in the Old Testament, the nation of Israel had Twelve Tribes.

The fact that Jesus chose twelve disciples wasn’t an accident.

Jesus was deliberately showing that He’s about to build a new, restored Israel and he wanted to have twelve leaders at the heart of it.

Just before Jesus left, the disciples asked Jesus when he would restore the Kingdom of Israel.

Jesus’ reply indicates that he’s looking at a new beginning, not just for Israel, but for the whole world.

However, it starts with Israel, and the Twelve Apostles, together, would be the heart of both the new and restored Israel, as well as the new and restored world.

Peter didn’t see this merely as a nice ideal to have; Peter see this as a fulfilment of the Scriptures.

Yes, the Holy Spirit mentioned the betrayal and the punishment for Judas, and it also mentions that someone else will take this position.

God’s word was fulfilled in what happened to Judas.

God’s word is now to be fulfilled as Peter and the apostles obey God’s word.

What God said about the replacement will be fulfilled, it will happen, and the apostles want to find out how that will happen.

Who is qualified to be one of the Twelve?

Look with me at verses 21-22, together, “Therefore, from among the men who have accompanied us during the whole time the Lord Jesus went in and out among us—beginning from the baptism of John until the day he was taken up from us—from among these, it is necessary that one become a witness with us of his resurrection.”

This man must be a reliable witness of who Jesus is, how he died and was raised again, and now ascended to heaven.

Can you see the rationale for their actions?

Their actions were based on this truth: It was necessary that the Scripture be fulfilled.

God has written his word, his story, his diary, as it were.

He has spoken it, he has recorded it, by the inspiration of the Holy Spirit.

The apostles are like characters in God’s story.

Their past, their present and their future has already been written.

What they have to do now is to figure out what God’s will is and follow it.

To know what God’s will is, they look at what God has already said in the Old Testament,

Not as they used to before they came to know Jesus.

Instead, they read God’s word in light of Jesus and his promises.

How do you know God’s will for your life?

What does God want written in the story of your life?

If you look back, you’ll see what he’s already written.

Give God thanks for them.

Both the good and the bad things happen as part of God’s plan, as part of what God has written.

Nothing surprises God.

Everything that happened to Jesus happened because they were all part of God’s plan.

But God hasn’t just written his will that have already been fulfilled in the past;

He’s written down what hasn’t been fulfilled yet, things that will happen both now and in the future.

And so, we look ahead to the future, knowing that God’s word must be fulfilled.

Jesus will come back one day, aby day.

The end will come one day, Jesus will come on a day when we’re not expecting him.

With the coming of Jesus in mind, we look around at our present.

What should our next step be?

In light of the teachings of the Bible, in light of Jesus’ death and resurrection, in light of how Jesus fulfilled God’s promises, what does living according to God’s will look like?

What is God’s will for my life?

Or better still, where in God’s story is my life? Where do I fit in God’s story?

Every now and then, people come and ask me what God’s will for them is.

They ask, “What path should I take? What decision pleases God?”

I’m glad that they’ve come to ask me, to discover God’s will.

And often, I’ll tell them that what they should be looking for is not necessarily the “What” but the “Whatever”.

Sometimes it’s not “What” job you take, “What” path you take, that matters,

But that “whatever” job you take, “Whatever” decision you make, you follow God and give him the glory.

For example, I can’t tell you whether you should date or who you should marry, but you know that whatever your decision is, it must be one that is in obedience to God and give him the glory.

And so, you won’t date and marry someone who is not a believer because that’s going against God’s teaching and won’t give glory to God.

I’m not talking about those who are already married but those who are thinking about marriage.

Not the what, and in this case, not the who, but whoever; whoever you marry, must love Christ and will glorify Christ with you.

Same thing goes with jobs.

I can’t tell you whether one job is better than the other.

But I can tell you that whatever job you take, you’re to glorify God with it, not letting the job consume you or take you away from serving Christ, but one in which you can glorify God by your hard work and integrity.

God has given his commands and his promises in his words.

Will these words be fulfilled in your life?

When I say that God’s word will be fulfilled in our lives, I’m not saying that God prophesied about you and your exact situation explicitly and recorded it in the Bible somewhere.

He hasn’t prophesied about you like he prophesied about Jesus.

However, Jesus, as the Son of God, commanded his followers to love one another as he has loved us.

Has that come true in our lives?

Has that word been fulfilled among us?

He promised that the world will know that we’re his disciples if we love one another.

Has that promised been realized yet? Have we fulfilled Jesus’ command to us and seen Jesus’ promises fulfilled in us?

God’s word must be fulfilled in Christ-honouring decisions

That’s what obedience to God looks like.

Obedience to God is our longing to see God’s word fulfilled in our Christ-honoring decisions.

That’s what the first Christians did.

When they recognized that it was God’s will to have Twelve apostles not Eleven, they looked for suitable candidates.

Two names stood out: Joseph, called Barsabbas, who was also known as Justus, and Matthias.

As far as we can tell, the two candidates are equally qualified.

How did the disciples choose the Twelve apostle?

Well, they didn’t call for an election.

No one made any “Vote for Joseph, aka (also known as) Basabbas aka Justus” posters.

No one wore, “Matthias for No.12” caps.

Read verses 24-25 with me, “Then they prayed, “You, Lord, know everyone’s hearts; show which of these two you have chosen to take the place in this apostolic ministry that Judas left to go where he belongs.”

They prayed!

They asked the Holy Spirit, who knows everyone’s heart, to choose for them.

After casting lots, they found out that the Holy Spirit wanted Matthias to be the 12th apostle.

Casting lots was how the people of God in the Old Testament sought God’s will; after this one time, and after the coming of the Holy Spirit, we don’t see God’s people using lots to find God’s will again.

That’s because the Holy Spirit will now guide them.

The apostles trusted that God would lead them for this is his mission, his Church after all.

And by His Holy Spirit, God has written his will in the Bible in both the Old and the New Testament.

It’s in God’s word that we see God’s will, and through eyes of faith, we see his will being fulfilled ultimately in Jesus.

And more loosely, through eyes of faith, we see the Holy Spirit writing the pages of our lives and his will being fulfilled in the pages of our lives.

Again, this is not in the same as what the Holy Spirit has written in the pages of the Bible.

The pages in our lives do not carry the same weight as the pages of Scripture.

However, as we obey the words of Scripture, as we obey the will of God as written in his word, we ought to see God’s will being fulfilled in our lives.

When he writes that Christians would suffer for the gospel, we ought to see that fulfilled in our lives.

When he writes that Christ’s followers would deny themselves, carry the cross and follow Jesus, we ought to see that fulfilled in our lives.

When we make decisions as a Church, when we make decisions as individual families and believers, we must seek the Holy Spirit’s guidance, since the Holy Spirit is the one writing the pages of our lives.

What does the Holy Spirit want to write in the pages of our lives?

Will the promises spoken by the Father and God the Son be fulfilled in our lives?

And it won’t do with us just knowing God’s will; we must actively obey his will.

We must actively seek, obey and do God’s will, actively working to see that his will is fulfilled.

I still remember when I was looking for work as a graduate pharmacist.

I wasn’t sure what God’s will for me was; did he want me to go into ministry or did he want me to find a job as a pharmacist.

My life was frozen; I didn’t know what to do because I wasn’t sure about his will.

But then, after much prayer, I realise that God’s answer wasn’t in my room but out there.

I have to go and trust God’s lead.

I went around looking for a job, and eventually found one that allowed me to work as a full-time pharmacist as well as serving at Church on a full day on Sundays, taught Scripture in a local school, taught Youth group and Bible studies.

I even had time to teach piano and going out with a girlfriend.

In his timing, God led me to full-time ministry, for which I’m very thankful.

Trust in God will look very different in different circumstances.

You will need to ask God what trust in him looks like.

Be honest about the state of your heart.

Is there something in your life that you depend on and trust in more than your trust in God?

It may be a secure job, it may be friendship, it may even be family.

Would you rather disobey God than to turn your back on that one thing you treasure more than God?

Friends, if there is something in your life that you trust in more than God, you have found your thirty pieces of silver.

You have found that one thing that will turn you into someone who defects from Jesus.

Don’t defect from Christ; turn to him now, before it’s too late.

And so, who writes the story of our lives?

God is the one who writes the story of the world.

He knows exactly what will happen to our lives – both the good and bad times.

He’s the ultimate author.

And yet, we’re also responsible for the pages of our lives.

We can choose to ignore God’s will for us, ignore Jesus, ignore the Holy Spirit and end up defecting from Christ.

We’ll end up going to the place where Judas belongs, a place reserved for the devil and his angels.

Or we can actively seek God’s will as we read God’s word and pray in the Holy Spirit for his guidance.

He wants us to seek his will; he wants to fulfil his will in our lives.

Just as God’s commands and promises were first and foremost fulfilled in Jesus, God wants to see his commands and promises fulfilled in our lives, as we trust in Jesus and obey him, in the power of the Holy Spirit.

Friends, God’s word must be fulfilled, be they hell-bound defections or Christ-honouring decisions.

How do we know whether we’re making decisions that will make us defect from Christ or honour Christ?

Try adding a ‘because’ at the end of your actions.

For example, “I didn’t go to this activity on Sunday because I’m going to Church, which brings honour to Christ”

“I chose this subject at school, because through it, I want to honour and glorify Christ.”

Or it could be like this, “I have chosen not to forgive this person, because I value my pride more than Christ’s command.”

“I choose to work regularly on Sundays, because I value earning $30 dollars more per hour, than worshipping God with God’s people”

May God forgive us when we foolishly and sinfully write our story without asking for his will.

May God the Holy Spirit give us wisdom as we seek to see his word fulfilled in our Church and in our lives,

as we read God’s word in light of Jesus, his death and resurrection and as we humbly pray.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Question one: What are some temptations that can lead to defections away from Christ?

Question two: How has God answered your prayer about a decision you had to make recently?

Question three: What does God want to see written in the story of your life?

Friends, God’s word must be fulfilled, be they hell-bound defections or Christ-honouring decisions.

So actively seek to make Christ-honouring decisions, longing to see his will being fulfilled in our lives.

Let’s pray, “Father, forgive us for our foolishness indeed. So often, we make decisions without asking you for your will. Thank you for showing us that your word must be fulfilled. You have fulfilled your word in the Lord Jesus Christ, even when chaos seemed to reign. Father, by your Holy Spirit, show us your will in our lives, show us what you want to write on the pages in our lives. May you will be done on earth, as it is in heaven. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
