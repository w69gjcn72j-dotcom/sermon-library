The High calling of Marriage 2

Passage: Revelations 21:1-4

Marriage is given by God for the well-being of mankind, to help them live out the gospel of grace, and is patterned upon the union between Christ and his Church

1. God patterns marriages according to the mystery of Christ and His Church

2. God provides marriages to help us live out gospel of grace

Cleovis and Arwilda met one another when they were children.

Soon, they grew up and married in July 1939.

A few months ago, they celebrated their 84th Anniversary, a little less than the longest marriage ever recorded.

That record still belongs to Herbert Fisher and Zelmyra Fisher, the story we heard last week.

Sadly, Hebert and Zelmyra passed away but Cleovis and Arwilda Whiteside are still alive!

They are probably the longest running marriage in the US at the moment.

Cleovis is 102 and Arwilda is 98; they still have a chance for the top spot!

What was Cleovis and Arwilda’s secret for a long marriage?

Here’s Arwilda’s advice:  “Know how to get on your knees, and get you a Bible, because that Bible is going to have to take you through all kinds of storms.”

That’s good advice!

Last week, we started looking at the high calling of marriage.

We found out from the Bible that marriage is God-given and it’s a high calling from God.

It takes hard work and sometimes, even if you put in the hard work, it might not always work out.

Marriage can be hard and difficult.

Who wants to get married?

Marriage is a high calling, it’s hard work, but that doesn’t mean we should avoid it.

While marriage may not be for everyone, marriage shouldn’t be something we run away from either.

It’s a high calling and with it, come great honour.

Today, we’re going to see why marriages are great and Christians, whether you’re single or married, should celebrate marriages, especially Christian ones.

Last week, we learnt that God gives the high calling of marriage.

Today, we’re going to learn two things about marriage:

1. God patterns marriages according to the mystery of Christ and His Church

2. God provides marriages to help us live out gospel of grace

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Father, we thank you for giving us and blessing us with the institution of marriage. By your Spirit, help us see how wonderful marriage is, especially in the light of our Lord Jesus Christ, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

Last week, I used the seven stars to help us understand what marriage is and I mentioned four of them.

Do you remember the seven stars?

Star 1: Creation: God is the Creator, we are the creatures.

God created us and he created marriage.

Star 2: Fall: God is the Holy One and we are the sinners.

We rejected God and what he commanded about marriage.

Instead, we make up our own rules about marriage.

Star 3: Judgement: God is the Judge; we are the judged.

God will judge us for our rejection of him and his teaching.

He judges us initially by simply leaving us to destroy ourselves as we seek to destroy marriage.

If we refuse to repent, he will judge us on the Day of Judgement for following our version of marriage instead of following him.

And Star 7: The Last Things: God’s focus on the Last Day is Christ; our hope on the Last Day must also be Christ

On the great and final day, God’s focus will be on Christ and His Church.

How God see the union between Christ and His Church has a direct impact on how we are to look at marriages today.

To understand the significance of the union between Christ and His Church, we need to go back to Star 4: Salvation: God is Saviour, in Christ we are saved.

In Star 4, we see the impact that Jesus’ salvation has had on the very institution of marriage.

Since God has saved us in Jesus, marriage will never be the same again.

2. God patterns marriages according to the mystery of Christ and His Church

For God patterns marriages according to the mystery of Christ and His Church.

Look with me at Ephesians 5:28-32,

“28 In the same way, husbands are to love their wives as their own bodies. He who loves his wife loves himself. 29 For no one ever hates his own flesh but provides and cares for it, just as Christ does for the church, 30 since we are members of his body. 31 For this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two will become one flesh. 32 This mystery is profound, but I am talking about Christ and the church.”

Marriage, as hard as it is, is a joy!

That’s because God patterns marriage according to the mystery of Christ and the Church.

That is, God designed, moulded, and gave us this thing called marriage, to give us an idea of what the union between Christ and the Church will look like.

The relationship between Christ and the Church is not an example of what the reality of marriage looks like.

No, it’s the other way around.

Marriage is the example of what the reality of the relationship of Christ and the Church looks like.

The beautiful life-long union of a marriage is a reflection of the even more beautiful, everlasting and perfect union between Christ and the Church.

It’s a little like this.

I like to listen to music; I enjoy listening to music.

And so, over the years, I collect and try to improve on the music I listen to at home and at work.

But I have a problem; I don’t have a lot of money to spend on good equipment.

And so, I would be on the look-out for good bargains to maximise the sound quality of my AV system.

I have an AV receiver that’s over ten years old, I have a subwoofer I inherited that’s probably twenty years old and I have two tiny speakers that I picked up from E-Bay for less than $1.00.

But when I put them together, they make a wonderful sound.

I know that the new thing is soundbar these days, but I still can’t find one that sounds quite as nice as my old AV system.

But as good as my system is, it’s no match for live music, which is much, much better by far!

Our marriages, as good as they may be, are like my AV system.

We do our best to keep them together and to make it work.

And every now and then, just like Cleovis and Arwilda Whiteside we have beautiful marriages.

But even the best marriage we have on earth is not worth comparing to the perfect union between Jesus and His Church.

These marriages are but glimpses of how good and beautiful the relationship between Christ and his Church is.

That’s how Paul describes the love a husband ought to have for his wife.

How should he love his wife? Just as he loves his own body, just as Christ loves the Church.

A husband ought to love his wife so much that he’s willing to give himself up for her, that is, a husband must be willing to give up his life for his wife.

Why? Because that’s how Christ loves the Church.

There is no place for a husband to lord over his wife, to make the wife his slave.

The Bible has never commanded the husband to lord over his wife.

The Bible tells the wife to be submit to Christ, and the wife obeys Christ when she does submit to the husband.

But the Bible does not command the husband to lord over this wife, to exert power over her or to control her life.

No, the husband is to give up his life for his wife.

The applies to the Christian couple who follows Jesus but in truth, it applies to every married couple, whether they believe Jesus or not.

Look with me at verse 31, “For this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two will become one flesh.”

We read this verse last week from Genesis 2:24 and Paul quotes from there.

This is Star 1: God created the woman to be a helper for the man.

God created marriage; it’s part of the creation story.

But interestingly, Paul doesn’t refer to the creation here.

He’s not talking about marriage in the context of creation.

Rather he’s talking about marriage in the context of the relationship between Christ and his church.

And so, here Paul gives a new reason for marriage.

As Christ loves the Church, and the Church submits to Christ, the husband is to love the wife and the wife is to submit to the husband.

In light of Jesus’ salvation, in light of Star 4, it is now for this reason that a man will leave his father and mother and be united to his wife, and the two will become one flesh.

Since the coming of Jesus, all marriages have a new purpose.

All marriages are now to reflect the union between Christ and His Church.

The original intent of marriage still applies; men still need women to run this world.

But now, there’s a new intent to marriage; men and women come together to reflect the new creation, the new union between God and the human race in Jesus.

Marriage is to be a glimpse of the perfect union between Christ and His Church.

This makes the calling of marriage even higher than what God had assigned for it in the beginning.

For not only is marriage given by God to provide security and intimacy, as well as order in the society.

After Jesus’ first coming, marriage is given by God to show a glimpse of the union between Christ and His Church,

A union that will last through the end of the world and into all eternity.

We see a glimpse of what this union will look like on the last day in the book of Revelations 21:1-4, “

We see this in Revelations 21:1-4, “Then I saw a new heaven and a new earth; for the first heaven and the first earth had passed away, and the sea was no more. 2 I also saw the holy city, the new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared like a bride adorned for her husband. Then I heard a loud voice from the throne: Look, God’s dwelling is with humanity, and he will live with them. They will be his peoples, and God himself will be with them and will be their God. 4 He will wipe away every tear from their eyes. Death will be no more; grief, crying, and pain will be no more, because the previous things have passed away.”

In Star 1, in creation, God brings the woman to the man.

In Star 7, on the last day, God brings the Church to His Son.

On that day, God will be with them and He will be their God.

On that day, there will be no more crying, no more pain.

On that day, we will be with God and God will live with us, forever.

This will be the true happily ever after.

In many marriages, there is a yearning that the marriage will forever.

However, if we look for a perfect happily ever after in our earthly marriages, we will be disappointed, without question.

Since the first marriage, no earthly marriage, not one has lasted forever.

All earthly marriages, even Christian marriages, even the longest ones, will come to an end, by the death of one of the spouses or by divorce.

However, there is one marriage that will last forever.

It is the heavenly marriage between Christ and His Church.

The union between Christ and His Church is the only one that will last forever.

Robertson McQuilkin was the president of Columbia International University for twenty two years.

During his time as the president, the Christian evangelical university grew dramatically and it was known throughout the world.

However, while he was still the president, his wife Muriel was diagnosed with Alzheimer’s Disease.

Muriel was happy when Robertson was there but never happy when he wasn’t.

The solution for Robertson was obvious.

He would resign as president to take care of his wife.

This is what he said, “I promised . . . in sickness and in health till death do us part. And I’m a man of my word…This was no grim duty to which I stoically resigned, however. It was only fair. She had, after all, cared for me for almost four decades with marvellous devotion; now it was my turn. And such a partner she was! If I took care of her for 40 years, I would never be out of her debt.”

He emphasized that caring for Muriel was not something that he “had to” do. “It’s not that I have to. It’s that I get to.”

It’s a high calling to be the husband God called Robertson to be.

There was a high cost, but Robertson considered it an honour to be sacrificial for his wife.

Robertson considered the difficulties, the hardship, the loss of prestige something that he got to do for his wife.

He loved her so much that he was glad to sacrifice all these for her.

He would gladly spend the next forty years, he would gladly spend the rest of his life caring for her.

That’s a glimpse of what the Lord Jesus has done for His Church.

Jesus loved us so much that he was willing to sacrifice his life for us.

It’s a love that not one of us deserved.

It’s a love that brought us true security and everlasting hope in a world of suffering and death.

And when we marry, when a groom makes a vow to his bride and a bride makes a vow to the groom, we promised to show a glimpse of that beautiful union between Christ and His Church.

This is the high calling of marriage and for those of us who are married, we get to be part of it!

We get to be a glimpse of the perfect union between Christ and His Church!

2. God provides marriages to help us live out gospel of grace

So, marriages are what God had created and given in the beginning, and marriages are patterns that God used to show us what the end, the union between Christ and His Church looks like.

What will we do with marriages in between the beginning and the end of the world?

In marriage, we help one another to live out the gospel of grace.

A few years ago I saw this video on youtube about a well-dressed, friendly family going to Church.

The parents came on time and were greeting everyone at Church.

The kids were behaving themselves.

But the video then showed what happened 30 minutes beforehand.

The family slept in, the daughter wasn’t co-operating, the breakfast was burnt, the son forgot his shoes, the Dad was shaving while he was driving, the Mum was putting on her lipstick.

It was a mess.

What the family looked like at Church was very different to what the family looked like in the way to Church.

Some of us are nodding our heads; you know what I’m talking about

I know what you’ve gone through because I went through it as well.

And so, I’m thankful for each one of you who come to Church, especially those who have come with a young family over a long distance.

It’s not easy to come to Church with a young family on time and I’m glad you do it week after week!

It’s great to come to Church and proclaim what we believe together.

It’s good to sing songs to praise our God and to be friendly and loving to one another…for 1.5 hours!

But it’s hard to do the same at home.

Sometimes, it’s a lot harder to be faithful and godly, loving and friendly to members of our own family, because we are all imperfect and fallen sinners.

We see the faults of others, and they see our own.

This is especially true of marriage.

In marriages, we see each other without the make-up, without the pretence.

And so it’s easy and tempting to keep pointing out the faults of our spouse, and for our spouse to do the same.

This is why the gospel of Lord Jesus is so vital to marriages.

In the sacrifice of Jesus, God has saved us and has forgiven us our sins.

Jesus himself has taught that when we pray, we ask God to forgive our sins as we forgive those who sin against us.

This is what we learn here at Church.

And this is what we put into practice in our marriage.

You can read many books about Christianity.

You can hear talks and even give seminars about Christianity.

But in a Christian marriage, where both the husband and the wife are followers of Jesus, there you will find one of the most excellent schools of Christianity.

For the Christian marriage, is the training ground for the Christian faith, firstly for the couple, and also for the children.

In the Christian marriage, we put the Christian lessons of sacrifice, submission, forgiveness, love and faithfulness into practice.

In the Christian marriage, we learn to love as Christ loved and we learn to submit as the Church submits.

But even as we learn to follow Christ in the School of Christian Marriage, we are reminded that we can’t do it on our own.

We can’t change our spouse even as we follow Christ; we can’t even change ourselves as we follow Christ.

This is where Star 6 comes in.

Star 6 reminds us of the teaching of Regeneration, or new birth.

God had sent His Spirit to his people and God’s Spirit lives within in his people.

It’s only as the Holy Spirit gives us new birth, as He gives new life in us, that we can change, that we can be transformed.

It is the Holy Spirit, sent by God the Father and God the Son, who transforms us and our marriages to be what God wanted marriage to be.

And so, we must pray and depend on the Holy Spirit, asking by faith that he will teach us to live out the gospel of grace in our marriage.

What will this look like?

As a couple reads God’s word together, and pray together, they will learn to sacrifice and submit, they will learn to love and forgive.

A Christian couple will intentionally help each other’s faith in Christ grow.

As they grow in their maturity, they will learn to help others grow in Christ also.

They will put what they’ve learnt in their own marriage about forgiveness and love into practice in the wider Church community.

They will be a blessing for God’s people.

In Christian marriages, God has provided a space to help us grow in our faith.

And from there, God will use Christian marriages to help others in the Church to grow.

Marriage is a high calling.

It’s a God-given calling, patterned after the perfect union between Christ and His Church.

Christian marriage is hard work but it’s also beautiful.

If you are already in a Christian marriage, keep working hard on it!

If you are a Christian but your spouse is not, keep praying for him or her.

Do not stop praying until he (if you are a wife) or she (if you are a husband) becomes a Christian!

Make it your priority, not for your sake but for theirs.

This is where the fifth star comes in.

Star 5 is the teaching of resurrection.

Christ is risen, those in Christ will be raised in him.

If your spouse is not a believer yet, then he or she is still dead in sin.

And you will feel both frustrated and saddened at the fact that their hearts are cold, and their necks are stiff.

I have spoken with many sisters and brothers, who are heart-broken because their spouse’s refusal to be saved.

Don’t give up! God is the God of the Resurrection and he has the power to raise the dead, just as he once raised you from the dead.

Pray for God to raise the dead in your family!

If you are a single Christian and want to pursue marriage, only look for Christians who are in the Lord.

Do not pursue or be pursued by people who aren’t Christians.

They cannot and will not lead you one step closer to Christ.

Until they follow Christ, do not pursue a romantic relationship with them.

If you’re not convinced that you shouldn’t be in a relationship with a non-believer, then please speak with me or Jenny, or any one of the older sister of brother in Christ who had lived through it.

I can show you from the Bible why it’s a very bad idea and these sisters and brothers who are living through it can tell you how heart-breaking it is

If you are a single Christian and don’t want to pursue marriage, there may be very good reasons to do so.

Just make sure your reasons are for God’s glory and not your own.

If I haven’t mentioned a category that you’re in yet, and you want to speak to me about it, I’ll be at the entrance of the Church after the service, and I’d love to have a longer chat with you.

And so, sisters and brothers, honour the high calling of marriage.

Long lasting marriages are rare.

Good long lasting Christian marriages are rarer still.

Let’s pursue the high calling of Christian marriages, in whatever situation God has placed us.

If you’re already in a marriage, keep working hard on it.

Keep reading the Bible to see the beautiful union between Christ and His Church and see how your marriage can reflect its reality.

In my family, we read the Bible as much as we can together every night.

We’ve been doing it ever since the children were babies.

When they were young, we read from Children Bible.

As they grew older, we use the Jesus Storybook Bible, which explained the Bible beautifully, for both children and parents.

If you’re not in marriage, encourage and support those who are in one.

They will appreciate your help and your support.

They will appreciate your prayer for them even more!

A good place for support will be in the Growth groups that meet throughout the week.

In particular, it’s great to be in the Brothers’ Group and the Sisters’ group, where we can go deeper in our sharing and concern for one another.

Range organises a Marriage course for the Mandarin speaking couples.

He will explain more about it later on.

We are looking at potentially starting a marriage course for the English speaking couples next year.

Friends, Christian marriage is a high calling.

And so, honour the high calling of Christian marriage.

Here are the three questions for this week:

If you are married, how does your marriage reflect on the beautiful union between Christ and His Church? (This applies to all marriages, not just Christian marriages)

Husbands, when was the last time you joyfully sacrificed for your wife? Wives, when was the last time you submitted joyfully to your husband?

If you are not married, but potentially looking for a marriage partner, what temptations must you avoid?

Friends, marriage is a high calling, let’s honour it as Christ would have us.

Let us pray,

“Father, we thank you for giving us the institution of marriage. We thank you that though we are frail and unworthy, you’ve given us a glimpse of the beautiful union between Christ and his Church in the institution of marriage. Please forgive us as a society for not honouring marriage, often desecrating it beyond recognition. By your Spirit, open our eyes to see the beauty of marriage, as it reflects on the beauty of the union between Christ and His Church, so that we may live out the gospel both in our marriage and in the Church. For Jesus’ sake we pray, Amen.”
