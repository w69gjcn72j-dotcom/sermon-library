An extravagant humility before Jesus

Jesus before all else; Jesus before it’s too late

Welcome to our 153rd Anniversary! It’s great to see everyone here!

Since it’s a day to celebrate our history, I brought some historical items over.

These are items I found around the church and around my house.

Exhibit one

Who can tell me what this is? How about someone under 10?

Yes, it’s a cassette tape.

Exhibit two:

Who can tell me what this is? How about someone under 40?

It’s called MD, Mini Disc.

It was supposed to be the next big thing after CD’s.

It records sounded digitally, which was a big deal back in the day.

People who loved recording songs digitally were very enthusiastic about it.

In Hong Kong Chinese, we call people who are very into audio quality 發燒友.

Literally it means a friend who has fever.

However, it came from the word ‘Fancier’, people who are feverishly enthusiastic about fancy things.

Many 發燒友 spent a lot of money upgrading to MD’s for better audio listening.

They’ve spent extravagant amounts of money on getting the state-of-the-art MD players and MD’s.

However, MD’s were replaced fairly quickly by things like MP3 players and ipods.

Do we have any audio 發燒友 here?

We spent a lot on what we’re enthusiastic about, don’t we?

If we’re into cars, we save up to buy the best car we can afford.

If we’re into sporting team, we save up to buy all the merchandise we can afford.

If we’re enthusiastic about something, we’ll spend a huge amount, an extravagant amount on money on it.

What are we enthusiastic about? What do we spend an extravagant amount on?

If someone asks your friend what you’re enthusiastic about, what you’re a 發燒友 / fancier of, what will they say?

In today’s passage, we’ll see someone who had spent an extravagant amount of money on something she’s devote to.

In fact, it’s not just something but Someone.

An extravagant humility before Jesus

In this passage, we see an extravagant humility before Jesus.

John 12:1-2, “Six days before the Passover, Jesus came to Bethany where Lazarus was, the one Jesus had raised from the dead. 2 So they gave a dinner for him there; Martha was serving them, and Lazarus was one of those reclining at the table with him.”

The last time we saw Jesus in this book was him leaving Bethany to go to the wilderness because the Jewish leaders had decided to kill him.

Jesus is now back in Bethany, back in the very town where he raised Lazarus from the dead.

The good people of Bethany give Jesus a dinner.

Martha is serving, and Lazarus is reclining at the table with Jesus.

They would’ve lied on the ground with their feet facing outwards.

I love how Lazarus came to be known: The one Jesus had raised from the dead.

In the last chapter, we read how Jesus raised Lazarus from the dead by calling him to come out of the tomb.

By raising Lazarus from the dead, Jesus showed that he had power over death.

That’s how Lazarus will be known from now on: the one Jesus had raised from the dead.

Yes, many people had come to see Jesus but in verse nine we read that many people had come to see Lazarus, the one Jesus had raised from the dead.

The very fact that Lazarus is there is living proof of the life-giving power of Jesus.

You see Lazarus and you see Jesus’ power on display.

Many people who saw Lazarus breathing, eating and living, believed in Jesus as a result.

His very life was a testimony, a confession of the power of Jesus.

Such was the power of Lazarus’ testimony that the chief priests had decided to not only to kill Jesus but to kill Lazarus also.

Lazarus’ testimony was so powerful that his very presence became a threat to the chief priests!

We don’t have any records of Lazarus doing anything extraordinary.

He simply ate with Jesus here; it looked ordinary, not spectacular.

But in truth it was spectacular because he was dead! He was dead but now he’s alive.

When the people saw Lazarus, they saw the living proof of the power of Jesus.

Then during the dinner, Mary did something extraordinary, something beautiful for Jesus.

Look with me at verse 3, “Then Mary took a pound of perfume, pure and expensive nard, anointed Jesus’s feet, and wiped his feet with her hair. So the house was filled with the fragrance of the perfume.”

The perfume that Mary had used was expensive; we were told later on that if they sold it, they could get 300 denarii for it.

That’s about one year’s worth of wages; in Australia, that’s $92,000.

In one go, Mary poured $92,000 worth of perfume on Jesus.

Mary poured the perfume and anointed Jesus’ feet.

She then wiped Jesus’ feet with her hair.

It was an extravagant act, a very expensive act.

It was also a very humbling act.

It was an extravagant humility.

And the fragrance of the perfume filled the house.

Before church started today, I sprayed some perfume around the Church.

In the last few minutes, I’ve asked a few people around the Church to spray the perfume again.

For those of us who have a good sense of smell, can you smell it?

The perfume of Mary would’ve been a lot more powerful and would’ve filled her whole house.

She gave Jesus an extravagant gift that benefited all those in the house.

But it wasn’t enough for Mary to give such an extravagant gift; she also did it with much humility.

The extravagance of her gift was only matched by the depth of her humility.

She wiped Jesus’ feet with her hair.

She wiped his dirty, muddy feet with the cleanest thing that she could find, her hair.

She made her hair a towel to wipe Jesus’ feet.

Her focus was on giving Jesus the honour and the glory and to express how unworthy she was.

She was utterly devoted to Jesus.

It was an act of extravagant humility.

She paid a great price, both financially and socially, to honour Jesus.

That’s’ what true, genuine devotion to Jesus looks like.

She loved Jesus, she adored Jesus and so she came before Jesus with an extravagant humility.

She would’ve been thankful to Jesus’ public ministry and for his teachings.

But on a personal note, she would’ve been immensely thankful to Jesus for bringing Lazarus back from the dead.

She was mourning for her brother and Jesus brought him back to life.

No words of Mary were recorded in this passage; perhaps she couldn’t think of any words adequate enough to express her thanks.

And so, she honoured Jesus with her extraordinary act.

She honoured Jesus with an act of extravagant humility.

That’s what genuine devotion to Jesus look like.

Before 1869, Kogarah had no church building.

People had to travel far away in order to find a place of worship.

A committee was set up to buy a piece of land in order to start building the Church.

Incidentally, the key movers of the committee were immigrants.

The committee was negotiating an offer to the owner of the land, Mr William Wolfen.

However, Mr Wolfen decided to give the land to the Church for free.

This land that we’re on now, was extravagantly given so that we can start a Church.

More recently, many of you have continued to give extravagantly during the times of COVID lockdown so that the Church can continue ministering.

I thank God for you.

Even now, you are still giving your time and your resources extravagantly to run the current ministries at Church.

Most, if not all of us, come together on Sundays.

Many of us also work throughout the week to serve in different ministries of the Church.

We do it, not to make ourselves look good.

We do it to make Jesus look good.

For those of us who have truly understood the significance of what Jesus has done for us, we gladly come before him with an extravagant humility.

Because Jesus done more for us than what raising our brother back from the dead.

Jesus has died to give us life, eternal life.

Jesus has raised us from spiritual death to spiritual life.

That’s why we devote ourselves to him with an extravagant humility.

The service that give to Jesus is like a beautiful fragrance; it will overflow to bless those around us.

Jesus before all else; Jesus before it’s too late

However, not everyone was convinced that what Mary did was a good thing.

Look with me at verses 4-5, “Then one of his disciples, Judas Iscariot (who was about to betray him), said, 5 “Why wasn’t this perfume sold for three hundred denarii and given to the poor?”

Judas asks, what Mary did was a bit too much, wasn’t it?

He criticised Mary’s extravagant humility.

Judas said if the perfume was sold, the money could’ve been used to help the poor.

Now of course, he wasn’t really caring for the poor.

He was a thief and would often help himself by stealing from the money bag, most probably the money that people gave to Jesus and his disciples for their ministries.

That is, Judas stole from Jesus.

Judas had a fake devotion.

He might have honoured Jesus with his lips, but his heart was far from Jesus.

A few days after this incident, Judas would betray Jesus for a small sum of money.

In contrast to the extravagance that Mary gave, Judas was willing to sell Jesus for a much smaller amount: 30 pieces of silver.

Some commentators had put it as low as the equivalent of $90 today.

And that’s how Judas would come to be known, the one who betrayed Jesus.

In Judas’ heart, there were other things more important than Jesus, more urgent than Jesus.

People like Judas would not come to Jesus with an extravagant humility.

His was never a true devotion to Jesus.

Whether it’s in Western families or Eastern families, a common experience I hear from young Christians is that their parents only want them to commit partially to being a Christian.

A small dose of Christianity is fine.

You can go to Church every now and then, you can go to Youth Group, every now and then.

But they would get upset and angry if the children decide to devote themselves to Jesus.

I’ve seen parents who have disowned their children, not even speaking to them, because they have decided to devote themselves to Jesus.

A dose of Christianity is fine, but a genuine devotion to Christ is not.

Sadly, they encourage a superficial, lip-service, and ultimately fake devotion to Jesus rather than a genuine, extravagant, humble devotion to Jesus.

Of course, it’s not just parents who fall in this trap.

All of us can fall into the trap of following Jesus, only offering Jesus fake devotion.

How does Jesus respond?

Look with me at verses 7-8, “7 Jesus answered, “Leave her alone; she has kept it for the day of my burial. 8 For you always have the poor with you, but you do not always have me.”

Leave her alone; the one who comes before Jesus with an extravagant humility is doing the right thing.

She did not know it, but Mary was in fact preparing for Jesus’ burial.

In less than six days, Jesus himself would die on the cross.

He would die for the sins of all those who believe in him.

Jesus died so that all those who would believe in him would live.

And so, this act of Mary of Bethany, this act of extravagant humility, is a significant and beautiful act,

For it prepares Jesus for his death, which will give life to all who would believe.

In response to Judas’ question about the poor, yes, it’s true that looking after the poor is important.

However, it’s not as important as serving Jesus.

Why? Because Jesus must have the priority and Jesus must have the urgency.

We devote ourselves to serving Jesus first; we devote ourselves to Jesus above all things.

Devoting Jesus will include serving and giving to the poor but it mustn’t become more important than serving Jesus.

Second, Jesus must also have the urgency.

Six days from this incident, Jesus would die.

He would rise again with a new body but would only remain with them a short period of time.

His ministry in his flesh would soon come to an end.

He would go back to heaven and remain there physically in his new body until he comes back.

In a short while, Jesus would see Jesus for the last time.

Tragically, he would not see Jesus after Jesus was raised from the dead because Judas himself would die before then.

It would be too late for him.

As a minister, I would often visit people who are very sick.

Earlier this week, I was called to go to a patient in a hospital as soon as I could because the patient would die at any time.

When I go to speak with a dying Christian, there’s almost always a patient, hopeful expectation of meeting Jesus.

And if they’re not sure, I can reassure them through the Bible that there is hope for those who trust in Jesus.

However, when I visited this particular patient, I wasn’t sure whether she believed in Jesus.

I shared the gospel with her, but she, as weak as she was, kept shaking her head.

Maybe she wasn’t ready; maybe she refused to believe.

My prayer is that she had come to know Christ before it was too late.

Because they will come a time when it is too late.

When we die, or when Jesus comes back, it will be too late.

Where does that leave us?

It comes back to whether we truly know Jesus.

If we know Jesus, we will devote ourselves to him.

Like Mary, we will come before him with an extravagant humility.

We will always seek to give more because we know that we can never give enough.

Like Lazarus, we will live a new, risen life.

The very fact that we’re living in obedience to Jesus will be a witness to the power and salvation of Jesus.

People around us will see the Gospel in us.

They’ll see the life of someone who’s been saved by Jesus, who lives according to the promises of Jesus.

That’s what true devotion looks like.

If we don’t know him, we won’t devote our lives to Jesus.

Like Judas, we will criticise and mock those who have given extravagantly to Jesus.

We’ll only pay Jesus, lip service but our hearts are far from him.

And we’ll need to remember this: God cannot be mocked.

God knows our hearts and he will know whether we’re only pretending to devote ourselves to him.

And if we don’t devote ourselves to him, we never really knew him.

Lazarus, Mary and Judas are all remembered for how they relate to Jesus.

Lazarus, the one whom Jesus raised from the dead.

Mary, the one who had shown extravagant humility to Jesus.

Judas, the one who betrayed Jesus.

How will you be remembered?

Friends, devote yourself to Jesus with an extravagant humility, before all else and before it’s too late.

Today’s the day to devote yourself to Jesus.

Come before the Lord Jesus with an extravagant humility.

Whatever you can think of as the worthiest thing in your life, he’s worth more.

Whatever emergency you are facing in your life, he’s more urgent.

As our Church begin our 153rd year, my prayer is that we’ll be an extravagantly humble Church of God.

I pray that our Church will be fragrance of life, bringing the good news of Jesus to those around us, even when it’s at great cost to us.

If you don’t know Jesus yet, my prayer for you is that you will follow the fragrance of Christ, the fragrance of life.

Find out about Christ through them.

For those of us who know Jesus, ask God to search your hearts, asking for repentance for the times when we placed other things above Jesus.

Pray that we will genuinely, truly, devote ourselves to him.

Pray that we will come and serve him and his people with an extravagant humility.

Do you have a dose of Christianity or a devotion to Christ?

How have you recently shown your thankfulness to Jesus?

How will you be remembered? A Lazarus, a Mary or a Judas?

Friends, lift up your eyes to Jesus, our Lord, our Saviour, our Shepherd, our King.

He is worth all that we are and more.

Come before him with an extravagant humility.

Be an extravagantly humble Church.
