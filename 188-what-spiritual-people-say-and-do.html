For over the past 20 months or so, the world has faced an enemy that we cannot see with our eyes.

I’m talking of course about COVID-19.

COVID-19 is a virus, and so, invisible to the naked eye.

Though we can’t see the actual virus with our eyes, we’ve seen the effect it’s having on many lives around the world.

Here in Sydney, we’ve seen an explosion of cases and sadly, we’ve seen deaths of people with COVID not only in hospitals but in their own homes.

However, even with so much evidence, there are still people who don’t believe in it.

I’ve seen a post on Facebook which says, “Do you know anyone with COVID?”

All the person has to do is to walk to any hospital, talk to someone from State Health, and they can quickly tell you that they know someone who has COVID.

Though we can’t see COVID with our physical eyes, we know that it’s there.

There are also people who say that there’s no God because they can’t see him.

They don’t have any evidence of him and so they don’t believe in him.

But what if just like those who deny COVID, they’re trying to see him with their physical eyes?

What if we can only see him through another method, another way beside physical eyes?

Jesus tells us that God is Spirit and those who worship him must worship in Spirit and in truth.

Somehow the person who comes to God must do so in spirit.

What does it mean to be spiritual?

What makes a person spiritual?

And more crucially, am I a spiritual person?

In today’s passage, we’re going to see what the Bible says makes a person spiritual.

Please open your Bible with me to 1 Corinthians 12, beginning from verse 1.

Hear the words of the Spirit

Look with me at 1 Corinthians 12:1-3,

“Now concerning spiritual gifts: brothers and sisters, I do not want you to be unaware. 2 You know that when you were pagans, you used to be enticed and led astray by mute idols. 3 Therefore I want you to know that no one speaking by the Spirit of God says, “Jesus is cursed,” and no one can say, “Jesus is Lord,” except by the Holy Spirit.

A spiritual person is someone who confesses that Jesus is Lord.

A spiritual person is someone who confesses that Jesus is the Master, the Boss, the King.

A spiritual person is someone who totally surrenders himself or herself to Jesus.

This is at the heart of what it means to be spiritual and it’s fundamentally different to what other religions say about spirituality.

On Thursday last week, I attended a Zoom meeting with Archbishop Kanishka, Bishop Lin and fifty other ministers and gospel workers from all around Sydney.

All of us have been involved with Chinese ministry in one way or another.

During the meeting, the Archbishop shared a little about his history.

He shared with us that, like many of the ministers from Chinese background, he was born into a Buddhist family.

When he reached University, he wanted to find out more about Buddhism and so he’s spent time reading on Buddhism meditations and Buddhism teachings.

One day, he spoke with his friend about their religious beliefs and his friend told him that he was a Christian.

After Kanishka shared what he’s studying more about Buddhism, his friend, Andrew, said, “I have lost control of my life to Jesus Christ.”

That shocked Kanishka because Buddhism is about controlling yourself to save yourself.

However, for Christians, salvation is about Jesus saving you; it is about losing control of your life to Christ.

That’s what Christians mean when we say “Jesus is Lord”.

It’s about giving control of ourselves to Christ.

And it’s fundamentally different to what every other religion teaches.

The Bible tells us that whatever we’ve learnt about spirituality in the past is wrong;

They’re teachings by mute idols and they lead people astray.

True spirituality means losing control of your life for Jesus and calling Jesus Lord.

It’s a total surrender to Jesus.

Truly spiritual people can’t curse Jesus.

This might had been threats that some Jews uttered against Christians in early Christianity as part of their persecutions against them.

They want people to curse Jesus to prove that they’re not Christians.

Even today, there are people who actively persecute Christians and force people to either deny Jesus or face pain, and even death.

But no one speaking by the Spirit of God says, “Jesus is cursed.”

The Spirit of God would not allow a person in whom he dwells to curse Jesus.

Rather, someone has the Spirit of God will confess that Jesus is Lord.

For it’s only by the Spirit of God that someone can honestly, truthfully confess that Jesus is Lord, no matter what situation he or she is facing.

Many of us would’ve heard of the worsening situation in Kabul, Afghanistan.

It has shocked the world how quickly the situation turned, with many western countries, including Australia, now working to rescue its citizens and, where possible, Afghans.

As I write this sermon on Friday morning, I received news that there was a suicide bomb at the Kabul airport.

Over 70 people were killed.

Amid the violence, I had also seen article about the danger that Christians are facing.

The report says that Talibans are now asking for people’s phones.

If someone has a Bible downloaded on their phone, they would be killed immediately.

Indeed, many Christians in Afghanistan would rather be killed than to deny that Jesus is Lord.

That’s what a truly spiritual people would choose.

Please continue to pray for our brothers and sisters in Afghanistan as they face a very difficult and dangerous time ahead.

While many of us may not face the same danger as our Afghan brothers and sisters, even so, many of us are facing difficult situations.

Many of us are facing frustrations over our COVID restrictions.

Many of us who work are experiencing uncertainties regarding our work; some of us have reduced hours.

In the midst of the frustrations, the chaos, the uncertainties, the Spirit of God will lead genuine believers of Christ to say, “Jesus is Lord.”

Jesus is Lord over every tragedy and every difficulty.

Jesus is Lord even when a country is thrown into chaos.

Indeed, Jesus is Lord even when a country is in lockdown.

To proclaim that Jesus is Lord is not something that human beings come up all on our own.

It’s a confession that comes from the Spirit of God.

Whatever situation you’re facing now, ask Jesus to show you how he’s reigning as Lord.

How is Jesus Lord over your situation at the moment?

Hear what a spiritual person will say in each and every situation, “Jesus is Lord”.

Jesus is Lord and by His Spirit, Jesus gives gifts to his people.

God the Spirit gives

Look with me at verse 4-6, “Now there are different gifts, but the same Spirit. 5 There are different ministries, but the same Lord. 6 And there are different activities, but the same God works all of them in each person.

God the Spirit is the one who distribute the gifts; he is the One who give those gifts to the believers.

God the Son is the one who ministers, the one who came to serve.

The gifts that the Spirit gives are also ministries or services.

believers are to serve one another through these ministries.

Different gifts, one Spirit; Different ministries, one Lord.

Finally there are different activities, but the one God who produces each gift in each person.

God the Father is the one who enables, empowers, each person with their unique gift.

All three of the Persons of the Triune God work to bring his gifts to believers of the Lord Jesus.

Look with me at verse 7, “A manifestation of the Spirit is given to each person for the common good”

God gives his gifts to his Church for a purpose.

At the moment, we’re applying a grant to help with the security around our Church.

We’re hoping that we’ll get the grant so that we can set up security cameras and maybe even more secure fences around our site.

If we get the approval from the government, we need to prove that we have used the money for these.

If someone from the government look at our books, we need to show that we have used the money for items that we have promised to spend our money on.

The government had given the grant for a purpose and they would rightly want to see evidence of it.

God has given his gifts to the Church for a purpose.

The Spirit distributes, the Father empowers so that the believers of the Lord Jesus may glorify God as we minister or serve one another.

God the Father, God the Son and God the Spirit had given gifts to the Church for the common good.

What he gives to the Church are not merely human talents but gifts; his gifts!

And these gifts are given to each person; each person in the Church has a gift from God.

No one misses out!

Each person has been given a gift by God for the common good of the Church.

But notice that it’s not just the common good.

When the people of God use their gifts for the common good, it’s a manifestation of the Spirit.

When we use our gifts for the common good, people around us see the works of the Spirit.

A few days ago, it was a windy day here at Church and in many other parts of Sydney.

In Kiama, just about 2 hours south of Sydney, wind was blowing at 130km/hr.

Strictly speaking, we can’t see the wind but we can certainly see the effects of the wind on the buildings and the trees as it blasted through.

Jesus describes the Spirit of God to be like the wind.

Just as we might hear the wind but we can’t control it, we can see and hear the effects of the Spirit but we can’t control Him.

And we see the effects of the Spirit when a spiritual people who confesses that Jesus is Lord use the gifts of the Spirit for the common good.

Your gifts are from God; God’s given you the gifts.

Make sure you use it as He’s intended.

Use his gifts for the common good so that together, as we serve one another, we might be a manifestation of the Holy Spirit.

See the works of the Spirit

What does a manifestation of the Spirit look like?

We see a manifestation of the Spirit as people use their gifts at Church.

We see the works of the Spirit as people use their gifts from the common good.

A few weeks ago, I went to get vaccinated with the COVID vaccine.

I booked out the whole morning for it just in case.

I got to St George Hospital and saw a line running from the elevator.

And I thought, “O great, this is going to take a long time”.

I looked at my watch and it says 9am.

Amazingly, the line moved quickly.

There was a great team of health professionals guiding the patients through.

They were professional and friendly.

I got the injection and sat in the room for 15 minutes just in case I get any reactions.

I stepped out of the elevator and looked at my watch: 9:29am.

It was a great experience.

All the team members did their parts well and got patients out very quickly.

There was a unity and fluidity in their work.

God has given his each of his people in the Church a gift for the common good and he wants us to use them together in unity.

In this passage, Paul lists a number of gifts that God’s given to the Corinthian Church.

It’s not an exhaustive list; it doesn’t cover every gift there is.

But it does give us a glimpse of the kind of gifts that God’s given to his church.

Take a look at the list from verses 8-10.

Which gift of the Spirit stands out the most to you?

Talk with the person next to you.

You can send in your responses via Zoom, Wechat and Whatsapp.

Which gift of the Spirit from verses 8-10 stands out to you?

You have 20 seconds.

Look with me at verse 8,

“To one is given a message of wisdom through the Spirit, to another, a message of knowledge by the same Spirit,”

A message of wisdom refers to a message of guidance for dealing with a situation in light of Christ’s wisdom.

Earlier in 1 Corinthians, we’ve seen that Christ’s wisdom is the opposite of the world’s wisdom.

Christ’s wisdom has to deal with Christ crucified, a stumbling block to the Jews and foolishness to the Gentiles.

It has to do with how to live a Christian life in light of Christ’s self-sacrificial love for his people.

It’s the wisdom to choose Christ’s way in a difficult situation rather than the world’s way.

A message of knowledge refers to scriptural and theological knowledge.

It’s knowing what God has already said in the Bible about a particular topic.

Verse 9, “9 to another, faith by the same Spirit, to another, gifts of healing by the one Spirit,”

While all Christians have faith in the Lord Jesus, there are times when the Holy Spirit gives an extraordinary amount of confidence in God’s saving powers.

There are numerous Christians who had faced extraordinary trials who testified afterwards that they had experienced an extraordinary comfort and confidence in God during those trials.

It’s a faith trusting that in these last days God will do extraordinary things, as well as the mundane things, to bring people to him.

It’s a faith that asks how will God show himself to the world in these extraordinary events?

How will God show himself through what he’s doing in my life?

The Spirit also give gifts of healing to the Church.

Notice that it’s a plural of gifts of healing.

There are multiple ways that God can heal different people.

Sometimes he’ll do it through scientifically inexplicable ways.

I think most of the time, though, he’ll heal people through scientifically explicable ways.

While it’s scientifically explicable, doesn’t mean it’s not incredible.

For example, the fact that so many pharmaceutical companies can develop so many COVID vaccines in a relatively short period of time is incredible.

While COVID-19 is a new virus, there’s actually quite a lot we already know about the type of virus it is.

There’s been many years of virology that has helped us develop different techniques to develop the various vaccines.

Though there is still a lot to learn about the virus, we thank God for giving many researchers the skills to quickly develop a vaccine.

We thank God that he has given different gifts of healing to his people!

Verse 10, “to another, the performing of miracles, to another, prophecy, to another, distinguishing between spirits, to another, different kinds of tongues, to another, interpretation of tongues.”

The performing of miracles probably refers to acts of salvation and judgements, much like Paul himself was struck with temporary blindness and subsequently healed as he turned to Christ.

The gift of prophecy here refers to a conviction (strong belief) about God’s word and the future consequence of our obedience or disobedience to him.

It’s a response to a special needs and issues that the listeners are facing.

This gift of prophecy is often a pastoral insight that can bring both a challenge and a comfort to the listeners.

Since it’s from the same Spirit who inspired the Scriptures, a genuine prophecy will resonate with the Scripture and never go against it.

Those with the gift to distinguish between the spirits are those who can help us see the true prophet, one that aligns with Scripture, as opposed to the false prophets.

The gift of speaking in tongues is the gift to speak to God in unlearned human languages.

And the gift of interpreting the tongues is the ability to translate what was said to God through the gift of tongues.

Verse 11, “One and the same Spirit is active in all these, distributing to each person as he wills.”

There’s One God, One Spirit who is active in all these gifts.

It’s God the Holy Spirit who distributes and designates each gift to each person.

It’s his gifts and he gives them according to his will.

When God’s people, use the gifts that God the Holy Spirit gives to them for the common good, it’s a marvellous sight to behold.

Just a little over thirty years ago, we didn’t have any Chinese congregations in any of the Anglican Churches in Sydney.

Though there were Chinese inside the Anglican Church, we didn’t have any Chinese speaking congregations.

Then the minister at Cabramatta Anglican, the Rev Neil Flower, decided to invest in Chinese ministries.

In time, that Chinese ministry grew.

Other Chinese ministries begin to grow in other churches.

As I mentioned earlier in the sermon, 53 ministers and gospel workers from all around the diocese came together with the Archbishop and the Bishop to pray.

It was a wonderful sight to see what God has been doing among the Sydney Chinese Anglicans over the past three decades.

We still got a long way to go but God in his wisdom, by his Spirit, gave different gifts to the Church to build and to nurture his people,

Particularly those from a Chinese background.

Among the Chinese Churches there are those who have messages of wisdom, others with messages of knowledge.

We definitely had people with faith among the Chinese brothers and sisters.

My hope is that God will continue to manifest his Spirit not only through the Chinese, but people of all cultures and all languages,

As we use the gifts of His Spirit for the common good.

My prayer is that everyone from the Church will come and work together, virtually for now and in person later,

As we come to Church on Sundays,

And as we gather in Growth groups during the week.

Friends, see with eyes of faith and observe how lives are saved and lives are changed in God’s Church.

See the works of the Spirit of God as people become spiritually alive, mature and grow in love, faith and hope.

When people see us work together, using our different gifts, may they see the manifestation of the Spirit of God.

And so, who’s a spiritual person?

A spiritual person is someone who confesses Jesus is Lord and who, together with their church, uses their God-given gifts for the common good.

What does this mean for us?

Speak the Spirit’s word, Do the Spirit’s work.

In a popular TV series called Ted Lasso, there’s a character called Dani Rojas.

Rojas is a young Mexican football (soccer) player who doesn’t speak much English.

But there’s an English phrase that he says all the time, “Football is life”.

For him, he lives and breathes football.

Football is his life!

For the spiritual person, for the Christian, Jesus is life!

We speak the Spirit’s word about Jesus; we confess that Jesus is Lord.

We do the Spirit’s work; we use the gifts that God’s given us for the common good.

We live and breathe the gospel of Jesus in all that we say and do.

My hope is that all of us at Church will know and use the gift that God’s given them for the good of his Church.

That’s what spiritual people do.
