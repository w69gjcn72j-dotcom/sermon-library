Come to the Wedding of the King's Son

Matthew 22:1–14 · Sunday 6 September 2026 · St Paul's Anglican Church Kogarah · For high-school students · Preached by Rev David Yung

Big Idea: Don't insult the Heavenly Father's invitation with excuses or defiance — come when he summons, clothed in what he provides.

Aim: (1) See that the gospel is an invitation to a party the Father has already paid for, not a to-do list. (2) Name honestly the good, ordinary things that quietly keep them from Jesus. (3) Take one concrete step this week: come, and ask one friend to come with them.

You'll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, the handout, about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • What is the best party or event you have ever been invited to — and what made you actually turn up? Now flip it: what's an invitation you said yes to and then quietly bailed on?
  • Think about your last week. If someone looked only at your calendar and your screen time, what would they say matters most to you? (No judgment — just be honest.)

Read — Matthew 22:1–14 (CSB)

1 Once more Jesus spoke to them in parables: 2 "The kingdom of heaven is like a king who gave a wedding banquet for his son. 3 He sent his servants to summon those invited to the banquet, but they didn't want to come.

4 Again, he sent out other servants and said, 'Tell those who are invited: See, I've prepared my dinner; my oxen and fattened cattle have been slaughtered, and everything is ready. Come to the wedding banquet.'

5 But they paid no attention and went away, one to his own farm, another to his business, 6 while the rest seized his servants, mistreated them, and killed them. 7 The king was enraged, and he sent out his troops, killed those murderers, and burned down their city.

8 Then he told his servants, 'The banquet is ready, but those who were invited were not worthy. 9 Go then to where the roads exit the city and invite everyone you find to the banquet.' 10 So those servants went out on the roads and gathered everyone they found, both evil and good. The wedding banquet was filled with guests.

11 When the king came in to view the guests, he saw a man there who was not dressed for a wedding. 12 So he said to him, 'Friend, how did you get in here without wedding clothes?' The man was speechless.

13 Then the king told the attendants, 'Tie him up hand and foot, and throw him into the outer darkness, where there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.'

14 For many are invited, but few are chosen."

Context

Jesus is standing in the temple courts in Jerusalem, days before the cross, and the religious leaders are trying to have him arrested. This is the third parable he tells them in a row, and each one gets sharper: two sons who won't obey, tenants who kill the owner's son, and now guests who insult the king. In Jesus' world, an invitation from a king wasn't a text you could leave on read. It was a summons — and turning it down meant you had picked a side.

Discuss

  1. v.2 — Jesus says the kingdom of heaven is like a king throwing a wedding party for his son. If that's what God's kingdom is like, what does it tell you about God — and who is the party actually for?
  2. vv.3–4 — The king sends servants twice, and the second time he lists what's already been done. What is the only thing the guests are asked to bring?
  3. v.5 — The two excuses are a farm and a business. What is strange about those excuses? Why does Jesus pick reasonable ones instead of obviously evil ones?
  4. vv.6–7 — The king's reaction seems huge. What does it tell you about how seriously he takes an insult aimed at his son?
  5. vv.8–10 — Who ends up filling the hall? What does "both evil and good" mean for who we're allowed to invite to church?
  6. vv.11–12 — This man got in for free off the street, and then got thrown out. What exactly was wrong with him? (Hint: it isn't that he was poor.)
  7. v.12 — The king calls him "friend" and gives him a chance to speak. Why does his silence matter so much?
  8. v.14 — "Many are invited, but few are chosen." How would you know whether you have actually come to the party — or only kept your name on the guest list?

Live It Out

Our whole church is living out a simple rhythm — 3 · 2 · 1 · 0. It fits this passage perfectly, because the King's servants were sent out to invite, not to screen.

  • PRAY FOR 3 — write down three friends who aren't Christians yet. Actual names. Put them in your phone.
  • CONNECT WITH 2 — this week, have a real conversation with two of them. Not a gospel presentation. A conversation.
  • INVITE 1 — ask one of them to something: youth, church, the Father's Day service. "Everything is ready" — you're not selling a course, you're passing on an invitation someone else already paid for.
  • 0 LEFT UNLOVED — look around youth group this Friday. Who is on the edge? Go and sit with them.

This week's challenge: Open your calendar this week and find your "farm and business" — the good, normal thing that most often crowds Jesus out. Move one of them so you're actually there: at church, at youth, in the Word. Then send one message to one friend inviting them along.

Memory verse: "Tell those who are invited: See, I've prepared my dinner; my oxen and fattened cattle have been slaughtered, and everything is ready. Come to the wedding banquet." — Matthew 22:4 (CSB)

Pray

  • Thank the Father that the feast is finished and paid for — that "everything is ready" and nothing is left for us to add.
  • Pray for your three friends by name. Ask that God's kindness would lead them to repentance, and for one opening this week to point one of them to Jesus.
  • Ask God to show each of you the excuse you've been protecting, and to give you courage to move it.

Leader's Notes

Lead this warmly and joyfully. The parable ends in judgment, but the heart of it is a Father who wants a full hall to honour his Son. Your job tonight is to make Jesus attractive, not to make anyone squirm.

Some students in the room may not yet be Christians, and some may have been in church their whole lives without ever having come. Both need the same thing: an open invitation, not a spotlight. Never corner a student or ask anyone to declare their spiritual status in front of the group.

Wrong turn 1 — decoding the robe. Groups love to argue about what the wedding garment "stands for" (faith? works? righteousness?). Don't go there. Jesus never explains it; what he shows us is the man's refusal. Suitable clothing was available and he could not be bothered. Preach the refusal, not the symbol.

Wrong turn 2 — turning v.14 into a predestination debate or an anxiety spiral. "Many are called, few are chosen" is not an invitation to inspect a secret list. Matthew's "called" is the wide outward invitation; we recognise the chosen by their obedient response. The verse is designed to make you come, not to make you panic.

Question helps

  • Q1 — God is a Father celebrating his Son. The party is for Jesus, not for us; we're invited into somebody else's joy. That's actually freeing: you don't have to be impressive enough to deserve a seat.
  • Q2 — Nothing but themselves. The oxen are already slaughtered, everything is ready. "All the fitness he requireth is to feel your need of him" (Joseph Hart). Preparation comes first, then the summons — which is why anyone can be invited.
  • Q3 — They're not sins. They're good, lawful, necessary things — which is exactly why they work so well. Nobody skips the feast because they decided to be wicked; they skip it because Saturday is the only day to get the mulch down. Apathy in a comfortable church never feels like rebellion; it feels like a full diary.
  • Q4 — In an honour-shame world, an accepted invitation later shrugged off is withdrawing loyalty from your king. And notice indifference hardens into hostility (v.6). Jesus is warning the Jerusalem leaders that privilege can be forfeited (see 21:43).
  • Q5 — Anyone the servants found at the road junctions. "Both evil and good" is the only screening criterion the King gives us — that is, none. Compare Paul at Pisidian Antioch: "we now turn to the Gentiles" (Acts 13:46). God will have a full table.
  • Q6 — Contempt, not poverty. Suitable clothing was available to him. He was already seated inside the feast and was still put out — being in the hall is not the same as being fit for the King. Judas was inside the group, at the supper, trusted with the money, and lost.
  • Q7 — "Friend" is a genuine appeal, not just irony. The King gives him an opening, and he has nothing to say. His silence is his own verdict. There's mercy in the question: while it is still being asked, there is still time to answer it.
  • Q8 — By an obedient response. Not by feelings, not by family history, not by attendance. "You accepted this invitation years ago: have you actually come to the marriage, or only kept your name on the guest list?"

Church vision — and what is live right now

Tie this to where our church is going. Our vision is a church growing from faith to faith — 75 households serving densely at St Paul's by the end of this year, on the way to 500 households and ten fellowships by 2035. That growth will not come from clever programmes. It comes from ordinary people standing at the road junction and saying, come.
