KOGYouth

Jesus Is the Light

John 8:12–20 · 27 September 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

For high-school growth groups · Preacher: Rev David Yung

Big Idea: Follow Jesus, the Light of the World, and shine as his lower lights, with his light of life.

Aim: By the end, students should be able to (1) explain what Jesus meant by “I am the light of the world” and why it was such a big claim; (2) say why we can trust Jesus — not because we’ve judged him by our standards, but because God the Father backs him up; (3) leave with 3 names and one real way to shine as a “lower light” this week.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • What’s the darkest place you’ve ever been — a blackout, camping with no torch, a cave? What did you grab first? How did it feel when the light came back?
  • Did you do anything for the Mid-Autumn Festival this week — mooncakes, lanterns, looking at the full moon? What other festivals use lights?

Read

12 Jesus spoke to them again: “I am the light of the world. Anyone who follows me will never walk in the darkness but will have the light of life.”

13 So the Pharisees said to him, “You are testifying about yourself. Your testimony is not valid.”

14 “Even if I testify about myself,” Jesus replied, “my testimony is true, because I know where I came from and where I’m going. But you don’t know where I come from or where I’m going.

15 You judge by human standards. I judge no one.

16 And if I do judge, my judgment is true, because it is not I alone who judge, but I and the Father who sent me.

17 Even in your law it is written that the testimony of two witnesses is true.

18 I am the one who testifies about myself, and the Father who sent me testifies about me.”

19 Then they asked him, “Where is your Father?” “You know neither me nor my Father,” Jesus answered. “If you knew me, you would also know my Father.”

20 He spoke these words by the treasury, while teaching in the temple. But no one seized him, because his hour had not yet come.

Context

Jesus says this in Jerusalem during the Feast of Tabernacles (John 7:2), a week-long festival remembering how God led Israel through the desert. The temple courts were lit up with huge lamps, and men who loved God danced with torches, singing praise. In the Old Testament, light is something that belongs to God. Standing under all those lights, Jesus says, “I am the light of the world.”

Discuss

  1. v.12 — What does Jesus say he is? What two things does he promise to “anyone who follows me”?
  2. v.12 — In the Bible, God himself is light: “The LORD is my light and my salvation” (Psalm 27:1). So why was it such a daring — even outrageous — thing for Jesus to say? When would it not be outrageous?
  3. v.12 — Hebrews 1:3 says the Son is “the radiance of God’s glory.” The sermon said the Father is like the sun and Jesus is like the sunbeams. If you want to know what God is like, where should you look?
  4. v.12 — The sermon said we’re like people walking through Hezekiah’s tunnel — 550 metres of total darkness — without a headlamp. Where do you see darkness in the world? Where do you see it in yourself?
  5. vv.13–15 — What do the Pharisees say is wrong with Jesus’ claim? Jesus says they “judge by human standards.” How do people at school judge Jesus (feelings, what friends think, “I just don’t believe it”)?
  6. vv.16–18 — Who is Jesus’ second witness? Why is God the only one who can really tell us who Jesus is?
  7. vv.19–20 — “You know neither me nor my Father.” How does Jesus say you get to know God the Father?
  8. Matthew 5:14–16 — Jesus calls his followers “the light of the world” — his “lower lights”. What could it look like for you to shine at school, at home or in the group chat, so people see Jesus and not just you?

Live It Out

KOG 3·2·1·0 — pray for 3, connect with 2, invite 1, 0 left out.

  • Pray for 3 — three friends who aren’t Christians yet. Picture their faces and pray for them by name.
  • Connect with 2 — two people at KOGYouth or church you don’t normally talk to.
  • Invite 1 — invite one of your three to youth or to church on Sunday.
  • 0 left out — who eats lunch alone, or never gets tagged in the group chat?
  • This week’s challenge: do one good thing at home, school or church without being asked — and don’t tell anyone. When you see a need, don’t just report it; help.
  • Memory verse: “I am the light of the world. Anyone who follows me will never walk in the darkness but will have the light of life.” (John 8:12, CSB)

Pray

  • Thank God for Jesus, the Light of the World — that we can trust what he says, because the Father backs him up.
  • Pray for your 3 friends by name — that God would shine the light of Christ on them, and give you one chance this week to point one of them to Jesus.
  • Pray that KOGYouth would shine together as lower lights — that we’d burn bright together and not burn out alone.

Leader’s Notes

Joyfully point to Jesus. Some students may not be Christians yet — this passage is a warm invitation to them: come to the Light, follow him, and have the light of life. Pastor David ended the sermon by asking everyone to close their eyes and picture three friends walking in darkness — and said one of the faces might be your own. If a student is honest about that, be glad, stay with them, and never put anyone on the spot.

Two likely wrong turns

  • “Shining means being an impressive Christian.” No — we don’t have the light of life in ourselves. Like the moon and lanterns, lower lights only shine because of the higher light. We shine by following Jesus and pointing to him.
  • Turning vv.13–18 into a debate club. The history is solid (the sermon quoted John Dickson), but the sermon’s main point is that only God can testify about God. We don’t put Jesus on trial by our standards; we receive the Father’s testimony.

Question helps

Q1 — The light of the world. Promise: never walk in darkness. Gift: the light of life. Both are for those who follow him.

Q2 — Light belongs to God (Psalm 27:1; 119:105). Jesus takes it for himself. “It’s an outrageous thing to say, unless, of course, it’s true.”

Q3 — Look at Jesus. You can’t separate the sun from its sunbeams. See the Son and you see the Father’s glory. He is also “the light of men” (John 1:4), leading people back to God.

Q4 — Wars, tragedies, a divided world. The deeper problem is that we’re spiritually blind — “blind as a bat” compared with God. Jesus is the light who leads through the deepest darkness; where there is light, shadows flee.

Q5 — “You’re testifying about yourself — not valid.” Jesus knows where he came from and where he’s going: from God, back to God. Human standards: no research, what our community says, feelings. The sermon: like blind people trying to describe the sun.

Q6 — The Father who sent him. Only God can testify about God. God exists whether we believe him or not.

Q7 — By knowing Jesus: “If you knew me, you would also know my Father.” There’s no way to God that goes around Jesus.

Q8 — Push for specific and doable. The sermon’s three: be at church on Sundays (“you can’t burn bright by yourself, not for long — you’ll only burn out”); love one another (John 13:34–35); do good works so people glorify the Father. Our church motto begins “See the Gospel”.

Our church vision

This is the “from faith to faith” vision in miniature: 75 households serving densely at St Paul’s (we are at 50), on the way to 500 by 2035. Two live goals this quarter touch this room directly — six more qualified helpers for KOGKids, KOGMini and KOGYouth, and three new mid-week growth groups. A lower light’s whole job is to guide people to the great light, which is exactly what a youth helper does. Ask a Year 11 or 12 student tonight whether that could be them.
