Don’t be faithless but believe

Passage: John 20.24_31

Big Idea: Don’t be faithless but believe the risen Jesus Christ

A disbeliever’s demand

The risen Lord’s verdict

An invitation to read, believe and live.

Today Christians all over the world celebrate the resurrection of Jesus Christ.

We celebrate that someone two thousand years ago had risen from the dead.

Jesus rising from the grave? Do Christians really believe that?

I’ve met some people who would accept most of the teachings of Jesus but not this, not the resurrection.

From a scientific point of view, it’s impossible! It breaks the laws of nature as we know it.

Christianity asks us to believe in something that’s contrary to nature.

Those who don’t believe the resurrection would find it ridiculous that someone could possibly believe that Jesus rose from the grave.

How can it possible be true? Seems ridiculous!

And yet, the resurrection of Jesus Christ is at the heart of Christianity.

If you don’t believe it, your whole belief about the Christian faith crumbles.

And so, is it possible to genuinely believe the resurrection?

Can we really believe the risen Jesus Christ?

In today’s passage, we read about a man who also thought that the idea that Jesus could rise from the dead was ridiculous.

If you have your Bible there, please open up to John 20, beginning from verse 24.

We’ll see three things:

A disbeliever’s demand

The risen Lord’s verdict

An invitation to read, believe and live.

Before we look into it, let’s pray,

“Our heavenly Father, by your Spirit, please help us understand your word today, for Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

A disbeliever’s demand

Our passage begins on sometime after Easter Sunday.

Jesus had just died on Good Friday a few days ago.

Two of the Twelve Disciples went on Sunday morning to find that the tomb was empty.

One of the women, Mary Magdalene, reported to the disciples later that day that she’s seen Jesus and that He’s alive.

On Sunday evening, ten of the Disciples gathered behind locked doors because they were afraid that the authorities would get them, just like they got Jesus.

However, all of a sudden, Jesus stood among them, even though the doors were locked.

Jesus then sent them on a mission to forgive sins.

It was an incredible evening! It was a significant evening, if not the most significant evening, in the life of the disciples!

However, only Ten of the disciples were there.

One of them, Judas Iscariot, who betrayed Jesus, died on Thursday night.

The other was Thomas, who wasn’t there for some unexplained reason.

The other disciples told Thomas, “We’ve seen the Lord!”

They sounded ridiculous to Thomas.

How can Jesus rise from the dead? No way!

And so, Thomas didn’t believe them

A while ago, there was a commercial on TV about Tik Tok.

There were a few people explaining that they’ve seen a hole behind a medicine cabinet cupboard.

Has anyone seen it?

The hole leads to another unit.

The people who heard it were confused; the people explaining it just told them to look at the Tik Tok video.

What Thomas went through was like a Tik Tok commercial.

What the other disciples said was so incredible, so unbelievable, that he would rather see the risen Jesus for himself.

While it’s unbelievable, the idea of Jesus rising from the dead wasn’t new to Thomas.

Thomas would’ve heard Jesus himself said on at least three occasions that he would rise again after three days.

And he knew the rest of the disciples; he knew that they would not be the kind of people who would just make these things up.

Even so, he didn’t believe what Jesus or the disciples said.

Instead, he gave the criteria, the conditions, by which he would believe that what they said about Jesus is true.

Look with me at the second part of verse 25, “But he said to them, “If I don’t see the mark of the nails in his hands, put my finger into the mark of the nails, and put my hand into his side, I will never believe.”

Thomas had seen the scars of Jesus and he wanted to see them, to put his fingers and hands into them before he believes the resurrection.

He wanted to examine the scars of Jesus for himself.

He wanted to come to Jesus on his own terms.

We can be like that too, can’t we?

We want to dictate to God the terms by which we’ll believe in him.

We give him a condition, If this happens, then I’ll believe.

If I get a good mark in my exam, then I’ll believe.

If God gives me a good job, then I’ll believe.

If I don’t get sick with COVID, then I’ll believe.

Like Thomas, we want to be the ones to set the criteria of belief.

If God fulfils these terms, if God passes the test, then I’ll believe.

But are they the right tests?

Do our tests verify whether God is believable?

Are our tests for God valid?

Over the past few years, a young woman by the name of Elizabeth Holmes, rose as a rising star in the biomedical field.

In 2003, at 19 years old, she founded a company called Theranos that promised to revolutionise medical testing.

Rather than using needles, she just needed a drop of blood to test whether you are healthy or not.

Many, many people were backing her revolutionary product; her company was worth $10 billion US dollars at its peak.

However, it was all based on lies.

The results from the Theranos machine were wildly inaccurate, leading to many misdiagnoses of medical conditions.

Elizabeth Holmes was found guilty earlier this year with fraud and conspiracy.

The wrong test can lead to false results.

The same can happen with the tests we apply to God.

If we apply the wrong test to God, we will get the wrong results.

That’s what happened to Thomas.

He thought that he would give the final word on whether Jesus was believable.

Instead, it was Jesus, the risen Lord, who gave the verdict on Thomas.

The risen Lord’s verdict

Look with me at verse 26, “A week later his disciples were indoors again, and Thomas was with them. Even though the doors were locked, Jesus came and stood among them and said, “Peace be with you.”

Next Sunday night, while the disciples were behind locked doors again, Jesus appeared to them.

This time, Thomas was with them and saw the Lord for himself.

It was physically impossible, just like the resurrection was physically impossible, but there he was, Jesus, in the midst of them.

He said to them, “Peace be with you.”

Then he turned his attention towards Thomas.

He told Thomas, “Thomas, put your fingers here. Here are the marks where they nailed me.

Hey, after that, look at my side.

See that hole there? There used to be a chunk of me there.

That’s where they stabbed me.

Go on, put your hand there”

Just a quick note on the side: You can’t hide anything from Jesus.

You can’t speak behind Jesus’ back.

What Thomas thought Jesus wouldn’t hear, Jesus heard word for word.

And here’s Jesus’ verdict on Thomas’ test: it’s faulty.

Thomas’ premises for the test was wrong.

Thomas’ premises for the test was based on what he knew of the world, but Jesus’ resurrection was a glimpse of a new world, a heavenly world

And so, Thomas’ test to believe Jesus was faulty.

A recent phenomenon in healthcare is the increase in people turning to the internet to self-diagnose their health problems and to seek medical advice.

This phenomenon, also known as Dr Google, has led to many people to misdiagnose their condition.

In fact, a recent study analysing 36 international symptom checkers has shown that Dr Google gives the correct diagnosis as the first result only 36% of the time.

That is, if you try to use the internet to diagnose your condition, you are more likely to misdiagnose yourself.

Dr Google, especially when it’s used in the hands of non-trained patients, is prone to be faulty.

Many real Doctors and Health professionals had to correct their patient’s diagnosis when they come in to see them.

That’s what’s happened with Thomas.

Thomas thought he knew how to test whether Jesus has actually risen.

But the risen Jesus, the one who rose from the dead, had to correct Thomas’ faulty diagnosis of Jesus.

However, if we think about it, is it really that ridiculous to believe that Jesus rose from the dead?

If God is God, then he can do anything he wants.

If God had made the universe, which he did, as well as designing the laws that govern his universe, then why would anything be too hard for this God?

If he could come down from heaven and born as a baby, then why not be raised from the dead as well?

If Jesus is who he said he is, namely the one through whom and for whom all things exist, then surely he can rise from the dead.

He makes life, he sustains life, he can come back to life.

Death can not stop the life-giving God.

Jesus then tells Thomas, “Don’t be faithless, but believe.”

After hearing Jesus’ verdict, Thomas finally gets it.

And he utters one of the most powerful confession of Jesus up until this point in the Gospel of John, “My Lord and my God.”

The sceptical Thomas now realises who Jesus is and confesses that Jesus is his Lord and his God.

This is a massive confession from Thomas, a Jew.

To the Jews, there is only one God, the Lord, the one who made the universe, the one that the Jewish people have worshipped for thousands of years.

But now, Thomas calls Jesus God.

Thomas gives Jesus the same honour and the respect as he would the God of the universe.

It’s a profound statement of faith for Thomas.

And yet it’s more than a personal statement; it’s also a universal statement.

Jesus is not just Thomas’ Lord and God; Jesus is our Lord and our God.

To have faith in Jesus is to call him our Lord and our God.

In reply to Thomas’ confession, Jesus says, “Because you have seen me, you have believed. Blessed are those who have not seen and yet believe.”

To be blessed is to be accepted by God.

Jesus is saying those have not seen Jesus and yet believe are in a better position than those who have seen Jesus, like Thomas.

That is, most Christians who have ever lived, were and are and will be in a better position than Thomas because we have not seen and yet believe.

Most Christians are not themselves eye-witnesses; we were never given the opportunity to put our hand into Jesus’ side.

But Jesus says that we’re more blessed than even disciples like Thomas.

Our faith is one that doesn’t depend on seeing.

Our faith is one that depends on trusting Jesus, on his terms.

Jonathan Edwards, the great 18th Century American reformer, said that “faith is the soul’s entirely embracing the revelation of Jesus Christ as our Saviour.”

Faith embraces Jesus.

Faith is surrendering your whole self to Jesus.

Faith is trusting that Jesus has died and rose from the grave, never to die again.

Faith is clinging onto Jesus, throughout this life and trusting that he will lead to us eternal life.

That’s Christian faith.

Jesus calls Thomas to not be faithless but believe.

He also calls us to not be faithless but believe.

You see, the problem is not whether Jesus is believable; He is.

The problem lies with us: will we believe the truth of who he really is?

Will we embrace him?

But how do we embrace him now, when we don’t actually see him?

This takes us to the purpose statements of the Gospel of John.

In these last two verses, there’s an invitation to read, believe and live.

An invitation to read, believe and live

Jesus did many things, performed many miracles that aren’t written in this Gospel.

It’s not an exhaustive list of everything that Jesus did.

However, look with me at verse 31, “But these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name.”

The Apostle John wrote these words down so that you’ll believe.

We can’t see Jesus physically but we can come to know him through his words.

That’s why genuine Christians love the Bible.

We meet Jesus through it.

That’s why we love the Church because when we gather around the Bible, we hear Jesus through it.

Here in the Gospel of John, the apostle breaks the fourth wall.

In plays, television shows and movies, when a character breaks the fourth wall, they acknowledge the existence of the audience,

And they address the audience directly.

Here in the Gospel of John, the author breaks the fourth wall and address you, the reader and you the hearer.

But it’s not just the apostle addressing us.

Jesus himself addresses us; we meet Jesus as we read through the Bible.

We come to believe him as we read through his word.

Jesus actively engages the world as his people live according to this word.

It might seem like a small book but there’s enough of recorded here of what Jesus’ said and done for you to believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name.

Could God have used another method?

Sure, Jesus is God! He can spell out his message across the sky if he wanted to.

But precisely because He’s God, he can use whatever way he chooses.

And he chooses to use his words as recorded in the Bible.

And so, don’t be faithless, but believe.

Read the word of God and be saved.

Embrace the risen Christ.

Millions and millions of Christians have simply read the Word of God and they’re saved.

It works! It’s effective.

Read the Bible, believe, and live.

If you don’t have a Bible, come and see me today.

We have Bibles that we can give you today.

Please take one home and read it.

Let the Word challenge and move you, stimulate and mould you.

As you read God’s word, meet Jesus.

Embrace him, believe him, love him!

And so friends, this Easter, don’t be faithless but believe the risen Jesus Christ.

He’s risen, don’t hold back! Embrace him.

Believe Jesus, our Lord and our God.

There’s nothing more wonderful in this life or the next than to call Jesus our Lord and our God.

Believe Jesus, our Lord and our God.

Here are the three questions for us to ponder on this week:

What are the criteria we have set for ourselves to believe in Jesus?

What will Jesus say about these criteria?

How am I embracing Jesus by faith?

Friends, don’t be faithless but believe.

Believe Jesus, our Lord and our God.
