How do you choose the right gift?

It’s so good to see you all here this morning.

Welcome to Christmas Countdown Sunday!

Our family began our official Christmas countdown a few nights ago, on the first of December.

We began by putting up our Christmas tree.

We were very excited because it’s the first Christmas tree we bought in a very long time.

What goes under Christmas trees? Presents!

Has anyone here finished getting Christmas presents?

Has anyone here started getting Christmas presents?

It can be hard to get the right Christmas present for someone, can’t it?

What does this person need? What does this person want?

Will the person treasure the present? Or will it end up in a junk pile on New Year’s Day?

How do we give good gifts to our friends and families?

When we give gifts, there are three things that need to remember:

It needs to be meaningful

We must think about what the other person needs.

It needs to mean something for the person.

It’s something that the person wants or it’s something that the person needs.

Or even better, it’s both something that the person want and need!

It needs to show our love

Don’t give a gift that costs you nothing to give.

Tomorrow morning around here in Kogarah, there’s a kerbside clean up.

It’s a time when people put their unwanted items, unwanted gifts, rubbish, for the council to pick up.

Don’t take one of those and give it as a gift; it’s a bit gross and a bit unloving.

When you give a gift to someone you love, give the person a gift that costs you something.

It might cost you money to buy it or time to make it.

But you’re willing to pay for it because you love the person.

Hope that it’s a gift that’s treasured

When we give gifts, we hope that the person would keep it and treasure it.

We hope the person appreciates our gift.

Many years ago, Zoe gave me the gift of service vouchers.

Service vouchers have services like: Use this card for an hour of peace and quiet.

And Zoe would promise to be peaceful and quiet for an hour.

I haven’t used any of the vouchers, but I kept them to remember what Zoe promised.

By the way, when I tried to use them, they expired! Still, I kept them because I treasure the gift.

Give a gift that will hopefully be treasured.

How does God choose a gift for us?

How about God? How does God go about choosing a gift for us?

Well, like any good gift giver, God begins with our need.

In fact, God wants to give a gift that addresses our greatest need.

And our greatest need is that we need to be right with him.

We need to be right with him because, left to ourselves, none of us are right with him.

The Bible calls not being right with him sin.

Sin describes all the wrong things that we’ve thought, said and done.

It may be an action that showed hate; it might be a word that was said to hurt.

It might be lying what we think is a white lie.

But they’re wrong according to God; they’re sins.

But it’s more than things we do that are wrong.

It’s also when we don’t do what’s right.

It’s when we don’t do the honest thing when we can; it’s when we don’t help people.

It’s when we don’t love God with all our hearts.

Sin describes all the good things that we don’t do as well.

Now we might think, that’s a bit too much, isn’t it?

What does God expect from us? Can’t he just overlook our sins? Just pretend that they’re not there?

Well, it’s a little like this.

A few weeks ago, this car parked on top of another car in Hurstville Westfield’s.

There’s not getting around it; the person parked badly.

Now imagine it’s your car underneath and the person who was in the wrong say to you, “Yes, it’s my bad, but can’t you just overlook it? Can’t you just pretend it didn’t happen?”

How would you respond?

It would be an outrageous thing for the person to say! Doesn’t he know what he did?

Even so, it is possible for someone to be incredibly generous and say, “Yeah, no worries, mate.”

Now, imagine your whole family was in the car when it happened, and they say the same thing, ““Yes, it’s my bad, but can’t you just overlook it? Can’t you just pretend it didn’t happen?”

It’s not just an outrageous thing to say; it’s a wicked thing to say.

He treats the injury and death of your loved ones as if it’s nothing.

It would be wrong, wicked even, for you to reply, “Yeah, don’t worry about it. It’s all good.”

No, you can’t say it because it’s not good; it’s bad! It’s wrong!

And that’s why God can’t overlook our sins; God can’t turn away from it.

God’s standard is far higher than any of our standards.

He can’t simply turn away from wrong; he can’t just pretend it didn’t happen.

That would be unjust of God!

You don’t want a God who ignores wrong; nobody in their right mind would!

It would be horrible to have an unrighteous God.

We thank God that God is righteous, he loves righteousness and hates wickedness.

But we sin against God, day and night.

God is rightly angry at us, day and night.

We can try not to sin, but we fail time and time again.

And so, here’s our problem: we can’t make ourselves right, no matter how hard we try.

We’re in the wrong and by default, we deserve God’s righteous anger against us.

We need to be made right with God but we can’t do it on our own.

This is where God’s gift comes in.

God gives us a gift that’s meaningful.

God gives us a gift that addresses our greatest need.

The greatest gift that God gives us is the gift of Jesus.

Look with me at Romans 3:25, “God presented him as the propitiation by his blood, through faith, to demonstrate his righteousness, because in his restraint God passed over the sins previously committed.”

In the Bible, Jesus is described as the propitiation.

The word propitiation means giving something to a God that turns his anger away from us.

God is rightly angry at us, but he presented us with Jesus, the propitiation, to take away his anger towards us.

Jesus, the Perfect and Holy One, who had always obeyed God and never sinned, died on the cross, to be the propitiation for those who trust in him.

It’s because Jesus the Perfect One died, spilt his blood, that God’s anger towards our sin and our wrongs can be turned away.

If God just overlook out sins and pretends they are not there, God would be unrighteous.

But now, by the death of Jesus, the price for our wrong has been paid for.

God can rightly look at us and not be angry at us, because of the death of Jesus.

In giving us Jesus, God has addressed our greatest need.

In Jesus, he made us right with him.

There’s nothing more important than being made right with him; it’s our greatest need.

God’s gift of Jesus also shows us his love.

The cost that God gave for his gift is the great price of his Son.

In order to make us right with him, it costs God the death of Jesus, the Sinless One, for only He is good enough to pay for our sins.

Imagine the biggest gift you’re getting this Christmas for someone special in your life.

It might be the latest toy or the prettiest dress.

Whatever it costs you, know that it costs a billion times a billion more to God to give you his one and only Son.

It was costly for God to send his one and only Son to the world tπo die.

But God is willing to give his Son to you, God watched his Son die, because he loves you.

To demonstrate his righteousness and his love for you, it costs God the most precious thing He has, Jesus HIs Son.

God did this to show his righteousness.

God presented Jesus as the propitiation to show that He has dealt with our sins does deal with our sins.

Whenever we sin, God has every right to strike us down.

Every time a nation sins, God has every right to send disasters down to wipe it out.

But God hasn’t punished us according to what we deserved – God was patient with us.

We thank God for his patience with us, by passing over sins that we’ve done previously.

And yet his patience doesn’t mean that he is unjust.

At the right time, God has dealt with our sin and God did punish for our sins.

At the right time, God has punished our sins in Jesus.

And because of what Jesus did, God has declared us righteous.

Look with me at verse 26,

“God presented him to demonstrate his righteousness at the present time, so that he would be just and justify the one who has faith in Jesus.”

God has declared thous who have faith in Jesus as righteous.

In the propitiation of Jesus, in his death on the cross, God has said those who trust in Jesus are made right with him.

This is something that lasts not just for a day, but forever.

This Christmas, I can guarantee you that some of your gifts will break, fade or decay in the next few months.

Some of us might get gifts that lasts a few years.

But a million years from now, everyone who has received God’s gift of Jesus and declared righteous will still enjoy being declared righteous.

It’s a gift that truly lasts forever.

A Christian pastor by the name of Sinclair Ferguson describes it like this “justification is God’s last-day verdict about us that’s been brought forward for us to enjoy this present day.”

On the last day, God will declare who is right and who is wrong.

For those who trust in Jesus, we can enjoy living life, knowing that in Jesus, we’ve already been declared right with God.

In Jesus, God has given us a gift that addresses our greatest need; it is the most meaningful of gifts.

It’s a gift that shows his love for us.

It’s a gift that shows God’s righteousness and through which God declares us righteous.

And so, how do we get it? How do we receive it?

3. Receive God’s gift with an empty hand

We receive God’s gift with an empty hand.

That’s what faith is.

Faith is to receive God’s gift of Jesus with an empty hand.

We can bring nothing to God to exchange for this gift.

We can only open our empty hands to God and receive with gratitude.

Faith can be described with two words: Trust and Treasure.

Those who have faith in Jesus will trust in Jesus.

We will trust what he has done; we will also trust that what he says is good.

Because we trust him, we will follow him and obey him.

We will also treasure him.

Jesus is God’s gift that we treasure above everything else.

We will hold on to Jesus even if everything else falls away.

Those who recognise how precious Jesus is will treasure him.

Those who don’t recognise who Jesus is won’t treasure Jesus, because they haven’t received Jesus as God’s gift.

The way we treasure Jesus, or not, tells us how whether we have truly received God’s most precious gift to us.

For those of us who realise that Jesus is God’s greatest gift to us, Jesus will mean more to us than all the treasures of earth.

Jesus will mean more to us than all the good and precious things we have in our lives.

Indeed, Jesus will mean more to us even than life itself.

For there is nothing greater, nothing more precious than to realise and to know that we’ve been made right in God’s eyes,

not because of what you’ve done, but because of what Jesus has done.

And in response, we open our empty hands and we put your trust in him.

In a while, you’ll see this in action; you will see the Gospel.

I will invite those who believe in Jesus to come and join us in the Holy Communion.

Those who come to the Holy Communion don’t have anything in their hands.

They come with empty hands; but they receive the wine and the bread, which symbolises what Jesus has done for us.

If you believe in Jesus, if you have realised how important he is and want to trust and treasure him, then come, this table is for you.

Come with empty hands, trusting in nothing that you’ve done but only in what Jesus has done.

If you want to find out more about what it means to believe, then please come and see me, or Hien, or Po Ki, or Range afterwards.

And so, as you get your gifts for your loved ones this Christmas, as you choose the right gift for them, remember God’s gift to you.

The greatest need of man requires the greatest gift of God.

And the Gift’s name is Jesus.

God has presented Jesus the propitiation to show his righteousness.

It’s the most meaningful of gifts.

It’s the most loving gift, the costliest of all gifts.

And so, treasure this gift, treasure Jesus above all else.

May you remember the God’s Greatest gift to us this Christmas!

Remember Jesus.

Amen!
