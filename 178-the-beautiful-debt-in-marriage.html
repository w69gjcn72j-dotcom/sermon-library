The Beautiful Debt in Marriage

Passage: 1 Corinthians 7:1-24

Big Idea: As God’s own, fulfil your duty to your spouse in peace, not leaving but sanctifying them and remain with God wherever he’s placed you

Fulfilling my duties to my spouse

Sanctifying an unbelieving spouse

Remaining where God allows

Intro:

Over the past few months, I’ve attended the weddings of two young men that I’ve discipled when they were still in high school.

It was such a joy to see them promised their wives that they will have them to hold them from this day forward, for better for worse, for richer for poorer, in sickness and in health, to love and to cherish, as long as they both shall live.

It’s a solemn promise that they’ve made before God and before their families and friends.

As Christians, we believe that it’s vital to make these promises at weddings because there will come a time in all marriages when these promises will be put to the test.

In every marriage, there will be times when the marriage will be challenged, either from threats from outside of the marriage or tensions from within the marriage.

There will be times when doubt may appear.

Perhaps there will be even times when one of the spouse will ask, “What if I’m attracted to someone else other than my spouse?”

For those who have become a Christian after they married, they might ask, “How do I live with an unbelieving spouse? We’re so different from one another?”

These questions aren’t new.

They’ve been asked by Christians for the past two thousand years.

In today’s passage, we’ll see how the Apostle Paul responds to questions like these.

Today, we’ll be looking at 1 Corinthians 7:1-24.

We’ll see three things:

Fulfilling my duty to my spouse

Sanctifying an unbelieving spouse

Remaining where God allows

I’ve printed a sermon outline with spaces for you to fill in thoughts and questions that might come up throughout the talk.

If you want to ask more questions from the sermon, please join a group of us after the service at my house and we can continue the discussion there.

Let’s pray as we come before God’s words.

“Father, you give wisdom generously and without reserve. Please fill us with your Spirit so that we’ll have wisdom to understand, believe and obey your word today. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

Fulfilling my marital duty to my spouse

Please open your Bible with me to 1 Corinthians 7, beginning from verse 1 (Read out – Peter Li, please type verses 1-2)

The Roman world that the Corinthians lives in was a world full of sexual immorality.

It was a culture saturated with all kinds of sexual practices outside of marriage.

In verse 1, the Chinese translation carries the idea that it’s not good for a man to be near a woman.

But the English translation here is very clear:

It is good for a man not to use a woman for sex.

In the original language, the word in this verse is often translated ‘touch’

And it carries the idea of a man touching a woman for sexual pleasure.

Unlike today’s use, it’s not a mutual term; it’s what a man in power does to others.

In Corinth, it was the culturally accepted for a man to touch women inappropriately who weren’t his wife.

The husband is to have a serious sexual relationship with his wife with the purpose of producing rightful heirs to his property.

However, the husband is permitted, and even expected to find sexual satisfaction in people who are not their wives.

The Corinthians had written to Paul about this and have said that it is good for a man not to use a woman for sex.

And they’re right! It’s not good for a man to use a woman for sex.

This is a massive counter-cultural approach to sexual relationship.

They’re not to have two sets of sexual relationships: the serious one inside a marriage for reproducing children and a casual one outside of marriage for pleasure.

No, they are to have one sexual relationship, the one with their spouse, for both reproducing children and for pleasure.

They are to have a monogamous marriage, loving only one spouse of the opposite gender.

In this loving marriage, they are to have safe, sexual intimacy.

Their marriage is a safe space where they can enjoy each other’s presence.

That’s how God’s designed marriage: it’s a safe space for you to be sexually satisfied and, God willing, to reproduce children.

In the Bible there are only two types of sexual relationships:

One is sex within a marriage, which is good.

The other is sexual immorality outside of marriage, which is not good.

There are many forms of sexual immorality but there is only one alternative to sexual immorality, namely sex within marriage.

And within this good, sexual relationship in marriage, there is duty that the husband and wife owe to one another.

Look with me at verse 3-4 (Read out – Peter Li, please type verses 3-4)

Your wife is married to you; she has rights over your body!

It’s a little like this.

Imagine you got a job working for a boss.

When the time comes for your boss to pay you, he doesn’t give you your wages.

What do you do?

You tell him that it’s not fair; it’s your right.

He owes you your wages; he should be paying you

That’s what it’s like when you’re married.

You have agreed to marry your wife.

She wants to be sexually intimate with you.

It’s her right, you owe it to her!

Look with me again at the second part of verse 4, “A husband does not have the right over his own body, but his wife does.”

The husband’s body belongs to the wife, just as the wife’s body belongs to the husband.

It’s a striking thing for people in the Roman Empire to hear.

It’s still a striking us today in modern Australia to hear.

Your spouse, if you’re married, has authority over your body.

And so, the spouse is not to deprive each other of sexual intimacy.

From the passage, it seems that it’s normal to expect spouses to frequently have sexual intimacy with one another.

Paul says that he can see a possibility where the couple might agree to pause for a little while for prayer.

After you finish praying, you come back together again lest the devil tempts you.

I don’t know how long it takes you to pray, but usually it’s not for a very long time.

And so, the Bible envisages that married couples frequently be sexually intimate in their marriage.

This helps the couple to stay focus on one another.

Rather than letting your eyes wander, you keep your eyes focussed on your spouse when it comes to anything sexual.

The Bible here is not shy to encourage you to talk about sex with your spouse.

There are many places in the Bible that talks about sexual relationships, as we’ve been seeing over the past few weeks.

For those of us who are parents, we should be ready to teach our children about sex when the time is appropriate.

I don’t know about you but growing up, our family never talks about sex.

However, if you don’t talk with your children about it, someone else will, whether it’s their friends or the internet.

And so, when you get the chance, talk about sex with your spouse, talk about it with your children.

Point them back at the what the Bible says; there are plenty of reference to it!

Of course, when it comes to sexual intimacy, it’s more than just talk.

We need to be sexually faithful.

To be sexually faithful is more than just not committing sexual immorality.

It’s about fulfilling your marital duty to your spouse.

You need to do this not just for your spouse’s sake but also for yours, lest you be tempted to sexual immorality.

2. Sanctifying an unbelieving spouse

What about those not in a marriage? Look with me at verses 8-9 (Read out – Peter Li, please type verses 8-9)

It’s good to be single but if they don’t have self-control, they should marry.

We’ll explore more on this next week from verses 25 onwards.

What if things don’t work out as it should?

There will be times in every marriage when you will face a crisis.

There will come a time when your marriage will be tested.

It’s not a matter of if but when.

What happens then?

Look with me at verse 10-11 (Read out – Peter Li, please type verses 10-11)

Don’t leave your husband.

Though not always but there are many cases where people divorce their spouse to marry someone else.

Sadly, I known a few cases; perhaps you do as well.

I’ve seen marriages that looked wonderful at first but crumbled after a few years.

While divorces are sad, when one spouse leaves the family for another, it adds an extra level of sadness.

The Apostle Paul echoes the teaching of the Lord Jesus by saying that a wife should not leave her husband and a husband is not to divorce his wife.

However, if a divorce does happen, the wife should remain unmarried or be reconciled to her husband.

Again, this is a very counter-cultural approach to marriage in the first century.

In the first century, marriage certificates were worded as though they will end in divorce, not death.

It’s as if their promises at their wedding are not “Till death do we part” but “till divorce do we part”.

It’s essentially a ‘no-fault’ divorce where the couple simply separates and part ways.

Of course, it happens in today’s world as well.

In Australia, the only ground for divorce is the irretrievable breakdown of the relationship, demonstrated by 12 months of separation.

Just like the Christians in the first century, Christians in our century are called to be counter-cultural.

We’re called to not follow our culture in its low view of marriage.

We’re to either reconcile with our spouse or we divorce and not remarry.

But what if one of the spouses is an unbeliever?

What if the husband or wife doesn’t agree with what the Bible teaches about marriage and sex?

Look with me at verses 12-14 (Read out – Peter Li, please type verses 12-14)

Incredibly, the Bible tells us that the believing spouse makes their unbelieving spouse holy.

Over the past year, with so much news about COVID, many of us have become armchair experts on the topic of virology and immunology.

We talk about vaccines that uses inactive viruses and mRNA encoding.

We talk about virus transmissions and the need to practice safe hygiene.

It brings back a lot of what I’ve learnt in first year microbiology!

Of course, lately, we’ve learnt that new strands of COVID can transmit differently and we need to be on alert lest we become infected by touching someone who has the virus.

In the Bible, the people of God are said to be made holy by what Jesus has done for us.

We’ve been made holy because Jesus died for our sins on the cross.

But those who have not trusted in Jesus are unholy; they’re still sinful before God.

Over the past few passages, we’ve been talking about how God’s people are to be kept holy and flee from sexual immorality.

But what happens if a Christian husband is married to an unbelieving wife or a Christian wife is married to an unbelieving husband?

In the sexually immoral society that is Corinth in the first century, it’s quite normal to expect an unbelieving husband to not have the same Christian sexual ethics as a believing wife.

It would almost be expected that the unbelieving husband would go to prostitutes, along with many other men in the city of Corinth.

How does a Christian spouse, especially the Christian wife, keep herself holy then?

How does she keep herself spiritually, ethically and sexually pure for God?

Rather than getting contaminated, made unholy, by the husband, the Christian wife makes the husband holy.

Yes, it’s a mismatched marriage; yes, there will be conflicts in many areas.

But though it’s mismatched, it’s a marriage that God recognises as holy.

God’s grace is greater than what the unbeliever can bring to the marriage.

Jesus himself brings healing when he touches the sick, the unclean.

Jesus had a contagious holiness.

While not exactly the same, the believer brings a contagious holiness to the family.

This doesn’t mean that the unbelieving husband or wife is saved.

He or she still needs to come to know Jesus.

And, in many instances, it’s the believe who forms the bridge between the unbelieving spouse and Jesus.

The believer connects the unbelieving husband or wife to the Christian community.

The unbeliever comes under the influence of their believing spouse’s holiness in Christ.

Again, this is very counter cultural.

Culturally, it’s the woman who comes under the influence of the man.

But in a mismatched marriage, it’s possible for the man to come under the influence of the woman in the eyes of God.

When the unbelieving husband sees the wife’s witness of Jesus, when he hears the prayers of his wife, he comes under her holy influence

And it places him closer to Jesus than to have a wife who doesn’t know Jesus.

Likewise, children are made holy by the influence of a believing parent, even if the other parent does not believe yet.

The children are closer to knowing Jesus and be saved if one of the parents is a believer.

It would be even better for the children if both parents believe, which, praise God, happens sometimes when a believing spouse leads the unbelieving spouse to Christ.

Even so, the Bible recognises that there will be times when the unbeliever chooses to leave.

(Read out – Peter Li, please type verse 15a)

When the unbelieving spouse chooses to leave, the believing spouse is to let him or her leave.

When that happens, they are no longer bound but free.

However, the passage ends in hope.

Look with me from the second part of verse 15 (Read out – Peter Li, please type verses 15b-16)

God has called you to live in peace with your spouse.

Do your best to make peace with them; who knows? You might save them.

A young couple by the name of Lee and Leslie lived a fairy-tale life.

They lived in an exciting and upscale neighbourhood.

They were climbing up the corporate ladder.

They had a strong and secure marriage and they loved each other.

But then something happened.

Listen to the words of Lee the husband,

“Then unexpectedly, someone came between us. It wasn’t an affair. It wasn’t the resurfacing of an old flame. That someone was none other than God himself.

Leslie announced one day that, after a long period of searching, she had decided to become a follower of Jesus Christ. To me, this was awful news!”

As a result, they started to have more arguments.

Lee was slamming doors and shouting.

Leslie had to read her Bible when Lee wasn’t watching.

Lee admitted that he was jealous of Jesus, because he’s providing for Leslie in a way that he couldn’t.

Though Lee was an atheist, she refused to define him as one.

Instead, she tried to see him as a human being, a wayward son whom God longed to connect with.

In time, seeing the change in Leslie, Lee wanted to find out more about Jesus for himself.

After doing his own research, he, too became a Christian.

In fact, Lee Strobel became an international Christian writer, whose writing had helped tens of thousands of people to come to know the Lord Jesus.

I know that there are many of us who have spouse who aren’t Christians.

If that’s you, be a sanctifying presence in your home.

Don’t hide your witness.

Whenever you get the chance, lovingly and wisely, share the love of Jesus Christ.

Let your spouse see the Gospel and hear the Gospel in your actions and in your words.

Pray for your spouse, openly, in front of your children or others in your household.

Love your spouse with the love of Christ.

And so, fulfil your duty to your spouse, sanctify your unbelieving spouse.

And remain where God allows

3. Remain where God allows

Sometimes, when we look at our lives and our marriages and we see how hard it is,

we might be tempted to look to others and envy what they have.

We might start thinking, if only I have a better upbringing, or if only I have climbed higher on the social ladder or maybe if I have a better husband or a better wife,

Then my life would be better.

As the saying goes, the grass always looks greener on the other side.

However, the Bible tells us not to be envious of others but to live the life that God has assigned for us.

Look with me at verse 17, (Read out – Peter Li, please type verse 17)

God has called us where we are and we’re to obey him where we are.

If God wants us to be somewhere else, he’ll let us know.

But until then, stay where you are in your life situation and serve him where he’s placed you.

As the saying goes, “Bloom where you’re planted”.

The Apostle Paul says those who are circumcised should not seek to get uncircumcised

And those who aren’t circumcised should not see to get circumcised.

Look with me at verse 19 (Read out – Peter Li, please type verse 19)

A person’s ethnic identity, even one as impressive as being a Jew, God’s chosen people in the Old Testament, does not matter.

Being a Chinese doesn’t matter, being an Australian doesn’t matter.

Being a Chinese-Australian doesn’t matter.

What matters is whether you’re obedient to God’s command.

To put it in the passage’s context, being married doesn’t matter, being single doesn’t matter.

What matters is whether you’re obedient to God’s command, especially when it comes to sex and marriage.

What about slaves?

The slaves in ancient Corinth didn’t have the freedom to choose who to marry.

They were their Master’s property.

Shouldn’t they want to change and become free?

How can they be holy and sexually pure when they are at the disposal of their Master and that they are to follow their Master’s wishes.

Paul tells them to not that concern them.

If they can become free, they should take the opportunity to be free.

But Paul wants them to know that their salvation is not dependent on their freedom.

They can still serve God, even as a slave.

In fact, because of Jesus, they are called by God to be the Lord’s freedman.

In Christ, they are freed from sin and they are called to serve Jesus instead.

As a slave, their identity lies not in who they are but whose they are.

Yes, they may be slaves of human masters but their real Master is God himself.

They belong to Christ; they are his slaves, just like all other believers, whether they are slave or free.

They were bought at a price; they belong to the Lord.

And so, they are to remain in the Lord, even as they serve as slaves.

There may not be any slaves here at Church but God has called us in our different situations.

God might have called some of us while we’re students.

God might have called some of us when we became adults.

Still God might have called some of us when we’re already married.

Whatever situation God has called us in, there serve him

Remember whose you are.

You are Christ’s and Christ is yours.

Remain where he’s placed you until he calls you elsewhere.

As God’s own, as someone who belongs to God, if you are married, fulfil your duty to your spouse in peace, not leaving but sanctifying them.

As God’s own, as someone who belongs to Jesus remain with God wherever he’s placed you.

What does this mean for us?

Honour God by honouring your marriage.

Your honour is a debt you owe it to your spouse.

One person who has demonstrated his honour to his wife was Robertson McQuilkin, former president of Columbia Bible College and Seminary.

Robertson met his wife Muriel when they were in college and they were married for 55 years.

In time, he became the president of the College.

However, about 42 years into their marriage, Muriel developed Alzheimer’s and was eventually terrified to be without Robertson.

Instead of sending her to an institution, Robertson decided to retire eight years early in order to take care of Muriel.

Here’s part of his retirement speech:

Muriel now, in the last couple of months, seems to be almost happy when with me, and almost never happy when not with me. In fact, she seems to feel trapped, becomes very fearful, sometimes almost terror, and when she can’t get to me, there can be anger, she’s in distress.

But when I’m with her she’s happy and contented, and so I must be with her at all times…and you see, it’s not only that I promised in sickeness and in health, till death do us part, and I’m a man of my word.

But as I have said, (I don’t know with this group, but I’ve said publicly) it’s the only fair thing.

She sacrificed for me for forty years, to make my life possible…

So if I cared for her for forty years, I’d still be in debt.

However, there’s much more…it’s not that I have to. It’s that I get to.

If you’re married, you’re in your spouse’s debt.

You owe it to her to love her.

Love her and think about what they would like you to do for them.

Honour her and keep an eye on her always.

Sanctify her by praying for her and bringing her to Jesus.

And wives, you do the same for your husbands.

Honour God in your marriage by honouring your marriage.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How would I like to be married to me? (This is a question that Lee Strobel asked himself regularly)

What’s one small step I can take to honour my spouse, especially when it comes to sexual issues?

If I have a spouse or a family member who’s not a Christian, what can I do to be a holy influence on them this week?

Friends, honour God by honouring your marriage.

As someone who belongs to Jesus remain with God wherever he’s placed you.

Please use us to sanctify our homes. Father, we pray especially for those of us who have spouses who aren’t Christians yet. Please be merciful and save them. Please use us as you will. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
