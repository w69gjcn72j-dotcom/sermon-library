Sir we want to see Jesus

Passage: John 12.20_36

Big Idea: Be drawn to the Son, believe and serve him, for his hour of glory, when he both glorifies the Father and judges Satan, has come

Trigger for the hour of Glory

The hour brings both Glory and Judgement

Be drawn to, believe and serve the Glorified Son

Things are getting more expensive now, aren’t they?

I was looking at the petrol prices just down the road from our Church and I just groaned.

It’s so expensive!

And it’s not just petrol, the price of food is going up, the cost of energy has gone up, the cost of rent has gone up, the cost or mortgage has gone up.

In fact, a few days ago, the Menzies Research Centre has done a poll and found that they number one issue on people’s minds is the cost of living.

The cost of living is getting more expensive!

Many of us will make adjustments to make ends meet.

It won’t be easy, in fact, in some cases, it will be very difficult.

But we’ll do it because it’s all part of life.

It might not be a big issue for those of us who have parents or other responsible adults to look after us.

But as we grow, as we take on responsibilities, we need to make sure that we can pay our bills, keep up with the cost of living so that we and those under our care, will, well, live.

As Christians, we live a Christian life.

And there’s a cost of living that comes with living the Christian life.

It’s a cost that has caused many people great anxiety.

What’s the cost of living the Christian life?

Today, we’re going to look at the cost of living the Christian life and how it made Jesus anxious.

Yes, you heard it right, Jesus was anxious about the cost of living the Christian life.

How will Jesus respond to the anxiety caused by the cost of living the Christian life?

If you have your Bible with you, please open up to John 12, beginning from verse 20.

We’re going to see three things:

Trigger for the hour of Glory

The hour brings both Glory and Judgement

Be drawn to, believe and serve the Glorified Son

Before we do, let’s pray,

“Father, please calm our hearts as we come before you. Please make us pay attention all the more to what we have heard, so that we will not drift away. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

Trigger for the hour of Glory

Firstly, we see the trigger for the hour of glory.

John 12:20, “Now some Greeks were among those who went up to worship at the festival.”

Up until this point of the Gospel of John, we’ve seen a lot of Jews coming up to Jerusalem in order to celebrate the Passover.

But now, we also see some Greeks, people who weren’t Jews, coming to celebrate the Passover as well.

They came to Philip and asked, “Sir, we want to see Jesus.”

Philip then went to Andrew.

The two of them then went and told Jesus about it.

Andrew was the one who brought his brother, Simon Peter, to Jesus.

Now, together with Philip, he brought the Greeks to see Jesus.

Look at Jesus’ response in verse 23, “Jesus replied to them, “The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified.”

For many chapters in the Gospel of John, we’ve been told that Jesus’ time has not come yet.

But now, Jesus says that the hour has come.

This is what the Gospel of John has been building up to.

The hour has come for Jesus, the Son of Man, to be glorified!

It’s finally here! But what is it? What is this hour of the Son of Man?

Jesus then uses a mini parable to describe what this hour is about.

Verse 24, “Truly I tell you, unless a grain of wheat falls to the ground and dies, it remains by itself. But if it dies, it produces much fruit.”

I have a grain of seed here in my hand.

Can I have one of the KOG Junior children come to the front and tell us about it?

It is hard, it’s by itself, it doesn’t do much.

Jesus says that unless a grain of wheat falls to the ground and dies, it remain by itself.

If it doesn’t fall to the ground and die, that’s all it’s going to remain, a seed.

But if it dies, it produces much fruit. (Video of seed growing)

This is what Jesus’ hour of glory is like: Jesus’ hour of glory is his hour of death.

This event that we read here would’ve happened just a few days before Jesus was crucified on the cross.

Jesus’ death coming, and yet at the same time, it will be his hour of glory.

By his death, Jesus will bring life to many.

His death on the cross and his glory go hand in hand; you can’t have one without the other.

Yes, Jesus will rise from the dead and ascend into heaven.

And yes, the resurrection and the ascension are glorious.

But here, Jesus focuses particularly on his death; he doesn’t talk about his resurrection here.

When Jesus talks of his hour of glory, he’s thinking specifically about his sacrificial death on the cross.

To see his glory is to see his death.

Jesus takes this and applies it to his followers too.

The Greeks wanted to see Jesus, but to see him as he truly is, they, too, must also be ready to die.

If you want to meet Jesus, if you want to be where Jesus is, then like him, and like the seed, you must be ready to lose your life as you know it.

Look with me at verses 25-26, “25 The one who loves his life will lose it, and the one who hates his life in this world will keep it for eternal life. 26 If anyone serves me, he must follow me. Where I am, there my servant also will be. If anyone serves me, the Father will honour him.”

The person who loves his life will lose it.

What does that mean?

Life is a good thing; life is created by God and a gift from him to us.

However, as good as life is, left to ourselves, we would choose life above God.

We treasure our life, what we gain from it, what we enjoy from it, above God himself.

When we do that, when we choose our life above God, we made our life to be an idol.

We idolise our life, prioritising our life above God.

We treasure the gift of life above the life giver.

When we do that, we will lose our life because we’ve rejected the Life giver.

However, the one who hates his life will find it.

That doesn’t mean the person hates living and doesn’t want to live.

It’s not an absolute hate but a relative hate.

To hate life in this world is to put it as second priority.

What’s the first priority?

It’s serving and following Jesus first and living life second.

Those who hate this life, those who serve and follow Jesus first, will have eternal life.

Those who follow and serve Jesus will be where Jesus is.

Here, Jesus is going to death on the cross.

For those who follow and serve Jesus, they will follow Jesus to the cross.

Jesus has said this in Luke 9:23, “If anyone wants to follow after me, let him deny himself, take up his cross daily, and follow me.”

To follow Jesus is to deny ourselves, taking up the cross, dying to this world, daily, and follow him.

It means not wanting to do the things that please you as much as you.

Rather, you want to do the things that please Jesus first.

If you are driven only by what pleases you, what your life wants, you will die.

If you are driven by what Jesus wants, you will die to the world and live eternally, you will live forever.

Love your life and you will lose it; hate life and follow Jesus and you’ll find it.

A while ago, I spoke with someone from a Chinese Church.

It’s a large Church and they love sharing the gospel with the Chinese.

However, when some of the younger ones wanted to invite their non Chinese friends to come, the leaders of the Church got upset.

They didn’t want to welcome anyone else in the Church who wasn’t Chinese.

In fact, they told the younger ones not to bring their non Chinese friends to Church.

While it is good to love your own culture and love people from the same background as you, in this case, their love of cultural identity became top priority.

These people were in grave danger of idolising their themselves above Jesus.

They weren’t ready to serve Jesus and follow his command to go into all the world.

May our Lord help us, lest we love life in this world more than him and lose life as a result.

May our Lord help us to hate our life in this world and serve and follow Jesus instead.

It won’t be easy; even our Lord Jesus finds it incredibly difficult as he faces his hour, the hour that brings both Glory and Judgement.

The hour brings both Glory and Judgement

Look with me at verse 27, “Now my soul is troubled. What should I say—Father, save me from this hour? But that is why I came to this hour.”

Here, we get a glimpse of Jesus’ heart.

His soul is troubled; that is, he has anxiety.

It’s a comfort to know that Jesus knows all about anxiety; he’s experienced it!

Jesus is anxious because he knows he’s about to face death.

He is anxious and troubled; he’s dreading his death on the cross.

While there is the physical suffering that comes from dying on the cross, Jesus is far more concerned about the spiritual suffering that he will face.

He’s about to face the white-hot anger of God against your sin and my sin.

He’s about to face God’s anger against man’s evil, whether it’s toward him or towards each other, whether it’s public or private.

God knows them all and he’s about to direct his anger towards our sin at Jesus.

It’s more dreadful than anything we could possibly imagine.

How does Jesus face it?

He prayed, “Father, save me from this hour?”

Jesus doesn’t want to face the hour of glory; he doesn’t want to face the Father’s anger.

And yet, this is why he had come.

Jesus had come to earth, Jesus has taken up a mortal, frail body, in order to take the judgement of God on behalf of all those who would believe him.

Jesus chose God’s glory above his own.

Jesus chose God’s glory over his life.

And so he has a second prayer, “Father, glorify your name.”

Jesus’ whole ministry on earth is about bring glory to God’s name.

He taught his disciples to pray, “hallowed be your name”.

We pray that God’s name be treated as holy, that his name may be honoured.

We pray that the Father will glorify his name.

God’s glory is what really matters to Jesus.

And so, Jesus chose to obey God.

Jesus chose to obey God, despite his anxiety for God is glorified by Jesus’ obedience.

And so Jesus prays, “Father, glorify your name”.

That should be our prayer too, shouldn’t it?

As followers of Jesus, we long to see the name of the Father glorified because Jesus himself longs to see the name of the Father glorified.

Like Jesus, despite our anxiety, we need to pray, “Father, glorify your name.”

And God answered Jesus, “I have glorified it, and I will glorify it again.”

God answered Jesus’ prayer to glorify the Father’s name.

God will glorify himself through the obedient, sacrificial death of Jesus.

God’s name will be honoured through Jesus.

This obedience of Jesus to the Father has massive ramifications.

Look with me at verse 31, “31 Now is the judgment of this world. Now the ruler of this world will be cast out.”

The World will be judged, now.

The world might think it’s judging Jesus; they judge Jesus according to his relevance and whether he is useful to it.

However, in truth, it’s Jesus’ death that judges the world.

God sent Jesus, his one and only Son into the world.

In rejecting Jesus, by nailing him to the cross, the world has rejected God himself.

The world has rejected the life giver, what can you expect but death?

And so, by his death on the cross, Jesus has judged the world.

As well as the world, Satan is also judged and will be cast out.

Satan, the ruler of this world, is the great accuser and deceiver.

He rules the world by leading it away from God.

He lies us by saying that sin is not that bad, that sin is not a big deal.

Then when we do sin, he lies again by saying that there is no hope for people like us and that we are too bad for God to forgive us.

It’s a double lie about sin and accuses us as sinners before God.

And there is a sense that he’s partly right; as sinners, we don’t deserve God’s forgiveness and God would be unjust to turn a blind eye on our sins.

However, the accuser is thrown out because Jesus has come to forgive sins.

Those who have truly repented and turn back to him need not be anxious.

Jesus has obeyed God in his hour of glory, died for their sins.

Those of us who believe him will free from judgement of sin.

We will not be judged by God; instead, the devil will be the judged, along with those who have not repented and so remain unforgiven.

This is the great confidence that Christians have.

The song writer Horatio Bonar puts it well:

“Upon a Life I have not lived,  Upon a Death I did not die,  Another’s Life; Another’s Death,  I stake my whole eternity.”

It’s upon the life and death of Jesus that Christians have staked our whole eternity.

Because Jesus has trusted God and glorified God’s name in his hour of trial, we can be confident that we will have eternal life, everlasting life in him.

The path to trusting God in trials is hard.

Even Jesus found it hard! Even Jesus had anxiety thinking about it!

But we thank God that he overcame.

And he can help those who follow him to overcome.

When you’re tempted to give in to your anxiety and follow the world instead, don’t give in.

Don’t give in to the devil’s lies.

Now is the judgement of the world; now is the ruler of this world cast out.

Don’t follow their ways any longer.

Be drawn to, believe and serve the Glorified Son

What are we to do instead? Be drawn to, believe and serve the glorified Son!

Look with me at verse 32, “32 As for me, if I am lifted up from the earth I will draw all people to myself.”

The cross draws all people to the Jesus.

That doesn’t mean that everyone will be saved.

However, it does mean that people from all nations, whether they’re Jews or Greeks will be saved if they trust in Jesus

It’s not just those who have come to look at Jesus and keeps on loving their lives in this world, as if nothing has happened.

Rather, those who are saved are the true believers, who hate their life in this world, who won’t idolised their life.

Instead, they serve Jesus and the follow Jesus.

They will be drawn to Jesus, even when it is difficult.

They will trust Jesus even when they’re afraid.

They will follow Jesus even when they’re anxious.

And they won’t delay in following Jesus.

Look with me at verse 35-36, ““The light will be with you only a little longer. Walk while you have the light so that darkness doesn’t overtake you. The one who walks in darkness doesn’t know where he’s going. 36 While you have the light, believe in the light so that you may become children of light.” Jesus said this, then went away and hid from them.

Jesus would be with the original hearers a little longer.

His light among them will only be available for a little longer.

Soon he will be physically taken from them.

Now’s the time to walk in the light.

Don’t wait any longer.

By default, darkness will try to overtake you.

When you’re in the dark, you don’t know where you’re going.

You have no hope.

Or worse, you hope in the wrong things.

It’s like a man trapped in the caves without light.

He might try to find a way out, but without light, it’s impossible.

Without Jesus, we can only grope in darkness.

We can try to find it in enjoyment, in wealth, and in power.

But these won’t lead us out of darkness.

If anything, loving and enjoying them will keep them from looking for the real way out.

Jesus is the Light. He’s the only light.

While he’s here, believe in him.

He’s the only way out.

Only he can take us safely from darkness to light.

But don’t delay.

Today is the day to believe in him.

I’ve met new Christians and old Christians, young Christians and old Christians, as well as Christian ministers, who have struggled with anxiety.

They might be anxious about the things of this life and they might be anxious about the things of God.

However, rather than letting anxiety overwhelm them, they turn to God.

In fact, when they realise how helpless we are without God, we desperately run toward God.

By faith we run toward God, in our time of anxiety, in our time of darkness, because Jesus is the Light.

He is the Light of the world.

He is our Light.

He’s also the Man of Sorrows; he knows our suffering better than anyone.

He knows our personal suffering, better than anyone.

And he knows the way out.

He is the Way out.

Friends, if you are anxious, if you are worried, go to Jesus, believe in him.

He is the Light.

What is the cost of living the Christian life?

It’s the highest cost possible.

It costs God the Father the life of his one and only Son, Jesus Christ.

There is no cost in the world, in all of cosmos, that’s greater than the life of Jesus Christ.

God has paid the high cost of the Christian life for you.

To receive it, we must forfeit our life and live for God’s glory.

We must hate our life in this world, deny ourselves and live for Jesus.

The hour of Glory has come for the Son of Man.

Jesus chose the glory of the Father above himself.

Will you choose the glory of the Father above yourself?

Friends, be drawn to the Son, believe and serve him.

His hour of glory, has come.

If you are a follower of Jesus, follow him and pray, “Father, glorify your name”

In your hour of trial, when you’re anxious and afraid, pray for the Father’s name to be glorified.

For some of us, we might not want to serve Jesus because we are afraid.

Friends, don’t be afraid of the coming trials; instead pray that the Father’s name be glorified.

Serve and follow Jesus, that’s how the Father’s name is glorified.

Pray that his name will be glorified in your life.

And don’t idolise your life in this world.

Don’t prioritise your life above God. You will lose your life.

You won’t have eternal life.

Instead, follow and serve Jesus, even if it means losing your life.

For the one who loves his life will lose it, and the one who hates his life in this world will keep it for eternal life.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What am I loving more than God?

What do I pray when I’m anxious?

How am I following and serving Jesus, the Light?

Follow Jesus and pray, “Father, glorify your name.”

Let’s pray, “Father, indeed, please glorify your name. Forgive us when we choose our life, our wants, even our needs above your glory. Please open our eyes, so that we can see your glory. Please open our eyes and draw us to the cross of Jesus. Please keep us in the hour of trial, that we may choose your glory always. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
