How to judge righteously

Passage: John 7.1_24

Big Idea: Don’t judge Jesus as the world does, but as someone who wants to do God’s will

Not giving the world what it wants

Wanting to do what the Father wants

Judge righteously, according to the Father’s will in Jesus

Earlier this week, the results of our 2021 census came out.

The result gives us a snapshot of Australia looks like.

Here are a few interesting things about Australia in 2021:

1) 37% of people living in Australia weren’t born here.

2) 12% of people have one parent who wasn’t born in Australia

3) India has taken over China as the second most common country of birth, after England.

4) The religious beliefs of Australia is become more diverse. With 43.9% saying that they’re Christians, 38.9% say they have a secular belief and 3.2% identifying themselves as Muslims.

Over the past few decades, our nation’s religious belief has changed enormously.

Whereas a large proportion of our people may say that they believed in Jesus two decades ago,

Now over a third will say that they don’t believe in Jesus at all.

How does our world judge who Jesus is?

On what basis do they make their judgement?

But even more importantly, for those of us here today, what standard am I using to judge Jesus?

How do I know whether what Jesus says is true?

That’s what we’re going to look at in today’s passage.

In today’s passage, we’ll see three things:

Not giving the world what it wants

Wanting to do what the Father wants

Judge righteously, according to the Father’s will in Jesus

Before we start, let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for your word. As we read your word, please help us to make wise choices, please help us to judge righteously. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

Not giving the world what it wants

How do we make sure we judge righteously?

Firstly, we don’t give the world what it wants.

Please open up your Bible with me to John 7, beginning from verse 1.

Jesus has been travelling in Galilee, in the north and avoiding Judea, down in the south.

The reason was because the Jews in Judea were trying to kill him.

Judea was a dangerous place to be!

At the beginning of our passage, the author the Apostle John tells us that the time of the Jewish Festival of Shelters was near.

Some of us who were here a last year might remember that we talked about the Festival of Shelters or the Festival of the Booths in Nehemiah.

The festival ran for seven days, from 15–21 Tishri, the Jewish lunar month, which was between our September and October.

In Nehemiah’s time, the Festival of Shelters included a time of reading from God’s word.

These festivals were wonderful times of celebration!

The closest modern Christian equivalent that I can think of are the Christian conferences that Christians attend.

Two weeks ago, Jenny and some of the sisters joined one online.

We hope the sisters will be attend one in person together sometime soon.

Later today, you’ll hear more about other Christian conferences for different members of our Church.

I’m hoping that we can organise a conference for our Church one day.

It would be like the Festival of the Shelters.

We would gather away from our homes for a little while to come together to share in God’s word.

The Festival of the Shelters was a big event back in Jesus’ days.

Many people would go to the temple in Jerusalem and live in temporary shelters there and to go to the temple.

Jesus’ brothers suggested that Jesus should go there as well!

He can do his thing there.

Listen to verses 3-4, “Leave here and go to Judea so that your disciples can see your works that you are doing. For no one does anything in secret while he’s seeking public recognition. If you do these things, show yourself to the world.”

They’re basically saying, “Jesus, do your thing there; do your miracles.

Make some noise! That’s how you become popular!”

That’s exactly what some people do in different parts of the world.

For example, in America, there used to be a very famous minister called Jim Bakker.

At the heights of his career, his show was broadcast to millions of people around the world and his theme park attracted six million visitors a year, second only to Disneyland and Disneyworld theme parks.

Jesus’ brothers would’ve pointed Jim Bakker to Jesus and said, “See, that’s how you do it! That’s how you grow big!”

Of course, Jesus’ brothers would eventually be disappointed, because Jim Bakker turned out to be a cheat who misused the funds given to him.

Jesus’ own brothers, thought they advised Jesus on what it meant to be successful, did not believe in Jesus themselves.

They wanted him to succeed by being popular.

They thought that being popular was what success looks like for Jesus.

But that wasn’t what Jesus wanted.

He told them in verse 6, ““My time has not yet arrived, but your time is always at hand.”

Jesus was following God’s timetable strictly.

He wasn’t afraid to go to Judea; he would go only when God tells him to go.

Jesus was very aware of how he was to use his time.

What Jesus chose to do his time was of high significance; his time was precious because his time belonged to God.

He was to use his time strictly to do God’s will.

On the other hand, Jesus’ brothers’ time wasn’t as significant.

They could go any time they wanted; their choices would be of little consequences.

Why were their time of such little consequence?

Verse 7, “The world cannot hate you, but it does hate me because I testify about it—that its works are evil. Go up to the festival yourselves. I’m not going up to this festival, because my time has not yet fully come.”

Jesus’ brothers’ time was of little significance because of their relationship with the world.

They didn’t believe in Jesus and so they still belonged to the world.

They were part of the world, they belonged to the world.

The world doesn’t hate its own; the world doesn’t hate unbelievers like Jesus’ brothers.

Jesus’ brothers did what they wanted in their own time and the world was fine with that.

That’s what the world wanted them to do.

But the world hates Jesus because Jesus did what God wanted to do according to God’s timing.

From God’s point of view, the world is evil.

And so, the world hates it when Jesus speaks on behalf of God against the world.

The world hates it when it’s exposed to God’s teaching.

And that’s why the world hate Jesus; Jesus keeps teaching, keeps proclaiming the evil of the world.

And so, if Jesus is going to keep testifying that the world is evil, the world is not going to like it.

They will hate Jesus for it and Jesus will not be popular.

It can be very tempting to be popular, can’t it?

We want to grow, we want to be influential, we want people to take notice of us.

We want to be an attractive Church, full of programmes to attract the world’s approval.

If we do these things, we might become popular, but we might become less like the Church that Jesus wants us to be.

If our main aim is to be popular, we might be more like a show than a Church.

We want to grow, but we don’t want to be popular.

We want 100 families coming to our Church, not merely as spectators but as people serving Christ together in the Church.

We don’t want to be an entertainment to the world; we want to be a testimony of God to the world.

However, to be a testimony of God to the world will attract hatred from the world.

The world will hate the Church of Jesus because it hated Jesus first.

But it’s only as we point out the evil of the world that we can also point to the salvation and love that’s found in Jesus Christ.

As we point to the judgment of God, we also point to the salvation of God in Christ Jesus.

And so, the Church is not a show but a place for us to grow.

When you come to Church, always come with an expectation to be challenged by the word of Jesus.

Is there an evil in me that Jesus is addressing?

Is there an evil in my community, in my world, that Jesus is speaking against?

And so, Jesus doesn’t give his brothers what they want because that’s what the world wants.

Jesus doesn’t want to be someone who’s popular in the world.

Neither should his Church.

Wanting to do what the Father wants

Instead, we should be a people wanting to do what the Father wants.

Eventually, under God’s timing, Jesus secretly went up to Jerusalem.

The Jews were looking for Jesus and the crowd was confused about who Jesus was.

After the festival has passed its half way mark, Jesus appeared, teaching in the temple.

When they heard Jesus’ teaching, the Jews were amazed.

They asked, “How is this man so learned, since he hasn’t been trained?”

Jesus didn’t get any formal training; how could he have taught so well?

In reply Jesus said this to them, “My teaching isn’t mine but is from the one who sent me.”

In other words, Jesus didn’t make up what he taught.

It was from the Father.

It was a humble statement that was also full of authority.

Jesus didn’t claim to be the origin of his teaching but did claim that his teaching was from God the Father himself.

But how can you know?

How can you know for sure that Jesus’ teaching was from God himself?

Listen to Jesus’ reply in verse 17, “If anyone wants to do his will, he will know whether the teaching is from God or whether I am speaking on my own.”

At school, we usually learn things one of two ways:

We learn things objectively, that is, we learn through scientific facts, historical facts.

Or we learn subjectively, that is, we learn to appreciate beauty, we learn to capture and convey emotions.

Often it’s a combination of both: we enjoy technical but beautiful pieces of music.

We design practical but aesthetically-pleasing buildings.

However, to know whether Jesus is truly from God or not, we need this:

We need a desire, a want, to do God’s will.

Before we come to a correct understanding of who Jesus is, we must have a desire to do God’s will.

That of course, means that you must believe that there is a God first.

More than that, this God is one whom you want to please.

That is, you can’t know for certain who Jesus is, unless you want to do God’s will.

If you don’t want to do God’s will, you will be like Jesus’ brothers or the Jewish leaders.

You might know of Jesus, but you won’t be able to know whether he’s from God or not.

On the other hand, if you want to please God, and you’re willing to be led by God, you will come to know who Jesus truly is.

Hebrews 11:6 tells us, “Now without faith it is impossible to please God, since the one who draws near to him must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him.”

You need faith to please God; you need to believe in him to please him.

You need faith in God in order to know who Jesus is.

It’s neither merely subjective nor objective knowledge.

Rather to know God and to know Jesus is to have relational, indeed, moral knowledge.

You need to have a desire to live in a way that pleases God; it’s a moral decision.

If you don’t want to please God, you’ll never come to know who Jesus really is.

But if you come with a heart willing to please God, you will know that he’s the one who seeks not his own glory but the glory of the one who sent him.

Those who want to do God’s will, who wants to please God, will know who Jesus is.

It’s good to see everyone here today.

I’m glad that you’ve made it.

However, the question we must ask ourselves is this:

Is God pleased to see us here?

Are we doing his will? Are we even wanting to do his will?

So often, we might assume that we’re pleasing God without thinking it through.

In the Book of Revelation, the rich church of Laodicea was described as a rich church, most probably a prosperous Church.

From the outside, it looks as though God was blessing the Church; it looks as if God is pleased with the Church.

The Church certainly looks good to the world!

But the Church was disgusting to God.

So disgusting, in fact, that it’s described to be like vomit that Jesus is ready to spit out.

The Church looked good on the outside and probably assumed that it pleased God but was in fact disgusting to God.

The Church needed to repent and turn back to God.

That’s the Church in Laodicea.

How about us?

Are we a Church that wants to please God?

Do we, as individuals, want to please God?

For it’s only as we want to please God that we can come to a true understanding of who Jesus is.

And in order to please God, we must go to Jesus.

That’s God’s will for us; God wants us to focus on Jesus.

When we put our focus on Jesus, God is pleased.

And what’s Jesus’ focus? Back on God himself!

Jesus focus is on God himself, seeking God’s glory.

God the Father want us to focus on God the Son; God the Son points us back to God the Father.

They have a mutually loving fellowship and they want to invite us in.

Both God the Father and God the Son invites us into their fellowship, for it’s only from within the fellowship they have between them that we can truly see the glory of both the Father and the Son.

It’s only as we’re in the fellowship of the Father and the Son that we can judge righteously.

Judge righteously, according to the Father’s will in Jesus

The last time we saw Jesus in Jerusalem in the Gospel of John, Jesus had healed a man who couldn’t walk for thirty-eight years.

After he healed the man, Jesus told the man to pick up his mat and walk.

The Jewish leaders who saw the man carrying the mat that day blamed Jesus for breaking the Sabbath law, which forbade people to work on the Sabbath Day.

Instead of praising God for healing the man, the Jews accused Jesus of breaking God’s law.

In fact, they hated Jesus so much that they were looking for a way to kill him.

That’s what Jesus was referring to here in verse 19.

It looked like they were still trying to kill him.

To show how inconsistent these Jewish leaders were, Jesus referred them to the practice of circumcision.

The practice of circumcision was first practiced by the fathers, Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, and then codified in Moses’ law, as a sign that says the boy being circumcised belonged to God’s people.

It was such an important sign that even if the date of circumcision falls on a Sabbath, they’ll still circumcise the boy, they’ll still ‘work’ on the day.

Jesus, by healing the man, showed that a new chapter has begun in the lives of God’s people.

In fact, Jesus says here that the practice of circumcision finds its fulfilment in him.

In verse 21-23, ““I performed one work, and you are all amazed,” Jesus answered. “This is why Moses has given you circumcision—not that it comes from Moses but from the fathers—and you circumcise a man on the Sabbath. If a man receives circumcision on the Sabbath so that the law of Moses won’t be broken, are you angry at me because I made a man entirely well on the Sabbath?”

If the Jews have no issue to circumcise a man on the Sabbath, they should have no problem whatsoever for Jesus to heal a man on the Sabbath.

For both the law of Circumcision and the Law of Sabbath find their fulfilment in Jesus.

That’s what the laws are ultimately about; who Jesus is.

From now on, it won’t be circumcision that defines their identity as God’s people.

It will be Jesus who identifies who they are as God’s people.

From now on, the sign of rest is not merely not working on the Sabbath.

The sign of rest will be Jesus, who alone can bring us true rest for our souls.

That’s how we’re to read God’s word the Bible.

It’s all about Jesus; it all points to him.

There are many people who claim to be teachers of the Bible.

There are many people preaching and proclaiming the Bible online and on the streets.

They will quote the Bible and explain the Bible thoroughly, even vividly.

But do they point to Jesus?

Do they point their readers to Jesus, just like Jesus have?

Friends, judge righteously.

Just because someone quotes the Bible doesn’t necessarily mean that they believe in Jesus.

Even Satan can quote the Bible but he certainly doesn’t believe in Jesus.

Listen to what Jesus says in verse 24, “Stop judging according to outward appearances; rather judge according to righteous judgment.”

When you read someone who claims to be a Christian, listen carefully to what he or she says. Do they give glory to Jesus or themselves?

Don’t just judge on outward appearances; judge according to righteous judgement.

And so, what standard am I using to judge Jesus?

Are we using the world’s way or we using God’s way?

Friends, don’t judge the Jesus as the world does.

Rather, approach Jesus as someone who wants to do God’s will.

What does God want me to do?

I know what God wants you to do.

He wants you to go to Jesus; once you go to Jesus, he will tell you your next step.

However, your first step, must always be going to Jesus.

We can try other standards, but no other way would lead us to a clear understanding of who Jesus is.

No other way pleases God.

In fact, if you try to know Jesus in any other way than coming from a desire to please God, you will end up trying to know Jesus the world’s way.

And if you go the world’s way, you will end up hating Jesus.

And so, Come to Jesus as someone who wants to do God’s will.

When we come to Jesus, we come to hear God’s will.

We come to the One who had obeyed God perfectly, who has already died and who has already been raised to life for us.

He knows what it means to please God.

When we come to him, be ready to open your heart; be ready to listen and obey.

As you come, ready to do God’s will, you will hear and recognise Jesus as the one who knows God’s will.

You’ll know that he’s someone whom we should obey.

That’s what we come together to do on Sunday mornings and in Growth groups and in KOG groups.

We come to listen to Jesus, as a people wanting to do God’s will.

We come to listen to what Jesus has to say about the way we are to love other believers.

We come to listen to what Jesus has to say about the way we love our neighbours.

We come to listen to what Jesus says about how we respect and love our families.

We come not as the world, we are not looking for what the world wants;

Rather we come, looking for the Father’s will.

And so, friends, Come to Jesus as someone who wants to do God’s will.

Here are the three questions for this week:

1. How might we be tempted to be popular in the world’s eyes instead of following God’s will?

2. Have I come today with a desire to do God’s will?

3. Do I see Jesus from the world’s point of view or from how God would want us to see him, his one and only beloved Son?

Friends come and see the glorious Jesus.

Come to Jesus as someone who wants to do God’s will.

Let’s pray, “Father, we thank you for giving us Jesus. He is the One who reigns with you and the Holy Spirit in glory forever and ever. Father, please help us see Jesus as you would want us to see him. Please open our eyes to his glory. By your Spirit, enable to follow him, that we might do your will, that we might live a life that pleases you, for in Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”
