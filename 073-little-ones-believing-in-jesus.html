This past week, my family and I went up to the Blue Mountains for the CMS Summer School Conference.

It’s a conference to hear from God’s word and to hear from missionaries who have been sharing God’s word around the world.

It’s also a conference where missionaries are sent to preach God’s word.

During the conference, an older couple was interviewed.

To keep their identities safe, let’s call them John and June.

John and June had been working in their churches for a very long time.

In fact, they’ve been helping missionaries for a very long time and are preparing to retire.

Last year, as they were thinking about what to do as John finishes up his work, he heard about the need for gospel ministry in an Asian country.

He heard about the how few workers in University ministries.

And so, rather than spending his time relaxing in retirement, he and his wide prayed and decided to become missionaries.

They will give up a retirement lifestyle for a life of sharing the good news of Jesus in a foreign country.

Why were they so sure in their belief, that they were willing to give up so much?

Why would they give up the security they have in Australia and go to a place which would be more dangerous for them?

We can look at it another way.

Rather than being amazed at how much belief they have; we can look at ourselves and be amazed at how much unbelief we have.

Why can’t I believe like them?

Why don’t I believe?

It’s not just as question for unbelievers.

There will be times when even believers, disciples of Jesus, who may struggle with unbelief.

Does the Bible really mean it when it says that Jesus is the only way?

Surely, in the multicultural world of the 21st Century, we have moved on from that, right?

Do I really believe that Jesus is who he says he is, that he is the Only Way?

What do I do with my unbelief?

Going from Unbelief to belief:

The Glorious Son of the Father

If you have your Bible, please open up with me to Mark chapter nine, beginning from verse 2.

Here, we’ll see the glorious Son of the Father

“After six days Jesus took Peter, James, and John and led them up a high mountain by themselves to be alone. He was transfigured in front of them, 3 and his clothes became dazzling—extremely white as no launderer on earth could whiten them.”

Jesus was transfigured, literally his form was changed.

He was an ordinary looking human one minute and became something glorious the next.

His clothes became so dazzling white that it was whiter than anything you can find on Planet Earth.

No one has ever since the brilliant white that Jesus was wearing.

The closest I can think of is the brilliance of the sun.

Ever since we’re small, we’ve been told to never look at the sun because looking at the sun can cause permanent eye damage.

That must have been how brilliant Jesus appeared.

On that High Mountain, God gave the Three Disciples, Peter, James, and John a brief glimpse of the true glory of Jesus.

It’s a glimpse of what Jesus would look like when he comes back in his glory.

At Jesus’ transfiguration, two other figures also appeared.

They were Elijah and Moses.

Elijah was a great prophet in the Old Testament.

Moses was seen as the Great Prophet in the Old Testament who also gave God’s laws to the Israelites.

Together, they represent the Law and the Prophets in the Old Testament.

They were both speaking with Jesus.

It would have been fascinating to hear what they were talking about, but their conversation was not recorded.

At the very least, it showed that Jesus must be of great importance to have these two prophets speak with him at his transfiguration.

The disciples were terrified.

How should they respond to something as glorious as the transfiguration of Jesus and the appearance of Moses and Elijah?

Peter said the first thing that came into his mind and suggested that he should build tents for each one of them.

Rather than going with Peter’s suggestion, God had something else in mind:

A cloud overshadowed them, and God said from the cloud, “This is my beloved Son, listen to him!”

The proper response to seeing the glory of Jesus is to listen to him.

Jesus isn’t just another great prophet like Moses or Elijah; He is God’s Son.

He’s the One God whom God tells us to obey.

That’s how we should respond to the Glorious Son.

Some of us might have heard of the Celebrity Chef, Gordon Ramsay.

Earlier this week, there was a show on TV which showed a video clip featuring Gordon Ramsay.

Gordon Ramsay is a Celebrity Chef who goes around to different restaurants to critique and give advice to other chefs and restaurant managers.

In the clip, it showed a Chef and his daughter who ran a horrible restaurant.

They served horrible food to the customers.

Rather than listening to Ramsay’s critique, they responded horribly and sworn repeatedly at Ramsay.

They would rather cook in their own inadequate, horrible way than to listen to the advice of Gordon Ramsay.

They did not respond well to the Celebrity Chef’s critique.

Not long after the episode, the restaurant closed down.

When it comes to responding to Jesus, sometimes we’re just like the chef and her daughter.

Rather than listening to Jesus, as God would have us, we want to respond in our own way.

We might be like the Chef and his daughter, violently disagreeing with what Jesus is saying.

There are some in our community who violently disagrees with what Jesus says.

I’ve seen and heard people swear at Christians when the Christians faithfully teach the teachings of Jesus.

Other times, we might be like Peter, trying to respond in our own way to Jesus.

Peter was spontaneous in his response and wanted to build three tents for Jesus, Moses and Elijah.

It was the first thing that came into his mind.

Surely that’s a good thing to do, right?

As good as the intentions may be, God wants us to firstly listen to Jesus.

Before you do anything for Jesus, have you been listening to Jesus?

Are you doing what he’s asking you to do?

Far too often, we want to give to Jesus, worship Jesus, even serve Jesus our way, instead of His way.

Before we do anything for Jesus, let’s listen to Jesus.

Listen to the Good News of what He’s already done for us; listen to what he’s already commanded us.

Listen to God who says of his Jesus, “This is my beloved Son; listen to him!”

What does Jesus say?

There’s a lot that Jesus says throughout the Bible.

I hope you can read it daily at home.

In today’s passage, we’ll see it in his response to a plea from an unbelieving father.

The plea from an unbelieving father

When Jesus, Peter, James and John came down the mountain to meet with the other disciples,

They saw a large crowd arguing.

When Jesus asked what the argument was about, a father came and said this to Jesus, in verse 17, “Teacher, I brought my son to you. He has a spirit that makes him unable to speak. Whenever it seizes him, it throws him down, and he foams at the mouth, grinds his teeth, and becomes rigid. I asked your disciples to drive it out, but they couldn’t.”

This boy was possessed by a demon.

The demon would seize him, throw him down, make him rigid and foam at the mouth.

This sounds a lot like what happens when someone has an epileptic seizure.

Epileptic seizure happens when there is a burst of electrical activities in the brain which causes the body to respond in uncontrollable ways.

It’s like an electric storm happening inside a brain.

Sometimes it can make someone to become rigid, foaming at the mouth and tremor, much like the boy that the man’s describing.

However, there’s a big difference between what this boy’s suffering and what generally happens to someone going through an epileptic seizure.

What generally happens to someone during an epileptic seizure is a physical, medical condition of the brain.

But what was happening with the boy in the passage came from a spiritual source.

It was demonic.

What Jesus was presented was not a simple medical issue but a spiritual issue.

It was a battle between Light and Darkness.

It was a battle between God and Satan.

When Jesus asked the father how long this has been happening to the boy, the father replied (in verse 21-22)

“From childhood, and many times it has thrown him into fire or water to destroy him. But if you can do anything, have compassion on us and help us.”

The father had come to Jesus, desperately asking for Jesus to help.

However, he wasn’t absolutely sure that Jesus could help.

After all, his disciples tried and failed.

Would Jesus be any better than his disciples?

If he can help, good.

If he can’t, well, at least he had a go with Jesus.

Listen to Jesus’ reply, “If you can’? Everything is possible for the one who believes.”

Jesus has just said that the people are an unbelieving generation and this man is an example of unbelief.

The man thinks there’s a possibility that Jesus can help; he might even believe that there’s a possibility that Jesus can do miracles.

But to have faith in Jesus, to believe in Jesus, is to trust him as Lord.

It’s to trust Him as the Son of God, the one whom God said, “This is my Beloved Son, Listen to Him.”

To have faith in Jesus is not to say to Jesus, “If you can, save me.”

No, to have in Jesus is to know for certain that he can save and say, “If you will, please save me.”

Jesus can do all things, even impossible things.

The question is whether he wills, whether he wants to do it.

If he wants to do it, it will happen.

When Jesus says, “Everything is possible for the one who believes”, he is not teaching about the power of positive thinking.

He’s not saying that if you have a strong belief in anything, in a plan, in a strategy, in yourself, then you can achieve anything.

That’s a pagan, non-Christian way of thinking.

No, when he says everything is possible for him who believes, he’s clearly talking about those who believe in him.

He’s the object, the focus of the belief.

If you believe in Jesus, everything is possible because Jesus can everything, even the impossible.

Nothing is impossible for Jesus; what do you want to ask of him?

So often, when we come to Jesus, we come with the ‘if you can’ attitude.

Maybe Jesus can do something about my difficult family situation.

Maybe Jesus can do something about my character.

If He can, it would be good.

If not, well, I’ll try a different way.

Jesus, if you can! What would Jesus say about our little faith?

I don’t know about you, but I know that I often pray with little faith.

Though I pray every day, I still don’t pray often enough.

And when I do, I often don’t ask Jesus to do hard things, let alone impossible things.

Too often I come before Jesus with a ‘If you can’ attitude. Don’t you?

If there’s one thing that we can ask of Jesus, a hard thing, an impossible thing, what would it be?

Listen to how the father in today’s passage responds.

In verse 24, “Immediately the father of the boy cried out, “I do believe; help my unbelief!”

Rather than asking Jesus to heal his son, he sees something even more urgent.

He needs to overcome his unbelief.

And he can’t do it by himself; it’s hard, more than that, it’s impossible.

He asks Jesus to help him with his unbelief.

Jesus didn’t respond to him directly.

Instead, he saw a crowd coming and commanded the demon to come out.

It’s almost as if he wanted to avoid the crowd coming and so he quickly commanded the spirit to come out.

It looked so effortless!

The demon didn’t give up without a fight and made the boy looked like he was dead.

But the demon’s fight was futile because the boy was healed and Jesus took the boy by the hand and raised him up.

When the disciples asked why they couldn’t drive out the demon, Jesus simply said, “This kind can come out by nothing but prayer.”

Did you see the prayer?

It was a prayer from the father to Jesus, “I believe, help my unbelief.”

That prayer started the process to rid the child of the demon.

It all begins with a prayer to Jesus.

That’s what faith in Jesus looks like.

Faith in Jesus is coming to Jesus in prayer, asking him to deal with our unbelief.

Belief in Jesus is expressed through prayer to Jesus.

Prayer to Jesus can do the impossible.

Prayer to Jesus can make an unbelieving person believe.

I’ve often told the story of George Mueller.

George Mueller was a 19th Century Christian man who kept a prayer diary.

Here’s an excerpt from his diary,

In November 1844, I began to pray for the conversion of five individuals. I prayed every day …Eighteen months elapsed before the first of the five was converted. I thanked God and prayed on for the others.

Five years elapsed, and then the second was converted. I thanked God for the second, and prayed on for the other three.

Day by day, I continued to pray for them, and six years passed before the third was converted.

I thanked God for the three, and went on praying for the other two. These two remained unconverted.”

Thirty-six years later he wrote that the other two, sons of one of Mueller’s friends, were still not converted. He wrote, “But I hope in God, I pray on, and look for the answer. They are not converted yet, but they will be.” In 1897, fifty-two years after he began to pray daily, without interruption, for these two men, they were finally converted—but after he died!

Would you pray for someone, every day of your life, for the rest of your life, that they’ll believe in Jesus?

George Mueller did because he didn’t pray with a ‘If you can’ attitude but an “If you will” attitude.

Can Jesus do the impossible? Can Jesus save this hard-hearted person? Can Jesus save me?

Of course he can! The question is whether he wills it and whether we’ve come to him in prayer for it.

There are so many souls that need saving and in his love and wisdom, Jesus wants us to be involved in saving people, in helping them believe in him.

He involves us by asking us to pray to him.

We need prayer to overcome our own unbelief.

We need prayer for others to overcome their unbelief.

From Unbelief to Belief

How does one person go from unbelief to belief?

Firstly, we need to look to the Glorious Son.

The disciples saw the glory of Jesus on the High Mountain.

They saw a glimpse of the glory of the Son of God.

Though the father didn’t see the same glory, he looked for Jesus and brought his son to Jesus.

For us today, we see glimpses of Jesus’ glory when we read God’s Word, the Bible.

These are not just words of an ancient book.

In these pages, you’ll see the glory of the Lord Jesus Christ.

While it’s not the same glory that the disciples had seen, the pages of the Bible won’t shine out with a brilliant light, the words will point to the glorious Lord Jesus.

Over the past two thousand years, millions upon millions of Christians have seen the glory of Jesus as they read these words.

We still do it today.

And so, this year, dive into God’s Word.

Join a Bible study.

Read the Bible with us using the Church’s Daily Bible Reading Plan.

Look for the glory of Jesus as you read from God’s word.

The father in the story realised his own unbelief and responded to Jesus by turning to Jesus for help.

That’s what we’re called to do as well.

If we’re honest with ourselves, there will be times when we find it to fully trust in Jesus.

We know of Jesus’ command, but we don’t do it.

We know what he asks of us, but we don’t listen.

We might feel that we’re not able to; we might feel that it’s impossible for us to obey Jesus at this point in our lives.

May God be gracious to us and help us overcome our unbelief.

May he help us to look to the glorious Son and to pray to the glorious Son, so that we’ll overcome our unbelief.

What does this mean for us?

This week, make this your prayer: Jesus, help my unbelief.

Before we start looking at the unbelief in others, let’s start with our own unbelief.

Let’s start with areas in our lives where we’re not trusting Jesus.

It might be not trusting Jesus with our time; it might be a matter of not giving Jesus enough time in our timetable.

It might be not trusting Jesus with our moral choices; it might be a matter of making the decision to do the wrong and easier thing rather than the hard but right thing.

Sometimes, these decisions to trust in Jesus seem impossible.

Sometimes, to trust in Jesus just simply seem impossible.

But Jesus specialises in the impossible.

Nothing is impossible with him.

Come to him in prayer, pray that he’ll help you with your unbelief.

This week, pray to Jesus to help you overcome your unbelief.

Pray for Jesus to help you with your unbelief when you wake up in the morning.

Pray for Jesus to help you with your unbelief in the middle of the day.

Pray for Jesus to help you with your unbelief when you go to bed.

Pray for Jesus to help you with your unbelief when you need to make decisions.

Don’t come to him with an ‘if you can’ attitude.

Come to him with an ‘if you will’ attitude, asking him to overcome your unbelief.

My prayer this week is that God will hear your prayers and mine, as we ask Jesus to help us with our unbelief.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Do I see Jesus’ glory when I read the Bible?

Do I pray with an ‘if you can’ attitude’ or ‘if you will’ attitude?

What area of my life do I need help with my unbelief?

Let’s spend a short time, reflecting on these three questions and even praying over these three questions.
