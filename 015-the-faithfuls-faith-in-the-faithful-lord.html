The faithful in the faithful Lord are justified

The faithful in the faithful Lord ascertains

The faithful in the faithful Lord Jesus finds peace

Good morning, everyone, great to see everyone here.

It’s been a very difficult week for many of us who live in Sydney.

Last Saturday, there was stabbing at Bondi Westfield’s in which six victims died plus the attacker.

Some of the people who were stabbed survived but are still in hospital.

There were many family members and community members who mourned for the loss of their loved ones at Westfield’s on Thursday when it reopened.

The police had confirmed that the stabbing was not terror related.

However, on Monday night, during a memorial service, while a bishop was preaching, a young non-Christian man stabbed the bishop as well as a few others.

News about this quickly spread because the service was livestreamed, and hundreds of people came to the Church in response.

The crowd who went started a riot and though there were many police officers there, over 50 were injured.

This incident had been officially seen as a terror attack.

It’s been a very tense week in Sydney.

Often, it’s in tragedies like these that we might ask ourselves, where is God in all these?

How can God let it happen?

The victims were just doing normal things like shopping, working, or even preaching in a Church.

For some people, tragedies like these can rock their faith.

Have you ever had an experience that rocks your faith in God?

What would it take for you to question who God is?

What would rock youth faith in God?

Today, we’re going to see what rocks Abram’s faith and how God responds to him.

And so, if you have your Bible there, please open up to Genesis 15, beginning from verse 1.

We’ll see three things:

The faithful in the faithful Lord are justified

The faithful in the faithful Lord ascertains

The faithful in the faithful Lord Jesus finds peace

But before we look into it, let’s pray.

“Father, your word is living and active. May your word pierce our hearts to show us what’s hidden there, the very thing that you see so clearly, and may your Spirit cause your word to live in our hearts and flourish in our lives, for Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

The faithful in the faithful Lord are justified

Abram had just had a resounding victory over powerful kings.

He was blessed by Melchizedek, the Priest of God Most High.

He should’ve been on a spiritual high.

And he was probably on a spiritual high was for a short time.

However, something made him afraid and God came to comfort him.

Look with me at 15:1, “After these events, the word of the LORD came to Abram in a vision: Do not be afraid, Abram. I am your shield; your reward will be very great.”

What was he afraid of?

The assurance the LORD gave to Abram offers us a clue; Abram needed to hear that the LORD is his shield and that Abram’s reward will be great.

Abram might have won the battle against the kings, but he didn’t receive any rewards for himself.

He gave 1/10th to Melchizedek the Priest and 9/10th to the King of Sodom.

He didn’t get any reward for his effort.

However, God assured him that he God himself will reward him and be his shield.

That should be enough, shouldn’t it? It should be more than enough.

But look at Abram’s responses in verses 2-3, “But Abram said, “Lord GOD, what can you give me, since I am childless and the heir of my house is Eliezer of Damascus?” 3 Abram continued, “Look, you have given me no offspring, so a slave born in my house will be my heir.”

Abram doesn’t have a son.

There’s no one who will continue his line; His family line will stop with him.

He will pass everything that he has gained in his life to an outsider called Eliezer of Damascus, a slave who was born in his house.

By now Abram is close to 80 years old if not older.

There’s no chance of continuing his line since Sarai his wife is barren.

He’s come to a stage in his life where he asks, is his faith in God worth it?

Is this it? Is this as good as it’s ever going to get?

I still remember the famous Sydney preacher Philip Jensen telling us what’s the best day of a man’s life.

What’s the best day in a man’s life? When he got his own boat.

Let me ask you again: what’s the best day in a man’s life? When he got rid of his boat.

What was so promising at the beginning end up costing a lot more in the long run not to mention a lot of additional work to maintain the boat.

That’s where Abram is in his journey of faith.

It started off so promising, with God promising him land, and offspring and blessings.

But along the way, he had to live through at least one famine in the land God promised to give him, almost lost his wife Sarai to the Egyptians, had to separate from his nephew Lot, fought hard against foreign kings with little to show for it.

And Abram doesn’t know how much longer he has left.

God’s said that he would make him into a great nation and that God would give him and his descendants the land around him.

However, time is running out and there’s still a key thing missing in how God’s been fulfilling his promises.

Abram and Sarai still have no children of their own.

How will God fulfil his promises? Through Eliezer of Damascus?

No! God said it won’t be Eliezer but a son from Abram’s own body.

God then took him outside and asked him to count the stars in the sky.

He challenges Abram to count them if he can because that’s how many children Abram’s going to have.

I love how God’s done this!

The LORD, the God Most High, the One who made the stars, arranged time and space so that they shine at that precise moment, told Abram to look up to the heavens.

The purpose of these stars, thousands upon thousands of them, was to give Abram the assurance that God will give him not just one child but many sons and daughters, as many as the stars in heaven that night.

Abram was inside his tent; he probably couldn’t sleep because he was frustrated and afraid that God wouldn’t be able to fulfil his promises.

And yet God lovingly takes Abram outside to look at the stars.

It’s as if God says, ‘Come out Abe, look at these stars, stars that I made; that’s how many children you’re going to have’.

If God can make all these stars by his powerful word,

If God can make all these galaxies by speaking them into existence,

Then God’s word, God’s promise to Abram, can be trusted.

And that’s what Abram did, verse 6, “Abram believed the LORD, and he credited it to him as righteousness.”

That’s what righteousness before God looks like; to be righteous before God is to be right with God.

What does it mean to be righteous before God?

The Bible scholar SH Hooke says that righteousness before God signifies ‘nothing less than the character of God Himself in His dealings with man. The original intention in man’s creation was that he should be in God’s image, after His likeness. By his act of disobedience the image was defaced, the likeness destroyed; now the work of restoration has begun; God has found the response of faith and obedience, He has found a man in whom His own character begins to be formed.’

God is righteous and so to be righteous is to be like God, to reflect God, and to be the image of God that God made us to be.

Of course, to be righteous includes doing the right things but at the heart of it, it must be a heart that’s in a right relationship with God.

To be in a right relationship with God means having an unwavering trust in him; it means taking God at his word.

If God has said it, it must be true. If God has promised it, it must happen.

The apostle Paul uses Abram’s example to describe what faith in Jesus is.

Turn with me to Romans 4:19-22.

19 He did not weaken in faith when he considered his own body to be already dead (since he was about a hundred years old) and also the deadness of Sarah’s womb. 20 He did not waver in unbelief at God’s promise but was strengthened in his faith and gave glory to God, 21 because he was fully convinced that what God had promised, he was also able to do. 22 Therefore, it was credited to him for righteousness.

Abram would have done many good and right things in his life, but it was not by any of these that God counted him as righteous.

It’s because Abram trusts God’s promises, no matter how impossible they might seem, that God counted Abram righteous.

Sometimes people ask me how people are saved in the Old Testament, that is how a person is declared righteous in the Old Testament.

It’s the same as the New Testament.

There aren’t two different ways to be made right with God.

People like Abram weren’t made right with God by obeying the law in the Old Testament and those after him were made right with God by faith in the New Testament.

No, people were counted righteous by faith in God in the Old Testament and people were and are counted righteous by faith in God in the New Testament and beyond.

If you want to be right with God, you must believe him, you must trust his promises.

Tragedies can often rock our faith, but at the same time, tragedies can also drive us to faith.

People generally are more open to finding out who God is in light of tragedies.

However, often I find many people’s faith are rocked when their faithfulness to God doesn’t seem to amount to much.

This especially applies to those of us who have been Christians for many years.

We’ve given so much to God in our lives because we want to be faithful to God and yet we don’t seem to have a lot to show for it.

I’ve seen gospel workers, and even some ministers, who seem embarrassed by how small their church is.

We understand that it’s important to remain faithful to God but like Abram, we look around and we think, is it worth it?

Are we making any difference at all in the world?

Are we making even a dent in the universe?

If that’s you, if your faith in God has been rocked, come before God, and be honest with him, just as Abram did.

Tell God what’s in your heart of hearts; He can take it.

Tell him what makes you afraid; He understands your fear and your anxirty, even if you don’t.

After you spoken with God, take a look at the stars.

Though He hasn’t promised to give you as many children as he did to Abram, he has promise you every spiritual blessing in Jesus Christ.

The God who made his promises to Abram is the same God who made his promises to you in Christ.

If he can make the stars by his word, his word can be trusted.

It’s those who trust in the Lord who will be made righteous, who are justified

The faithful in the faithful Lord are justified.

2. The faithful in the faithful Lord ascertains

The faithful in the faithful Lord also ascertains.

Though Abram was made righteous because of his faith, he still has questions.

Look with me at verses 7-8, “He also said to him, “I am the LORD who brought you from Ur of the Chaldeans to give you this land to possess.” But he said, “Lord GOD, how can I know that I will possess it?”

How will Abram know? How does he know for sure that the Lord will be able to deliver as he has promised?

The faithful in the faithful Lord ascertains; they want to find out how they can be certain about their faith.

There’s a Misconception of faith that goes something like this: once you believe, that’s it.

You don’t need to understand; you just believe.

While it’s true that we might not understand everything, true faith does seek to understand, true faith seeks to grow.

No person of genuine faith in Christ will ever say that their faith has grown to a stage that they don’t have any more questions about faith.

No one ever grows so much in their understanding of Jesus on this side of the grave that they don’t need to study the Bible in depth anymore.

True, genuine faith has a desire to grow.

It’s not a case that we must understand in order to have faith.

Instead, we have faith in order to understand.

It’s a matter of faith seeking understanding.

Those of us with a genuine faith in the Lord wants to know how our faith works in our day to day lives.

How do we know what we believe with even more certainty?

And so, out of his trust for God and wanting to trust God even more, Abram questions God.

How can I know?

God then told Abram to bring him a cow, a goat, a ram, a turtledove and a young pigeon.

With the exception of the birds, Abram cut these in half.

Birds of prey came to take these away and Abram fought them off.

After what must have been a long day, Abram fell into a deep sleep and was suddenly overcome with terror and darkness.

Abram was afraid at the beginning of the passage but now he’s terrified.

What was happening to Abram?

Look with me at verse 13, “Then the LORD said to Abram, “Know this for certain: Your offspring will be resident aliens for four hundred years in a land that does not belong to them and will be enslaved and oppressed.”

Abram’s question to God was “How can I know I will possess the land?”

God’s answer to Abram was “Know this for certain”.

God is about to tell Abram what he must know.

He must know that his offspring, his children will one day possess this land, but the process won’t be straightforward.

There will be parallels and contrasts with Abram’s own journey.

Just like Abram, Abram’s children will be in a foreign land.

But unlike Abram, who merely lived as foreigner on the land, Abram’s children will be slaves in this foreign land.

Abram’s children will be oppressed for four hundred years.

It will be a dark time to be Abram’s children.

However, God will judge the nation that enslaved them, and they will go out with many possessions, just as when God judged Egypt, and Abram left with many possessions.

God gave a clearer glimpse of what the future looks like for Abram’s children and the land.

They will possess it one day, but life will be hard for them before then, even harder than what it was like for Abram.

God has promised Abram that they will be oppressed and they will go through a dark time.

But even then, even in their darkest hour, God is still in control.

Abram can know that for certain.

To make it even more certain for Abram, God answers is a very dramatic way.

Look with me at verse 17, “When the sun had set and it was dark, a smoking fire pot and a flaming torch appeared and passed between the divided animals.”

The smoking fire pot and the flaming torch represented God’s presence.

In ancient Middle East, treaties were often confirmed by blessings and curses.

Here’s an example of such a treaty between Ashurnirari V of Assyria and Mati’ilu of Arpad: ‘If Mati’ilu sins against this treaty, so may, just as the head of this spring lamb is torn off … the head of Mati’ilu be torn off.…’13

God himself initiated and bound the treaty with Abram.

If God should fail to keep his promises to Abram, then God himself will be torn to pieces.

It’s as strong as an oath as you can get!

Abram provided the animals of the treaty, but it was God himself who took on the full responsibility of fulfilling this treaty, this covenant, with Abram.

In this treaty, we see a glimpse of what Jesus himself with do one day.

Like Abram and Abram’s children. Jesus was a foreigner to his own creation, even though he created us all.

Like Abram’s children, Jesus was oppressed.

However, Jesus’ oppressor took one step further and treated Jesus even worse than a slave.

They treated Jesus like a criminal worthy of death on a cross.

God’s judgement for our sins fell on him.

He was nailed and pierced; He was broken.

But through his death and resurrection, Jesus triumphed over sin and death and offers freedom for all those who confess that they are slaves to sin.

Look with me at Hebrews 10, “Therefore, brothers and sisters, since we have boldness to enter the sanctuary through the blood of Jesus — 20 he has inaugurated for us a new and living way through the curtain (that is, through his flesh )— 21 and since we have a great high priest over the house of God, 22 let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, with our hearts sprinkled clean from an evil conscience and our bodies washed in pure water.”

Jesus himself opened up the way for us to receive his reward for his faith in God the Father.

It was through his broken body that Jesus fulfilled God’s promises.

This is what we’re now to know with absolute certainty.

How does Abram know that God will keep his promise?

Abram knows that God will keep his oath, even when those who receive his promises are covered by a great darkness.

God would be like torn in pieces before he breaks his promises.

How do we know God will keep his promises?

God has given us Jesus, the Son of God who died for the sins of men.

Has there been any day darker than the day men killed the Son of God?

Even on that darkest day, even when his body was broken, the Son of God kept his promise.

Jesus’ death and resurrection is how we know God will keep his promises.

This is something that we must keep reminding ourselves of whenever we can.

We need to work together to ascertain our faith in our Lord Jesus; we need to work together so that our certainty and our faith in the Lord Jesus continues to grow.

Don’t let anything stop you from meeting together in Christ; Meet together as much as possible to strengthen each other’s faith.

This is what we do as we come to Church, we come to strengthen faith.

It’s so good to catch up with so many of you in our conversations over morning tea.

It’s good to hear how your life is growing but it’s even better to hear how many of you are actively seeking to grow in faith.

Don’t stop meeting one another; keep strengthen each other’s faith.

As the Apostle Peter reminds us, “make every effort to confirm your calling and election, because if you do these things you will never stumble.”

Growing in your faith and certainty in the gospel is hard work; It takes every effort to confirm your calling and election.

Don’t do it alone; grow in your faith together, as a Church.

3. The faithful in the faithful Lord Jesus finds peace

And so, the faithful in the faithful Lord are justified, the faithful in the Lord ascertains.

Finally, the faithful in the Lord finds peace, even in the face of death.

As well as telling Abram what will happen to his offspring in the far distant future, God tells Abram that he will die in peace at a good age.

Abram will rest in peace when he dies, knowing that God is faithful to keep his promises to Abram, even when Abram himself dies.

Though his children will be slaves in a foreign country, they will come back in the fourth generation, in 400 years’ time.

Why 400 years? It’s because the iniquity of the Amorite have not reached its full measure.

God’s promise to Abram is bigger than Abram.

It will span centuries and it will cross international borders.

When the time is right, God will give the land of the Kenites, Kenizzites, Kadmonites, Hethites, Perizzites, Rephaim, Amorites, Canaanites, Girgashites, and Jebusites into the hands of Abram’s children.

We can’t see the whole picture at any instant, let alone seeing the whole picture over hundreds and thousands of years.

But we can trust God with his plan.

God will be faithful to the end, to the last day of the world, and in Christ, we’re called to trust in him, we’re called to be faithful to our last day in the world.

Because our Lord is faithful, because he can be trusted, we will have peace, even if the world should fall around us.

That doesn’t mean that we won’t ever be afraid or terrified.

That doesn’t mean that we we’ll always suffer without question.

No, just as Abram was afraid and asked God about it, we too, are invited to come to ask God about what makes us afraid.

We, too, can ask God questions, seeking his will and his answer in all these.

For ours is a faith that ascertains; we want to make our faith sure.

And we come to him through Jesus, the Prince of Peace, who was willing to have his body broken for us to make us whole, who was willing to die for those who believe him so that we might live.

Jesus knows our fears; he’s experienced the very worst of pains, and he will help us through it.

His very presence will give us the peace that the world so desperate needs.

What rocks your faith?

I don’t know what’s troubling your heart right now.

I don’t know what keeps you awake at night.

But when you get a chance, go out and look at the stars at night, and tell God what’s on your heart.

Pray to God for the peace that the world can’t give and the world can’t take away.

Pray for the peace that only the Lord Jesus can bring.

And so friends, remember: The faithful in the faithful LORD Jesus are justified; their faith ascertains and finds hope in Him alone.

As I mentioned earlier, this has been a painful week for many Sydneysiders.

I have a friend who’s a member of the NSW Faith Affairs council, and the chairman of that council is our own region’s bishop, the Bishop Michael Stead.

My friend asked us to pray as there are many rumours among different religious groups that can start very serious conflicts in our neighbourhood.

Those who don’t know Jesus will gossip, spread rumours, actively seek vengeance and bring the other group down.

Those who know Jesus will do something far more powerful and effective.

They will pray by faith, and they will forgive.

They will walk in righteousness and won’t return evil for evil.

They will act as the Lord himself had taught and lived out.

It’s those who believe in Jesus who are justified, not those who tries to justify themselves.

It’s the faithful ones who will ascertain, find out the truth of God and grow in Him.

It’s the faithful ones who find hope in Christ alone.

Can I ask you to please pray for all those who are affected this week, whether they call themselves Christians or not?

Pray also for our local member, the Premier Chris Minns, as he handles this very delicate situation along with the faith leaders in our community.

Pray that when we are afraid, we will be faithful and turn to our faithful Lord Jesus.

Pray that all of us here and more, will trust in Jesus and so be counted righteous.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Name a crisis of faith you dread. What would it look like if you respond to that crisis by faith?

How is your faith seeking understanding?

How does your faith in the Lord Jesus help you find peace in him?

The faithful in the faithful LORD Jesus are justified; their faith ascertains and finds hope in Him alone.

Let’s come before our faithful LORD together in prayer.

“Father, thank you that we can come before you with boldness, not because of our faithfulness but because of Jesus’ faithfulness. Thank you that it’s by his faithfulness that we can be justified before you. Father, let us come before you in faith, knowing with certainty that you can do the impossible. Father we pray now for the potential tension in our community. The tragedies over the past week has caused a lot of anxiety and grief, fear and anger in our city. Please be merciful to our city and give our city the hope and peace that only Jesus can give. Father we want to make our faith sure; please show us what we must do by faith to see you bring the peace and hope of Christ to our city. And Father, whenever we are afraid, let us hide ourselves in you, the only one who can give us true peace. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
