Doctor Jesus, heal yourself

Luke 4:16–44 · Sunday 25 October 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah · For high-school students · Sermon by Rev David Yung

Big Idea: Jesus came to tell the gospel, and he wouldn’t back down — not when his own town turned on him, not when crowds only wanted a free doctor, not even at the cross. Now that you’ve heard it, don’t just admire him: serve him, because he served you first.

Aim: by the end, students will

see that Jesus did not back down when his own town turned on him, and did not get stuck when the crowds only wanted what he could do for them;

spot where they are tempted to laugh along when people sneer at Jesus, or to treat Jesus as someone who just fixes problems;

answer what they’ll do now they’ve heard the gospel: serve Jesus, because he served them first.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, paper, about 60–75 minutes.

1. Connect (10 min)

  • What’s the embarrassing story your family always brings up about you from when you were little? Why is it so hard for the people who’ve known you longest to see you as anything new?
  • Has anyone ever put you down or left you out because you were doing well — a good mark, making the team, getting picked? Why do people do that?

2. Read: Luke 4:16–44 (CSB)

16 He came to Nazareth, where he had been brought up. As usual, he entered the synagogue on the Sabbath day and stood up to read. 17 The scroll of the prophet Isaiah was given to him, and unrolling the scroll, he found the place where it was written: 18 The Spirit of the Lord is on me, because he has anointed me to preach good news to the poor. He has sent me to proclaim release to the captives and recovery of sight to the blind, to set free the oppressed, 19 to proclaim the year of the Lord’s favor.

20 He rolled up the scroll, gave it back to the attendant, and sat down. And the eyes of everyone in the synagogue were fixed on him. 21 He began by saying to them, “Today, as you listen, this Scripture is fulfilled.” 22 They were all speaking well of him and amazed by the gracious words that came from his mouth. They said, “Isn’t this Joseph’s son?”

23 Then he said to them, “No doubt you will quote this proverb to me: ‘Doctor, heal yourself. What we’ve heard that took place in Capernaum, do here in your hometown also.’” 24 He also said, “Truly I tell you, no prophet is accepted in his hometown. 25 But I say to you, there were certainly many widows in Israel in Elijah’s days, when the sky was shut up for three years and six months while a great famine came over all the land. 26 Yet Elijah was not sent to any of them—except to a widow at Zarephath in Sidon. 27 And there were many lepers in Israel in the prophet Elisha’s time, yet not one of them was healed—only Naaman the Syrian.”

28 When they heard this, everyone in the synagogue was enraged. 29 They got up, drove him out of town, and brought him to the edge of the hill that their town was built on, intending to hurl him over the cliff. 30 But he passed right through the crowd and went on his way.

31 Then he went down to Capernaum, a town in Galilee, and was teaching them on the Sabbath day. 32 They were astonished at his teaching because his message had authority. 33 In the synagogue there was a man with an unclean demon’s spirit, and he cried out in a loud voice, 34 “Leave us alone! What do you have to do with us, Jesus of Nazareth? Have you come to destroy us? I know who you are—the Holy One of God!” 35 But Jesus rebuked him. “Be silent and come out of him!” he said. And throwing the man down before them, the demon came out of him without hurting him at all. 36 They were all struck with amazement and kept talking to each other, saying, “What is this message? For he commands the unclean spirits with authority and power, and they come out!” 37 Reports about him went out to every place in the vicinity.

38 After he left the synagogue, he entered Simon’s house. Simon’s mother-in-law was suffering from a high fever, and they asked him about her. 39 So he stood over her and rebuked the fever, and it left her. She got up immediately and began to serve them.

40 When the sun was setting, all those who had anyone sick with various diseases brought them to him. He laid his hands on each one of them and healed them. 41 Demons also came out of many, shouting, “You are the Son of God!” But he rebuked them and would not allow them to speak because they knew he was the Messiah.

42 When it was day, he went out and made his way to a deserted place. The crowds were searching for him, and they came to him and tried to keep him from leaving them. 43 But he said to them, “It is necessary for me to proclaim the good news about the kingdom of God to the other towns also, because I was sent for this purpose.” 44 So he kept on preaching in the synagogues of Judea.

3. Context

Jesus has just started his public work. He goes back to Nazareth, the town where he grew up, and reads a promise from Isaiah about God’s chosen one bringing good news. Then he says, “That’s me.” At first everyone loves it. Then he tells them God’s favour is going to outsiders, and they try to throw him off a cliff. After that he goes to Capernaum, where he teaches with authority, drives out a demon and heals many people, and the crowds don’t want him to leave. Three responses to Jesus: contempt (Nazareth), confusion (Capernaum), and one right response — Simon’s mother-in-law gets up and serves him.

4. Discuss

  1. vv.16–21 — Jesus reads Isaiah’s promise in the synagogue and says, “Today, as you listen, this Scripture is fulfilled.” Who is he saying he is? What is the good news, and who is it for?
  2. vv.22–23 — “Isn’t this Joseph’s son?” and “Doctor, heal yourself.” They watched Jesus grow up, and now they look down on him. Why does it sting most when it’s the people who know us best who put us down? What did Jesus not do?
  3. vv.24–27 — Jesus talks about a foreign widow and Naaman, an enemy general. What’s his point? Why is the real problem not that Nazareth rejected Jesus, but that they were missing out on God’s favour?
  4. vv.28–30 — They try to throw Jesus off a cliff, and he walks right through the crowd. When friends or family roll their eyes at your faith, what does Jesus’ example say about how to respond — and who outside your circle might actually be open to hearing about Jesus?
  5. vv.31–37 — The demon says, “I know who you are—the Holy One of God!” but he never serves Jesus. How is that different from the people in Capernaum who were amazed? Why isn’t knowing facts about Jesus enough?
  6. vv.38–44 — Jesus heals many in Capernaum, then leaves, saying he must preach in other towns. Why wouldn’t he stay? What’s the difference between being amazed by Jesus and understanding him? When do we treat Jesus like a free doctor or a supplier?
  7. vv.38–39 — Simon’s mother-in-law “got up immediately and began to serve them.” Why did she serve? What does it show about how being saved and serving fit together?
  8. James 2:19–20 — “Even the demons believe—and they shudder.” What would it look like this week for you to move from admiring Jesus to actually serving him, in one small, real way?

5. Live It Out

  • Pray for 3 — three friends or family members who don’t know Jesus yet. Pray for them by name every day.
  • Connect with 2 — be a real friend this week: a lunch, a walk or a proper chat (not just a reaction in the group chat).
  • Invite 1 — invite one of your three to KOGYouth, to church on Sunday, to the Discover Course or to the Christmas concert on 13 December. Don’t forget to ask the new person too, not only your best friend.
  • 0 left unloved — spot the person who’s on their own at school or at youth, and sit with them.

This week’s challenge: Stop admiring Jesus from a distance. Choose one small way to serve this week — help set up or pack down, welcome someone new, or ask your leader how you can help at the Christmas concert rehearsals. Serve because Jesus served you first.

6. Pray

  • Thank God for sending Jesus to tell us the gospel, and for not backing down even on the cross. Say sorry for any time you’ve sneered at Jesus, or only wanted what he could give you.
  • Ask God to make you someone who serves because you’ve been served — and to show you one way to serve this week.
  • Pray by name for your 3 friends — that they would hear the good news and meet Jesus.

7. Leader’s Notes

Framing. Joyfully point to Jesus. Some students may not yet be Christians, or may be here because of their parents; this passage is good news for them — Jesus came to preach good news to the poor, free the captive and give sight to the blind, and he did not back away even at the cross. Never put anyone on the spot, and let students pass on a question if they want to.

Two likely wrong turns

Wrong turn 1 — “serve to earn it.” Students may hear “consecrated service” as a way to get God to like them. Keep coming back to the order: grace comes first and service follows. We do not serve to be healed; we serve because we have been healed.

Wrong turn 2 — “those who sneer are just bad people.” The sermon’s point is gentler and harder: their contempt is a sign they need the gospel more urgently than they realise, and God’s favour can rest on strangers. Don’t retaliate and don’t retreat. Keep inviting. Also ask: where do I sneer?

Question helps

Q1 — Jesus is saying God has anointed him with the Spirit; he is the one who will preach good news to the poor, release captives, give sight to the blind and free the oppressed. “Today” — because he is here, the prophecy is fulfilled.

Q2 — They knew him for over twenty years and thought they knew him. “How dare he think he’s better than us?” Jesus didn’t feel sorry for himself; he turned the situation upside down.

Q3 — The heart of the problem is not that they rejected Jesus, as bad as that was, but that God had rejected them: the good news wasn’t for the Nazarenes. God’s favour rested on a foreign widow and a foreign general (compare John the Baptist, Luke 3:8). It’s like taking the birthday cake from my own child and giving it to a stranger. Favour rests on those who acknowledge Jesus as the Son of God, not merely the son of Joseph.

Q4 — Contempt for your gospel work doesn’t make it worthless; it’s a sign they’re not favoured by God and that their need is urgent. Jesus passed right through them, because it wasn’t time for him to die yet. Invite strangers and foreigners too: often it’s the stranger who’s more likely to engage in a good gospel conversation, because God has prepared them. Most of our baptisms this past year were strangers we shared the gospel with. Invite to the Welcome Home course and the Discover Course.

Q5 — The demons knew exactly who Jesus was, but theirs is not a saving knowledge: they know him and refuse to serve him. Jesus speaks with unmatched authority, then and now. Don’t show contempt for Jesus’ words, or for those who do his work.

Q6 — Capernaum wanted to keep Jesus for the benefits: a free doctor with a 100% success rate. Amazement is not understanding. Jesus came to be the Saviour, not just the Doctor; he must do what God sent him to do (v.43). Don’t reduce Jesus to a supplier of your needs; he is firstly your Lord.

Q7 — She was saved when Jesus served her by healing her, and her service arose from knowing he served her first (James Edwards calls this a “saving-serving relationship”). True knowledge of Jesus is an acknowledgement of him that is accompanied by service.

Q8 — Faith without works is useless, dead, demonic. Consecrate means to make holy: we serve because the Holy One has come to serve us. Not contempt, not confusion, but consecration.
