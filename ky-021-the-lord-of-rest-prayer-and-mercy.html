The Lord of Rest, Prayer and Mercy

Luke 6:1–19 · Sunday 15 November 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah · Sermon by Rev David Yung

Big Idea: Jesus is the Lord of Rest, the Lord of Prayer and the Lord of Mercy: true rest is not “I stop working” but “Christ has already finished the work for me”; prayer comes before action; and God often uses people who seem “impossible”.

Aim: by the end, students will

see that Jesus is Lord of the Sabbath, and that rest is a gift we receive from him, not something we earn;

learn from Jesus’ night of prayer to pray first when facing big decisions;

know that Jesus chooses people who feel “not good enough”, and bring their weak spots to him.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • Be honest: when did you last feel properly rested? Not just asleep, but rested. What keeps your brain switched on: homework, group chats, sport, games?
  • Ever been picked last for a team, or felt you weren’t good enough for something? How did it feel?

Read: Luke 6:1–19 (CSB)

1 On a Sabbath, he passed through the grainfields. His disciples were picking heads of grain, rubbing them in their hands, and eating them.

2 But some of the Pharisees said, “Why are you doing what is not lawful on the Sabbath?”

3 Jesus answered them, “Haven’t you read what David did when he and those who were with him were hungry—

4 how he entered the house of God, and took and ate the bread of the Presence, which is not lawful for any but the priests to eat? He even gave some to those who were with him.”

5 Then he told them, “The Son of Man is Lord of the Sabbath.”

6 On another Sabbath he entered the synagogue and was teaching. A man was there whose right hand was shriveled.

7 The scribes and Pharisees were watching him closely, to see if he would heal on the Sabbath, so that they could find a charge against him.

8 But he knew their thoughts and told the man with the shriveled hand, “Get up and stand here.” So he got up and stood there.

9 Then Jesus said to them, “I ask you: Is it lawful to do good on the Sabbath or to do evil, to save life or to destroy it?”

10 After looking around at them all, he told him, “Stretch out your hand.” He did, and his hand was restored.

11 They, however, were filled with rage and started discussing with one another what they might do to Jesus.

12 During those days he went out to the mountain to pray and spent all night in prayer to God.

13 When daylight came, he summoned his disciples, and he chose twelve of them, whom he also named apostles:

14 Simon, whom he also named Peter, and Andrew his brother; and James, John, Philip, and Bartholomew;

15 Matthew and Thomas; James the son of Alphaeus, and Simon called the Zealot;

16 Judas the son of James, and Judas Iscariot, who became a traitor.

17 After coming down with them, he stood on a level place with a large crowd of his disciples and a great number of people from all Judea and Jerusalem and from the seacoast of Tyre and Sidon.

18 They came to hear him and to be healed of their diseases; and those tormented by unclean spirits were made well.

19 The whole crowd was trying to touch him, because power was coming out from him and healing them all.

Context

In Jesus’ time, the Pharisees had made a huge list of extra Sabbath rules: 39 kinds of “work” you couldn’t do, like reaping and threshing grain. God’s own law actually let hungry people pick grain with their hands (Deuteronomy 23:25). The Sabbath was meant to be God’s gift of rest, but they had turned it into a burden.

Discuss

  1. (vv.1–2) What were the disciples doing, and why did the Pharisees say it was wrong? Have you ever been judged by someone’s extra rules (or judged someone else by yours)?
  2. (vv.3–5) Jesus points to King David, then says, “The Son of Man is Lord of the Sabbath.” Only God made the Sabbath. So what is Jesus saying about himself?
  3. (vv.1–5) Real rest isn’t “doing nothing”, it’s “finding rest in God”. What would it look like to have Jesus with you in the busy stuff: homework, training, chores?
  4. (vv.6–9) Why were the Pharisees watching Jesus so closely? Why does Jesus make the man stand up in front of everyone?
  5. (vv.10–11) Jesus heals with just one word. The man couldn’t fix his hand himself. What is something in your life you can’t fix yourself?
  6. (vv.11–12) The Pharisees are furious. What does Jesus do next? What do you usually do first when something big or stressful happens?
  7. (vv.13–16) Look at the twelve Jesus picked: fishermen, a tax collector, a rebel, even Judas. Would you have picked this team? What does it tell you about who Jesus uses?
  8. (vv.17–19) Jesus comes down to where the crowds are, and power comes out of him to heal. What does it mean that you can come near Jesus, not because you deserve it but because he is willing?

Live It Out

KOG 3·2·1·0 — pray for 3, connect with 2, invite 1, 0 left unloved.

  • Pray for 3 — write down three friends or classmates who don’t know Jesus yet and pray for them by name every day this week.
  • Connect with 2 — sit with two of them at lunch, play sport with them, or send a message that isn’t about homework.
  • Invite 1 — invite one of your three to KOGYouth, or to our Community Christmas Concert on 13 December. We are praying for 120 people to come and hear about Jesus.
  • 0 left unloved — look for the person sitting on their own at school or at church, and be the one who welcomes them, the way Jesus came down to the crowd.

This week’s challenge: Phone off for 20 minutes one night this week. Bring one decision or worry to God and ask, “Lord, what do you want me to do?” Then tell the group next week how it went.

Memory verse: “The Son of Man is Lord of the Sabbath.” (Luke 6:5)

Pray

  • Thank Jesus that he is Lord of the Sabbath, and that rest is his gift to us, not something we earn.
  • Ask God to help you pray first when big things come up, and to give you courage to bring your weak spots to Jesus: “Stretch out your hand.”
  • Pray by name for your three friends: that they would come to Jesus and find rest, and for a chance to invite one of them.

Leader’s Notes

Framing. Joyfully point to Jesus. Some students may not yet be Christians; never corner anyone. Many teens feel constantly “switched on” and “not good enough”. The good news: rest is a gift of grace, and Jesus chooses ordinary, unlikely people.

Two likely wrong turns

  • Wrong turn 1: “So Sundays and rules don’t matter.” Jesus didn’t abolish the Sabbath; he redefined it. He is the one it points to, and rest is found in him.
  • Wrong turn 2: “I need to be good enough before Jesus will use me.” The Twelve weren’t qualified; Jesus made them his instruments. He chooses the willing.

Question helps

Q1 — Picking grain counted as “reaping” and rubbing it as “threshing” under the Pharisees’ extra rules. But God’s law allowed it. They used human tradition to condemn others.

Q2 — Only God made the Sabbath (Genesis 2:3), so only God can say what it means. Jesus is saying, “I am the God who made the Sabbath,” and “I’m the one it points to.”

Q3 — In the sermon, a busy mum learned to pray while cooking and sing while settling the kids. Rest is knowing God is with you in everything you do.

Q4 — They wanted to catch him out (v.7). Jesus doesn’t hide; he puts the man in the middle so everyone sees. Their silence showed they cared more about rules than mercy.

Q5 — No medicine, no surgery, just a word. Real rest is “letting God do the work”. We can’t fix ourselves either; when we obey Jesus, God’s power comes into our weakness.

Q6 — No revenge, no rallying his followers. He prayed all night before choosing the Twelve. The biggest decisions are made in prayer, not by more data.

Q7 — Matthew worked for Rome; Simon the Zealot wanted Rome overthrown. Jesus put them on one team. God doesn’t look for the “qualified”; he uses the willing.

Q8 — Jesus came down: from heaven to earth, from glory to the cross. His power is grace, flowing freely to anyone who comes near.
