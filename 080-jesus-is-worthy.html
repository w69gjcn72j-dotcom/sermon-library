Jesus is worthy

Mark 14:1-31

Big Idea: Jesus gives his precious body and blood for the unworthy; no gift to him, however costly, will ever be wasted.

A beautiful, extravagant gift for Jesus

Giving up Jesus for a fee

Jesus’ precious gift to the ugly, the unworthy

As I was preparing this sermon, I had a message from my son Ezekiel.

He just finished school and he wanted some afternoon tea.

The family then had a discussion on message.

After a while, we decided: it’s KFC tonight!

And so, for the next 20 minutes, we searched online to get the best KFC deal money can buy.

We got the family deal!

It was a nice deal, thought the chickens were smaller than what they advertise on their website!

We all love a good deal, don’t we?

We want to make sure that what we’re paying with our hard-earned cash is worth what we’re paying for.

That’s why we have so many ads about sales.

We watch for sales because we want to spend our money on the best deal!

Ads make us think that we, the consumer, gets the best deal for our buck.

And it’s not just in shopping centres and online stores that we the consumers look for the best deal.

Some people carry the same attitude when they think about religion.

What does this religion have to offer?

What can it give me, if I give it my attention, my money, my time?

Do I get healing, do I get miracles, do I get rich? Is it a good deal?

In today’s passage, Mark 14:1-31, we’ll see two very different approaches to how someone determines whether Jesus is a good deal.

In short, they will be asking the question, is Jesus worthy?

There’ll be three parts to today’s talk:

A beautiful, extravagant gift for Jesus

Giving up Jesus for a fee

Jesus’ precious gift to the ugly, the unworthy

A beautiful, extravagant gift for Jesus

If you have your Bible there, please open up with me to Mark 14, beginning from verse 1.

“It was two days before the Passover and the Festival of Unleavened Bread. The chief priests and the scribes were looking for a cunning way to arrest Jesus and kill him. 2 “Not during the festival,” they said, “so that there won’t be a riot among the people.”

The Gospel of Mark is a record of what Jesus has done in his public ministry.

It has 16 chapters.

The first 10 chapters covers what the author believes to be the important things that Jesus has said and done in his three years of ministry.

However, from chapter 11 onwards, it slows right down and focuses on Jesus’ last week of public ministry.

Here in chapter 14, we’re down to the final two days before he’s crucified on the cross.

Quick question for the congregation here and those at home:

On what day of the week is the beginning of Mark 14?

This is a quick one, only 5 seconds.

Message your answers in if you’re at home. Go!

Jesus died on Good Friday.

And so, two days before the Passover would be Wednesday.

On Wednesday, the chief priests and scribes were looking for a way to arrest Jesus.

They wanted a quiet arrest because the last thing they want is a riot of from the people, between those who support Jesus (his disciples and maybe others from Galilee) and those who oppose (that is. the scribes and the priests).

While they were plotting against Jesus, Jesus and his disciples were in Bethany at the house of Simon the leper.

We don’t know much about Simon the leper, except that he was probably a leper who had been healed of leprosy.

While Jesus sits at the table, an unknown woman comes in with an alabaster jar of very expensive perfume of pure nard.

We’re told later on that it’s very valuable, up to three hundred denarii.

A denarius was about one day’s worth of wages in Jesus’ day.

And so, in today’s terms, the perfume that this woman was carrying would be up to $74000.

What does she do with a $74000 bottle of perfume?

She breaks the jar (and so she has to use all of it at once) and she pours it on Jesus’ head.

It would be like this.

One of the most expensive bottles of perfume in the world is Chanel No.5 .

Each ounce costs $5,400.

If the woman was alive today, she would’ve used a perfume like Chanel No.5 and poured it on Jesus’ head, the whole bottle, in one go.

When those around her saw this, they scolded her, they yelled at her.

“Why has this perfume been wasted,” they said, “For this perfume might have been sold and given to the poor!”

What a waste of money!

$74000 all gone in a few seconds. What a waste!

And so, they look down on her gift as a waste, a bad call of judgement.

But more than that, they look down on her.

What a stupid thing to do! What kind of stupid person would waste money like that?

But we can even go one further.

Not only are they looking down on the gift and the giver.

They’re also looking down on Jesus, the receiver of the gift.

Why spend such an expensive gift on Jesus, in one go?

Is he worth this $74000 gift? Surely not!

How does Jesus respond? Verse 6,

“Leave her alone. Why are you bothering her? She has done a noble thing for me.”

In Jesus’ eyes, she has done something noble, something beautiful.

She has done what she could and anointed Jesus’ body for burial.

Up to this point in the Gospel of Mark, Jesus has told his disciples at least three times explicitly, as well as through a number of parables implicitly,

That he’s going to die.

Jesus has been saying time and time again: he is going to die!

However, it seems like the disciples haven’t been listening or at the very least haven’t understood what Jesus has been saying.

Though not one of the disciples, this woman anoints Jesus’ body with this expensive gift, probably her most treasured possessions, to prepare Jesus for his death.

Even though the disciples don’t get it, this woman seem to have understood that suffering and death is central to the good news that Jesus has been preaching.

Here, Jesus is not dismissing the poor.

It’s important that we look after the poor; it’s something that we don’t do enough.

And giving money to the poor would definitely be a good thing to do.

But at that moment, two days before his death, it’s even more important to prepare Jesus for his death than to give to the poor.

There will be poor people always, but they only have Jesus for a short period of time.

And so, the woman does the right thing and anoints Jesus with her most precious gift.

And Jesus adds in verse 9, “Truly I tell you, wherever the gospel is proclaimed in the whole world, what she has done will also be told in memory of her.”

Jesus truly appreciates her gift to him.

A gift to Jesus, no matter how extravagant, no matter how costly, is never wasted.

Many years ago, I met a very promising young man who was working for an international corporation.

He was very smart and would’ve travelled far up the corporate ladder.

However, in time, he decided to give his life to full-time gospel ministry.

Now, some people might think, what a waste!

He’s done so well at School and University, he’s obviously got the skills to excel.

He could’ve kept travelling up the corporate ladder, made a lot of money, give them to the Church, look after this family and, even give some money to the poor.

But when asked about his past, he simply says, “they’re all rubbish.”

That’s not to say that he doesn’t appreciate what he’s had.

Rather, all these are not worth comparing with what he has in Christ.

Though it feels like he’s given up a lot for Christ, it’s not a waste.

Just like the woman in the passage, my friend has given a beautiful gift to Jesus.

There are many Bible passages that calls Christians to be generous to God, probably because it’s not in our human nature to be generous to God.

But here, Jesus says that whatever gift we give to God, if it’s given sincerely and sacrificially, will never be wasted.

I’m so glad that a number of us here give sacrificially to God.

It might be your money, it might be your time, it might be your talents.

Know that our gifts to God will never be wasted

Even though other people might not see or appreciate what you’ve given to God, God knows, Jesus knows.

And so, don’t hold back, give of your best, your most precious to God.

However, even while Jesus praises the woman for what she’s given him, there is one man in the room who did hold back.

Whereas the woman gives an extravagant gift to Jesus, this man is thinking how much he can make off Jesus.

His name is Judas Iscariot.

Giving up Jesus for a fee

And he’s giving up Jesus for a fee.

Look with me at verses 10-11, “Then Judas Iscariot, one of the Twelve, went to the chief priests to betray Jesus to them. 11 And when they heard this, they were glad and promised to give him money. So he started looking for a good opportunity to betray him.”

Here, Jesus is setting a limit to Jesus’ worth.

He wants to give Jesus a monetary value.

He wants to know how much Jesus is worth and he’s willing to give up Jesus at the right price.

That’ what we do now in our society, don’t we?

In English, we use to say to one another, “How do you do?”

Now we say, “What do you do?”

We want to know what someone does, then figure out how much he earns, and then place him or her either above or below us on the socio-economic ladder.

You meet someone who’s the owner of a company: she’s up here.

You meet someone who’s a doctor: he’s here.

You meet someone who’s a plumber: maybe here.

You meet a famous actor: Up here

You meet someone who’s unemployed: down there.

You meet an unemployed actor: down there.

We place a person’s worth according to how much they earn.

That’s essentially what Judas was doing to Jesus.

How much does Jesus worth?

Maybe Judas was reacting to the extravagant gift that the woman has given to Jesus.

Surely Jesus is not worth that much! Surely he’s not worth having one year’s of perfume poured down on his head.

He doesn’t agree with what the woman thought of Jesus and decided to set his own value on Jesus instead.

Now, remember that this is happening on Wednesday.

Jesus will be arrested late on Thursday night and he will be dead on a cross on Friday.

And so, that means that Judas only takes 24 hours to plot Jesus’ betrayal.

24 hours! 24 hours for a disciple to sell Jesus out.

When it comes to executing betrayals, Judas is ruthless.

But it comes as no surprise to Jesus

Jesus knows exactly what’s happening.

On Thursday, on the first day of Unleavened Bread, Jesus’s disciples asked Jesus where he wants them to go to prepare the Passover.

Jesus tells them exactly who to follow and what to do once they get there.

Jesus knows about the room that’s already set up for the Passover.

Once they start to have the Passover meal proper, he tells them plainly in verse 18, “Truly I tell you, one of you will betray me—one who is eating with me.”

When they heard it, they each replied to Jesus, one by one, “Surely Not I?”

This reminds me of a game that many of the youth are playing.

Has anyone here played “Among Us”?

Basically, it’s an online game where each person has to guess who the imposter is.

That’s what happening here, with the exception of Judas Iscariot, no one knows who the imposter is.

Judas is just like anyone of them; he’s not more suspicious than anyone else, otherwise they would’ve guessed it.

No, Judas the imposter, would’ve also said, “Surely not I”.

But Jesus affirms it by saying that it’s one of the Twelve, one who is dipping bread in the bowl with him.

The betrayer is definitely here!

Then he says this in verse 21, “21 For the Son of Man will go just as it is written about him, but woe to that man by whom the Son of Man is betrayed! It would have been better for him if he had not been born.”

Even though Judas is responsible for betraying Jesus, even that betrayal is under God’s plan.

That’s the comfort that Christians can have:

In the midst of human evil, God is still in control; God will still bring his purposes about, even though people have evil intent.

Even so, this doesn’t take the responsibility away from Judas.

It would have been better if Judas the Betrayer, Judas the Imposter, hasn’t been born.

What led Judas to betray Jesus?

He’s set a price on Jesus; he’s decided to give Jesus a price tag.

What price do we set on Jesus?

To find out, I went on EBay and typed in Jesus.

A lot of necklaces came up and so I typed in Jesus head.

Here’s the first thing that comes up” Dashboard Jesus Figure: $16.00.

It’s a Jesus that you stick on top of the dashboard in your car.

I don’t have one and I don’t suggest you get it either.

But for $16, you can get Jesus bopping on your dashboard.

It’s a cheap Jesus; it’s a disposable Jesus; it’s a $16 dollar Jesus

That’s what happens when you place a price on Jesus.

When Judas put a figure on Jesus, Jesus becomes a 30 silver-coin Jesus, the amount that he’d agree to in order to betray Jesus.

Judas has cheapened Jesus to a dollar figure.

You cheapen Jesus when you put a price tag on Jesus.

But we can fall in the same trap too, can’t we?

Though we might not pit a 30 silver-coin price tag on Jesus, we can put other price-tags on Jesus.

Maybe to us he’s a $20 Jesus, or whatever spare change / spare time I have in my pocket at that time.

Jesus is only worth what I can spare.

To others, Jesus might be the $80K Jesus, or whatever wages I get in a year Jesus.

He’s a Jesus that I can put up with as long as he’s or a threat to my job.

If having Jesus means losing my job, then I have to give up Jesus.

What price tag do we put on Jesus.

Friends, beware of putting a price-tag on Jesus.

Whenever you put a price tag on Jesus, you cheapen Jesus and you open yourself to the very real danger of betraying Jesus.

Just as Judas has set a price on Jesus and betrayed him for that price, we, too, will be tempted to betray Jesus if we come across a price that we’ve set on Jesus.

It might be monetary, it might be a relationship, it might be a career.

If we choose something over Jesus, if we value something or someone more than Jesus, then we’re no better than Judas, who betrayed Jesus.

Judas’s price tag for Jesus was thirty silver coins; for thirty silver coins he would give up Jesus.

What’s your price tag for Jesus?

Though only Judas out of the Twelve disciples betrayed Jesus, the others don’t do better.

They, too, would leave and desert Jesus when the going gets tough.

Jesus has said that all of them would fall away.

And here they all are, having the Passover, the most holy meal in the Jewish calendar, with Jesus.

Though all the disciples will prove to either betray or desert Jesus, there’s good news for them.

Jesus’ precious gift to the ugly, the unworthy

In his Last Supper with them, Jesus shows them that he’s about to give his most precious gift to the ugly and the unworthy.

Jesus will give his body and blood to these very disciples and all who would be his disciples in the future.

Look with me at verse 22, “As they were eating, he took bread, blessed and broke it, gave it to them, and said, “Take it; this is my body.”

The bread that Jesus gave out would be unleavened bread, much like this one.

As the head of the household, Jesus would break it and give it to his disciples.

As Jesus gives out the bread in the Passover meal, he changes the usual words and applies it to himself.

Jesus says, “This is my Body”

And taking the cup and giving thanks, he gave it to them and they drank it.

And he said, in verse 24, ““This is my blood of the covenant,, which is poured out for many.”

In the Old Testament, Moses sprinkled the blood of animals to mark the Old Covenant, the promise that God makes with his people Israel.

Here, Jesus’ blood marks the beginning of a new covenant, a new promise that God makes with all those who would trust in Jesus.

Jesus gives his body, his very self.

Jesus gives his blood, his very life for a people who don’t deserve it, for a people who are unworthy to receive it.

This reminds me of some of the awards that used to be given in schools (I’m not sure whether they do this anymore)

It’s called the Participation Award. Do schools still give it?

It’s for people who have participated in a competition but they didn’t do well enough to win any major awards.

And so, rather than making them feel bad because they haven’t received anything, they receive a Participation award.

They got an award for simply participating.

It’s a very gracious award because they receive it simply by participating, not by winning.

That’s a little like what’s happened in what Jesus has done for the disciples.

Jesus is about to give them his body and his blood.

He will give up his very self to save many people, including the disciples who were about to desert him.

Did they deserve Jesus? No!

They don’t deserve Jesus’ death for them, just as we don’t deserve Jesus’ death for us.

None of us deserve Jesus’ death for us.

We’re totally unworthy.

That’s why this gift of Jesus is even more beautiful than the woman’s gift to Jesus.

The woman gave her beautiful, extravagant gift to a worthy recipient, Jesus.

But Jesus gave his beautiful, extravagant gift to unworthy recipients like us.

This is what the Bible calls grace!

He does it not because we’re good and beautiful on the inside.

He’s Jesus; he knows who we are really like on the inside.

He sees our attitude towards him.

He even knows the price we set on him; he knows how cheaply we view him.

But he still loves us the same!

It’s an amazing, incredible love! It’s an extravagant, abundant grace!

Jesus gives his precious, precious life for unworthy sinners like us.

That’s what striking about this passage that we’ve just read.

Throughout the whole passage, we see unworthy priests, unworthy disciples, people who would betray him and people who would desert him.

Even the great Peter, who promises to never fall away, Jesus said would deny him not once but three times.

And this is the message we see not only here in this passage but throughout the whole gospel, throughout the whole Bible.

That Jesus, the Worthy One, gives his life for the unworthy.

That’s God’s grace!

It’s in light of God’s grace that Christians live and serve the Lord Jesus Christ.

One such Christian is a bishop by the name of Polycarp.

Polycarp was Bishop of Smyrna, a Greek city in first century AD.

When he was very old, he was captured by the Roman army.

The Roman governor asked him to deny Jesus Christ and promises that if he will, his life will be spared.

He’s asking for Polycarp to betray Jesus at the price of his life.

But Polycarp replied, “Fourscore and six years (that is 86 years) have I served him, and he has never done me injury; how then can I now blaspheme my King and saviour?”

Even though Governor put a price tag on Jesus, namely Polycarp’s life, Polycarp didn’t think that even his life was enough to betray Jesus who’s never done him injury.

For Polycarp and for all genuine Christians, Jesus is worthy of all that we have, all of our praise.

In fact, he’s worthy of more than all that we have, even more than all of our lives.

Even if we can give Jesus our all, it’s not worth what he’s given us.

When we come together, especially during Holy Communion, we remember that Jesus, the Worthy One, died for unworthy ones like us.

It’s in light of Jesus’ precious gift of himself to unworthy people like us,

That we give to Jesus our most precious gifts, knowing that it’ll never be wasted.

What does this mean for us?

Jesus is more worthy than even our most precious gifts.

Don’t think about giving Jesus the minimum of what you can give.

Don’t think what’s the minimal gift I can give to Jesus.

Don’t think what’s the minimal time I can give to Jesus.

Think about the maximum time, the maximum gift that I can give to Jesus.

My wife Jenny is a lot better at this than me.

When we work on our budget to think about what we can give to Church and give to Christians in need, I would give a figure.

She’s usually happy with that figure.

However, if I say we can give more, she’s even happier.

She’s happy for us to give as much as we can, even if it means we live with less.

And I’m so happy that we’re doing this as a Church too!

Later on, in the AGM, you’ll see in the report that we’re giving a substantial amount of what we have to missionaries and to prepare to train other people outside of our Church.

You’ll see that we’re not a big Church and we don’t have a lot of income.

But under God, we’re using what God’s giving us so that others will hear about Jesus, so that God will be glorified.

It might sound like a lot but it’s an unworthy gift from an unworthy people.

Indeed, if we can, we want to give more because Jesus is more worthy than even our most precious gifts.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How have I responded to how precious Jesus is?

Have I set a price tag, a minimal cost, for Jesus?

Have I ever been accused of being wasteful for giving too much for Jesus?

Friends, Jesus is far more worthy than even our most precious gifts.

Let’s give him thanks as we come together in prayer. Let’s pray:

“Father, we thank you for Jesus. We thank you for his most precious gift of his very self: his body and his blood. We’re not worthy of such love; we’re not worthy of such grace. Father, by your Spirit, increase our love for Jesus. Please help us get a better glimpse of the glorious grace that’s found in him. Though we’re utterly unworthy, please use all that we have, all of our lives, for the glory of Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
