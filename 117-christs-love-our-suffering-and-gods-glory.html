Intro

Over the past few months, Jiawu and Range have been leading a number of people through the Christianity Explored course.

Po Ki and I have used the same course in English in the past.

It’s a wonderful course to help people understand Christianity.

One of the resources in this course is the “tough” questions section.

Here, there are a number of common questions that people ask about the Christian faith.

For example, doesn’t science explain everything?

Aren’t all religions basically the same?

One of the most common questions that many people ask is this: If God exists, why is there suffering?

You might have heard this question before; you might have even asked this question yourself.

Why would a God who is all-good, all-loving, all-powerful allow bad things to happen to good people?

Someone might say, if God is love, he mustn’t be very powerful because he couldn’t stop the bad things from happening to good people.

And if God is all-powerful, he mustn’t be very loving because he’s allowing these things to happen to good people.

And so, he’s either not very loving or not very powerful.

Christians, too, can often find ourselves asking the same question.

Jesus has often said that he loves his people.

If he loves his people, why does he let them suffer?

That’s what we’re going to think about today as we look at our passage.

So if you have your Bible there, please open up to John 11, beginning from verse 1.

It’s a big topic and we won’t be able to cover everything there is to know about how Christians face suffering.

But it’ll be a start.

We’ll see three things in today’s passage:

Why does Jesus let his own people suffer?

Jesus loves and leads his own to glory

Walk in his glorious light

Before we look further into it, let’s pray, “Father, please open our hearts and our minds so that we can have a better glimpse of you and your glory as you reveal yourself through your Son. It’s in his name that we pray, Amen.”

Why does Jesus let people he loves to suffer?

In today’s passage we meet a family from Bethany, whom Jesus know and love.

We know that Mary anointed Jesus with perfume and wiped his feet with her hair.

We’ll find out more about it in chapter 12.

We know from Luke 10 that Martha is a hard worker who can get distracted by her busyness.

We don’t know much about Lazarus, except for the fact that there’s no record of any words that he’s said.

And of course, we know that Lazarus has been sick.

In each of the first four verses of this chapter, we’re reminded that Lazarus was sick.

Look with me at how Jesus responded to the news about Lazarus, in verse 4, “When Jesus heard it, he said, “This sickness will not end in death but is for the glory of God, so that the Son of God may be glorified through it.”

That sounds very positive.

The sickness won’t end in death! Sounds like Jesus is going to heal him.

It would’ve been a relief for the sisters to hear, for it must have been very had for the sisters Mary and Martha to see their brother’s condition.

It would’ve been very hard to see Lazarus suffer.

For those of us who have treated sick kids, or other family members who were sick, you’ll know how difficult it is to see someone you love suffering through a debilitating sickness.

It’s a horrible feeling; you try your best, but you often feel like you can’t do much to relieve their suffering.

That’s why what Jesus does here seem so strange.

Verses 5-6, “Now Jesus loved Martha, her sister, and Lazarus. 6 So when he heard that he was sick, he stayed two more days in the place where he was.”

Throughout the passage, there’s plenty of evidence to suggest that this family is not just one of many.

It’s a family whom Jesus love.

Jesus loves all three members of this family and yet Jesus deliberately waits for two more days.

The key word to notice is the first word of verse 6: So

If you have the Chinese Bible, add the word, “所以”.

Jesus loved Martha, her sister and Lazarus, so he stayed two more days.

It’s because he loved them that he stayed two more days and waited until Lazarus died.

Why would he do that?

They were some of his closest friends!

To use the language from the previous chapter, they were his sheep!

They were people whom he said he would lay down his life for.

Why did he wait until Lazarus died?

Jesus waited not because it was an error or accident.

Jesus deliberately waited and would eventually turn up, four days after Lazarus died.

Why did he allow this to happen to this family whom he love?

This is a theme that we see throughout the Bible.

Jesus allows his people to suffer, Jesus allows bad things to happen to his people.

In fact, in Philippians 1:29, we read, “29 For it has been granted to you on Christ’s behalf not only to believe in him, but also to suffer for him,”

Jesus asks God that we not only believe in him but to suffer for him.

The suffering that Christians face are not random; they are not bad luck.

Rather, it’s suffering that Jesus asks on behalf of us.

Jesus allows suffering, indeed, asks for suffering for those who are his.

Why? How could a loving God do that!

Surely, there’s something wrong about that!

It’s wrong for good people, especially people who believe Jesus, who love Jesus, to suffer.

However, let’s think about that for a minute.

Why do we think that it’s wrong?

Why do we think that suffering is bad and shouldn’t happen?

Is it because we know instinctively that suffering and death are innately wrong in this world?

That suffering and death aren’t what life is supposed to be about?

Often, when we see the absence of what is good that we long for its presence.

And if I can push it further, often it’s the absence of the goodness and kindness of God that pushes us to long for, to yearn for the presence of a good and loving God.

The beloved Christian writer, CS Lewis, who watched his beloved wife died of cancer, put it this way, “But pain insists upon being attended to. God whispers to us in our pleasures, speaks in our conscience, but shouts in our pains: it is his megaphone to rouse a deaf world.”

Some of us here might be going through a difficult time, maybe even a painful time.

And in our sorrow, in our despair, we might jump to the conclusion that God doesn’t love us or that he can’t do anything to save us.

But before you jump to any of these conclusions, come simply before his presence in prayer.

He knows full well the suffering and the pain that you’re going through, better than anyone around you and better than you yourself.

Come before him and ask him why; it’s Ok to be honest with God and ask him.

There’s nothing that you can say that will surprise him.

While there are different reasons why we suffer, what Jesus says to the family and Bethany gives us a glimpse on why Jesus allows suffering to happen to his people at all.

Jesus loves and leads his own to glory

Jesus allows his people to suffer because his glory is better than life.

Jesus says, “This sickness will not end in death but is for the glory of God, so that the Son of God may be glorified through it.”

The main thing here is not whether Lazarus will be healed.

Rather the main thing on Jesus’ mind is the glory of God, how God will be glorified.

Here, the glory of God is less about singing praises and more about God revealing himself to us.

By ourselves, we can’t come to see God, we can’t comprehend God.

Indeed, left to ourselves, we are blind to God’s goodness and glory.

But in the suffering and indeed, the death of Lazarus, we will see a glimpse God’s glory.

And seeing that, seeing God’s glory, is better than life.

In fact, it’s precisely because Jesus loves this family in Bethany so much, that he waited for two days, waited for Lazarus to die, so that this family, along with those around them, will see the glory of God and that he Son of God may glorified.

God’s glory is that good!

Leo Reppond was a little boy born with a rare form of disorder affecting his vision.

And so, for the first few months in his life, he couldn’t see.

He would’ve heard his parents’ voice and see shapes but he couldn’t see his parents.

That was until the optometrists put a specially made glasses for Leo.

He smiled at his parents when he saw them with his own eyes!

Jesus wants us to see something even greater!

Jesus wants us to see the very glory of God.

We might have seen echoes of God’s goodness in our world before.

But now, Jesus wants us to see God’s glory.

To show us his glory, God’s sent his One and Only Son into the world.

To see his glory, we must come to the Person of Jesus.

That’s what life is all about.

One of the songs that we’ve learnt recently starts off with a question:

What is our hope in life and death? Answer: Christ alone, Christ alone

Romans 8:18 reminds us, “18 For I consider that the sufferings of this present time are not worth comparing with the glory that is going to be revealed to us.”

Whatever suffering we face will pale in comparison to the glory that will be revealed to us!

Jesus loves you and Jesus loves me so much that he will allow you to go through the pain and the suffering so that you can see his glory.

Jesus allows suffering to happen to us because he knows God’s glory is worth it.

God’s glory is even better than life.

And so, he must trust him and his timing.

Throughout this passage, we see that Jesus is not passively driven by circumstances.

Rather, Jesus is in control the whole time.

At precisely the right time, he goes to Judea.

The disciples remind him that there are risks involved but they will still go.

Jesus tells the disciples that Lazarus is asleep, and Jesus will go to wake him up.

Jesus had to explain to the disciples that he’s not talking about natural sleep but death and Jesus will wake him up from the dead.

He tells them that he’s glad he wasn’t there so that they may believe.

He stays two days, until after Lazarus has died, to show God’s glory and to give an opportunity for the disciples to believe.

Though we might not always see it at the time, we know that Jesus’ timing was, is, and will always be perfect.

Last week, many Chinese Christian ministers and fellow workers met together at a conference to encourage one another and to hear from God’s word.

The Archbishop Kanishka shared a story about a man that he met at a Sydney church.

It was a church in a fairly poor suburb in Sydney.

Seeing that it was a special Sunday, the people were handing out pamphlets to people on the street to invite people to come.

A man came to the Church service for the first time.

Later on, the man told the minister that he was about to end his life.

As he was going, someone gave him a pamphlet and so he came to Church instead.

And as a result, he decided to keep living and was reading the Bible with the minister.

In God’s perfect timing, he saved this man’s life.

We may not understand why we’re going through a difficult time.

We may not understand why we’re suffering.

But God does; nothing happens outside of his perfect plan.

Even the suffering of the ones he love are not outside of his plan.

And so, we can trust him.

It’s out of his love that he’s placed us exactly where we are.

Whatever situation you might find yourself in, seek God’s glory in it.

Ask, how is Jesus glorified in this?

Pray that Jesus will be glorified in this.

Trust his perfect timing; the God who loves you is in control.

Walk in his glorious light

And walk in his glorious light.

Look with me at verse 9, “Aren’t there twelve hours in a day?” Jesus answered. “If anyone walks during the day, he doesn’t stumble, because he sees the light of this world.”

The safe time to walk is daytime.

And so, walk in the light.

Do the work that God’s given you to do in the light; live the life that God calls you to live in the light.

Jesus is the light of the world; walk in him and you won’t stumble.

Now’s the time to follow him, now’s the time to obey him, now’s the time to walk in the light.

On the other hand, if you don’t have Jesus, if you don’t have the light of the world, you will stumble in darkness.

You will stumble and fall.

And so, walk in Jesus, walk in the light of the world.

You won’t stumble but it won’t be easy.

Listen to what Thomas says at the very end of our passage, “Then Thomas (called “Twin”) said to his fellow disciples, “Let’s go too so that we may die with him.”

He was calling his fellow disciples to join him as they follow Jesus to his death.

Like many others in the Bible, he spoke more than he knew.

Though they fled when Jesus was led to his death, most of the disciples would indeed ultimately follow Jesus, even if it meant death.

Christian legend has it that Thomas himself was killed by Hindu priests along the western coast of India.

The call to follow Jesus to the death is not only for the first disciples but for all who would follow Jesus, for Jesus said, “If anyone wants to follow after me, let him deny himself, take up his cross daily, and follow me.”

Though it will be difficult, though there will be suffering, though there might even be death, it will be worth it.

God’s glory is worth it.

Jesus loves his own, Jesus is all-powerful, and Jesus will take us to God’s glory.

I was in the GAFCON conference in Canberra a few weeks ago and I met an ex-missionary by the name of Alan Wood.

Here’s a picture of us.

Alan told me that he was a missionary in Nigeria.

Though there are many Christians in Nigeria over the past fifteen years or so, it’s been very difficult.

That’s because am Islamic militant group by the name of Boko Haram had came to power.

For almost two years, many Christians stop going to Church, not because they were afraid of COVID but because they were afraid of being attacked by terrorists.

It was a very difficult time.

However, Alan and his wife continued to pray.

As a lecturer in a Bible College, Alan was teaching the students who would be pastors, to remain faithful to God, even if it means death.

Listen to what Alan says, “These young men were offering themselves to train as pastors to go out into villages in the north, and possibly lose their lives because of their faith. It was a work of God, I believe.”

These young men have seen for themselves how dangerous it is to be a Christian, especially a Christian minister.

But they gave themselves to God first, for they know that Jesus loves them and that he is able to lead them to glory.

They know that God’s glory is worth it.

I thank God for people like Alan who faithfully teaches God’s word and sends ministers in the mission field.

Can we say what Thomas has said?

Can we say with our fellow Christians, “Let’s go too so that we may die with him.”?

That means that we’re willing to go with Jesus to his death and invite others to join us in following Jesus.

My prayer is that we will encourage one another, weekly, daily if we can, to follow Jesus.

For his love and his glory are better than life.

Friends, our Lord Jesus loves you, so trust him even in suffering to lead you to his glory.

He loves you and he’s all-powerful; trust him to lead you through your pain and suffering.

Trust him as he leads you to glory.

Trust Jesus, he loves you!

Follow Jesus in his light; follow him by obeying his teaching.

Trust him with whatever difficulty you may be facing.

Some of us might be grieving over the health of a family member.

However, I suspect many of us will be grieving over the spiritual health of our family members.

I think everyone in our Church has at least one family member who isn’t a Christian yet.

I think that many of us will know the tension and the difficulty of having family members who are not interested or even oppose Christianity.

It’s very hard and we are saddened for them.

Even so, pray that God will be glorified, even in the midst of the tension and difficulty.

Look to see how Jesus will be glorified in this.

Trust him to lead you to his glory.

Trust Jesus, because he loves you.

Friends, here are the three questions for this week:

How much do we trust in Jesus’ love for us?

Jesus is keen for us to see God’s glory. Are we keen to see God’s glory?

Is there an area of my life that I’m finding it hard to follow Jesus?

Friends, Jesus is keen to lead you to glory.

Trust Jesus, because he loves you.

Let’s pray, “Father, we thank you for the love you’ve shown us in the Lord Jesus. We thank you that it’s in him that we see your glory. Forgive us for the time when we doubted your love for us. By your Spirit, please open our eyes, that we may see your love and your glory. Help us to treasure your love and glory more than life itself. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
