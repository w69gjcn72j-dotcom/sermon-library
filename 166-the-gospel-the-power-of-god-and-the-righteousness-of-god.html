The Gospel: The Power of God and the Righteousness of God

Romans 1:16-17 · nursing home, 28 June 2026
Big IdeaDon't be embarrassed by the gospel — it is God's power for salvation, and it shows God's righteousness from faith to faith.

Romans 1:16–17

Preached: 28 June 2026 | Nursing Home Adaptation

Big Idea: Don't be embarrassed by the gospel — it is God's power for salvation, and it shows God's righteousness from faith to faith.

Introduction

Many of you have spent decades walking with the Lord. You have seen the world change around you — fashions in thought, popular ideas about God, people who once respected Christianity now dismissing it. Perhaps you have had conversations with family members who pushed back on your faith. 'Isn't that a bit old-fashioned?' they might say. Or simply, 'I just don't believe that.'

How do you respond? And how do you hold firm yourself, when the world suggests that trusting the gospel is somehow naive or outdated?

The Apostle Paul faced the same challenge. He was about to visit Rome — the greatest city in the ancient world, full of impressive temples, sophisticated philosophers, and a hundred competing religions. And yet he writes with complete confidence: 'I am not ashamed of the gospel.'

Let's look together at Romans 1:16–17.

"For I am not ashamed of the gospel, because it is the power of God that brings salvation to everyone who believes: first to the Jew, then to the Gentile. For in the gospel the righteousness of God is revealed — a righteousness that is by faith from first to last, just as it is written: The righteous will live by faith."

Paul shows us three things:

1. Unashamed: The Gospel Is God's Power to Save

2. Revealed: The Gospel Unveils God's Righteousness

3. Received: Righteous by Faith, from faith to faith

Let us pray. Heavenly Father, please open our minds and our hearts that we may understand your word rightly, for Jesus' sake. Amen.

1. Unashamed: The Gospel Is God's Power to Save

"For I am not ashamed of the gospel."

What a striking way for Paul to begin. The word 'For' connects this sentence to everything he has just written — his longing to visit Rome, his eagerness to preach. To outsiders, that eagerness must have seemed absurd. Rome had grand temples, celebrated philosophers, and the full might of the empire. Christianity, by comparison, was a movement centred on a crucified Jewish carpenter. It did not look powerful. It did not look prestigious.

And yet Paul says: I am not ashamed.

Many of you know what it feels like to hold fast to something the world dismisses. Perhaps over the years you have sat at a family dinner table and quietly but firmly said what you believe — only to be met with a polite smile or a gentle change of subject. Perhaps you have wondered, in the quiet of the night, whether your decades of faith were well-placed. Whether it all holds up.

Paul says: it holds up. Better than that — the gospel is the power of God.

Not merely an inspiring story. Not just good moral advice. The gospel is God's own power at work, bringing salvation to everyone who believes.

Consider this: many of us, as the years go on, feel our physical strength diminishing. Things that once felt easy now require effort. And yet — here is the wonder of the gospel — God's power does not diminish. It is not weakened by time or age or infirmity. The same gospel that brought you from death to life when you first believed is just as powerful today, in this very room, at this very moment.

God's gospel transforms those who are spiritually dead into people who are spiritually alive. No human effort brings this about. The gospel does. And it is available to everyone who believes — whether Jew or Greek, young or old, whether you came to faith in childhood or in old age.

If you have believed the gospel, you have experienced the power of God. That is nothing to be ashamed of. That is everything.

2. Revealed: The Gospel Unveils God's Righteousness

Look at verse 17: 'For in the gospel the righteousness of God is revealed.'

The word righteousness simply means rightness — what is truly, perfectly right. And Paul's point is this: if you want to know what God thinks is right, you will not find it by looking within yourself, or by consulting the shifting opinions of the age. You find it in the gospel.

This was the turning point for a man named Martin Luther, five hundred years ago. Luther was a deeply devout monk who had spent years trying to make himself right before God — prayer, fasting, confession, penance. And the more he tried, the more wretched he felt. He knew he fell short. He was frightened of God.

Then one day he read this very passage — Romans 1:16–17 — and everything changed. He came to understand that the righteousness of God is not primarily the standard that condemns us. It is the gift that saves us. In the gospel, God does not merely hold up a perfect standard and demand we meet it. In the gospel, God gives us his righteousness — the perfect righteousness of Jesus Christ — and credits it to all who trust in him.

Luther said he felt 'born anew, as if the gates of heaven had opened.'

That, friends, is what the gospel reveals. God in his righteousness looked at people like us — people who fall short, whose record is not spotless — and sent his own Son to be perfectly right in our place. Jesus lived the life we could not live, died the death we deserved, and rose again in victory. And in the gospel, what Jesus did for us is declared to be ours by faith.

If you have spent years — perhaps your whole life — knowing that you are not perfect, that you have sinned and fallen short: good news. God's righteousness in the gospel is not about your record. It is about Christ's record, freely given to you.

That is what God thinks is right. And it is glorious.

3. Received: Righteous by Faith, from faith to faith

"A righteousness that is by faith from first to last" — or as some translations put it, "from faith to faith."

This phrase captures the way the gospel travels through history. It came first to the Jewish people, who had been waiting for their Messiah. The disciples — all Jews — believed in Jesus and began telling others. From their faith, the gospel spread to the Gentiles. And from one believer to the next, across centuries and continents, the faith was passed on.

You are part of that chain. Someone first told you about Jesus. Perhaps a parent or grandparent, a Sunday school teacher, a faithful friend, a minister now long gone to glory. Their faith kindled yours. And over your lifetime, you have passed that faith on — to children, grandchildren, friends. You may not fully see all that God has done through your witness and your prayers. But the chain continues.

This is exactly what the Old Testament promised: 'The righteous will live by faith.' Not by sight, not by strength, not by achievement. By faith. And that is as true lying in a bed as it is standing in a pulpit. Faith is not a young person's occupation. It is the posture of every soul before God, at every age, in every condition.

Some of you may feel that your most active days of service are behind you. But hear this: your prayers are not behind you. Your faith is not behind you. Every prayer you offer for your family, for the church, for the spread of the gospel, is a real and powerful act of participation in the work of God. You are still bearing fruit. You are still, in the best sense, from faith to faith.

The Apostle Paul himself, near the end of his life, wrote from a Roman prison: 'I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith.' That is the crown held out to each one of you — not a crown won by doing more, but by keeping the faith to the end. By trusting, still, in the gospel that has sustained you all these years.

Conclusion

Don't be embarrassed by the gospel. It is God's power for salvation — the same power that first reached into your heart and brought you to life. It reveals God's righteousness — not a righteousness you had to earn, but one freely given in Christ. And it is received by faith — a faith that has been handed down through generations, and now rests with you.

Your church family is praying for you. We long to see you. And we give thanks to God for every year of faithful witness your lives represent. The gospel that brought you to faith will carry you home.

Closing Prayer

Heavenly Father, thank you for your gospel — the good news that in Jesus, your righteousness has been revealed and given to all who trust in him. Thank you that your power does not weaken with age, and that faith is never wasted. Please strengthen us to hold fast to what we believe. Help us not to be ashamed of the gospel, even when the world dismisses it. Remind us that we are beloved, that our prayers are heard, and that the faith we have kept is precious in your sight. And where we have passed this faith on to others, please let it bear fruit — long after we are gone. We ask this in the name of Jesus, our righteousness and our hope. Amen.

Questions for Reflection

1. When have you been tempted to feel embarrassed or uncertain about your faith? What has helped you hold fast to the gospel over the years?

2. What does it mean to you personally that God's righteousness is a gift — not something you have earned? How has understanding this shaped the way you think about God and about yourself?

3. Who first passed the faith on to you? And who has God given you to encourage in that faith — in whatever ways are open to you now?
