The Son’s absence and the Spirit of Truth

John 16.1_24

Big Idea: Ask the Father and you will receive complete joy, for the Son had sent the Spirit of Truth, who convicts the world and guides the disciples into all the truth.

Why isn’t Jesus here, in our suffering?

The Spirit who convicts and guides

Ask the Father, and receive complete joy

Let’s pray,

“Father, by your Spirit, please burn your words into our hearts, that we may see Jesus more clearly. And please continue to let your words burn in our hearts, so that we will not rest until we share your words of life with someone this week. In Jesus’ name, Amen.”

1. Why isn’t Jesus here, in our suffering?

As many of you know, Jenny and I have three children.

One is about to teach children the Bible in a Holiday Club, one’s about to finish high school and one’s flown to the Sunshine Coast for a conference yesterday morning.

We thank God for them! It’s a joy to see them growing into young adults as they continue to walk in the Lord.

I still remember when they were a lot younger, Jenny and I had to find a baby-sitter whenever both of us need to be away somewhere.

Sometimes, it will be our relatives; other times, it will be some of our Church friends.

We didn’t want to leave the children on their own.

What if they were in danger? What if they hurt themselves?

We wanted to make sure there was a responsible adult who can look after them.

And even though they’re older and bigger now, and they can generally look after themselves, we still want to make sure they’re safe, we still want to make sure they don’t fall into danger.

And if do fall into any danger, we want would want to be there to protect them.

That’s why it’s a little strange when we read in the Bible about Jesus getting ready to leave his disciples behind, even though he knew that they would be in danger.

In fact, Jesus says that it’s good that he’s left us behind to suffer in the world.

Why would he leave his followers behind by themselves knowing that they will suffer?

Why wouldn’t he stay to personally protect us?

Why is it good that Jesus has left us behind to suffer in the world?

If you have your Bible there, please open up to John 16, beginning from verse 1.

For our Chinese brothers and sisters, we’re using the Chinese New Version on the screen today.

I think this translation is a little clearer for today’s passage.

We’re printed copies of this passage out for your reference.

John 16:1, “I have told you these things to keep you from stumbling. 2 They will ban you from the synagogues. In fact, a time is coming when anyone who kills you will think he is offering service to God.”

Jesus has just said that the world will hate the disciples because of him.

They will ban the disciples from synagogues.

Synagogues are places where the Jews gather to hear from God’s word, to worship God and to share life with one another.

The synagogue is where the Jewish community meets.

To ban a Jewish person is to basically ban the person from the Jewish community.

In today’s world, it’ll be like “cancelling” them from the community.

That’s what the first Jewish disciples of Jesus were told to expect: They would be banned from synagogues.

More than that, people would kill them and think that they’re doing God a service.

That is, people in the world would kill Christians, thinking that they’ve done God a favour.

And that’s what we’ve seen happening to Christians over the years.

The early Christians were sentenced to death for their beliefs.

They were fed to wild animals, they were framed for crimes they didn’t commit.

Even today, in some countries whole families would be sentenced to prison for believing the Bible.

Why does the world hate Christians and do all these thing against them?

Verse 3, “They will do these things because they haven’t known the Father or me.”

These things didn’t surprise Jesus; he knew that they would happen to his disciples.

And Jesus told these things to them ahead of time so that when they do happen, the disciples themselves would remember what Jesus said and so wouldn’t be surprised.

Jesus said these things to them so that they won’t stumble.

Where will Jesus be when all these things happen to his followers?

Jesus would be in heaven by the Father’s side.

That’s why the disciples who heard it were filled with sorrow.

Jesus kept talking about going away as well as telling them that they would be facing sufferings.

Why didn’t Jesus stay behind? What was so important that Jesus would leave them behind to face suffering and death?

Think about it.

Wouldn’t it be good if Jesus did stay behind?

Wouldn’t it be great if Jesus is physically here with us today?

Jesus could’ve fought off the Christian’s enemies; the could’ve saved the lives of thousands upon thousands of Christians.

On average, all around the world today, 13 Christians are killed and 12 churches are shut down.

Wouldn’t it be good for Jesus to be there to protect these Christians and these churches?

Why did Jesus leave the Christians behind?

2. The Spirit who convicts and guides

Look with me at verse 7, “Nevertheless, I am telling you the truth. It is for your benefit that I go away, because if I don’t go away the Counsellor will not come to you. If I go, I will send him to you.”

Jesus said it’s for our benefit that he left.

For if Jesus stayed, the Counsellor would not come.

But If Jesus went away, he would send the Counsellor, the Holy Spirit to come to us.

This is what Jesus is saying: Yes, Jesus could’ve stayed but it was far better to have the Holy Spirit come instead.

It’s better to be here meeting together this morning with the Holy Spirit than to be physically with Jesus in Galilee.

For the coming of the Holy Spirit is a sign that the last days are here.

Look with me at Acts 2:17-18 and see how the Bible describes the significance of the coming of the Holy Spirit.

“And it will be in the last days, says God, that I will pour out my Spirit on all people;

then your sons and your daughters will prophesy,

your young men will see visions,

and your old men will dream dreams.

18 I will even pour out my Spirit

on my servants in those days, both men and women

and they will prophesy.”

The coming of the Holy Spirit tells us that these are the last days.

Now’s the time to turn back to God.

The King of the Kingdom has arrived in the Person of Jesus.

The people of the Kingdom is now filled with Spirit of the Son who rules the kingdom of God.

When you see a people filled with the Holy Spirit, following Jesus the Son of God, you see the coming of the Kingdom of God.

After Jesus left them, there were 11 disciples.

Just before the coming of the Holy Spirit, 120 of them, including the 11 disciples and other followers met together.

It would’ve been just a little bigger than the people gathered here this morning.

On the day the Holy Spirit came upon them, 3000 people were added.

Since the day the Holy Spirit came, billions have come to known and believe in Jesus.

A long, long time ago, people had to physically go to a movie theatre to watch the news.

You have to turn up, often in fancy clothes, to find out what’s happening around the world.

The news is brought to a fairly small audience who could afford it.

But now, you just need to have a phone with good connection to see all kinds of news from all around the world.

The news is brought to a far bigger audience, making a far bigger impact.

When Jesus was here, only He had the Holy Spirit in Him since the Holy Spirit is His Spirit.

But now, after he ascended to heaven Jesus has sent His Spirit to all those who believe in him.

Firstly to those disciples at the Pentecost, then to all those who would believe in his name.

Billions upon billions of people have been filled with the Holy Spirit and pointing people to Jesus, the King of kings.

The Kingdom of God has come!

Jesus says the Holy Spirit will do two things:

He will convict the world of sin and he will guide the disciples into all the truth.

The Holy Spirit will convict; he will expose what’s wrong in our world, and call the world to repentance.

The Holy Spirit is not just telling us what we go wrong at an intellectual level; he will tell us what we got wrong at a moral level.

For the Holy Spirit will show us how we have personally wronged God.

Sin is not just something bad that we have done, thought, or said.

Sin is ultimately a wrong that we have done against God.

The Holy Spirit will expose the world, he will expose us, on how we have wronged God.

It will be a paradigm shift; the Holy Spirit will turn the world’s worldview upside down.

Firstly, the Holy Spirit will convict the world about sin because they don’t believe in Jesus.

That’s what sin is; sin is not believing in Jesus.

God has sent Jesus to perfectly reveal himself to us.

To reject Jesus as the Son of God is to reject the God who had sent Jesus.

To not believe in Jesus is not just a personal preference but an active sin against God.

That is, to not believe in Jesus is sin against God, to not believe in Jesus is wronging God.

The Spirit convicts the world because it does not believe in Jesus.

Second, the Holy Spirit will convict the world of righteousness.

What is the right thing? What is the righteous thing?

The world will say that to live in harmony with one another, to live in harmony with our family, to help the poor, to aspire for greater things, to have freedom to do whatever you want,

These are the righteous things.

But these are not the definition of righteousness.

They might be nice, but they are not in and of themselves righteous.

The Holy Spirit identifies righteousness as Jesus going to the Father after his death and resurrection.

The world thought it was right to kill Jesus, but in contrast, God sees the obedience of Jesus at the cross as righteousness.

What Jesus did is the most righteous thing a person could ever do.

That’s what righteousness looks like.

That’s what the Holy Spirit exposes; he shows us how inadequate our righteousness is and God’s perfect righteousness in the death, resurrection and ascension of Jesus.

Thirdly, the Holy Spirit convict the world of judgement.

The ruler of the world, the devil, sways the world to believe his definition of what’s right and what’s wrong.

He sways the world to deny the existence of God and who he is.

He sways the world to not believe in Jesus and to doubt God’s world.

But at the cross, the devil is judged.

The Spirit shows that the devil was wrong about everything in the world.

On the cross, God demonstrates his own love for us by sending his Own Son to die in our place to save us.

On the cross, Jesus has shown that God is trustworthy by fulfilling God’s promises about saving his people from their sins.

Through the cross, the Holy Spirit had exposed the judgements of the devil and all those who follow his judgement to be wrong.

And so, the Holy convicts the world about sin, righteousness, and judgement.

As well as convicting the world, the Holy Spirit also leads the disciples into all the truth.

The disciples weren’t ready to hear what Jesus had to say.

They couldn’t handle the truth.

But when the Spirit comes he will guide them into all the truth.

Look with me at verse 13, “When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all the truth. For he will not speak on his own, but he will speak whatever he hears. He will also declare to you what is to come.”

The Holy Spirit won’t just guide them to one truth, but into all the truth.

That’s because Jesus is the only place where you will find all the truth.

On the day the Holy Spirit comes upon them, they will be able to handle the truth because the Spirit of truth is in them.

The Holy Spirit will speak what he hears from God the Son, who himself hears from God the Father.

And since Jesus, God the Son, owns everything God the Father has, Jesus also has access to all the truth.

All the truth is at Jesus’ disposal and Jesus has spoken all the truth to the Holy Spirit, who in turn, guides us into all the truth.

And so, the Holy Spirit, the Spirit of Truth guides us into all the truth.

How does the Spirit convict and guide Jesus’ disciples?

It’s only as the Holy Spirit convicts us of the sin within us as we read from the Bible, that we can come to know the truth about God.

It’s only as the Holy Spirit convicts us of the inadequacies of our own righteousness and the supremacy of God’s righteousness, as shown in the Bible, that we can come begging for God’s righteousness.

It’s only as the Holy Spirit convicts us in the Bible of the deficiency of the devil’s judgement that we turn our back on the world and follow Jesus.

Jesus, God the Son sends his Spirit of truth to his disciples, so that they will speak and act in a way that convicts the world of sin, righteousness and judgement.

Jesus sends the Holy Spirit to us to not only to understand and believe the truth but to also give us the power to live out the truth.

The Holy Spirit doesn’t merely give us the facts about the gospel.

He empowers us to live out the gospel.

Someone who has the Holy spirit will follow Jesus and remain in him, and Jesus’ word will remain in them.

It’s about having a fruitful, spiritual life in Jesus.

We do that as we read the Word of God and as we pray. We do that as we love and serve one another, just as Jesus has commanded.

When we do these things by the power of the Holy Spirit, we will glorify God, honouring God, giving God thanks for all that he’s done for us in Jesus.

That’s what we see when someone is converted to Christianity.

It’s a blessing and a joy to see someone finally understand and believe the gospel.

There are times when we see people light up as they finally understood the importance of Jesus.

And it’s all the Spirit’s work!

Without the Holy Spirit, we won’t be able to live as disciples of Christ.

We need the Holy Spirit to live fruitful, spiritual lives for Jesus.

3. Ask the Father, and receive complete joy

If you want proof to see how desperately those of us who follow Jesus need the Holy Spirit, all we need to do is to read verses 16-18.

“In a little while, you will no longer see me; again in a little while, you will see me.”

Then some of his disciples said to one another, “What is this he’s telling us: ‘In a little while, you will not see me; again in a little while, you will see me’ and, ‘because I am going to the Father’?” 18 They said, “What is this he is saying, ‘In a little while’? We don’t know what he’s talking about.”

Jesus is referring to his death and resurrection.

In a little while, Jesus will die on the cross; the disciples won’t see Jesus.

And yet a little while again, Jesus will rise from the dead, and the disciples will see Jesus again.

Jesus has mentioned this to them on more than one occasion over the past week.

And yet, they didn’t understand it; it was beyond them.

They didn’t know what Jesus was talking about.

And that’s exactly what Jesus has been saying all along.

They haven’t received the Holy Spirit yet; the Holy Spirit hasn’t guided them into all the truth yet.

But on the day the Holy Spirit comes on them, they will understand.

Even so, Jesus, the Great Teacher, uses another category to teach them.

Jesus says that they will weep and mourn, and they will be sorrowful, while the world rejoices.

But one day, their sorrow will turn to joy.

He then uses an example that many of us would understand.

Verse 21, “When a woman is in labour, she has pain because her time has come. But when she has given birth to a child, she no longer remembers the suffering because of the joy that a person has been born into the world.”

Jenny’s brother’s wife, Ness, is about to have a baby very soon.

In fact, the due date was Thursday last week and we’re waiting for the good news.

If you see Jenny scream with excitement, you will know why.

Ness, like many mothers, is about to experience excruciating pain, pain that not many brothers will ever fully understand.

Some of our sisters have experienced it.

But when the child is born, the suffering is replaced with joy, for a little person is brought into the world.

Jesus is using the birth of a child to explain what the disciples will be experiencing soon.

They will feel pain, but the pain is temporary.

They will experience joy afterwards.

It’s an experience that Jesus himself went through.

Jesus has experience great pain at the cross; not only was he nailed to the cross, he was also abandoned and judged by God the Father.

But there was great joy when he rose from the dead, and even greater joy when he returned to the Father’s side.

The disciples will also experience great sorrow at the death of Jesus, but they will also experience great joy when they see him again after he rose from the grace.

It’s a joy that can’t be taken away, not by people, not by events, not by sickness, not even by death.

This ‘un-stealable’ joy is the sign of the beginning of a new creation.

It’s the joy of having life, eternal life in Jesus.

Jesus will see them again! They can look forward to that day.

In the meantime, Jesus tells them to pray.

Unlike what the disciples had been doing up to this point, there will come one day when they’ll have no need to ask Jesus questions about what he was saying anymore.

The Spirit of truth will guide them into all truth.

They won’t know everything, but they will understand the fundamental truth about who God is, who Jesus is, who they are and what sin, righteousness and judgement are.

Rather than asking Jesus, the disciples are to ask the Father in Jesus’ name and the Father will give it to you.

The theme of the power of effective prayer comes up again.

In John 15:8, Jesus says if you remain in me and my words remain in you, ask whatever you want and it will be done for you.

In 15:16, “I appointed you to go and produce fruit and that your fruit should remain, so that whatever you ask the Father in my name, he will give you.”

And here in 16:23-24, “ “In that day you will not ask me anything. Truly I tell you, anything you ask the Father in my name, he will give you. 24 Until now you have asked for nothing in my name. Ask and you will receive, so that your joy may be complete.”

If you want to know whether you’re a true branch in the true vine, if you want to know whether Jesus has chosen you to bear fruit, if you want to know whether the Holy Spirit has convicted you of your sin and is guiding you into all truth,

Look at what you’ve been praying and the effects of your prayer.

What have you been praying for? Has God answered them? If God has answered them, you will rejoice and thank God for what he has done in your life.

If God hasn’t answered your prayer, then pray that God will show you why he hasn’t answered your prayer.

Is it because you’re asking for something that won’t make you fruitful? Is it because he has something better to give? Is it because you don’t know Jesus yet?

If his words aren’t in you, if you don’t believe in him, then why would he listen to your prayers?

But if you’re like me, perhaps the problem is far simpler.

Maybe we just haven’t been praying enough.

If we don’t trust him enough to pray and expect him to answer, how can we blame God for not giving us growth?

But even as we pray and wait for God to answer, we need to be prepared to face sorrow.

There will be difficulties and troubles.

There will be sufferings and even death.

But we know that one day, our sorrow will turn to joy.

Out tears of sorrow will be replaced by tears of joy.

Ask the Father according to what the Spirit of Truth is leading you to say.

Ask the Father and you will receive and you will be filled with joy, it’s a joy that’s un-stealable, a joy that’s complete.

Throughout the 1800’s many foreign missionaries arrived in China to share the gospel.

Though many were healthy when they first came, half of them resigned or died after less than ten years of service.

It was more likely that infants born to these missionaries will die in China than if they were born in England.

These missionaries have shed many, many tears of sorrow for the souls of China and for the souls of their families.

They could’ve safely stayed in England and shared the gospel there.

But despite the hardships, despite the sorrow, despite the pain, they chose to go to China to share the gospel because the Holy Spirit has guided them to know the truth of Jesus Christ.

Many of them died without seeing many fruits from their missions.

But God has blessed the efforts of these English missionaries.

Since the 1800’s millions upon millions of Chinese have become Christians, including many here today.

Their tears of sorrow have paved the way to many great tears of joy.

And the gospel must not stop with us.

Someone have gone through great hardships, shed tears of sorrow to bring the gospel to the Chinese,

Those of us who are Chinese must go out, ready to shed tears of sorrow also, to bring the gospel to those who are different from us.

Last week I met a Chinese missionary who has two PhD’s.

He’s a very smart man and had worked to the medical industry for many years.

However, as he was studying indigenous medicine, he noticed how badly they’ve been treated over the past 200 years with many actively resisting the gospel of the white man.

And so, he gave up his job to become a missionary to some of the poorest people of our country.

It’s very, very hard work for a Chinese missionary to bring the gospel to the First nation people of Australia.

I’m sure many tears of sorrow were shed, by him as well as those who work with him.

But he keeps going because he has been convicted by the Holy Spirit and the Holy Spirit has guided him to the truth about the gospel of Jesus Christ.

What tears of sorrow have you shed recently because you’re a follower of Jesus Christ?

My prayer is that our church will be one that’s willing to follow the guidance of the Spirit, obedient to go to places where we will face hardships and sorrow,

Knowing that one day, our tears of sorrow will become tears of joy.

And so, why has Jesus left us behind in our suffering?

It’s because he loves us and wants to send us the Holy Spirit, who convicts the world of sin, righteousness and judgement and who guides us into all truth.

Because God the Son has sent God the Spirit of Truth, we can now pray to God the Father in full confidence, knowing that we will receive what we ask so that our joy may be complete.

And so, friends, pray to the Father in the Son’s name according to the Spirit of Truth, and you receive joy.

Pray that the Holy Spirit will expose the sins of the world, so that it will come in repentance before God.

As you read the Bible, pray for the guidance of the Holy Spirit.

Pray for God’s kingdom to come.

Keep a reminder of how God’s answered your prayers to help you be thankful for the prayers he’s answered.

Rejoice when you see God’s answers to your prayer.

Here are the three questions for this week:

1. What are you praying for at the moment?

2. Are you willing to face situations where you will shed tears of sorrow because you are following Jesus?

3. In what area are you asking for the Spirit’s guidance?

Friends, pray to the Father in the Son’s name according to the Spirit of Truth, and you receive joy.

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you for the promise of the Spirit of Truth to all those who believe in your Son Jesus. Please forgive us when we try to understand you and the world apart from the guidance of the Holy Spirit. Please lead us by your Spirit to wherever you want us to go, knowing that even when shed tears of sorrow, these will give way to tears of joy when we see Jesus glorified, along with you and the Holy Spirit. Amen.”
