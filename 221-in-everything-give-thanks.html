Human beings are deeply curious creatures, aren’t we?

Our world is a full of wonders and as human beings we want to find out more about it.

That’s why some of us like to go to different places and explore; we want to find out what’s out there.

That’s why others spend years studying because they are curious about a particular topic.

The brilliant physicist, Albert Einstein once said, “I have no special talent – I am only passionately curious.”

Curiosity helps us to learn, to explore and to discover the world around us.

And every now and then, we make exciting discoveries about our world, or we arrive at some great truth and we’re glad that we started our journey which started with our curiosity.

And curiosity is not limited only to science and exploration.

Since the ancient times, people were curious about what the gods wanted them to do.

Do the gods want us to settle down or to go and attack a city?

Do they want us to marry that person or to have another child?

And they would go to the priest or the temple and look for a sign.

There are many people today, in many different cultures, who are curious about what their gods think.

They want to know to explore their gods’ will.

As Christians, we believe that there’s one God who made us.

He’s our Creator who’s made that sense of curiosity deep inside each of us.

God’s put that curiosity there for us to explore the world that he’s made.

But more importantly, he’s put that curiosity there so that we can seek and find him.

He wants us to follow our curiosity about him and find out about who He is.

However, unlike other religions where it starts with us trying to reach God for ourselves, God has reached down to us.

He’s shown us his will in the Bible; He’s shown us what he wants in the Bible.

Now, that doesn’t mean that you don’t need to read any other book beside the Bible.

You still need to read the instruction manual for the car and the Maths textbook for Maths.

But what we do have in the Bible is everything we need for life and salvation.

If you want to know the important things about life and God and what his will for you is,

It’s here, in the Bible.

God’s will for you is found in here.

Are you curious about what God’s will for you is?

That’s what we’re going to find out in today’s passage.

We’re going to see two things:

God’s will for you in Church

God’s will for you as a Christian

Before we begin, let’s pray, “Dear Lord, we thank you for your word. We thank you for everyone who’s joined us here, whether they’re in person or online. Please reveal your will for us today. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”

God’s will for you in Church

To love leader-labourers

What does God want you to do in the Church?

You’re to love your leader-labourers

Open up with me to 1 Thessalonians 5, beginning from verse 12.

“Now we ask you, brothers and sisters, to give recognition to those who labor among you and lead you in the Lord and admonish you, 13 and to regard them very highly in love because of their work. Be at peace among yourselves.”

The Apostle Paul has just been talking to the Thessalonians about what it means to live as a Christian in a dark world.

Christians are to shine together as a community of light as we live our lives as children of the day.

And among our community are Christians who labour, who work hard for other Christians in the community.

The Bible tells us to give recognition for those who work hard among God’s people.

These are those who work hard to look after God’s people.

They look after their spiritual needs by teaching them God’s word, but they also provide for practical, physical needs as well.

That’s God’s will for the Church.

God’s will for the Church is for the Church to recognise their leaders who work hard among them.

God’s will for the Church is to love these labourer-leaders.

It’s not something that comes naturally, even to the Church.

It’s something that has to be commanded.

Perhaps it’s because it’s our human tendency to take people, especially our leaders, for granted.

We often don’t appreciate them when they lead us and we certainly don’t thank them when they admonish us, that is, when they tell us that we’re wrong.

But the Bible tells us to love and recognise our leaders.

I’ve heard that it’s said that some members in the Church listen to Church leaders merely because they are in a position of authority.

But that’s not what the Bible is saying here.

The Church doesn’t recognise and love Church leaders because of their position.

Rather, the Church recognise and love Church leaders because of their labour in the Lord for them.

I thank God for the leaders we have in our Church.

Our leaders have laboured and worked hard for the Church.

I thank God that many of them worked hard for you, the Church, during the lockdown.

Many have spent hours practising and recording songs for us.

The wardens and the parish council worked hard making important decisions to keep the Church safe so that you can come back today.

The translators have worked hard to make the sermon available in Chinese as well as in English.

The teachers have spent a lot of time producing videos and Zoom lessons for our children.

Friends, thank God for these labourer-leaders.

Also, thank them, in person; give them recognition; regard them highly in love.

That’s God’s will for you in the Church.

To care for one other

God also wants you to care for one another.

Look with me at verse 14, “And we exhort you, brothers and sisters: warn those who are idle, comfort the discouraged, help the weak, be patient with everyone.”

God wants his people to warn people inside the Church not to be idle.

They are not to be passive and let other people do all the hard work for them.

The Bible commentator Bruce Smith uses the word “truant” to describe people who are idlers.

Who’s a truant?

A truant is someone who stays away from school without explanation.

It’s student who neglects their responsibility to study at school.

Paul tells the people at Church not to be truants.

He tells Christians to warn against Christians who stay away from Church and neglect their responsibility to serve at Church.

Don’t be an idle Christian; don’t be a truant.

Rather, fulfil your responsibility to serve the Lord by serving his people at Church.

Comfort those who are discouraged.

In our context, comfort those who might be discouraged because of the pandemic.

Help the weak among us, especially those who find it difficult with the lockdown.

Be patient with everyone; be patient with your families, your friends, and other people in your community.

Be patient with those who are discouraged and weak.

And don’t pay back evil for evil.

Even if they meant to hurt you or ridicule you, don’t repay evil for evil.

Instead, always pursue what is good for one another and for all.

Actively look for what is good for the people sitting next to you in Church and beyond.

It’s been such a blessing even in the past few weeks as I see people in our Church looking out for the good of the other,

Making sure they’re taken care of.

It’s good to see that members of our Church are doing God’s will.

As we come back together as a Church, there are many opportunities for us to not be idle, to comfort the discouraged, to help the weak.

What are some ways for you to do these?

Choose one of the things that God’s instructed us to do in these two verses.

What are some practical ways for you to do these things here in Church?

Talk with the person next to you.

What are some practical ways for you to do the things listed in these two verses here in Church?

If you’re at home, you can message me by whatsapp, wechat or zoom.

What are some practical ways for you to do the things listed in these two verses here in Church?

Don’t be an idle Christian; talk with the person next to you.

You have twenty seconds.

These are great ideas!

May God help us to care for one another as we come back together.

This is God’s will for us in Church.

What about those times when we’re not at Church?

What is God’s will for me when I’m away not at Church?

God’s will for you as a Christian

Look with me at verses 16-18, “16 Rejoice always, 17 pray constantly, 18 give thanks in everything; for this is God’s will for you in Christ Jesus.”

Rejoice, pray and give thanks.

This is God’s will for you in Christ Jesus.

Firstly rejoice.

We are to rejoice, to be joyful, always.

We are to rejoice because we have the Lord Jesus Christ.

The Lord Jesus has saved us from the darkness of sin.

The Lord Jesus has saved us from being an enemy of God to being a child of God.

Because of the Lord Jesus, we have hope.

Even when things don’t go our way, we know that in the end, everything will work out because everything will go God’s way, according to God’s will.

It’s because we have been saved in Jesus and because we have hope in Jesus that we can rejoice.

A few years ago, Toyota brought out a series of commercials that has the tagline “still feeling it”.

Have you seen them?

They would show new Toyota owners going through awkward situations like being blown along the beach by a strong wind, or getting a parking ticket, children making a mess at the back.

But instead of complaining, they would just say, “I’m still feeling it”.

They’re still feeling it, still enjoying the joy of having the new Toyota even though they’re going through unpleasant situations.

That’s a glimpse of what Christians mean when we have joy even in the most dire situations.

We don’t have a new car but we’ve got something, Someone, far better.

We have the joy of knowing Jesus Christ and even in the midst of the most difficult of trials, we still feel the joy.

It’s like a song that we often sing at Church: Rejoice, when you cry to Him He hears Your voice, He will wipe away your tears

Rejoice, in the midst of suffering He will help you sing Rejoice.

Second, God’s will for us is that we pray constantly.

We are to keep praying and not give up.

We don’t get disappointed when we don’t seem to get an answer to our prayers even after many days, months and even years of prayers.

We keep praying, confident that God our heavenly Father will give good gifts to his children.

We thank God that we have been having our prayer meetings every Tuesday over this year.

It’s been wonderful to see God’s people prayer and even more wonderful to see God’s answers to prayers.

Even here at Church this morning, I’ve seen God’s answers to your prayers and my prayers.

If you haven’t joined yet, come and join us in our prayer meetings via Zoom or in person.

We’re on every Tuesday night, starting from 8:30pm.

We’d love to pray with you.

And so, keep praying, don’t give up, for this is God’s will for you in Jesus.

Thirdly, give thanks in everything.

God’s will is that we give thanks in everything.

We’re to give thanks for the good and the bad.

Giving thanks is of course, linked directly to prayer.

We give thanks to God when we pray.

We give thanks to God for what he’s given us.

To not give thanks to God, to be ungrateful, is a sign of those who have turned away from God.

Don’t be ungrateful.

Instead, give thanks in everything.

This is what we do on Sundays; we give thanks to God in everything, and in particular, we give thanks for Jesus Christ.

And this is what we have come to do this Sunday morning, as we give thanks to God for all that he’s done.

This is what we’ve come to Church to do.

But our thanks to God mustn’t only happen at Church.

We’re to give God thanks in our personal lives as well.

We’re to give God thanks for the good things we’ve received from him.

It might be our good health, or for keeping us safe during the lockdown.

But we’re also to give God thanks even for the bad things, because God’s told us to be thankful in everything, including both the good and the bad things.

We’re to be thankful for the difficult person we’re facing at work, in our family and even at Church.

We’re to give God thanks for them.

We’re to give God thanks for the difficulties we face.

Of course, we have good reasons not to want difficulties in our lives and we might even pray to God to remove them from our lives.

But God in his wisdom has placed them there in our lives and even though we might not fully understand it, we’re to be thankful to God for them.

Last week, I read the story of Duane.

Duane was born a healthy baby but he had Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, a rare form of epilepsy.

The doctors didn’t think he could live past one year but he lived for 31.5 years.

The family was very loving and the parents, along with the five siblings, some older, some younger, helped look after Duane.

It was very hard, physical and emotional work looking after Duane.

I wouldn’t be surprised if, as Christians, they would wonder why God has brought Duane into the world?

Why did God allow this to happen?

As Maureen, one of Duane’s sister reflected on Duane’s suffering, she wrote these words:

‘It is not that Christian teaching denies that sickness should, and will, be healed. Rather, we are convinced that God is in the business of exalting the lowly, that he takes his place in the frailest of bodies, that his “power is made perfect in weakness.”’

Through Duane’s weaknesses, God shows that his power is made perfect.

Part of that was to involve the Church community around Duane’s home to come and help look after Duane.

Young men in their twenties, who had never gone through any major testing before, were given the task to look after Duane.

They had to look after Duane, making sure that he’s safe when Duane has a seizure, feeding him when he would spill most of the food on his clothes, help him move his muscles by pushing him around in a tricycle for hours.

Though Duane hasn’t spoken a word to them, he became their teacher in patience, in perseverance, in learning how to care and in learning how to protect.

They learned leadership, humility, and the necessity of prayer.

In time, many future families were to benefit.

Many families would give God thanks for Duane.

Even when we face the most difficult of situation, the person who trust in the Lord Jesus can truly give thanks.

This is God’s will for you in Christ Jesus: to rejoice, to pray and to give thanks.

This is what God wants you to do; this is his will for you.

Do it!

God has made it as clear as day that he wants us to rejoice, to pray and to give thanks.

This is God’s will for you. Will you do it?

There are a lot of people who want to find out about God’s will for their lives but they don’t want to obey what he’s already revealed to them in the Bible.

If you don’t obey God’s will as he’s already revealed it clearly in his word, why should he reveal any more of his will to you?

If you don’t listen, why should he speak to you any further?

Don’t dismiss his will for you in the Lord Jesus.

Firstly, go to Jesus, put your trust in him.

Then rejoice, pray and give thanks to God in the Lord Jesus.

That’s God’s will for you in the Lord Jesus.

And so, what’s God’s will for us?

In the Church, God’s will for us is to love and recognise our leader-labourers and to care for one another.

In our lives, God’s will for is to rejoice, to pray and to give thanks in everything.

What does this mean for us?

Rejoice, pray and give thanks in everything.

One helpful way that I found is to breakdown what I’m experiencing at the moment into there categories:

The good, the bad and the ugly.

How do I give God thanks in the good things I’m experiencing?

How do I give God thanks for the bad things I’m experiencing?

And if we go a step further, how do I give God thanks for the ugly things I’m experiencing?

How do I pray for these things? How do I rejoice in these things?

Rather than going through them myself, I would share these things with a fellow Christian and we would pray through these things together.

The good, the bad and the ugly.

Give thanks for them, pray for them and rejoice in them in the Lord Jesus.

What’s God’s will for you in the Lord Jesus?

This is God’s will for you in the Lord Jesus:

“Rejoice always, 17 pray constantly, 18 give thanks in everything; for this is God’s will for you in Christ Jesus.”

Here are the three questions for us to think about this week:

How do I show love and respect to the labourer-leaders in Church this week?

How do I care for others in the Church this week?

In what difficult area of my life do I need to give thanks to God?

Rejoice, pray, and give thanks in everything.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for instilling in us a curiosity to explore the world you’ve made. Please ignite in us a holy curiosity to know your will. We thank you that you have revealed your will to us in the Lord Jesus Christ. Please open our eyes to see the glory of Jesus and to come to know him. Please let us never stop bowing in wonder before him. In all that we do, let us rejoice always, pray constantly, and to give thanks to you in the Lord Jesus Christ, for this is your will for us in Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
