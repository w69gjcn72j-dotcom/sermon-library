Young Adults (19–30)

Jesus Is the Light

John 8:12–20 · 27 September 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

For young adults (19–30) · Preacher: Rev David Yung

Big Idea: Follow Jesus, the Light of the World, and shine as his lower lights, with his light of life.

Aim: By the end of tonight, each person should be able to (1) explain what Jesus meant by “I am the light of the world” and why it was such a daring claim; (2) see that we don’t get to put Jesus on trial by our own standards — the Father has already testified; (3) leave with three names and one concrete way to shine as a “lower light” this week.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • Worst blackout or darkest place you’ve ever been — a power cut in the share house, a hike after sunset, a cave? What did you grab first?
  • “Live your truth.” “Follow your heart.” “You do you.” Where do you hear people telling you to light your own way? Does it actually work?

Read

12 Jesus spoke to them again: “I am the light of the world. Anyone who follows me will never walk in the darkness but will have the light of life.”

13 So the Pharisees said to him, “You are testifying about yourself. Your testimony is not valid.”

14 “Even if I testify about myself,” Jesus replied, “my testimony is true, because I know where I came from and where I’m going. But you don’t know where I come from or where I’m going.

15 You judge by human standards. I judge no one.

16 And if I do judge, my judgment is true, because it is not I alone who judge, but I and the Father who sent me.

17 Even in your law it is written that the testimony of two witnesses is true.

18 I am the one who testifies about myself, and the Father who sent me testifies about me.”

19 Then they asked him, “Where is your Father?” “You know neither me nor my Father,” Jesus answered. “If you knew me, you would also know my Father.”

20 He spoke these words by the treasury, while teaching in the temple. But no one seized him, because his hour had not yet come.

Context

Jesus says this in Jerusalem during the Feast of Tabernacles (John 7:2), a week-long festival remembering how God led Israel through the wilderness. The temple courts were lit up with huge lamps, and men who loved God danced with torches, singing his praise. In the Old Testament, light belongs to God: “The LORD is my light and my salvation” (Psalm 27:1). Under those lights, Jesus says, “I am the light of the world.”

Discuss

  1. Verse 12: what does Jesus claim, and what does he promise to “anyone who follows me”? Notice what he doesn’t say — he doesn’t offer to be a torch for your own path.
  2. Read Psalm 27:1 and Psalm 119:105. Why was it so daring — even outrageous — for Jesus to say this to a Jewish crowd? What’s the only thing that would make it not outrageous?
  3. Hebrews 1:3 says the Son is “the radiance of God’s glory.” The sermon compared the Father to the sun and Jesus to its sunbeams. If you want to know what God is like, where does that picture tell you to look?
  4. The sermon said we’re spiritually blind in a dark world — like walking Hezekiah’s tunnel, 550 metres of pitch black, without a headlamp. Scroll your news feed: where do you see the darkness? Where do you see it in yourself?
  5. Verses 13–15: the Pharisees say, “You’re testifying about yourself — not valid.” Jesus says they “judge by human standards.” How do people our age put Jesus on trial — vibes, what the group chat thinks, a TikTok explainer, no research at all?
  6. Verses 16–18: who is Jesus’ second witness? Why is the Father’s testimony the only one that can finally settle it? What does that do to our habit of being the judge of everything?
  7. Verse 19: “You know neither me nor my Father.” How does Jesus say anyone gets to know God? What would you say to a friend who’s “spiritual but not into Jesus”?
  8. Read Matthew 5:14–16. The sermon said we burn bright as “lower lights” by making Sunday church a priority, loving one another warmly, and doing good works so others glorify the Father. Which one is hardest for you in this season — uni, work, share house, weekend plans? What’s one step this week?

Live It Out

KOG 3·2·1·0 — pray for 3, connect with 2, invite 1, 0 left unloved.

  • Pray for 3 — housemates, colleagues, friends from uni. Picture their faces and pray for them by name every day.
  • Connect with 2 — actually message or catch up with two of them this week. Not a pitch; a friendship.
  • Invite 1 — invite one of them to church on a Sunday morning, or to the next young adults gathering.
  • 0 left unloved — who at church is on the edge and never gets messaged? Loving one another is how people know we’re Jesus’ disciples (John 13:35).
  • This week’s challenge: do one good work nobody asked for — at church, at home, in the share house — and don’t post about it. When you see a need, don’t just report it; help. And ask about serving: we need assistants for KOGKids, KOGMini and KOGYouth, and accompanists for the Chinese Parallel congregation.
  • Memory verse: “I am the light of the world. Anyone who follows me will never walk in the darkness but will have the light of life.” (John 8:12, CSB)

Pray

  • Thank God for Jesus, the Light of the World, whose word is true because the Father himself backs it.
  • Pray by name for your three — that God would shine the light of Christ on them, and give you a chance to point one of them to Jesus.
  • Pray that the young adults at St Paul’s would burn bright together, not burn out alone — and for the helpers and accompanists our church is praying for this quarter.

Leader’s Notes

Joyfully point to Jesus. Some here are still working out what they believe. In a culture that says every claim must pass our personal review, verses 13–18 can feel confronting. Let people voice doubts; the sermon’s answer is not “stop thinking” but “who has the right to testify about God?” Never corner anyone — and remember the sermon’s gentle closing: one of the faces we picture might be our own.

Question helps

Q1 — Light of the world; never walk in darkness; the light of life. The sermon: Jesus is the brightest light, and where there is light, shadows flee.

Q2 — Light belongs to God (Psalm 27:1; 119:105). Jesus takes it for himself. “It’s an outrageous thing to say, unless, of course, it’s true.”

Q3 — Look at Jesus. You can’t separate the sun from its radiance, or the Father’s glory from the Son’s. See the Son and you see the Father’s radiance.

Q4 — Wars, tragedies, a world divided despite science and wealth. We are blind “as a bat” compared with God, and there is spiritual darkness we don’t even recognise.

Q5 — Jesus knows where he came from and where he’s going: from God, back to God. Human standards: no research (John Dickson — his existence and crucifixion are “doubtless facts of ancient history”), what a community says, feelings. The sermon: “like blind men trying to describe the brilliance of the sun.”

Q6 — The Father who sent him. Only God can testify about God. We don’t judge God by our standards; God’s existence doesn’t depend on what we think of him.

Q7 — Knowing Jesus is how you know the Father. There is no way around him.

Q8 — “You can’t burn bright by yourself, not for long. You’ll only burn out.” We don’t have light in ourselves; we have it as we follow Jesus, together.
