When I was younger, I was given a little compass as a gift.

It was so long ago that I can’t remember whether it was for birthday or for Christmas!

I didn’t know much about it, except that if I keep following the arrow, I’ll get to the magnetic North Pole.

Has anyone used one before?

Now days, many people, even children, use their mobile phones for directions.

But when the phone is out of battery, we can always use a compass to help find out way home, even when we’re lost.

I don’t know about you, but often this year, I feel a little bit lost.

We made plans for months and then suddenly, the plans had to change because of COVID.

That seems to be what I’ve been hearing from many people as well.

Excursions were cancelled, job conditions were changed, exam timetables were altered.

It’s no wonder that many of us feel a little lost.

When you’re lost, what do you use to guide you?

What do you rely on to making your decisions?

Maybe it’s the next COVID update at 9am.

Maybe it’s hearing what your friends are saying and forwarding in social media.

What do you use as your compass as you make decisions?

In today’s Bible passage, we meet a man who was in a chaotic world.

He was part of an oppressed people being occupied by different oppressors for hundreds of years.

And yet, though he seemed to be helplessly stuck in his situation, the Bible tells us that he’s not lost.

In fact, in the apparent chaos and confusion, he’s been guided by none other than God.

How do I know whether I’m being led by God?

Where does God lead me to?

If you have your Bible with you, please open up to Luke 2 beginning from verse 25.

In today’s passage, we’ll see three things:

1. The righteous, devout, Spirit-led Simeon

2. God’s encompassing salvation in Jesus

3. God’s divisive revelation in Jesus

Before we look into it, let’s pray,

“Father, by your Spirit, please open our eyes and guide us according to your word, so that we may come to know, love and follow Jesus Christ, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

The Righteous, devout, Spirit-led Simeon.

In this passage, we’ll mee the righteous, devout, Spirit-led Simeon.

Luke 2:25-26, “here was a man in Jerusalem whose name was Simeon. This man was righteous and devout, looking forward to Israel’s consolation, and the Holy Spirit was on him. 26 It had been revealed to him by the Holy Spirit that he would not see death before he saw the Lord’s Messiah.”

Simeon was righteous: He did what was right before God.

He was devout: He loved God.

And God’s Holy Spirit was on him: He was led by God.

God’s Holy Spirit was on him and so he lived according to the Holy Spirit: He was characterized as someone righteous and devout.

The Bible doesn’t tell us much about Simeon except that he was looking forward to the consolation, or the comfort, of Israel.

He, along with Anna in the next passage, was part of a group of Old Testament believers who looked forward to Israel being comforted.

You see, for many years, Israel had lived under the oppression of different foreign powers.

At one time it was the Babylonians; at another time it was the Greeks.

And now, it’s the Romans’ turn.

From historical records, we know that it would’ve been a bitter and humiliating time for many Israelites.

There was a time when they had their own kings, lived according to their own customs, following the one true God.

But now, they are the subjects of a foreign power, who follows a pagan religion and worships lifeless idols.

However, if they were honest with themselves, as I’m sure Simeon and Anna were, they would know that their sad situation came about as a result of their own making.

Despite God’s warnings, as a nation, they had rebelled against God, time and time again.

And so, God made good his threats and judged them as a nation.

For many hundreds of years, the Jews longed for relief for their suffering.

They were looking for some comfort from God.

They were looking to be set free from the foreign oppression.

As important and welcoming that would be, Simeon wasn’t ultimately longing for political freedom.

He knew that the problem with the Israelites is not ultimately political but spiritual.

The heart of their problem is the problem of their heart.

Their heart has left God.

And so, Simeon looks forward to seeing the Lord’s Messiah.

It’s only as the Lord’s Messiah lead the people back to the Lord that Israel will have true consolation, true comfort.

Over this past year in 2021, many of us might feel restricted.

Like the Israelites of old, we might want to see the restrictions lifted.

While it’s hard to live with the current COVID restrictions, there are many countries in the world that are far more restrictive than our own.

Some of these restrictions may have to do with COVID, others might be due to political oppression, not unlike those experienced by the Israelites.

There are many people around the world longing for freedom, longing for comfort.

However, the comfort that we need is ultimately not freedom from political oppression, or a lifting of COVID protocols or even a drop in COVID case numbers.

What we need is what Simeon has been waiting for: We need the Lord’s Messiah.

One day, Simeon just happened to be in the temple.

That’s probably what Simeon thought but we as readers know that he was led there by the Holy Spirit.

And so, we know it wasn’t a coincidence when he met a young couple with a baby boy.

As was the custom under the law, Simeon received the boy from the parents, as he would’ve done so on so many other occasions to presumably to pray for them.

However, as soon as he received the boy from the parents, he knew.

This boy was the One whom God’s promised he would see before he dies.

This boy is the Lord’s Messiah.

The parents’ names were Mary and Jacob (the adopted father).

They boy’s name was Jesus.

As Simeon was receiving the boy, it was as if he was receiving the comfort that he’s been waiting for.

He’s now received God’s promise in this Baby Boy Jesus.

He praises God as he receives Jesus!

That’s what ultimately happens if you are guided by the Holy Spirit of God – he will take you to your greatest comfort.

He will lead you to Jesus!

One of the things that we kept doing during this crazy year is to pray!

We pray on Sundays as a Church, but we’ve also been praying every Tuesday night as a Church.

As a Church, we want to be guided by the Holy Spirit and so we pray that God will lead us by his Holy Spirit.

As important as it is to have visions and strategies, people and finances, the Holy Spirit’s guidance trumps them all.

We go whether the Spirit leads us, know that he will lead us ultimately to Christ and to his glory.

As a church, we’ve faced many situations that seem impossible for us to go through and in response we prayed for God’s help.

And God is faithful! He has carried us through!

No matter how difficult things get, no matter how uncertain things seem, we must always, always, follow the Spirit’s lead.

That’s why we pray; that’s why we read the Bible.

We must follow the Spirit’s lead to Christ.

Make it your goal as you head into 2022 to follow the Spirit’s lead.

Follow his lead, wherever he might lead you.

St Paul’s Kogarah, follow the Spirit’s lead, that’s where we need to go!

God’s encompassing salvation in Jesus

In response to receiving the baby boy Jesus, Simeon responds in a song.

It’s a song that praises God’s encompassing salvation in Jesus.

Look with me at verse 29-30, “Now, Master, you can dismiss your servant in peace, as you promised. For my eyes have seen your salvation. You have prepared it in the presence of all peoples—a light for revelation to the Gentile and glory to your people Israel.”

This is the song known as Nunc Dimittis, which means, Now, you let depart.

It’s a wonderful song of confidence.

Simeon is ready to go in peace for his eyes have seen God’s salvation!

He has peace in knowing that God’s kept his promise!

He’s been waiting all his life to see the comfort that God’s been promising the Israelites for hundreds of years and now he’s holding the Lord’s Messiah in his arms.

Not many people can lay claim that they’ve held the Lord’s Messiah in his arms but now Simeon can!

In bringing this Child, God is fulfilling his promise to bring salvation for all peoples.

It’s not just for the Jews or even just the Old Testament saints like Simeon.

It’s a salvation for every person from every people group.

This Baby will be a Light for the Gentiles (that is, those who aren’t Israelites) and glory for Israel.

That covers everybody!

Together, they’ll see the salvation that God’s prepared for them in Jesus.

This is not a localized event or a culture-specific event.

This is an open invitation for all people.

Previously, they were walking in the darkness; they had no light.

There’s darkness from without as well as darkness form within.

There’s darkness outside in the form of corruption and media and criminals.

But there’s also darkness inside of us like greed, lust and desire that fuels what we see on the outside.

The darkness that we experience in the world is both outside of us and inside of us.

But now, there’s light in Jesus.

In Jesus, God shines a light in the darkness to save us from our darkness.

That’s why we work very hard to bring the light of Christ to many people.

This message of the light of Christ is not restricted to the English speakers of the Chinese speakers but to every speaker of every language.

Last week we had our Christmas Carols evening and we had visitors who came to see us.

It was great to see a Chinese speaker and an English speaker speaking with a Filipino speaker!

They used English as the common language as it’s the only one that everybody knew.

But it was great to see us crossing cultural barriers to reach people from other backgrounds.

We want to continue to bring God’s light in Jesus to as many people as we can.

Right now, if you come to our Church, you can speak to us in Mandarin, English, Cantonese, maybe a little German.

What other languages do we have here?

Talk to the person next to you and see how many languages we have at Church!

You can send your answers to us via Message, Whatsapp, Wechat, using whatever language you want!

What other languages do we have here?

You have 20 seconds!

That’s great! We want to use whatever language to share the message about God’s salvation, God’s light in Jesus to everyone around us,

Because God’s salvation is for everyone!

God’s salvation is encompassing!

God wants to give true peace to everyone.

However, as well as giving this encompassing salvation in Jesus, God also gives a divisive revelation in Jesus.

God’s divisive revelation in Jesus

Look with me at verses 34-35, “Then Simeon blessed them and told his mother Mary, “Indeed, this child is destined to cause the fall and rise of many in Israel and to be a sign that will be opposed— 35 and a sword will pierce your own soul—that the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed.”

Jesus is destined for the fall and rise of many in Israel.

Some people will find him offensive and oppose him.

Even though his offer of salvation is for all, He won’t be universally liked by all.

A few years ago, my family and I went on a holiday.

We hired a little house and spent the week there.

At the end of the week, I received a text message from the owner, asking us to give him a five out of five star for his house.

He explicitly told us not to give anything less than five, not even a four.

If there’s anything that makes us unhappy, let him know and he’ll fix it so that he’ll get a five.

He wants everyone to like his house and he wants every reviewer to give him full marks.

At the end, I didn’t give him a review because I didn’t want to give him a five star review.

Even though Jesus has come to offer salvation for everyone, he won’t get a five star review from everyone.

He will face opposition.

Being God’s light, his teaching will reveal the thoughts of many hearts.

In the light of Jesus, it’ll be revealed that no everyone want God’s salvation in Jesus.

Not everyone wants to be saved, as good as salvation is!

Those who love darkness will prefer the darkness over light.

John 3:19 tells us that, “The light has come into the world, and people loved darkness rather than the light because their deeds were evil.

Those who love darkness will speak against the light.

Jesus’ salvation will divide the world into those who love light and those who love darkness.

Those who reject Jesus, who speak against Jesus will reveal themselves as people who love darkness.

Those who love Jesus, who praises Jesus, who follow Jesus will be revealed as someone who love the light.

This is shown most clearly at the cross of Jesus.

At the cross of Jesus, almost everyone in Jesus’ days is shown to prefer darkness over light.

It was a dark day indeed when the world would rather nail their Saviour to the cross than to make peace with God.

Led by the Spirit, Simeon said that a sword will pierce Mary’s heart.

Perhaps Simeon had a glimpse of the day on which Jesus would be nailed to the cross and how Mary’s heart will be pierced on that day.

However, it’s probably more likely that Simeon was referring to the fact that the light of Jesus will pierce and reveal Mary’s heart also.

Even though Mary had heard received the news about Jesus from the angel of the Lord, even though she’s heard the shepherds’ testimonies about Jesus, and even though she heard what Simeon had said about Jesus,

It appeared that she initially didn’t believe in Jesus.

She, along with her family, certainly wasn’t supportive in his ministry.

When Jesus was teaching the people, she, along with her family, wanted to speak to Jesus, presumably wanting to interrupt him and to take him home.

Jesus’ teaching and Mary’s response to it reveal the state of Mary’s heart, that she, too, needed his salvation, just like the rest of us.

We thank God that after Jesus died and rose from the dead, Mary joined the Church, along with some of her family members.

We thank God that she, too, along with the rest of the Church, was filled with the Holy Spirit, who led her to believe Jesus not only as her Son, but more importantly as her Lord.

The message about God’s salvation in Jesus, that is, the Gospel, is for the whole world.

However, each of us need to come face to face with Jesus.

Even Mary, his own mother, must come, have her heart pierced, as it were and find her salvation in him.

If we’re to find God’s salvation, we, too must also have our hearts pierced, for Jesus to reveal to us who we really are on the inside.

For until our hearts are pierced, until we confess the darkness inside of us and we ask God for forgiveness, we can never receive God’s salvation for us in Jesus.

Friends, let Jesus speak to you through his teaching.

Let his teaching pierce your heart and by his Holy Spirit, let him reveal to us the darkness inside of us.

We can’t hide our darkness from him, since he’s God’s light and can see all that’s inside of us.

Rather than trying to hide it, let his word pierce us so that we may confess our darkness and turn to him so that we can find forgiveness and salvation in him.

When we come together on Sundays, we regularly confess our sins together as a Church, we always admit and confess our sins and our darkness before God.

We let Jesus and his words pierce our hearts so that we can be made whole.

It’s only when let his words pierce us, that we’re ready to receive his forgiveness and his healing.

It’s only when we’re ready to fall in humility before him that we’re ready to rise in glory in him.

Like Simeon, be led by the Holy Spirit so that we’ll receive God’s encompassing and yet divisive salvation in the Lord Jesus Christ.

What does this mean for us?

Follow the Spirit’s lead to Christ.

As we prepare for the New year, many of us are weary of making any plans.

How do we plan for next year when our plans for next week can change all of a sudden?

What goal can we set for next year when there are so many uncertainties?

Friends, follow the Spirit’s lead!

Ask yourself, “How is the Spirit leading me to Christ?”

Just think back over this year, how has the Spirit been leading you to Christ?

If you have grown in Christ, then thank God that you’ve been following the Spirit’s lead.

If you haven’t grown in Christ, then think about what’s been guiding you.

You might have been following the wrong lead.

Follow the Spirit’s lead by praying and by prayer.

Ask Him to help you grow in your devotion and your love to Jesus.

Ask him to help you make righteous decisions, decisions that will glorify Jesus.

Follow the Spirit’s lead as he leads you to Christ.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How have I been following the Holy Spirit’s lead?

Is there a specific people group that God has equipped me to share his Gospel with?

How has Jesus’s words been piercing my heart?

Friends, as we come to the conclusion of a one year and prepare to begin the next, may we always follow the Spirit’s lead.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for fulfilling your promise to us in Jesus Christ. We thank you that he’s the one whom you’ve sent to bring salvation for all people. Father, by his words and by Your Spirit, please pierce our hearts. Help us see the darkness inside us and let us come back to your light in Christ. Please lead us by your Spirit so that we come to Christ, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
