A New Way to Repent

Luke 5:27–39 · Sunday 8 November 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah · Sermon by Rev David Yung

Big Idea: Fellow sick sinners, Jesus calls us to a new repentance centred on him, to leave everything, follow him, feast with him and fast in his absence.

Aim: by the end of the study, students will

see that real repentance is not just saying “sorry” but standing up to follow Jesus, the Doctor who came for the sick;

understand why Jesus’ friends feast while he is with them, and why they will fast when he is away;

pray for three friends who don’t know Jesus yet, and choose one thing to stop keeping back from Jesus.

You’ll need: Bibles, pens, about 60–75 minutes.

1. Connect (10 min)

  • “Sorry, not sorry.” Think of the last time someone said sorry in a group chat, on the sports field or at home. Did you believe them? What made it feel real — or fake?
  • Has anyone ever made you say sorry — a teacher, a parent, a brother or sister? How did it feel? Is that the same as actually being sorry?

2. Read: Luke 5:27–39 (CSB)

27 After this, he went out and saw a tax collector named Levi sitting at the tax office, and he said to him, “Follow me.”

28 So, leaving everything behind, he got up and began to follow him.

29 Then Levi hosted a grand banquet for him at his house. A large crowd of tax collectors and others were guests with them.

30 But the Pharisees and their scribes were complaining to his disciples, “Why do you eat and drink with tax collectors and sinners?”

31 Jesus replied to them, “It is not those who are healthy who need a doctor, but those who are sick.

32 I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.”

33 Then they said to him, “John’s disciples fast often and say prayers, and those of the Pharisees do the same, but yours eat and drink.”

34 Jesus said to them, “You can’t make the wedding guests fast while the groom is with them, can you?

35 But the time will come when the groom will be taken away from them—then they will fast in those days.”

36 He also told them a parable: “No one tears a patch from a new garment and puts it on an old garment. Otherwise, not only will he tear the new, but also the piece from the new garment will not match the old.

37 And no one puts new wine into old wineskins. Otherwise, the new wine will burst the skins, it will spill, and the skins will be ruined.

38 No, new wine is put into fresh wineskins.

39 And no one, after drinking old wine, wants new, for he says, ‘The old is good.’”

3. Context

Levi was a tax collector. He was a Jew who collected taxes for the Romans, who ruled over his own people. Everyone saw him as a traitor, and he probably charged extra to make himself rich. The Pharisees were the serious religious people of the day. They prayed a lot, and they fasted (went without food) twice a week to show God they were sorry for sin. Jesus often called himself a doctor, and here he is eating at a party full of the people the Pharisees looked down on.

4. Discuss

  1. (v.27) Levi was the kind of person everyone at school would call a sell-out: hated, rich and cheating his own people. Jesus walks up and says, “Follow me.” What do you think Levi felt when he heard that?
  2. (v.28) What does Levi do? What did he give up? Why does it make sense to leave everything when the person asking is the Son of God?
  3. (vv.29–30) Levi throws a big party. Who comes, and who is left out and starts complaining? Why do you think the Pharisees were so upset?
  4. (vv.31–32) Jesus says he is a doctor for the sick. Who is “sick” and who counts as “healthy”? Why can a doctor only help people who admit they are sick? What does “I’m fine” sound like at school?
  5. (vv.33–34) The Pharisees say that real sorrow for sin looks sad and hungry, so why are Jesus’ friends eating and drinking? What is Jesus saying about himself when he calls himself the groom at a wedding?
  6. (v.35) When will Jesus’ friends fast? How can a Christian be full of joy that Jesus is with us, and still ache because he hasn’t come back yet and the world is broken?
  7. (vv.36–38) In your own words, what are the patch on the old garment and the new wine in old wineskins about? What would a “Jesus patch” look like in a teenager’s life?
  8. (application) Where do you keep Jesus as a patch: Sundays only, church friends only, switched off at school or online? What is one area you could hand over to him this week?

5. Live It Out

KOG 3·2·1·0 — pray for 3, connect with 2, invite 1, 0 left unloved.

  • Pray for 3 — write down three friends or classmates who don’t know Jesus yet and pray for them by name every day this week.
  • Connect with 2 — sit with two of them at lunch, play sport with them or send a message that isn’t about homework.
  • Invite 1 — invite one of your three to KOGYouth, or to our Community Christmas Concert on 13 December. We are praying for 120 people to come and worship Jesus.
  • 0 left unloved — look for the person sitting on their own at school or at church, and be the one who welcomes them the way Levi’s party welcomed outsiders.

This week’s challenge: Stand up, don’t stay seated. Name one thing you have been holding back from Jesus (a habit, a friend group, what you do online, a grudge). Tell one other person in the group, and pray for each other all week.

Memory verse: “It is not those who are healthy who need a doctor, but those who are sick. I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.” (Luke 5:31–32)

6. Pray

  • Thank Jesus, our great Doctor, for coming for sick sinners like us. Say sorry for the times you have stayed seated when he called, or complained that others got more grace than they deserved.
  • Ask the Holy Spirit to give you a new heart, and to help you follow Jesus with all of your life, not just a patch of it.
  • Pray by name for your three friends: that they would hear Jesus’ “Follow me”, and for a chance to invite one of them.

7. Leader’s Notes

Framing. Joyfully point to Jesus. Some students may not yet be Christians, so never corner anyone or make them say “sorry” on the spot. The good news is not “say sorry harder” but that Jesus, the Doctor, calls the sick and welcomes them to his table. Let students admit what is wrong without shame.

Two likely wrong turns.

  • “Repentance means feeling really bad (and looking sad).” The Pharisees thought so, with their fasting twice a week. Jesus does not say sorrow for sin doesn’t matter, or that fasting is wrong; he says the heart of repentance is how we respond to him when he calls.
  • “A Jesus patch will do.” Students may think they can add Jesus to their life and change nothing. Jesus says the new can’t be patched onto the old: he wants all of our life, not a corner of it. Equally, don’t let students hear “all in” as “be perfect”. Levi’s all-in was standing up and following.

Question helps

Q1 — Levi was despised and knew it. Being called by name, while still at work, by someone who knew all about him, would have been shocking. Jesus doesn’t say, “Fix yourself first.” He says, “Follow me.”

Q2 — Levi got up and left everything behind, including his income. Be very clear that this isn’t about money; it is about Jesus. If the Son of God asks you to follow, of course it makes sense: he is God and we are not. Levi’s action said what words couldn’t.

Q3 — Tax collectors and “others” came; the Pharisees, normally guests of honour, were left out. The shunners were shunned, and they grumbled that others were getting more grace than they deserved. Ask: where do we do that?

Q4 — Everyone is sick, spiritually. Only people who admit it come to the Doctor. “Righteous” people (who think they don’t need him) won’t say sorry to Jesus, so they don’t get forgiven. Jesus heals so that we repent and turn to God, not so that we can sin again.

Q5 — The Pharisees fasted 104 days a year; God asked for only one fast (the Day of Atonement). Jesus asks, “What time is it?” It is time to celebrate because the Bridegroom is here. You don’t skip the food at a wedding. Jesus is the one God promised to give people a new heart.

Q6 — “The groom taken away” points to the cross. Now we feast, because Jesus is with us by his Spirit, and we fast and long for his return. Both are repentance. Help each other: be there for the friend who needs to feast, and the one who needs to fast.

Q7 — New cloth shrinks and tears the old garment; new wine gives off gas and bursts old, hard wineskins. The new way of repentance, centred on Jesus, is too alive for the old way to hold. A “Jesus patch” is Jesus added on; the heart of repentance is the Person of Jesus.

Q8 — Give time for honest answers. Models: “I keep Jesus out of my group chats”, “I’m a different person at school”. Don’t shame anyone. Point back to Jesus: he gives life and he is worth all of it. Saying sorry is only the beginning of a new life.
