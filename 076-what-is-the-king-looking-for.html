Intro:

Happy New Year!

恭祝大家新年蒙恩蒙福!!

May we all have a new year filled with grace and blessing!

One of the traditions that we have in new year is that elders give Red Envelopes to children.

Have any children received theirs?

Have any adults received theirs?

Why are people so excited about the red envelopes?

It’s because there’s money inside.

My children look forward to Chinese New Year because it’s when their bank account increases significantly during New Year

(and because they don’t get much in the rest of the year)

Now, imagine a person coming in with many envelopes and handing them out to all the children of the Church.

And the children are all excited to receive them.

But when they open it, there’s nothing inside.

They’re just empty envelopes.

It would’ve been very disappointing.

That’s because the person who gave them just wanted to be known as generous without actually being generous.

Sadly, there are some people who call themselves Christians who do the same thing.

They wanted to be known as Christians, without actually being Christians.

They’re all show but no substance.

They have no real faith.

But before we point the finger too quickly, we have to ask ourselves,

Will Jesus see real faith in me?

What does Jesus look for as signs of real faith?

In today’s passage, in Mark chapter 11, we’ll see that Jesus calls out those who pretend to trust God and identifies the signs of those who truly trust God.

We’ll see three things.

Each of these things happen on the first three separate days of Jesus’ time in Jerusalem.

Day One: King Jesus comes

Please open up with me to Mark chapter 11

Jesus is about to enter into Jerusalem.

But before he does, he tells two of his disciples to go into the village ahead of them.

There they will find a colt tied, on which no one has ever sat.

If someone asks them, they are to reply saying the Lord needs it and will send it back straight away.

And it happened just as Jesus’ said.

Look with me at what happens next, beginning from verse 7,

“They brought the colt to Jesus and threw their clothes on it, and he sat on it. Many people spread their clothes on the road, and others spread leafy branches cut from the fields. Those who went ahead and those who followed shouted: Hosanna!

Blessed is he who comes

in the name﻿ of the Lord!,

Blessed is the coming kingdom﻿

of our father David!

Hosanna﻿ in the highest heaven!”

Talk about a dramatic entrance!

People from all over Jerusalem would’ve heard about Jesus coming in!

You wouldn’t be able to miss it!

It reminds me of the Queen’s entrance into the London Olympics in 2012.

As they were starting the Olympics, they were showing a video of how the people in England were getting ready for the Olympics.

In one clip, they showed James Bond, the famous but fictitious English Spy, being summoned to the Queen.

They then walk together to a helicopter.

They next thing we see is the queen jumping out of the helicopter in a parachute in front of a live audience to attend the Olympics.

The audience were shocked!

The queen then appears and opens the Olympics.

Of course, it wasn’t the real Queen but a stunt double but even so, it was a dramatic entrance.

There was a lot of planning beforehand, but it paid off.

Everyone knew when the Queen arrived!

A lot of planning went into Jesus’ arrival into Jerusalem.

Jesus didn’t just randomly got his disciples to get a colt for him to enter the city.

He carefully prepared the way to enter into Jerusalem in order to fulfill what was written in the Book of Zechariah in the Old Testament,

In Zechariah 9:9

Rejoice greatly, Daughter Zion!

Shout in triumph, Daughter Jerusalem!

Look, your King is coming to you;

he is righteous and victorious,

humble and riding on a donkey,

on a colt, the foal of a donkey.

Jesus deliberately entered Jerusalem on a colt to announce that he is the King prophesied in Zechariah.

Jesus is saying to Jerusalem, “Here I am, your king is here!”

And the people responds by quoting parts of Psalm 118:25-26, shouting out Hosanna!

Blessed is he who comes

in the name﻿ of the Lord!

In Psalm 118:25, it says Lord, save us! Lord, pleases grant us success!

That’s where the word Hosanna comes from – it means Save!

The people are shouting Hosanna, they are shouting for God’s salvation.

They are shouting for the One who comes in the name of the Lord, who will save them.

In Jesus’ time, the people of Jerusalem were under the oppression of the Romans.

And now, finally, God’s going to rescue them through Jesus!

This Jesus will bring about the kingdom of David.

Hosanna in the Highest heaven!

The coming of Jesus is a time of great happiness and hope for God’s people!

Two years ago, one of Caitlyn’s friends was leaving for Abu Dhabi.

And so, Caitlyn organised a surprise farewell for her friend.

Caitlyn got all her friends and their parents, to go to a park near the school.

She also got the girl’s mother to bring her to the part, without the girl knowing.

Caitlyn and her friends were hiding in the park, all giggling with excitement.

When the friend came, they burst out in excitement!

They were very happy to celebrate their friendship and send their friends off to a new start.

When Jesus came into Jerusalem, the people were filled with excitement because Jesus will bring about a new start, a new chapter in the history of God’s people.

They were filled with hope as they welcomed Jesus into their city.

When Christians come to Church, we should be filled with joy and hope because we’re here to meet Jesus.

Jesus says that when two or three are gathered in his name, he is here among us.

When we come together as Church, we come before the presence of the Great King.

It’s both a privilege and a joy to be able to come before the presence of King Jesus.

Even though we can’t sing as a congregation yet, we can recite psalms together, we can recite creeds together, we can recite prayers together.

Later on, in the rest of the service, let’s recite these with joy for Jesus our King!

For our singers and musicians, lead us with joy as you sing praises to God on our behalf.

After Jesus enters Jerusalem, he enters into the temple.

The Bible has a curious reference, in verse 11, “After looking around at everything, since it was already late, he went out to Bethany with the Twelve.”

Why does the Bible record this?

Jesus comes into the temple, looks around and goes home because it’s late.

After the dramatic entrance into Jerusalem, doesn’t this seem like an anticlimax?

The quiet end to Day one leads us to the theme of Day two.

Day 2: The King curses

On Day Two, we’ll see the King curses.

As Jesus walks back to Jerusalem, he gets hungry and wants to eat.

He sees a fig tree with leaves and goes to look for figs there.

The tree has a lot of leaves, but it has no figs.

The Bible writer tells us it’s not the season for figs.

The season for figs is from mid-August to mid-October.

Seeing that it’s a week before the Passover, it would’ve been between March and April when Jesus sees the fig tree.

However, even during this time, the fig tree sprouts buds known as paggim in Hebrew.

This is a unique feature of the fig tree – it produces these edible paggim before it produces leaves.

When there are leaves on a fig tree, you’ll expect to find some of these paggim there.

That’s what Jesus would’ve been expecting, since the fig tree is full of leaves.

But this leafy tree had no fruit.

And so Jesus curses the tree, in verse 14, “May no one ever eat fruit﻿ from you again!” And his disciples﻿ heard it.’

Jesus curses the fruitlessness of the tree.

Why is Jesus so harsh on the fig tree?

It’s because he wants to illustrate a point.

It’s a point that’s highlighted by Mark’s use of the Markan Sandwich.

Do you remember the Markan Sandwich?

It’s when Mark illustrates a point by putting one main point between two parts of a story.

In our passage, we see Jesus cursing of the fig tree in one part, here in verses 12-14, the actual withering of the fig tree in another part, verses 20-21,

And in between, we find the meat of the Markan Sandwich, in between verses 15-19.

The leafy but fruitless fig tree is a symbol of the showy but faithless worship in the temple.

Look with me at verses 15-16, “They came to Jerusalem, and he went into the temple﻿ and began to throw out those buying and selling. He overturned the tables of the money changers﻿ and the chairs of those selling doves, and would not permit anyone to carry goods through the temple.”

Remember Jesus came to the temple only the day before to look for something.

What was he looking for? Did he find it?

Just as he couldn’t find the fruit that should be there in a leafy fig tree, Jesus couldn’t find the faith that should be there in an active temple.

The temple was all show but no substance.

Instead, he found people buying and selling.

He found a market in God’s temple.

And so, Jesus overturned the tables and chairs of those who were doing business there.

What was Jesus so upset about what was happening in the temple?

So much so that he had to physically stop what was happening there?

Look with me at verse 17, “He was teaching them: “Is it not written, My house﻿ will be called a house of prayer﻿ for all nations? But you have made it a den of thieves!”

The Temple in Jerusalem was divided into a number of sections, with some places accessible only to Jewish men, others to Jewish women and still others to the Gentiles.

The Gentiles were not allowed to go places reserved for Jews.

Some commentators have made the point that the money changers and those buying and selling doves probably did it in the Court of the Gentiles.

And so, by setting up their stalls in the Court of the Gentiles, they effectively stopped the Gentiles, people from other nations, to come in and pray.

That’s what Jesus was looking for in the Temple.

The Temple of God, God’s house, was to be a house of prayer for all nations.

God made his house to be a place where people of all nations can come and meet God and to pray to the one true God.

God is not the God of the Jews only. He’s also the God of the English, God of the Chinese, God of the Indians, God of the Arabs and the God of the Australians.

There is one God, the God of the nations and his house is a house of prayer for all nations.

That’s what Jesus was looking for.

He was looking for a house that offers prayers to God, a house that’s opened for all nations to come and pray to God.

And he couldn’t find it in the Temple of Jerusalem.

A few years ago, my family and I had the opportunity to go to Paris to see the Notre Dame.

It was a magnificent Church building and there were many people going in to see it.

This was before COVID and before part of it got burnt down in a fire.

Even though there were representatives from many nations going there, we weren’t praying.

We were just there to look at the Church building.

When we were visiting it, we were in a Church building but we weren’t in Church.

When Jesus visited the Temple, he saw a lot of activities, he saw many people, he might have even seen people singing praises and offering sacrifices to God.

But he didn’t find what he was looking for.

He couldn’t see people of all nations praying to God.

He couldn’t see faithful leaders, leading God’s people in prayer.

In fact, when the leaders of the temple heard what Jesus said about the temple, they started to look for a way to kill him.

These were not the fruits that Jesus was looking for in the temple.

What would happened to the temple?

The fig tree that Jesus cursed withered the next morning.

The temple which does not invite nations to pray to God, who only pretend to worship God, will end up being like the withered fig tree.

It will be cursed by God.

God looks for people who would pray to him in communities that claim to trust in him.

Will God find what he’s looking for here, in our Church?

Will God find a Church who invites people of all nations to pray to him?

Or will he find us leafy but fruitless, all show with no substance?

God is serious about us being a fruitful Church rather than being a showy Church.

How do we become a genuine, substantial Church that bears the fruit that God looks for?

That’s what we see in Day Three.

On Day three, the King calls.

Day 3: The King calls

After noticing that the tree Jesus cursed had withered from the roots up, Peter said to Jesus, “Rabbi, look! The Fig tree that you cursed has withered.”

Listen to how Jesus responds, beginning from verse 22, “Have faith in God. Truly I tell you, if anyone says to this mountain, ‘Be lifted up and thrown into the sea,’ and does not doubt﻿ in his heart, but believes﻿ that what he says will happen, it will be done for him. Therefore I tell you, everything you pray﻿ and ask for﻿—believe that you have received﻿, it and it will be yours.”

Over the past day, Jesus has demonstrated what would happen to a those who only pretend to worship God will be cursed.

Rather than pretending to worship God, Jesus calls his disciples to have faith in God.

Be real about God, have faith in God, trust Him!

When you trust God to do the impossible, even if it’s telling a mountain to be thrown into the sea, it will be done.

Again, there are hints that Jesus is here talking about the temple worship.

As you walk to the city of Jerusalem from the Mount of Olives, you will see Jerusalem on a mountain, Mount Zion.

In Jesus’ day, you would also see the Temple on the mountain.

When Jesus said about telling the mountain to be thrown into the sea, the mountain in front of the disciples was probably the one with the Temple on it.

Though worship God on the temple had been central to the Jews, it hasn’t produced the fruit that God was looking for.

The time has come for its replacement.

In place of the temple will be a Person who has absolute faith in God.

This Person is King Jesus, the one who has the power to curse the fig tree and it withers, the one who can throw the mountain into the sea.

And King Jesus invites others, call for others, to have faith in God.

He calls for a people to have faith in God.

It’s a people who truly trust that God can do anything.

It’s a people who believes that there is nothing impossible with God.

It’s a people who believes that nothing can stand in God’s way.

And it’s out of their belief in God that they pray.

Look with me at verse 24, “Therefore I tell you, everything you pray﻿ and ask for﻿—believe that you have received﻿, it and it will be yours.”

If you take this verse in isolation, it sounds like the power of positive thinking.

If you believe that you have it, it’s yours!

But this verse is not talking about positive thinking, it’s not even talking about how much faith you have.

It’s not talking about if you want something bad enough, you’ll get it.

Rather it’s about what happens when you have faith in God.

When you have faith in God, when you know who He is, when you know His will, when you trust him, you can come and ask him and he will give it to you,

Because you are asking for what he’s looking for.

When you have faith in God, you want what he wants.

And when you ask what he wants, he will give it to you.

For example, someone who doesn’t know God might ask for a house.

He might know in his head that God can answer his prayer, but he doesn’t know God and doesn’t know what God wants.

The one who knows God will ask for what God wants, and God will listen and answer his prayer.

How do we know what God wants?

God’s already told us want he wants; God’s told us what he wants in His word, the Bible.

But have we been listening?

So often we hear God’s word read and taught but it comes in one ear and out the other.

If you want God to listen to your prayers, we have to listen to his Word first.

If we pray according to his will, he will hear us.

What’s one thing God want us to pray for? What’s one thing we’ll know he’ll answer?

Spend the next twenty seconds speaking with someone next to you to see if you can find it in today’s passage.

You can respond via WeChat, Zoom or Whatsapp.

What’s one thing God want us to pray for?

God has shown us many things that he wants us to pray for.

We see one of these in verse 25, “And whenever you stand﻿ praying, if you have anything against anyone, forgive﻿ him, so that your Father﻿ in heaven﻿ will also forgive﻿ you your wrongdoing.”

Even as we pray, God wants us to forgive others.

If there’s one thing he wants to see in us, it’s forgiveness.

When we come together at Church, and we pray the Lord’s prayer, we ask God to forgive us our sins, even as we forgive those who sin against us.

That’s one thing that you know God wants you to do: forgive others.

That’s one of the most powerful characteristics of Christians: ours is not a condemning culture, nor a cancel culture.

Rather, we have a forgiving culture.

This time last year, tragedy struck in Oatlands, a suburb next to Parramatta in Western Sydney.

Four children, Antony, Angelina and Sienna Abdallah and their cousin Veronique Sakr, were struck and killed by a ute that mounted the footpath as they were on the way to buy ice cream.

Three other children were injured as well.

It was a horrible tragedy.

The parents of the three children who were killed, Danny and Lelia, stood before the media on the fay of the funeral and said, “I am not angry at the driver, that is not who we are”.

Rather than being angry at the driver, they forgave the driver.

It was a powerful and moving demonstration of what forgiveness looks like.

One year on, just last week, they launched i4give Day.

They hope it will be a deep and thoughtful day prompting each one of us to commit to forgive.

God wants us to pray and God wants us to forgive.

Wouldn’t it be wonderful if that’s what people see among us, our prayers and our forgiveness?

Friends, King Jesus came, King Jesus cursed fruitless pretence and King Jesus calls for faithful prayer.

King Jesus came to look for fruits of true faith in the temple but found none.

Will he find faith in you and me?

Imagine Jesus comes and stays with you for the week, and he looks at your life like he looks at the fig tree, what will he find?

Will he find what he’s looking for?

One of the best things we’ve done as a Church is to start the weekly prayer meetings.

If you haven’t been yet, it’s on Tuesday evenings, beginning from 8:30pm.

This week, it’ll be in Chinese.

As we begin this new year, let’s commit to being a House of Prayer.

Of course, I know that you don’t have to come to the Prayer Meeting to pray; you can pray wherever you are.

But to be together, either in person or via zoom, and pray is a wonderful privilege.

To hear how God answers our prayer is a wonderful encouragement.

And so, can I commend you to join us in our Prayer meetings, wherever you might be on a Tuesday evening.

As well as praying, be forgiving.

We live in a community that would rather silent the opposition than to forgive them.

Let’s by God’s grace start making a difference.

Be forgiving of the faults of others, even when they seem unforgivable.

Like Danny and Lelia Abdallah, forgive in light of God’s forgiveness.

Here are the three questions for this week:

When I come to meet Jesus, do I come with joy and hope?

Is my faith showy or substantial?

Do I have the fruits of faith and forgiveness?

Will Jesus find what he’s looking for in me?

Will Jesus find faith in me?

May Jesus finds in us a substantial, joyful and hopeful faith in God.
