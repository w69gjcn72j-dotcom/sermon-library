When did you see Jesus sick and visited?

Big Idea: If you love Jesus, be kind to the little ones of Jesus when they’re sick.

Judgement Day

The sheep visit the sick; the goats don’t

Be kind to the little ones of Jesus

Judgement Day PPT

We live in a very divided world, don’t we?

During election, you’re a liberal voter or a labor voter (or maybe a green voter or some other minor parties)

A few years ago, you’re either pro same-sex marriage or you’re against same-sex marriage.

Over the past few weeks, it’s as if the world is divided into two groups of people, those with COVID-19 or those without COVID-19.

That’s what we see in many of the graphs on the media: how many people have contracted the virus?

How many people have recovered from the virus?

How many people have died from the virus?

That’s how we’ve divided our world: those with viruses along with its complications and those without.

When Jesus talks about the Judgement Day, he divides the world into two groups of people as well.

He doesn’t divided them into those with viruses and those without.

Rather, he divides them into those who have done something for him and those who haven’t.

He calls those who have done something for him sheep and those who haven’t he calls goats.

What does Jesus mean?

The sheep visit the sick; the goats don’t PPT

Look with me at Matthew 25:31,

31 “When the Son of Man comes in his glory, and all the angels with him, he will sit on his glorious throne. 32 All the nations will be gathered before him, and he will separate the people one from another as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats. 33 He will put the sheep on his right and the goats on his left.”

What’s so special about the sheep on his right?

Is it because they come to church?

Is it because they prayed to Jesus?

Those who are righteous, those who are blessed, are those who actively loved Jesus’s brothers.

Look with me at verse 36,

36 I needed clothes and you clothed me, I was sick and you looked after me, I was in prison and you came to visit me.’

These people were kind to Jesus.

They’ve done all sort of good deeds for Jesus.

The one I want to focus on today is the fact that when Jesus was sick, they looked after Jesus.

When Jesus was sick, they looked after Jesus.

When Jesus was sick?

There’s no record in the Bible of Jesus ever being sick.

The people that he was speaking to certainly couldn’t remember taking care of a sick Jesus.

They said, “When did we see you sick or in prison and go to visit you?’

Look with me at verse 40, “The King will reply, ‘Truly I tell you, whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers and sisters of mine, you did for me.’

Jesus says that when one of his brothers or sisters are sick and we look after them, we’re looking after Jesus.

That’s how much Jesus identifies with his brothers and sisters.

When we look after each other when we’re sick, we’re looking after Jesus.

Jesus identifies completely with his brothers and sisters.

When we show kindness to a brother or sister in Christ, we’re showing kindness to Jesus Christ.

Our brothers and sisters are important to Jesus and Jesus appreciates us looking after them. PPT

Someone who had exemplified what it means to show Christian kindness is the renowned obstetrician Dr Catherine Hamlin.

Dr Catherine Hamlin was an evangelical Sydney Anglican woman who co-founded Hamlin Fistula Ethiopia with her husband, Dr Reginald Hamlin, back in 1975.

Obstetric fistula is a medical condition in which a hole develops in the birth canal as a result of childbirth.

Women and girls who suffered from obstetric fistula have been described as our modern-day lepers.

These women and girls are often rejected by their husbands.

Dr Hamlin and her husband Dr Reginald Hamlin travelled from Sydney to Addis Ababa in 1958 and they stayed there to treat these women with obstetric fistula.

Reginald stayed and worked there until his death in 1993.

Catherine also stayed and worked there until her death earlier this week.

What inspired Dr Catherine Hamlin and her husband Reginald Hamlin to dedicate their lives to helping the women of Addis Ababa?

The very verse we just read, Matthew 25:40, “whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers of mine, you did for me.”

The verse is below Dr Reg Hamlin's photo which hangs in the hospital.

When the two Dr Hamlins serve the girls and the women in Addis Ababa, they do it for the glory of Jesus.

If they were treating the sick Christian girls in Addis Ababa, then Jesus says it’s as if they’re doing it for him.

Both Dr Hamlins were beautiful examples of what Christian kindness looks like in practice.

They’re beautiful examples of the sheep on the right hand of Jesus.

But what about those on his left? PPT

Look with me at verses 41, “Then he will say to those on his left, ‘Depart from me, you who are cursed, into the eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels.”

What did they do to deserve this?

It’s not about what they did as opposed to what they didn’t do.

They didn’t look after Jesus when he was sick; they didn’t visit him.

Listen to what Jesus says to them in verse 45, ‘Truly I tell you, whatever you did not do for one of the least of these, you did not do for me.’

These people don’t love Jesus’ brothers and sisters.

And if they don’t love Jesus’ brothers and sisters, they don’t love Jesus.

But when someone doesn’t love Christian believers, even if they call Jesus Lord, they will eventually end up in the eternal fire reserved for the devil and his angels.

Theirs is the sin of omission; their sin is that they don’t do what they’re supposed to do.

They’ll go to hell for not taking care of the brothers and sisters of Jesus when they’re sick.

To be honest, that’s easy to do because it’s easy just to look after yourselves and not others.

There’s a video emerged of a nurse filming herself going to the supermarkets after doing a 48 hours shift looking after sick people. PPT

What does she find at the supermarket? Nothing!

She broke down in the video explaining that she just wants to stay healthy so that she can get ready for another 48 hours shift.

But now, she’s got nothing.

She’s been taking care of sick people for 48 hours straight but as a society, we’re not taking care of her.

To use Jesus’ analogy, the goats ate all the grass and left nothing for the sheep.

If you’re concerned only for yourself and you have no concern for Christian brothers or sisters in need, then you’re going to spend eternity with the devil and his angels.

That’s how the Judgement Day will end according to Jesus, in verse 46,

“Then they will go away to eternal punishment, but the righteous to eternal life.”

Those who are kind to the vulnerable brothers and sisters of Jesus will spend eternity with Jesus.

Those who don’t show any concern or kindness to the vulnerable brothers and sisters of Jesus will spend eternity with the devil.

This is not a salvation by works.

Rather your works reveal whether you have been saved.

For if you have been saved, you will love the brothers and sisters of Jesus, not only with words but also in deeds.

Be kind to the little ones of Jesus

What does this mean for us?

We’ve been just introduced new social distance guidelines.

We’re told we can’t meet in Churches physically on Sundays now by none other than our Archbishop.

If we’re not to meet together, how can we help those who are vulnerable, those who are sick among us?

In a pandemic, how do you show love to other Christians without making it worse, both for you and your family and also the community at large?

Christians have struggled with this as we face different plagues over many centuries.

There were of course, many Christians who have stayed and helped but there are also many Christians who have struggled about staying.

Should Christians stay and help those in need?

What if they don’t?

In 1527, the bubonic plague came to Wittenberg in Germany.

It’s a plague far worse than the coronavirus our world is currently facing.

The death rate for the Bubonic Plague has been calculated as between 30 to 60 per cent of the population

The great reformer theologian Martin Luther, who lived in Wittenberg, was asked to leave.

And many people were asking Luther, should they stay or should they flee?

Here’s Luther’s response, which I think is very helpful. PPT

“This I well know, that if it were Christ or his mother who were laid low by illness everybody would be so solicitous and would gladly become a servant or helper. Everyone would want to be bold and fearless; nobody would flee but everyone would come running… If you wish to serve Christ and to wait on him, very well, you have your sick neighbour close at hand. Go to him and serve him, and you will surely find Christ in him…”

Luther affirms that it’s a good thing to stay behind and be a servant and help those in need.

That’s how you serve Christ!

But Luther also recognises that some had strong faith in the face of death, others not.

He continues, PPT

“To begin with some people are of the firm option that one need not and should not run away from a deadly plague. Rather since death is God’s punishment, which he sends upon us for our sins, we must submit to God and with a true and firm faith patiently await our punishment. …Others take the position that one may properly flee, particularly if one hold no public office.”

If someone has a position of responsibility in the community, he should stat and help, even if it means engaging with those who are sick.

Again, from Luther, PPT

“Those who are engaged in a spiritual ministry and pastors must remain steadfast before the peril of death. We have a plain command from Christ, ‘A good shepherd lays down his life for his sheep.'”

Ministers, pastors, rectors, says Luther, who have a position of responsibility, will remain to look after the sheep.

Father and mothers, who are responsible for their children, will remain to look after the children.

The same will go for Doctors, the nurses, the police, teachers, as well as others looking after children, should remain and fulfil their responsibility to the community.

That’s how Christians show love; that’s what Christian love looks like.

We show concern for others in need, especially those who belong to Jesus.

However, Luther also says that some who don’t have the responsibility may choose to flee.

If they are not needed to help in the outbreak, they may choose to flee.

And so, there may be some among us who ourselves are more vulnerable, more susceptible to contracting the coronavirus.

If that’s you, there’s no need for you to stay and help.

But if God has so moved your heart to help others in need during this hour, then be brave and help.

Help those around us who are in need.

As for me and my family, we’ll be staying and helping those in need.

And there are others in the Church who will help us as well.

Here are a few things that we’ll be doing.

Firstly, we’ll want to take care of the physical needs of our community wherever possible. PPT

Phear and her Go team have organised cards to be printed which will be distributed around our neighbourhood to help those who are in need.

We’ll ask whether they want help with shopping, gathering supplies, collecting mail or just to have someone to speak with.

If you want to help me with this, please let me know.

Secondly, we’ll also address the social needs of our community, particularly those in our Church community.

We are setting up networks for us to call one another to make sure we’re ok during this time of social isolation.

And we’re working on new technologies like Zoom to connect with you over the internet.

Thirdly, we’ll want to look after the spiritual needs of our community.

Po Ki and Range from the Grow team will look at ways to help us continue to grow in Christ.

We’ll also set up a Prayer Wall outside our Church for people to write down their prayers.

As well as the Prayer wall, we also want to set up the Word wall for us to put up words of Scripture so that people will find comfort in the words of God.

Friends, we do live in an extraordinary time.

It’s a time when our faith might be tested and our love might grow cold.

But it doesn’t have to be this way.

Let me finish with a quote from Dr Catherine Hamlin,

"I have sometimes been asked if these tragic cases ever test my faith. I can truly say that, if anything, my faith has been strengthened and my soul more firmly anchored to Jesus as the only one to whom we can turn. Jesus never promised us a smooth or trouble-free passage through life. He did promise to be with us to share the sorrow and the pain"

Friends, during these testing times, keep trusting in Jesus, keep obeying Jesus.

Keep loving his brothers and sisters, even when they’re sick.

He will be with you to share the sorrow and the pain along the way.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How am I showing love and kindness to our Church community?

How am I showing love and kindness to our wider community?

How does God want me to grow during this time?

Let’s pray,

“Our heavenly Father, we thank you for loving us so much that you gave us your one and only Son. Our Lord and Saviour, thank you for your love for us. Please fill us with the love that you have for them, that we might show genuine and practical love. There are many who are living in social isolation now; please send someone, even if that person is us, to someone in need. Holy Spirit, please fill us with your presence so that we can do what our Lord Jesus has asked us to do. In For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
