Whose Blessings Are You Receiving?

Genesis 14:17-24 · 2024
Big IdeaDetermine to be blessed only by Jesus, the Priest-King, whom we shall bless forever and ever.

Whose blessings are you receiving?

Passage: Genesis 14.17-24

Additional passages: Psalm 110:1-4, Hebrews 7:1-9

Big Idea / Take home sentence: Determine to be blessed only by Jesus, the Priest-King, whom we shall bless forever and ever.

Sodom’s king’s pseudo offer

Salem’s king’s genuine blessings

Be blessed by and bless Jesus the Priest-King

Last week, we had a wonderful wedding here at St Paul’s.

The wedding was between Jake and Chantel, and somehow, we managed to fit 170 people inside the building.

It was incredible.

Now before they got married, Jake would most probably have spoken with Chantel’s father, John.

In western tradition, the man would need to ask the father of the woman he wanted to marry in private, usually without the woman there.

He had to ask the father for his blessings first, before asking the woman to be his wife.

Did any of our married men do that? Did any of us ask our potential father-in-law’s blessings for his first?

Well, in Jake’s case, Chantel’s father must have said ‘yes’! A resounding ‘Yes’ at that.

In their wedding speeches, both Jake and John had expressed their respect and gratitude for one another.

It’s good to have an in-law’s blessings, isn’t it? It makes a big difference!

As we go through different stages in our lives, we want the blessings, or the approval, of different people.

After the wedding, we want the approval of our spouse.

As we work, we want the blessing and approval of our boss.

For the teenagers, they want the approval of their friends.

For the politician, they want the approval of the public.

Whose approval do you want? Whose blessings do you seek?

In today’s passage, we’re going to see that `not all approvals, not all blessings are the same.

And we have to take care and decide whose approval we should be seeking.

Please open up with me to Genesis 14, beginning from verse 17.

We’re going to see three things:

Sodom’s king’s pseudo offer

Salem’s king’s genuine blessings

Be blessed by and bless Jesus the Priest-King

But before we look into it, let’s pray, “Father, we bless you with all that we are! May your words enter our ears, into our hearts, be understood by our minds, and be actioned by our hands, that your word will accomplish that for which it is given, through Christ our Lord, Amen.”

Sodom’s king’s pseudo offer

Here’s what’s been happening so far.

Abram’s nephew was living near a place called Sodom.

After Sodom’s king Bera fought against the King of Elam and other kings and lost, Lot was taken away.

Abram went after the King of Elam with his 318 trained men, along with men who were bound by treaty to Abram.

Abram won and chased them as far as Hobah, to the north of Damascus.

Here’s the map of where they were and where the north of Damascus was.

Can anyone estimate how far that is, given that the scale for 100km is here.

Yes, it’s more than 200km away.

Abram came back with all the good, his nephew, as well as women and people as well.

It was a resounding victory!

Bera the King of Sodom went to meet Abram in the Valley of the king.

Then there’s as pause in the passage; the passage goes in a different direction and we’ll get back to it.

However, open up with now to verse 21, “Then the king of Sodom said to Abram, “Give me the people, but take the possessions for yourself.”

The King of Sodom (I’ll just call him Sodom for now) says that Abram gets to keep all the possessions as long as Sodom gets to keep his people.

Sounds like a good offer. But is it really?

There’s a saying in the marketing world, “He’s such a good salesman because he can sell ice to an eskimo (also known as an inuit)”

Have you heard that saying?

An inuit is someone who lives in the artic area, where it’s cold and full of ice.

To say that someone can sell ice to an eskimo or inuit means that a person is so persuasive that they can get someone to do something unnecessary or to buy something that they don’t need.

For example, why would an eskimo need more ice, since he’s got so much already?

That’s what Sodom is trying to do here.

Sodom lost his battle against he King of Elam; he’s lost everything, people and possessions included.

It was Abram who fought and won; it was Abram who brought back all that Sodom had lost.

Since it was Abram who won, it should’ve been Abram who gets to decide what to do with his spoils, his winnings!

But Sodom still thinks that he is in charge.

He forgets that if it wasn’t for Abram, everything that he had would’ve been lost.

As far as we know, he didn’t thank Abram for what he’s done; he’s shown no appreciation for what Abram had accomplished.

Instead, he tells Abram what to do with what now rightfully belongs to Abram: Give me the people; you take the possessions.

Sodom’s arrogant, rude, and deceptive.

He’s had nothing but contempt for Abram.

Contempt for Abram…does that sound familiar?

Have we read about this before? Yes, in Genesis 12:3, “I will bless those who bless you, I will curse anyone who treats you with contempt.”

Sodom is in danger of being cursed by God because he’s shown contempt to Abram.

Abram saw through Sodom’s lies and responds by saying that he made an oath to the LORD, the God Most High, that he would not take anything from Sodom.

Abram won’t take anything, whether people or possessions, whether it’s something as small as a thread, or a sandal strap, or anything at all.

He refuses to take anything from Sodom so that Sodom can never say, “You see how rich Abram became? I had a hand in that.”

Abram refuses to receive anything from Sodom for himself, but he does ask for the food that his servants have already eaten along the journey, as well as what the three men who came with him, Aner, Eschol and Mamre, are entitled to.

Besides these, Sodom can have everything back.

Abram would not take any offers from Sodom.

They’re pseudo offers, fake offers.

Around two thousand years later, the devil himself made an offer to one of the descendant of Abram.

To this descendant of Abram, the devil said, “I will give you all these things if you will fall down and worship me.”

This descendant, this Son of Abram, this Jesus, didn’t need to suffer on the cross, didn’t need to go through any pain or sorrow.

If he wanted the world, all he needed to do was bow before the devil.

But the thing is, Jesus already owns everything; all things were created by him and for him.

Jesus saw through the devil’s lies, Jesus saw through his pseudo offers and he said, “Go away, Satan! For it is written: Worship the Lord your God, and serve only him.” ‘

Sodom offered a pseudo gift to Abram and Abram rejected it.

The devil made a pseudo offer to Jesus and Jesus rejected it.

The world, the flesh and the devil will keep offering us pseudo gifts and offers, to lure us away from worshipping the one and only God.

They may sound something like this:

“Your Church is small, and it needs the community’s approval.

And so, don’t talk about Jesus and you will be accepted.”

Or, “You need this person’s love and approval more than anything, do whatever they say, even if it’s against Jesus’ teachings.”

These temptations can be very strong.

It doesn’t matter whether you’re young Christian or old, you will always be offered something other than Jesus and often they look like a good deals.

But don’t fall for it.

It’s a lie. It’s a trap.

It looks like love but it’s lust, that is, love for the wrong thing or the wrong person.

It looks like wisdom in the world but it’s foolishness in God’s sight.

It’s wrong and it’s a sin because it’s looking for approval from men instead of approval from God.

It’s a longing to receive blessings from men rather than receiving blessings from God.

A youth group leader asked her group of girls how they struggle with lust.

What she heard made her heart sank (made her very sad):

Will someone love me? How can I long for God’s love more than a boy’s love?

What made the girls vulnerable to the sin of lust is not their desire to be physically lustful.

What made them vulnerable was their fear of being rejected.

What made them vulnerable was their fear of not being approved by the boy they like.

For the teenage boy, it might be their fear of not being approved by the girl they like.

For the married couples, it might be their fear of not being approved by their spouse or by their in-laws.

We want their approvals and it’s hard when we don’t have it.

We might understand with our mind that the lies of the devil, world and flesh are wrong but our hearts don’t want to be rejected by men and women we care about.

And the heart almost always wins.

We almost always listen to our hearts and choose the approval of men and women rather than the approval of God.

Though we know it’s wrong, we compromise.

We take the devil’s offer; we do exactly what the devil wants us to do.

How do we stop our hearts from falling for the pseudo offers of the devil?

Salem’s king’s genuine blessings

We need to believe and live out what it means to be in a genuine, personal relationship with God.

We need to live and breathe the gospel.

We see this in the middle session of this passage.

The passage is like a sandwich.

Surrounded by the pseudo offers of Sodom in verse 17 and verses 21-24, is the good part, the genuine blessings of the King of Salem, who’s called Melchizedek.

Look with me at verses 18-20, “Melchizedek, king of Salem, brought out bread and wine; he was a priest to God Most High. 19 He blessed him and said:

Abram is blessed by God Most High, Creator of heaven and earth, and blessed be God Most High who has handed over your enemies to you.”

The Bible only has three verses with Melchizedek in action.

The three verses we just read is the only record we have for what Melchizedek has done.

And if you take these verses about Melchizedek out, the story will still make sense.

However, who he is, how he has appeared in this passage is vital to understanding other key passages in the Bible.

The next time his name comes up again in the Bible is Psalm 110, the most quoted psalm in the New Testament.

You see Psalm 110 quoted in Matthew, Mark, Luke, Acts, Romans 1 Corinthians Ephesians, Colossians, 1 Peter and Revelations.

And in Hebrews, there’s an expanded explanation of who Melchizedek is.

Children, if you have your notes there, spell the name, write it down.

Adults, read up about Melchizedek in Psalm 110 and Hebrews 7.

Get to know who he is.

Firstly, his name Melchizedek, literally means the king of righteousness.

Melchi – king. Zedek – righteousness.

Melchizedek is the king of SALEM, which is highly likely where JeruSALEM would one day be built.

Melchizedek brought bread and wine as refreshments for Abram and his men after their long journey.

As many as you know, Po Ki and Phear, Catherine and Rupert moved a few weeks ago to be a lot closer to the Church.

A few members of our Church went and helped with the move.

They worked many hours and travelled many kilometres.

We had the pleasure of providing them with some food for lunch.

Hope they felt refreshed afterwards!

That’s what Melchizedek did – he brought food for Abram and his men to refresh them.

As well as the king, Melchizedek’s also the Priest to God Most High.

We don’t know how he came to know God, but Abraham accepted him as a genuine priest of the real God since Abram accepted his offer of bread and wine and his blessings.

Also, Abram gave Melchizedek a tenth of everything.

And so, Abram recognised Melchizedek as both king and priest, and that Melchizedek’s blessings are blessings from the one true God.

When we think about blessings, we tend to think about having good things happening to us.

But in the Bible, the language of blessing is far richer.

To be blessed by God is to be approved by God.

God has shown that he’s blessed Abram by causing these good things to happen to Abram.

Abram is blessed by God because God has led him to victory; Abram’s victory is a blessing from God.

But Melchizedek didn’t stop at blessing Abram.

Melchizedek blessed God; he said, “Blessed be God Most High”.

He’s not blessing God in the sense that he’s giving God his approval, as if God needed his approving.

When Melchizedek said, “Blessed be God Most High” he was affirming who God is and affirming that God is both good and powerful.

God is the one who handed Abram’s enemies to him. God has been good to Abram.

And so, Melchizedek is the Priest-King who blessed Abram.

This Priest-King combination in one person is rare in the Old Testament.

Throughout the Old Testament, the king, the one whom God’s put in charge politically is not the same man God’s chosen to be his representative regarding religious sacrifices.

King Saul tried to be both the King and the Priest by offering an illegal sacrifice, but he was then condemned by the Prophet Samuel as foolish.

By foolishly disobeying God’s command, Saul lost his kingship to someone else.

Besides this incident, there was no mixing of kingship and priesthood in one individual in the Old Testament history, except for Psalm 110, the most quoted psalm in the New Testament.

Look with me at Psalm 110, beginning from verse 1.

“This is the declaration of the LORD to my Lord: “Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies your footstool.” The LORD will extend your mighty scepter from Zion. Rule over your surrounding enemies. Your people will volunteer on your day of battle. In holy splendor, from the womb of the dawn, the dew of your youth belongs to you. The LORD has sworn an oath and will not take it back: “You are a priest forever according to the pattern of Melchizedek.”

There will be a time when there will be a King-Priest.

This King will be at God’s right hand; he will be powerful, he will rule over his enemies and he will be a priest forever in the pattern of Melchizedek.”

Who will this King-Priest be like?

How can a person be both the powerful King as well as the priest who represents us before God and represents God to us.

The answer is in Hebrews 7, beginning from verse 1,

“For this Melchizedek, king of Salem, priest of God Most High, met Abraham and blessed him as he returned from defeating the kings, 2 and Abraham gave him a tenth of everything. First, his name means king of righteousness, then also, king of Salem, meaning king of peace. 3 Without father, mother, or genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life, but resembling the Son of God, he remains a priest forever.”

Melchizedek the King-Priest is like a pattern which ultimately resembles Jesus, the Son of God.

He’s like a foreshadowing of Jesus.

Who is Jesus like?

Well, He’s like Melchizedek, a King-Priest who has no beginning of days or end of life.

He’s both the King and the Priest and he will be the King-Priest forever.

Unlike all kings and priests before him, who all died, Jesus died and he rose again to indestructible life.

Unlike all kings and priests, who will die and be replaced by other kings and priests, no one will ever succeed Jesus as priest for he will never die.

And no one will ever succeed Jesus as King because His Kingship will last forever.

He will be the true Melchizedek, the King of Righteousness who rules the true Salem, the heavenly Jerusalem, a Kingdom of true Peace, forever and ever.

It’s his blessings that count; it’s his approval that you want.

If you know who Jesus is, you will want his blessings, you will want his approval, above everyone, above all else.

And he wants to give his blessings to you; he wants to give you his approval.

Jesus is the Priest of the God Most High, he has sacrificed his own life for all those who belong to him, for all those who believe in him.

He’s the Great High Priest who prays for us, in front of God’s presence.

He’s the Great King of Righteousness who will reign forever and ever.

No matter how dark your world is, no matter how chaotic your world is, he will overcome, and he will take you to be with him, if you would put your trust in him.

3. Be blessed by and bless Jesus the Priest-King

How do we respond to Jesus’ genuine offer of blessings?

We want to be blessed by Jesus and we want to bless by Jesus above all else.

In response to Melchizedek’s blessings, Abram gave a tenth of all he had to the Priest-king.

This was common practice to give a tenth of what you have to the priests, not as a fee, but to show your thankfulness for receiving blessings from the gods though the priests.

Again, we see Genesis 12:3 at work.

People who show Abram contempt will be cursed by God, but people who bless Abram will be blessed by God.

It’s like an ecosystem of blessings between God, Abram and Melchizedek.

There’s a lot of giving and receiving of blessings, it’s a community marked by blessings.

It’s a little like what happens in Chinese New Year.

In Chinese New Year, we wish for many good things for one another.

We wish that our elders will have good health, we wish for our children to get better marks and we wish everyone else to make a lot of money (Gun Xi Fat Choi)

When Christians come together, we should also bless one another in God’s name.

We don’t pray for God to make us all rich; we pray for something far better for each other.

We say, the LORD be with you, and also with you (主与你同在，也与你同在).

God has set up this ecosystem of blessings which we call church, where we bless one another in the name of Jesus Christ because of what He’s done!

However, before we enter into this ecosystem of blessings, before we bless one another in God’s name, we must have been blessed by God himself first.

God has given us every spiritual blessing in the heavenly realms in Christ.

God’s chosen to give all his blessings to us through Christ.

We must come to Jesus Christ first.

He is the One Person, the Only Person, through whom we receive God’s every blessing in the spiritual realms.

It’s his blessings that we seek above all else.

It’s not just knowing about his blessings in your mind but to know and love his blessings in your heart of hearts.

Once you know and have experienced his blessings, you will be thankful for his blessings.

And as much as I like your approval, I will always seek Christ’s approval first.

I want to be blessed and approved by Christ more than I want to be blessed by anyone else.

If you have been blessed by Jesus Christ you will want to bless Jesus Christ.

When we say we bless Jesus, we’re not saying that we’re approving Jesus, as if he needs our approving.

Rather, when we bless Jesus, we’re affirming Jesus.

We give God thanks for Jesus before all other blessings.

We praise the name of Jesus, we lift our eyes to him.

And we want to bless him above all else.

We will thank him in words, we will thank him in song, and we will thank him in giving, just like Abram did.

If you have been blessed by Jesus Christ and you bless Jesus Christ, then you will bless one another in the name of Christ.

You will bless them by affirming them with the words of Jesus; you will bless them with the forgiveness that comes from Jesus.

When they persevere in faith, we will bless them, we will thank God for them.

In fact, tell someone this week that you’re thanking God for them.

Bless them by serving them, as Christ commanded you.

Bless them by giving and supplying them what they need.

Bless them by praying for them and pointing them to Christ.

Be blessed by Jesus, bless Jesus and bless those who belong to Jesus.

Whose blessings do you seek? Whose blessings do I seek?

Seek Jesus’ blessings, seek his approval above all else.

Bless Jesus before blessing anyone else even during the hard times, especially during the hard times.

Bless Jesus, because of what he’s already done for you and what he will do for you. He’s the Priest who prays for you. He’s the King who leads you.

And so,

Determine to be blessed only by Jesus, the Priest-King, whom we shall bless forever and ever.

Ashley Null, the famous English theologian once said, "what the heart loves, the will chooses, and the mind justifies."

We need to desire Jesus, we need to desire his blessings, we need to desire him, love him above all else.

Out heart needs to be in tune with him.

What does it mean to tune our hearts to him?

When musicians come to play for Church, one of the things they must do is tune their instruments.

With the exception of the piano, which gets tuned every now and then, all instruments need to be tuned.

What happens if they are not tuned? This (untuned instrument) and this (more untuned instrument).

It’s horrible, it’s painful to the ears. My ears literally hurt.

And so we need to all tune to the same frequency.

Once we’re tuned, we have harmony.

That’s what needs to be done to our hearts.

Our hearts constantly go out of tune.

Our hearts sway according to the temptations and lures of the world; our hearts move according to the sin that still lives inside our heart.

And so we always need to tune our hearts to delight in God’s praises, in God’s blessings, in blessing God.

Jesus is perfectly living in tune with God – he’s in perfect harmony with God.

The Spirit of God tunes us to Jesus as we hear from the Bible and as we pray.

Is your heart in tune with God?

Whose approval do you seek?

How have you responded to God’s blessings in Jesus and how have you blessed God?

The devil, the world, your flesh, your heart will give you pseudo offers that will one day vaporise,

They will give you promises that will one day be broken.

Don’t fall for their lies. Instead, come to Jesus, the Priest-King, the One who had and who will always keep all of God’s promises.

Pray that your hearts will be in tune with Christ who is in tune with God.

Be blessed by him and bless him, on this day and forever and ever. Amen.

Here are the three Questions for this week.

How have I been blessed by Jesus, the Priest-King?

How have I been tuning my heart’s desire to what God desires?

How am I blessing Jesus, the Priest-King?

And so, friends, determine to be blessed only by Jesus, the Priest-King, whom we shall bless forever and ever.

Let’s pray, this prayer is based on the words of John Owen, a famous Puritan theologian,

“Father, if we have hatred in the world, we will go to where we are sure of love. Though all others are hard to us, yet, our heavenly Father you are tender and full of compassion: We will come to you, and satisfy ourselves in you. Here we are accounted vile, frowned on, and rejected; but with you we have honour and love, for your kindness is better than life itself. Father, thank you for all the blessings that you promise to give us in Christ. As we come before you, bless us with these blessings in Christ as you have promised in your word. And by your Spirit, let us bless you and praise you in all that we say and do, for in Christ’s name we pray, Amen.”
