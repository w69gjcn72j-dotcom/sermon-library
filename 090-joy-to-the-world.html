Joy to the world

Luke 2:25-39

Big Idea: Joy, comfort and redemption belong to those whose hearts have been pierced and renewed by Jesus

Additional passages: Psalm 118:22

1. Waiting eagerly for God’s gifts

2. God’s gift pierces hearts

3. God brings joy to the world through Jesus His Gift

Good morning, everyone, on this Christmas morning!

During this time of the year, many people organise Christmas parties and have fun with one another.

Every now and then, people come together to play games.

Has anyone been to a Christmas party this week? Did you play any special games?

Some of the game we play might be card games and other times it might be more active games.

I love how kids come up with different game ideas when they get together.

I remember watching this video on the internet where two toddlers were having fun by playing with the garbage bin lid.

The garbage bin is one of those where you press a switch with your foot and the lid comes up – like this one right here.

What the boys were doing was one of them would put their face next to the lid and the other would put their face right next to the lid.

When the boy press the switch, the lid comes up quickly and hits the other boy.

They would then swap and start the process again!

They endure a little bit of pain, but at least they were having fun!

That little bit of pain probably didn’t hurt too much, until about the 5th turn!

Then it might start to hurt a little!

Generally speaking, of course, our pain doesn’t lead to happiness.

However, in today’s passage, it seems to suggest that there is one sort of pain that leads to joy.

It’s talking about a pain that pierces the heart.

In fact, it suggests that this pain has a direct connection with Christmas.

How can a pierced heart lead to comfort and joy?

If you have your Bible there, please open with me to Luke chapter two beginning from verse 25.

We’ll see three things in today’s passage:

1. Waiting eagerly for God’s gifts

2. God’s gift pierces hearts

3. God brings joy to the world through Jesus His Gift

Before we begin, let’s pray, “Father, thank you for bringing us together on this Christmas Day! Help us to see the joy that you bring to us through your Son our Lord Jesus Christ, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

1. Waiting eagerly for God’s gifts

In this passage, we meet two strong Jewish belivers, Simeon and Anna.

Simeon probably was already quite old; Anna was definitely old, possibly one hundred.

They were both waiting for God to do something about their nation.

Look with me at Luke 2:25, “There was a man in Jerusalem whose name was Simeon. This man was righteous and devout, looking forward to Israel’s consolation, and the Holy Spirit was on him.”

And also verse 38, “At that very moment, she (that is, Anna) came up and began to thank God and to speak about him to all who were looking forward to the redemption of Jerusalem.”

Simeon was looking forward for Israel to be comforted, and Anna was looking forward to see Israel redeemed or saved.

Israel was under Roman rule and needed rescuing from their political oppressors.

But more than that, they both knew that it wasn’t just about a political salvation.

A deep change needed to happen in the people’s hearts.

They knew that God had promised this much-needed comfort and salvation and so they waited and longed for the day God gives Isreal what he promised.

Today, many children around the world would wake up on Christmas morning and open their Christmas gifts.

They were promised by their relatives that they would receive presents on Christmas day and so they waited, many very eagerly, for Christmas morning to come

However, there are many children who won’t have any presents.

According a report released by the Australian institute, there are many children around Australia living in poverty.

They projected that In 2025, 1 million children will be in poverty. This is a hard time for many families who are struggling to make ends meet.

That’s why I’m so glad that our Church has partnered with Anglicare for the Toys N Tucker programme.

It’s a programme to gather food and toys for children in need and to also bring toys and food for children in need in our area.

It’s great that some of the brothers and sisters came to church to help.

It’s great for these kids to open up presents on Christmas Day as well.

Simeon and Anna were in a sense also looking forward to Christmas Day as well; it’s just that they didn’t know it at the time.

All they knew was that God had promised to bring comfort and redemption for his people,

and they had been waiting for most of their lives for the day when God would fulfill his promises.

They didn’t know exactly what they were looking for, but they trusted that God would show them clearly when it happened.

However, one thing Simeon knew for sure.

Simeon was told by the Holy Spirit that he wouldn’t die until he saw the Lord’s Messiah.

And so, one day, guided by God’s Holy Spirit, Simeon was walking in the temple area when he saw a young couple bringing their child to him for a blessing.

When Simeon took the baby, he knew instantly.

He knew that he was holding God’s salvation in his arms.

And that baby was none other than Jesus!

Simeon was so overwhelmed with joy, that he praised God saying (in verse 29), “Now, Master, you can dismiss your servant in peace, as you promised.”

He could finally be released in peace for his life had reached its peak.

It couldn’t get any better than holding Jesus in his arms!

He had seen God’s salvation and knew that this little baby boy would bring salvation and comfort to all people.

Anna, a prophetess, who had lived with her husband for seven years before becoming a widow for the next 84 years was also at the temple.

Anna loved God and she loved worshipping God at the temple.

If you wanted to find Anna, you would almost certainly find her in the temple.

When Simeon was praising God about this baby, she came up and thanked God as well.

She then spoke to others who were also searching for God’s salvation.

Simeon and Anna both praised God and thanked Him for fulfilling His promises through this little baby.

They had waited with eager anticipation for God to fulfill His promises, even more than kids who couldn’t wait to open their gifts on Christmas morning!

God’s promise of comfort and redemption, this promise that they had been waiting for many years had finally happened!

You know, it’s great to see all the kids and families at church, but it’s equally wonderful to see the older saints here as well.

They’ve known Jesus since they were young and have walked with him for many years.

It’s amazing to see their faith in action too.

It’s so good to see some of our older brothers and sisters talking with new people and actively sharing snippets of the gospel with them last Sunday at the Christmas concert! Praise God! They trusted in Jesus through all the ups and downs, the pain and tears, and they can teach the younger ones to keep growing in their faith.

Friends, treasure those who have been walking with Christ for 10, 20, 30, or even 100 years, like Anna, and are still growing in Jesus as they continue to wait for the Jesus to come back for them.

Simeon and Anna had an eager expectation to see God give his gifts of comfort and salvation to his people.

They’ve seen God’s answer in Jesus, and they praised God for Jesus.

However, in Simeon’s answer, there’s also an element of seriousness as we think about how to respond to this baby.

2. God’s gift pierces hearts

Look with me at verses 34-35, “Then Simeon blessed them and told his mother Mary, “Indeed, this child is destined to cause the fall and rise of many in Israel and to be a sign that will be opposed— 35 and a sword will pierce your own soul—that the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed.”

This child won’t bring happiness to everyone.

He is destined to bring the fall and rise of many in Israel.

He will be a sign that will be opposed; he will face strong opposition.

With his coming, the thoughts of many hearts will be revealed.

In the middle of all this, he adds an extra note, almost like a paratheses that was referring directly to Mary.

A sword will pierce Mary’s own heart also.

When I was a kid, steak wasn’t a regular part of our diet.

So, when I did get to eat it, I used any knives I could find.

After what must have been many years, someone told me to use steak knives to cut steak instead. It made eating steak a lo easier!

The steak knife easily cut through!

Simeon says that this child will be even more piercing than a steak kife.

He will be more like a sword

Both his words and his actions will pierce to the heart, not literally, but metaphorically.

The heart as referred to here is the deepest part of our being, where we think and hide our thoughts.

Simeon is talking not just any heart, but Mary’s heart.

He’s not talking about the impact the Child will have on the nation as a whole, but he is talking about Mary personally.

How will this child be like a sword piercing Mary’s own heart?

The sword isn’t physical; the sword represents the person and the message of Jesus.

Jesus will challenge Mary’s very being.

The sword is the message of Jesus getting through to Mary, piercing her heart, and revealing her thoughts.

Throughout Jesus’ earthly ministry, Mary didn’t seem like she believed in Jesus in the way you and I would.

Jesus’ message and actions pierced Mary’s heart to show her what she was really thinking in her heart of hearts.

It wasn’t pretty.

The gospel of Jesus is like a surgeon’s knife, cutting deep into our hearts to reveal our true selves. Here are three possible reactions to Jesus’ piercing.

Ignore it: If you ignore the gospel, it means you’re not ready to accept God’s gift of Jesus.

React violently: The gospel can cause people to oppose Jesus, just like the Jews and Romans opposed him so intensely that they nailed him to a cross.

It was a painful time for Jesus on the cross and it would’ve been painful for Mary, his mother to watch.

Be pierced and healed: The Holy Spirit can lead people to Jesus, just like He did with Simeon. His words pierce our hearts to show how broken we are.

But more than that, He can also heal broken hearts.

So, if you’re not ready to have your heart pierced by the words of Jesus, you won’t find the comfort and joy that comes from knowing and being known by him, loving and being loved by him.

Everyone, including Mary, needs to have their hearts pierced before they can be restored by Jesus.

If we oppose Jesus, we might get some physical comfort and transient joy.

However, if we let Jesus pierce our hearts with his word and we confess our sins before him, we’ll be redeemed and we’ll be saved!

Christmas is a great time of hope and joy. There are lots of celebration all around Sydney.

But if the only image of Jesus we’re comfortable with is the little baby in the manger, then we have a very deficient view of who Jesus really is.

Jesus pierces our soul with his word, exposing what’s on the inside.

It’s only when our hearts are pierced by Jesus that we find true healing for our hearts.

Our KOGMen group has gone through several chapters of Al Stewart’s the Manual. The author Al Stewart is a blokey bloke. A man’s man. But as you read through the book, he shares stories about his struggles, how the words of Jesus exposed what was inside his heart. Al’s an experienced Christian man, mature in Christ, but still not perfect. He is learning to becoming a better man in Christ. How? By keep reading the words of Jesus. His heart is pierced by the words of Jesus but it’s also restored by the words of Jesus. That’s what the word of Jesus does– it pierces your heart, brings you down and also at the same time, restore and build you up in Christ.

How has God pierced your heart this year? What does his word tell you about the state of your heart? Even now, on this Christmas morning, what has God’s word been revealing about your heart?

3. God brings joy to the world through Jesus His Gift

God’s word isn’t just for us here today, but for everyone around the globe. He wants to give his joy through Jesus, His gift.

This gift is for everyone - a light for the Gentiles and glory to Israel. No matter who you are, Israelite or not, you can receive it.

He wants the whole world to experience the comfort and salvation that comes from knowing that Jesus Christ has come to save us from our sins.

He died on the cross, having his hands and feet pierced, to take away our sins.

To receive the gift of his comfort and salvation, we need to let his words pierce us.

We need to listen and let his words go deep into our hearts. We need to confess our sins before him and turn away from them, turning to him instead.

Our Church and many other churches around the world want to share God’s word with as many people as possible, in as many languages as possible. We do this because God wants us to bring joy to the world, and he does that through his powerful and piercing words.

When someone hears the word of Jesus and has their heart pierced, there’s great joy in heaven.

That’s the joy that God wants to give to the world!

How can a pierced heart lead to comfort and joy?

A heart pierced by the words of the Lord Jesus is the one who finds comfort and joy.

For those with pierced hearts see their true state before God and has the opportunity to turn back to him.

And so, let the good news of Jesus pierce your heart this Christmas!

Joy, comfort and redemption belong to those whose hearts have been pierced and renewed by Jesus

I hope you have a great time over this week as you celebrate this Christmas.

But before you continue with your celebration, let the word of Jesus speak to you.

Let it pierce your heart, so that you may celebrate with true, genuine comfort and joy.

Joy, comfort and redemption belong to those whose hearts have been pierced and renewed by Jesus

Here are the three questions for this week:

What do you wait eagerly for this Christmas?

Have you ever opposed / disagree with Jesus? If so, on what matters?

Do I have the joy that comes from a heart pierced and restored by Jesus? If so, what does that look like?

Do you want Joy, comfort and redemption this Christmas?

Then get ready to have your hearts pierced and renewed by Jesus.

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you that you are the God who brings joy to our hearts. Please forgive us for trying to hide our sins and our faults when nothing could be hidden from you. We pray that you word will pierce our hearts, so that we can find comfort and salvation in the Lord Jesus. Pray that you will keep all those travelling to be safe and that they will come back soon. This we ask in the name of Jesus, Amen.”
