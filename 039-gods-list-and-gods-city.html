What’s the best city in the world to live in?

The list is different every year, but according to the Conde Nast Traveler magazine, the best city in the world was Auckland New Zealand.

I’ve never been to Auckland, but I was assured by the magazine that it’s a beautiful place to hike, it has great diversity in culture, and it has a great infrastructure.

It sounds like a great city to live in!

What would you give to live in a great city like Auckland, the best city in the world to live in?

If you’re serious about living in Auckland, you will need to go to the New Zealand embassy and apply for a visa.

You’ll need to give up your job here, say goodbye to your friends and families, sell your car or maybe even your house, pack up your bags and go.

Many of us have probably had some experience of that when we moved to Australia. Am I right?

It takes a lot of effort to move from one city to another and you won’t do it unless you have really good reasons to.

Today, I want to tell you about a city that’s worth moving to.

According to the Bible it’s the best and most important city in the world.

And it’s not Auckland, New Zealand.

It’s a city that’s worth moving to, even if it cost you everything you own.

Do you want to live in the best city in the world?

What is the best city in the world?

If you have your Bible there, please open up to Nehemiah chapter 7.

Let’s pray and ask God to help us understand his words:

“Father, by your Spirit, please open our minds so that your word can penetrate our head, make a home in our hearts, and express itself through our hands. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

What’s the best city in the world according to the Bible?

Well, it’s Jerusalem of course and that’s where we find ourselves as we look come to Nehemiah 7.

Before we look into the passage, here’s a quick recap of what’s been happening so far.

Israel has been living in the Promised Land for many years.

The most important city in the land of Israel was Jerusalem.

It was where the King of Judah ruled for many years.

However, over time, the kings along with the people rebelled against God.

Though God had warned them many times that he would punish them, they would not listen.

In the year 586BC, God made good his word and sent the Babylonians to destroy Jerusalem.

The Babylonians won and exiled God’s people to Babylon, where God said that they would spend 70 years.

When the 70 years were up, God moved the heart of a new king who allowed God’s people to come back to Jerusalem.

They stayed there for many years but were very slow to rebuild Jerusalem.

The city was left in shambles for many years.

It wasn’t until 445BC, some ninety years after the exile went back to Jerusalem, that Nehemiah led the people to rebuild the city walls of Jerusalem.

In Nehemiah chapters 1-6, we see a record of the struggle that Nehemiah went through to rebuild the walls.

But under God’s provision and Nehemiah’s leadership, the city walls were rebuilt in 52 days.

That’s where we find Nehemiah here in Nehemiah 7.

Look with me at 7:1, “When the wall had been rebuilt and I had the doors installed,a the gatekeepers,b singers, and Levites were appointed.”

Nehemiah installed the doors and appointed gatekeepers to improve the security of the city.

Interestingly, he also appointed singers and Levites.

The Levites were a tribe of people chosen by God to assist with the worship of God.

Nehemiah was keen to make sure the people were ready to worship God soon!

Nehemiah’s also chosen good leaders to be in charge of Jerusalem.

Hanani, his brother who was the first person to tell him about the state of Jerusalem

And Hananiah, commander of the fortress, a faithful man who feared God more than most.

What a brilliant description! The commander of the fortress is a faithful man who I take is a brave commander not because he feared God more than most.

They were instructed to keep the city safe by keeping a close eye on the gates and to station the citizens of Jerusalem as guards at their posts and even at their homes.

It looked like they were really stretched with man power because there weren’t that many men in the city.

Look with me at verse 4, “the city was large and spacious, but there were few people in it, and no houses had been built yet.”

In China, in the region of Ordos, there’s a city called Kangbashi.

It’s a city with thousands of houses as well as an airport, parks, theatres, street sculptures, and even a museum.

It’s big enough to support one million people but at the time of the report that I read, it was at only 2% capacity.

Some people call cities like Kangbashi ghost cities.

That’s what Jerusalem was like except it had even less infrastructure.

There wouldn’t have been many buildings there; there were not even houses.

There was hardly anyone there.

Even though the Bible called it the perfection of beauty, the joy of the whole earth,

It sure wouldn’t have looked like it in the time of Nehemiah.

It would’ve looked more like a war torn city than a place where you want to raise your family.

It was also a vulnerable city, open to attacks from the enemies.

That’s why they have to get the very few citizens living there to keep a close eye on the security of the city.

Some people’s homes double as a watch post.

For many of God’s people, it would’ve been easier to live in their hometowns, where it was safe and more comfortable, than to live in the city of Jerusalem.

It’s a phenomenon that we notice happening in Churches around Sydney as well.

During the lockdown, most if not all Churches had online services.

We praise God that we can livestream online to reach those who couldn’t come, including those who are vulnerable, as well as the elderly.

We were watched by people in Port Macquarie, in England and in America.

But one of the unintended side-effects of online services is that even when it’s safe to come back to in-person services, people don’t.

It’s a lot easier to watch the service from the comforts from your own home.

But just like the ghost cities, churches, too, can become vulnerable when only a few people attend the in-person services.

I know that quite a few of our people are already quite stretched.

But it’s not just the people who meet in here who are at risk of getting stretched.

People who don’t come long term in person can be spiritually at risk as well.

Over time, there’s a risk that their desire for worship cools and they think that not meeting with other Christians is normal.

What are some dangers that Christians face if they don’t meet other Christians for long periods of time?

Have a chat with the person next to you.

Send in your answers by WeChat, Whatsapp or Zoom.

What are some dangers that Christians face if they don’t meet other Christians for long periods of time?

You have twenty seconds.

Of course, there are those among us who are vulnerable who can’t physically come.

I’ve spoken to someone in that situation, and he’s been calling a lot of people on the phone to keep the fellowship with other believers going.

We thank God that many in our community are now feeling more at ease about going to shopping, work and school, places that have a higher risk of catching COVID than the Church.

Our prayer is that people in our community won’t delay in coming back to in-person gathering if they are able to come.

How did God go about encouraging his people to come back to fill the empty city of Jerusalem?

He starts by getting Nehemiah to find a list of names.

2. God’s list of names

Look with me at verse 5, “Then my God put it into my mind to assemble the nobles, the officials, and the people to be registered by genealogy. I found the genealogical record of those who came back first, and I found the following written in it.”

What a strange strategy!

To encourage people to fill the empty city of Jerusalem, God begins with a list of names.

This is a list of names of people who came back to Jerusalem first.

Most of them were probably not alive by the time Nehemiah read the list.

It was a list from around 90 years ago.

The names on the list were exiles who came back from Babylon to Jerusalem all those years ago.

These were the exiles who came back to Jerusalem and the home towns.

They were the names of the fathers and the grandfathers of God’s people in Nehemiah’s time.

He was listing the names of their families.

In Brighton-Le-Sands, there’s a memorial to all those who came in the First Fleet to Australia back in 1788.

For many Australians, the names of their ancestors, their great-great grandfathers, would be on that memorial.

They would come for different reasons; some as convicts, others as free men.

But all of them have this in common: they made Australia their new home.

It’s fascinating to see this long list of names here in Nehemiah 7.

I won’t go through the whole list; I probably can’t pronounce most of the names.

But there are some interesting things to note about these names.

Some of these are names of people; others are names of places.

There are names of priests, Levites and singers, gatekeepers, temple servants and even Solomon’s servants.

As well as Jewish names, there are also some foreign sounding names.

Why does God put this long list of names in the Bible?

It’s because God knows his people by name.

There’s a sense in which God knows everyone by their name, of course, but when the Bible wants to emphasise the fact that someone has special relationship with God, it would often say that God knows that person by name.

Moses, whom God’s chosen to lead Israel out of Egypt, was someone whom God knew by name.

Moses knew that he wasn’t the only one whom God knew by name.

He described a book that God has with names.

He knew that his name was in God’s book.

He pleaded for God to forgive the Israelites and was willing to have his name blotted from God’s book along with the rest of the Israelites, should God decide not to save them.

In the Psalms, the Psalmist refers to the book of life, which is associated with the righteous.

God knows his people by name.

And so this list we have here in Nehemiah is not a meaningless list of names.

These are the names of God’s people, who longed to return to God’s city.

These are the names of people who had left the comfort of Babylon to rebuild Jerusalem, the city of God, the best city in the world.

It’s the best city in the world not because it’s a comfortable city but because this is the city through which God will bring about his salvation.

It’s through this city that non-Jewish people like you and me can come to know God.

These are the names of faithful servants of God, many of whom serve in God’s temple.

In fact, this list of names is one of those rare incidences in the Bible where the Bible repeats itself almost word for word.

If you go to Ezra chapter 2, you will find essentially the same list, with the same names.

It’s a list so important that it’s listed twice in the Bible, almost word for word!

As Nehemiah makes the list public and organises God’s people according to the list, he wants to remind the people of what their fathers and grandfathers had done.

These fathers and grandfathers left the comfort of Babylon to come to rebuild the city of Jerusalem.

Will their sons and grandsons follow their footsteps?

Will they finish the project their ancestors started but didn’t get to finish?

The project has been delayed for ninety years. Are they going to delay it any further?

Not many of us are like those in Nehemiah’s days.

Not many of us have fathers and grandfathers who had followed God and left spiritual legacies for us.

But all of us had had spiritual fathers, spiritual mothers, who brought us to Christ and showed us his way.

They’ve spent time showing us how good Jesus is and pointed us to walk God’s way.

They’ve shown us the way to the Kingdom of heaven.

That’s the spiritual legacy they’ve left us.

Don’t let their spiritual legacy stop with us.

Their message, the message of the Gospel of Jesus Christ, was never designed to be terminated.

It’s never meant to terminate at us.

It’s supposed to keep growing in us and expand beyond us.

And so, continue on the spiritual legacy of our spiritual fathers and spiritual mothers.

Follow their footsteps.

Give the time to point someone to Christ today.

With God’s help, keep building and nurturing a spiritual community.

Help and encourage your friends and families to live and breathe the gospel.

Don’t delay any longer!

For us here as a Church, this starts with us slowly returning back to in-person gathering.

I guarantee that your very presence here is a huge encouragement to the brothers and sisters who are serving here today.

In particular, our children’s ministry is currently one of the most urgent needs of our Church.

It’s important for adults to come back together but it’s even more important for children to come back together.

They need to see that there are other children and adults like them, worshipping Jesus.

Pointing people to Jesus is the legacy we want to pass on to our children.

Don’t delay any longer; now’s the time to bring them before Jesus.

We want them to come before Jesus because we want to see their name in God’s city.

3. Your name in God’s city

The Bible’s teaching on the Book of Life carries from the Old Testament to the New Testament.

Look with me at Hebrews 12:22-24,

“Instead, you have come to Mount Zion, to the city of the living God (the heavenly Jerusalem), to myriads of angels, a festive gathering, 23 to the assembly of the firstborn whose names have been written[j] in heaven, to a Judge, who is God of all, to the spirits of righteous people made perfect  and to Jesus, the mediator of a new covenant, and to the sprinkled blood, which says better things than the blood of Abel.

”

No offence to Jerusalem, but the best city in the world is Jerusalem but it’s not the Jerusalem situated in the modern country of Israel anymore.

The best city is not of this world but is in heaven.

The Bible describes it as the heavenly Jerusalem, the city of the living God.

This heavenly city of Jerusalem is the best city there is because it’s where God lives.

This is the best city because it is literally heaven.

It’s a city whose citizens have their names written in heaven.

To come to this city, you need to come to Jesus.

If you trust Jesus and believe all that he’s said and done, you will find your name in the registry of heaven.

Last week, I went to a training conference for those teaching Scripture at Schools.

I had registered online but I couldn’t find my online ticket.

And so, I brought the email confirmation to prove that I had registered.

But I didn’t need to worry; my name was on the register, and they’ve already printed a nametag for me.

The conference where I was supposed to be.

My name was in the registry and I was allowed to attend the conference.

All of us, at one stage or another, were outside of the Kingdom of heaven.

We didn’t belong in heaven.

We had no right to be there.

We’re too sinful, too tainted to be in heaven.

The price of entering heaven is beyond any of our reach.

The price of entering heaven is to pay back what we owe God.

We owe God our highest love; we owe God all of our hearts, all of our minds, all of our strength.

Since we could not give God what we owe Him and since we have failed miserably, by ourselves we are not allowed to enter heaven.

But God in his mercy, sent His Son to secure our place in heaven.

Jesus does love God with the highest love.

He alone can give God what we owe him.

When Jesus died on the cross, he was willing to give God what we owe God on our behalf.

From then on, anyone who trusts in Jesus is included in the people of God; their names are written in heaven, in God’s book of life.

That’s where our security is.

Our security is no longer in whether I live in a good and secure house or whether I have good health or a good family.

Our security is based solely on what Jesus Christ has done for us.

Not only did Christ died but he was also raised from the dead and is right now in heaven preparing a house for those who trust him.

He’s preparing his home for us.

Because our security is with him we don’t need to dread what this life may bring.

We don’t need to be afraid if we do lose our house or our wealth, our careers or our health, because we’re safe in God’s hands.

The Archbishop of Sydney, Kanishka Raffel, shared this with us in this issue of Southern Cross:

“The days of weariness and trouble will not only pass away, but will be dwarfed by a weighty, heavy, substantial glory that not only outweighs them, but will endure into eternity.

As the hymn writer puts it:

When we’ve been there ten thousand years,

Bright shining as the sun,

We’ve no less days to sing God’s praise

Than when we first begun…

We can’t see our eternal home in heaven, built by God and kept for us (2 Corinthians 5:1).“

If you trust in Jesus, your name is in God’s book, your eternal home is in heaven.

Pray for the Kingdom of heaven to come.

Long for the Kingdom of heaven to come.

By faith, do your part in building God’s kingdom and pointing people to God’s kingdom.

Don’t be afraid but trust that because of Jesus, God has written your name in the Book of Life.

If your name is in the book of life, you will long for God’s kingdom to come.

Your spiritual fathers and mothers, those who have led you to Christ, longed for God’s kingdom to come.

How about you?

Friends, what do you long for?

To use a form of a phrase that Jesus used, where your name is, there your longing is also.

If your name is in heaven, that’s where your longing will be.

It’s the best city to belong to.

You’ll long to go there.

You’ll want to see your friends and families there.

And while you’re on earth, you’ll long for Church.

It doesn’t have to be this one in particular, but you’ll long to go to a Church that longs for Jesus.

For the Church is a glimpse of what heaven looks like.

It’s a broken, imperfect image but an image nevertheless.

The Church is a glimpse of heaven because it’s a people praising and thanking God for what he’s done for us in Jesus.

Now what other place on earth will you find that?

Friends, long for heaven, long for the gathering of God’s people around God’s throne, for your name is written up there.

And where your name is, there your longing will be also.

Here are the three questions for this week:

1. What am I longing for?

2. Do I know that my name is written in heaven? How am I sure?

3. Am I continuing with the spiritual legacy that was passed on to me by those who led me to Christ?

Friends, if your trust is in Christ, long for where your name is written; long for the heavenly Jerusalem.

Give us the courage to do your will. May your kingdom come, and your will be done in our lives, here on earth, as it is in heaven. For Christ’s sake, Amen.”
