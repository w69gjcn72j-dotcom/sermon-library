Pray for enlightened eyes

Passage: Ephesians 1:15-19

Big Idea: Give thanks and pray that those in the Lord Jesus will be enlightened by the Spirit to know the hope, the inheritance and the power of the Father of Glory.

1. Never stop giving thanks and praying for loving believers in Christ

2. Pray for the Father’s Spirit to help us see

3. Pray for believers to know the Father’s hope, inheritance, and power

Theo Zgoda is a 11 month old little boy who lives in New York.

Since birth, little Theo was deemed legally blind.

However, last week, little Theo was given glasses for the first time.

And for the first time in his life, he could see his parents clearly.

As soon as he saw them, he burst into a huge smile!

What an incredible experience it must have been, to be able to finally see clearly for the first time!

How happy Theo’s parents must be, to be able to see that Theo could finally see the beauty and the wonders of this world!

In today’s passage, we read about eyes that we have that we might not know about.

Look with meat verse 18, “I pray that the eyes of your heart may be enlightened (that is, to see clearly) so that you may know the hope of his calling, what is the wealth of his glorious inheritance in the saints.”

The Bible is here talking about the eyes of your heart.

Is the Bible saying that your heart has eyes?

No, but it is talking about the deepest part of your being, your heart of hearts.

What does your heart of hearts see? What does the deepest part of you understand about yourself, your world, your God?

Does your heart of hearts see? Are the eyes of your heart closed, or worse, blinded, so that you cannot see?

How does someone, how do I, see with the eyes of my heart?

If you have your Bible there, please open up to Ephesians 1:15-19.

We’re going to see three things:

1. Never stop giving thanks and praying for loving believers in Christ

2. Pray for the Father’s Spirit to help us see

3. Pray for believers to know the Father’s hope, inheritance and power

Please put your hands up if you need the sermon script or our Church’s sermon notebook.

Before we begin, let’s pray, “Father of glory, by your Spirit of wisdom and revelation, please help us see Jesus, the radiance of your glory, even more clearly, for we pray in Jesus’ name, Amen.”

1. Never stop giving thanks and praying for loving believers in Christ

Over the past three weeks, we’ve been reading some very deep and spiritual truths about God.

We read the God the Father is the One who chose his people before the foundation of the world.

We read that God the Son, Jesus Christ, is the One in whom we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of our sins.

We read that the mystery of God is this: that Jesus is the focal of all reality and God’s focus for all eternity.

We read that God has promise us His very Own Spirit, the Holy Spirit, as a down payment, a guarantee of our inheritance, until the redemption of the possession, to the praise of his glory. These are all very deep thoughts about who God is.

How does Paul respond to all these deep and incredible truths of God?

Look with me at verses 15-16, “ This is why, since I heard about your faith in the Lord Jesus and your love for all the saints, I never stop giving thanks for you as I remember you in my prayers.”

Paul gives thanks and Paul prays for his readers, fellow believers in the Lord Jesus.

Paul gives God thanks for the their trust in the Lord and their love for all the saints.

Paul knows that all the truths that he has just described applies to them, for they have faith in Jesus, they trust in Jesus and they love all the saints.

They show that they genuinely believe these truths by how they behave, namely by their faith and by their love.

As someone who was brought up in Church, I thought I knew the Bible pretty well.

My Sundays would go from 8am till about 6pm, I knew the Bible stories, I taught the Bible stories, I led Bible studies.

I knew my stuff.

It wasn’t when I was working in a very unchristian workplace that my faith was put to the test.

It was my first real full-time job, and I really needed it to qualify as a pharmacist.

I was grateful that I was given the opportunity but over time, I realise that there was a massive difference between what I believe and what the others believe.

About six months in, I saw my boss did something that I could not tolerate as a Christian.

The boss saw that I saw what he did and said that we’ll talk on Monday.

I struggled hard that weekend and decided to quit, because I know God wouldn’t want me to remain silent.

I knew that it would’ve been very hard for me to find a job at that time but I would rather be unemployed than to bring shame to Christ.

When I got to work, I was about to tell my boss the news when I found out that tragedy struck: my boss died over the weekend.

To be clear, that wasn’t what I was praying for, but God has his perfect timing.

As a result, I stayed on to help out because my boss’ children were all very young back then.

My faith in God was really put to the test then, it was a tough lesson, but one that helped me grow as a Christian.

Sometimes, it’s hard to put what we believe into practice, right?

But if we do genuinely believe what God says, we will follow what God says, we will put it into practice.

The first readers of Ephesians trusted Jesus in the way that they lived and they loved all the saints.

People could see their faith and their love in action.

It’s public, it’s visible.

News about their faith and love spread to Paul.

These first readers of Ephesians weren’t faking it; they didn’t talk about faith and love in the Church then off to do something else.

They were genuine.

They trusted God with their lives and livelihood and they loved all the saints.

They loved God’s people, no matter where they came from, no matter what language they spoke.

They loved all the saints, all the Christians, not just saints who looked and sounded like them.

When people hear about us, when people hear about our Church, will they hear about our faith in Jesus and our love for all the saints?

What stories will they tell?

There are so many stories that God has been doing around our Church.

It’s great to see some of us bring people who couldn’t come by themselves to the Church.

It’s great to see the Rymans take Grace here and for all the parents who bring their children here.

They do this because they love the saints, whether the saints are old or young.

It’s also great to see some of our young people give to the Church.

They don’t have a lot to give; I’m sure they earn far less than most of us here.

But what they have, they give, because their trust is not in their riches but in God.

Give thanks for them.

If you don’t know them, get to know them so that you can give God thanks for them and pray for them.

If you don’t know them, how can you pray for them? How can you give God thanks for them?

What a great way to pray for our brothers and sisters.

Before we pray for their needs, before we pray for what they lack, and definitely before we complain to God (and to others) about them, give God thanks for them.

And so, learn deep theological truths about God, and live these out by the way you trust God and by the way you love all the saints.

Thank God for them, pray for them.

But what do we pray for?

What’s a good prayer to pray?

2. Pray for the Father’s Spirit to help us see

Pray that the Father of glory will give Christian believers eyes to see.

Look with me from the beginning of verse 17, “I pray that the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the glorious Father, would give you the Spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of him. I pray that the eyes of your heart may be enlightened”

Paul describes God as the glorious Father.

God is the Father of glory; he is full of light.

God alone is immortal and who lives in unapproachable light (1 Timothy 6:16).

Throughout the Bible, God is described as the God glory, the King of glory

He is the Father of glory, the Source of all glory.

It is God alone who can open our eyes to see his glory.

And so, Paul’s prayers for the believers is that God will open their eyes, that the eyes of their hearts might be enlightened.

Just like Theo’s parents who long to see the eyes of their little Theo to open so that Theo can see their world clearly,

the Father of glory longs to open our eyes, so that we can see his world, his glory, even more clearly.

Rather than giving us glasses, God gives his Own Holy Spirit, to open the eyes of our hearts.

There’s a sense, of course, that those who believe in Jesus already had their eyes opened.

They had already seen a glimpse of how good and glorious God is, when they believed who Jesus is and what Jesus did.

Before believing in Jesus, we were blind to God’s glory for we were blinded by sin.

We could not see God’s glory.

But now, by God’s Spirit, we can see a glimpse of God’s glory.

It’s a glimpse because we still can’t see the full extent of God’s glory yet.

We still need His Holy Spirit to open our eyes so that the eyes of our hearts might be enlightened, so that our heart of hearts can truly see the glory of God.

The famous preacher John Stott once said, “Keep praising God that in Christ all spiritual blessings are ours and to keep praying that we may know the fullness of what he has given us.”

We thank God for the glory that we have already seen but we must never be complacent.

We must never get complacent about knowing God.

For you will never know enough about God.

You must keep asking for God to reveal himself to you.

We can’t come to know God by our own methods.

The eyes of our hearts are not enlightened by meditation or by medication.

Our heart of hearts can’t come to know God by some mental exercises or by being in some heightened physical state.

We can only come to know God when God sends his own Holy Spirit to enlighten us.

Once you truly know God, once your heart of hearts has come to know God, you will want to know him more.

If you don’t want to know him more, if you think you already know enough about God, then it shows you’ve never actually seen his glory.

It shows that you don’t know God.

Charles Spurgeon once wrote about this account of prayer for the Spirit’s enlightenment:

“I suggest to you all the prayer of a Puritan who, during a debate, was observed to be absorbed in writing.

His friends thought he was taking notes of his opponent’s speech;

but when they got hold of his paper, they found nothing but these words, ‘More light, Lord! More light, Lord!’ Oh, for more light from the great Father of lights!”

When someone believes in Jesus, he is saved by God’s grace.

In Jesus Christ, he received salvation and with it, wisdom and understanding.

If you have believed in Jesus Christ, ask God to make that happen in your life.

Ask God to give you that wisdom and understanding that he said he’s already given.

Ask God of the Holy Spirit to help the eyes of your heart see his glory.

Ask God how the gospel of Jesus now applies in your lives.

Ask for more light!

3. Pray for believers to know the Father’s hope, inheritance and power

What will happen if God does answer our prayer and give us more light?

We will know the Father’s Hope, the Father’s inheritance and the Father’s power.

Look with me from verses 18-19, “I pray that the eyes of your heart may be enlightened so that you may know what is the hope of his calling, what is the wealth of his glorious inheritance in the saints, 19 and what is the immeasurable greatness of his power toward us who believe, according to the mighty working of his strength.”

When God the Father gives you God the Spirit to enlighten the eyes of your heart, you will have wisdom to see what the world could never see.

Earlier this year, a man named Eric from Portchester Castle in England found a vase next to a rubbish tip.

It looked nice and he asked the owner how much he wanted it for.

The owner said one pound and so Eric bought the vase for one pound.

When Eric got it assessed by an expert on Antiques Roadshow, he was surprised to learn that the vase was made by a famous type of vases.

The vase was made four hundred years ago and is now worth over two thousand pounds.

Neither the former owner of the vase nor Eric knew how valuable the vase was.

It wasn’t until the expert who knew about the vases told Eric that Eric realised that he had been the owner of this precious treasure all this time,

That’s what we, as Christians, do time and time again.

We base our values on what the world values.

As a result, we miss out on what’s truly valuable.

We miss out on the treasure that we had all along.

We need the Holy Spirit to make us see the glory of God and the glorious things that he has already given us.

Paul here list three things that the Holy Spirit will open the eyes of our hearts to see:

1. Hope of his calling.

The hope to which he’s called you is so glorious, so precious!

He has hope, sure hope, for all those whom he calls.

Our world is one of hopelessness.

Many people who have no hope are plagued by the thought of the emptiness and the meaningless of this world of hopelessness.

Why do we get up every morning, why look forward to tomorrow, when there’s no hope?

Our hope is also a world of false hopes.

We live in a world that promises much.

We live in a world where many people are anxious of missing out.

And for the few who don’t miss out, who do get what they want, they end up with dissatisfaction, discontentment, and frustration.

In this world of hopelessness and false hope, the Father of Glory gives us real hope.

Those who are called by God has the sure hope of being saved by God.

For those who are called by God were predestined and chosen by God before the foundation of the world.

Those called by God are redeemed, forgiven, chosen and adopted by God the Father in the Lord Jesus Christ.

God’s call is always effective and secure.

His call is our hope.

Those who are have their eyes of their heart opened will see the hope of his calling; they will also see The wealth of his glorious inheritance in the saints.

2. The wealth of his glorious inheritance in the saints

We thank God for what we have already received from him in the Lord Jesus.

We have received an inheritance (v11).

We have seen that the promises God made in the Old Testament fulfilled in Jesus, and we will receive the complete fulfilment of these promises one day.

We thank God for the wonderful inheritance that we are receiving and will receive from him one day.

But here, inheritance seems to be described in a different way.

Whose inheritance is being referred to here?

It’s his glorious inheritance; it’s God the Father’s inheritance.

Where is God’s inheritance? In the saints.

Throughout the Old Testament, God’s people is described as God’s inheritance.

For example, in Deuteronomy 32:9. “But the LORD’s portion is his people, Jacob, his own inheritance.”

Though everyone belongs to God, God’s people belong to him in a special sense.

We see this also in 1 Peter 2:9, “But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for his possession,”

God’s people belong to God, we are his special possession.

And so, the inheritance here can refer to God’s inheritance, namely the saints.

However, at the same time, we see the Bible described inheritance as something that the saints themselves receive.

1 Pet 1:3-4, “Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ. Because of his great mercy he has given us new birth into a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead and into an inheritance that is imperishable, undefiled, and unfading, kept in heaven for you.”

As Christians, we look forward to the inheritance that we will receive from God.

It’s the inheritance of having a new birth into a living hope, it’s the hope of having a home in heaven, living with God forever.

And so, whose inheritance is being referred to here?

It’s both God’s and ours.

When God, by his power and grace saves us in the Lord Jesus, he gives us eternal life, a sinless, perfect life, which we will enjoy fully in heaven.

That’s the inheritance God has in store for us.

When we receive the inheritance God prepared for us, we show ourselves to be God’s own inheritance.

On that day, when we receive our inheritance fully, God will rejoice in having us, the very people that he’s been looking forward to spending eternity with from the very beginning!

God looks forward to spending eternity with us because we are in Christ, his one and only Son, whom he has loved for all eternity.

In saving us through the death and resurrection of Jesus, we see the riches of God’s glory and we praise him for his glory.

When we see the riches of God’s glory, the glamour and the glories of the world will fade away.

For when we compare the riches of the world to the riches of God that is ours in Christ,

All the wealth, all the riches of the world, are rubbish, utter rubbish, in comparison to the riches of God that is ours in Christ.

Finally, when the Holy Spirit opens the eyes of our hearts, we will see the immeasurable greatness of his power.

3. The Immeasurable greatness of his power

It’s by his great power that God will saves us.

He gives His unlimited, immeasurable power toward those of us who believe, so that we can trust him and love all the saints, until the very end.

It’s by his immeasurable power that we will be saved on the last day.

He will make it happen by his might.

Though the powers and authorities of this world might seem powerful, insurmountable, they cannot and will not overcome the power of God, the very power that God gives to ensure his people will receive their inheritance on the last day.

When God opens the eyes of our hearts to see the hope of his calling, we’ll be less prone to hopelessness and false hopes.

Instead, we’ll find comfort, rest and joy in knowing that we have a sure hope in Jesus Christ.

When God opens the eyes of our hearts to see the wealth of his glorious inheritance in the saints, the glamour of this world’s wealth grow dim.

Instead, we’ll see how precious God’s inheritance, that is, his church is.

Instead of selfishness, we’ll see selflessness. Instead of stinginess, there will be generosity. Instead of wanting to be masters, there will be desires to be servants.

When God opens the eyes of our hearts to see his immeasurable greatness, we’ll stop being cowards, not willing to sacrifice ourselves for God’s glory.

Instead, we will do amazing things, accomplish unthinkable achievements, not for our glory but for God’s glory. According to his unlimited and immeasurable power.

And so, here’s a prayer that will always be right to pray for believers.

Pray that the eyes of their hearts be enlightened so that they will see God’s hope of his calling, God’s wealth of his inheritance in the saints, and God’s immeasurably great power.

What do the eyes of your heart see? Do you see the beauty and the glory of God?

Do you want to see more?

My prayer is that the eyes of all our hearts be enlightened.

But even more, my prayer is that this is not just my prayer but the prayer of everyone here.

My prayer is that all of us will give thanks and pray for the eyes of God’s people.

Give thanks and pray for the person sitting next to you.

Don’t stop praying for them.

Pray for stories of their faith and love, that people will hear and praise God for his glory, when they hear the stories of faith and love of the people sitting next to you.

And I’ll go through the roll and pray for each one who’s in the service today, individually, personally, that each one of us will have the eyes of our hearts opened.

Who will you be giving thanks for and praying for this week?
