How Can God Forgive and Still Be Good?

Romans 3:21–31 · Sunday 2 August 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Kogarah

Big Idea: Since all have sinned, all are justified by faith in Jesus Christ, whom God presented as an atoning sacrifice to show his righteousness.

Aim: By the end of this study, students will (1) see that God’s righteousness comes to guilty people through faith in Jesus alone; (2) understand that forgiveness is not cheap — God paid the price at the cross so he could be both just and the justifier; (3) take one concrete step this week to confess sin daily and to pray for 3 friends who don’t know Jesus yet.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, this sheet, and about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

Pick one (or both) to get talking:

  • Off the hook. Tell about a time you broke or scratched something that belonged to someone else. Did you own up? What happened — did they let you off, or did you have to pay? How did it feel either way?
  • Empty hands. What’s the best gift you’ve ever been given that you did absolutely nothing to earn? Who gave it to you — and do you still think of them when you use it?

Today’s passage answers a huge question: how can God forgive guilty people and still be a good God?

Read — Romans 3:21–31 (CSB)

21 But now, apart from the law, the righteousness of God has been revealed, attested by the Law and the Prophets. 22 The righteousness of God is through faith in Jesus Christ to all who believe, since there is no distinction. 23 For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God; 24 they are justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.

25 God presented him as the mercy seat by his blood, through faith, to demonstrate his righteousness, because in his restraint God passed over the sins previously committed. 26 God presented him to demonstrate his righteousness at the present time, so that he would be just and justify the one who has faith in Jesus.

27 Where, then, is boasting? It is excluded. By what kind of law? By one of works? No, on the contrary, by a law of faith. 28 For we conclude that a person is justified by faith apart from the works of the law. 29 Or is God the God of Jews only? Is he not the God of Gentiles too? Yes, of Gentiles too, 30 since there is one God who will justify the circumcised by faith and the uncircumcised through faith. 31 Do we then nullify the law through faith? Absolutely not! On the contrary, we uphold the law.

Context

For three chapters Paul has been building a case in court. People outside Israel are guilty before a holy God. People inside Israel, who had God’s law, are guilty too. Last week ended with the verdict: “no one will be justified in his sight by the works of the law” (3:20). Every mouth is shut. Then comes verse 21 and the most important “But now” in the Bible. God has revealed a righteousness of his own — not earned by us, but given to us — and it comes through Jesus Christ.

Discuss

  1. Read v.21 and notice the word “But”. What has Paul just spent three chapters proving about us — and why is that little word such good news?
  2. Verse 21 says this righteousness comes “apart from the law”, yet is “attested by the Law and the Prophets”. What does that mean? Is God changing the rules, or keeping a promise?
  3. Verse 22 says God’s righteousness is “through faith in Jesus Christ to all who believe, since there is no distinction.” What does it mean to have faith in Jesus — and why is Jesus the only way?
  4. Our memory verse (v.23) says all have sinned and “fall short of the glory of God”. We were made to reflect God’s glory. Where do you see people at school — and yourself — living “inglorious” lives instead?
  5. Verse 24: we are “justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.” To redeem is to pay a price to buy something back. Free for us — but what did it cost God?
  6. Some people say God is too soft: how is it fair that terrible people can be forgiven “just like that”? Using v.25, how would you answer them? (What does “propitiation” mean?)
  7. Verse 26 says God is “just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus.” Why couldn’t God simply overlook our sin and let us off the hook?
  8. Verses 27–28 say boasting is “excluded”. If we receive God’s righteousness with empty hands, what does that change about how you treat people who seem more — or less — “religious” than you?

Live It Out

Faith can be summed up in two words: trust and treasure. Those who have faith in Jesus trust what he has done, and treasure him above everything else. You come to God with empty hands — and he fills them with his Son. Here’s how to live that out this week:

  • Confess daily. Every day this week, name your sin honestly before God — and then thank him that it is already paid for in Jesus. Don’t stop halfway through. Confession without thanksgiving leaves you carrying what Christ already carried.
  • Tell one person. Confess one thing to a Christian brother or sister you trust and ask them to pray for you. Sin loses its grip in the light.
  • KOG 3·2·1·0. Pray for your 3 friends who don’t know Jesus yet, connect with 2 people at church or youth group this week, invite 1 friend to KOGYouth, church or Father’s Day (6 September) — and leave 0 people unloved or left out at school.

This week’s challenge: When you catch yourself thinking “I’m doing alright — better than most people”, stop and pray the words of verse 23 back to God: “I have sinned and fallen short of your glory. Thank you that Jesus is my righteousness.” Empty hands, every time.

Memory verse: “For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God; they are justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.” — Romans 3:23–24 (CSB)

Pray

  • Thank God for the “But now”. Thank him that he did not let us off the hook cheaply, but paid the price himself in Jesus so that he could be both just and the one who justifies us.
  • Pray for your 3 by name. Go around the group and each pray for your three friends who aren’t Christians yet — that God’s kindness would lead them to repentance, and for a chance to point one of them to Jesus.
  • Pray for our church. Ask God for six additional qualified helpers for KOGKids, KOGMini and KOGYouth, for our three new mid-week Growth Groups, and for the Father’s Day celebration on 6 September — that many would come with empty hands and receive Jesus.

Leader’s Notes

Warm framing: some of your students may not yet be Christians, and this passage is exactly where the good news lands. The tone is not “try harder” but “stop trying to pay — it’s already paid.” Never corner anyone. Joyfully point to Jesus and let the passage do the work. One of our youth said it best while we were in Romans 3 last term: “No one is righteous — except for Jesus.” That’s the whole study in one sentence.

Question helps

  • Q1: Chapters 1–3 prove that everyone — religious and irreligious — stands guilty. “But now” signals that our hope rests on what God has done, not on an excuse we make for ourselves.
  • Q2: “Apart from the law” means this righteousness isn’t earned by law-keeping. “Attested by the Law and the Prophets” means the Old Testament pointed to it all along (e.g. Abraham in ch.4). God isn’t changing the rules; he’s keeping his promise.
  • Q3: Faith is trusting who Jesus is and what he has done. There is one way because there is one problem: all have sinned. Not one way for Christians and another for everyone else — “no distinction” cuts both ways: everyone is guilty, and everyone is welcome through Jesus.
  • Q4: We were made to reflect God’s glory; sin makes us inglorious — walking as creatures of the dark rather than children of light. Watch for students who assume the “really bad” people are elsewhere. The mirror is for us.
  • Q5: Free for us, costly for God. Justification is free; it is not cheap. Use the image of redeeming a voucher at the counter — the prize is free to you, but the voucher had to be handed over. We could never pay our own redemption price.
  • Q6: “Propitiation” (CSB: “mercy seat”) means a sacrifice that turns God’s just anger away. God is rightly angry at sin; Jesus, the sinless one, bore that anger in our place. Far from being soft, the cross is where God took sin most seriously.
  • Q7: If God simply pretended our sin wasn’t there, he would be unrighteous — an unjust judge. God had shown restraint, passing over sins previously committed; at the cross he showed that he had not ignored them at all.
  • Q8: Boasting is excluded — we contribute nothing. That kills both pride toward those we think are worse, and shame before those we think are better. Watch how it reshapes group chats and school friendships.
