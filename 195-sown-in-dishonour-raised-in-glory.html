Sown in Dishonour, Raised in Glory

1 Corinthians 15:35-58 · 2021
Big IdeaWe belong to Jesus, the Last Adam; like him we will be sown in dishonour but raised in glory.

Sermon Title: Sown in dishonour, raised in glory

Passage: 1 Corinthians 15.35_58

Big Idea: We belong to Jesus, the Last Adam; like him we will be sown in dishonour but raised in glory

The glory of the raised body

The Last Adam who gives us the risen life

The lightning fast and “swallowing” victory of the Lord Jesus

Earlier this year, a video of an interview between a reporter and a woman was shown on the internet.

It shows a woman who kept referring to God, so she probably thought that she was a Christian.

She was speaking to the reporter and said that it’s time for God to separates the sheep from the goats.

She’s probably referring to the parable that Jesus gave in Matthew 25 where the Son of Man is said to separate the sheep from the goats.

The sheep were the righteous ones on his right and the goats were the unrighteous ones on his left.

And the reporter asks the woman: which are you?

And she hesitated for a moment and said, “I am a goat because I’m not a sheep.

I’m not doing what they’re telling me to do.”

Sadly, even though she might think she’s a Christian, she’s got the parable completely messed up.

She didn’t want to be the weak sheep; she wants to be the strong minded goat.

People are afraid of looking weak, like sheep.

They want to be strong, like lions, or at least a goat.

Even Christians often don’t want to appear weak.

However, if we’re honest with ourselves, sometimes we appear weak because we are weak.

Sometimes we are not as strong as we hope ourselves to be.

As Christians we struggle.

I’m not just talking about new or weak Christians.

I’m talking about all Christians; all Christians will struggle at times.

Even for those who appear to be strong, they, too, will struggle, especially as they serve the Lord faithfully while trying to be a good parent, a good worker, a good spouse.

Indeed, if you don’t struggle in your Christian life, chances are you’re probably not serving as faithfully as you might think.

Living as a genuine Christian life is hard, laborious work.

There might come a time when Christians might wonder, “why bother? Why do I keep struggling and labouring?

I’m weak and barely hanging on; why bother?”

In today’s passage, we’ll see the Bible’s reason why a Christian should keep going, even when times tough, even when they do feel weak.

Please open your Bible to 1 Corinthians 15, beginning from verse 35.

We’re going to focus on one thing in today’s passage:

The glory of the raised body

Before we look into it, let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for your Word. Please convict us by your Spirit so that we will follow Jesus, come what may. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

The glory of the raised body

Firstly, we’ll see the glory of the raised body.

Last week, we read all about the resurrection, how Jesus is our hope in this life, in death and in the life beyond death.

Paul here anticipates someone asking more about the resurrection.

1 Cor 15:35, “But someone will ask, “How are the dead raised? What kind of

body will they have when they come?”

Now we need to remember that most people in Corinth in Paul’s days didn’t believe in the resurrection, just like many people around us today.

If you ask a child today what they think about someone who comes back from the dead, they might ask whether you’re talking about Zombies or vampires.

It’s strange to talk about people coming back from the dead.

How does Paul describe what happens when someone comes back from the dead?

Look with me at verse 36, “36 You fool! What you sow does not come to life unless it dies”

Resurrection is like sowing a seed.

On Friday, I’ve sent a message asking people to send me pictures of them and their seeds.

Here are some of them.

And today, some of us might have brought some seeds to Church as well.

Bring them up and show us.

Don’t tell us, let us guess.

(Guessing seeds)

When we sow and plant different seeds into the ground, different plants come up.

Paul says that it’s ultimately God, as the Creator, who decided what plant should come from the various seeds.

We don’t decide what plant comes from which seed; God does.

As well as plants, Paul applies this idea to animals as well.

God decides what body humans take, what body animals take, what bodies birds take and what bodies fish take.

I don’t know about you but when I watch documentaries, I’m always amazed at how the different features of different animals work in their natural habitat.

It’s fascinating!

Paul also applies this to the heavenly bodies like the sun, the moon and the stars.

Again, it’s fascinating to observe and study the bodies in the sky.

They are thousands, millions of times bigger than us, travelling through space and the case of the sun and moon, regulating the lives of billions of organisms on planet earth.

It’s amazing!

Now notices the way Paul list the different earthly bodies and heavenly bodies.

Does it remind you of another part of the Bible?

Yes, creation!

If you have your Bible there, you might want to go back to Genesis 1 and see on which day God created these different bodies.

Or if you trust your memory, you can try to work it out in your head.

On which day did God create the following:

The sun, the moon, the stars

The animals

The birds and the fish

Humans

Seeds

The sun, the moon, the stars – Day 4

The animals – Day 6

The birds and the fish – Day 5

Humans – Day 6

Seeds – Day 3

Paul points us to incredible splendour of the different types of bodies in heaven and earth, what God made in creation, and he says,

“So it is with the resurrection of the dead.”

When the dead are raised, they will be given a new body, with new splendour.

It will be a completely different order of bodies because they will be bodies of the new creation.

Listen to how different the new bodies will be from the old ones, beginning from the second half of verse 42,

“Sown in corruption, raised in incorruption; sown in dishonour, raised in glory; sown in weakness, raised in power; sown a natural body, raised a spiritual body.”

How does Paul know what the resurrected bodies, the raised bodies, look like?

It’s very likely that Paul, like many Jews, would’ve read about the resurrection in the Old Testament, in Daniel 12:2,

“Many who sleep in the dust of the earth will awake, some to eternal life, and some to disgrace and eternal contempt. 3 Those who have insight will shine like the bright expanse of the heavens, and those who lead many to righteousness, like the stars forever and ever.”

While Daniel would’ve given him an idea of the resurrection looks like, Paul’s greatest lesson in the resurrection would’ve been on the Damascus Road,

When he saw the risen Lord for himself.

When Paul was on his way to Damascus to persecute more Christians, he saw the risen Lord Jesus, brighter than the sun.

On that day, Paul saw with his own eyes what the risen life looks like.

Since Jesus is the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep, seeing Jesus will give us a glimpse of what our resurrection would look like.

Here Paul describes it using four aspects:

Physiological

Sown in corruption; raised in incorruption.

Jesus was sown, that is buried, in corruption.

Jesus had a physical body that decays over time.

That’s the body he was given when he was born into our world.

Like the rest of humanity, like the rest of creation, Jesus was given a body that would corrupt and decay.

The end of that decay was of course, death.

In Jesus’ case, it was his death on the cross.

However, when Jesus was raised, his body was incorruptible.

Jesus’ new body would not decay.

That’s what those who believe in him can look forward of having as well.

Though our body is corruptible, we will be raised in an incorruptible body.

Social

At the cross, Jesus was dishonoured.

His enemies publicly taunted him; his disciples left him, even God the Father turned his face away.

Jesus was left alone on the cross.

But he was raised in glory.

For those who follow Jesus, we, too, might be dishonoured as a result of following Jesus.

Our friends, even our families, might look down on us because we’re Christians.

People might consider us useless and feel that they are justify discarding us and ignoring us.

Have you ever felt like that as a Christian?

If so, you’re in good company!

Throughout Paul’s letters, we’ve seen him, the great Apostle Paul, being ridiculed and dishonoured,

Thrown from one jail to another.

In his words, he’s said he’s been treated like the scum of the earth, like everyone’s garbage.

But Paul would gladly take the suffering if it means sharing in the resurrection of Jesus Christ.

The glory that comes with being raised with Christ far outweighs any dishonour that he might be experiencing.

Power Level

Sown in weakness, raised in power.

As the all-powerful Son of God, Jesus would’ve felt a tremendous sense of weakness coming to earth.

If we ever felt weakness in what we do, imagine the weakness that Jesus felt when he was spat upon and hung on the wooden cross,

All the time knowing that he had the power to destroy the world with a blink of his eyes.

Jesus experience weakness in death but he was raised in power in the resurrection.

We, too, weak as we are, can look forward to the day when we will be raised in power.

Knowing that we will one day be raised in power gives us both the hope and the power to be resilient even in the midst of our weaknesses.

Even though we may appear weak and in fact are weak, our final chapter will not be marked by our weaknesses.

We can look forward to the day when we will be raised in power.

Spiritual

We are sown in a natural body, we will be raised in a spiritual body.

This is not referring to the natural body as a tangible body and the spiritual body as an invisible body, like a ghost.

No, here, Paul talks about a body that belongs in the first creation and driven by the natural things of this world,

As opposed to a body that belongs in the new creation and driven by the things of the spirit,

That is, a body that’s driven by the things of the new heaven and the new world.

Are we driven by nature or are we driven by the Spirit?

There’s a sense in which Jesus is always driven by the Spirit but when he was raised from the dead, his body is completely driven by the Spirit in a new way.

It’s literally a new mode of existence.

That’s what we can expect to experience as well when we, too, are raised from the dead.

Like Jesus, we will have a spiritual body, living in a new mode of existence.

How will our bodies be changed in the resurrection?

Sown in corruption, raised in incorruption; 43 sown in dishonour, raised in glory; sown in weakness, raised in power; 44 sown a natural body, raised a spiritual body.

The changes will be physiological, social, level up in power and spiritual.

As Christians with the hopes of resurrection, we anticipate these changes by the way we live today.

In particular, I want us to think about dishonour and glory.

As Christians who have the hope of resurrection, we know that the glory of the risen life far outweighs any glory that we have on earth.

However, far too often, the way we live shows that what we value, what we honour, is not that much different to how people who have no hope at all.

That happened in the Corinthian Church.

They honoured speech and worldly wisdom; they most likely honoured those who are richer and looked down on those who were poorer.

That’s what we’ve seen throughout 1 Corinthians.

Do we have the same value system as them, in today’s Church?

Do we honour those who have money more than those who have none?

Do we praise those who are charming over those who are socially awkward?

And, sadly, do we pay attention to those who are more beautiful, more pleasing to the eyes, than those who are more common?

When we honour things that the world honours and despise the things that the world despises, aren’t we behaving as if we have no hope of the resurrection?

Friends, re-evaluate your honour system in light of the resurrection.

Don’t honour things that will be forgotten in the resurrection.

Instead, honour those things which will be treasured by the Lord Jesus in the resurrection.

What does the Lord see as precious?

The Lord sees the sacrifices his servants make for the gospel as precious.

We should see them as precious too.

The Lord sees the servants of the gospel, even the most insignificant ones, as precious.

We should see them as precious too.

Jesus himself gave more honour to the widow who gave two coins than those who gave out of the surplus.

Who would we have honoured more? The widow who gave everything or the rich who gave out of their excesses?

As the Lord Jesus has shown us by his death and resurrection, his value system is completely opposite to the world’s.

The world dishonoured him, crucified him in his weakness, but he was raised in glory and power.

Though the world thinks differently, may we honour what our Lord honours for at the end of the day, on that first day of eternity, it’s only his view that counts.

I was hoping to go through all the way to the end of this chapter but there’s so much to share just in the first part, I decided to divide the sermon in half.

We’ll keep looking at what it means for us to be raised in Christ next week.

But here’s a sneak preview: Jesus is the Last Adam who gives life, and the victory belongs to Jesus.

What do I want us to take home today?

This life is like the dishonourable seed; the risen life in Christ is the glorious body.

Just like a seed, our life may look insignificant right now.

It might be despised by the world.

But in Christ, a glorious body, a glorious life awaits us.

Even though we might not experience a glorious life now, we can look with confidence at the risen life ahead of us.

It’s because of this confidence that we have in Christ that we will face the dishonour that comes with being called a Christian.

It’s because we have this risen hope that we will fight the good fight, resilient in the face of trouble, despite how weak we might feel.

It’s because Jesus has risen from the dead that we’ll keep labouring, keep doing the thankless tasks, because it’s these very tasks that give him honour.

On that final day, he will honour those who have honoured him by raising them from the dead.

On that great day, we will look back in our risen body, through our incorruptible eyes and declare through our incorruptible mouths, “it’s true, our momentary light affliction has produced for us an absolutely incomparable eternal weight of glory.” (2 Cor 4:17)

Friends, if you are discouraged, if you are weary, if you feel weak, remember:

This life is like the dishonourable seed; the risen life in Christ is the glorious body.

Here are the three questions for this week:

When was the last time you felt weak or discouraged as a Christian?

How has that weakness / discouragement honoured Jesus?

In what way do I need to re-evaluate what I honour in light of the resurrection?

Friends don’t be like the woman who were afraid of being labelled as a sheep because she was afraid of looking weak.

Read the Bible for yourself; see what the Bible says about weakness and strength, and while you’re there, the difference between sheep and goats.

And remember: This life is like the dishonourable seed; the risen life in Christ is the glorious body.

Let’s pray,

“Father, thank you that you’ve sent Jesus, the firstfruits of all those who have fallen asleep. Thank you that in him, we have a glimpse of what the risen life looks like. Please lift our eyes to the risen life, that we may value what is valuable in your eyes, even if, and especially when the world around us despises what you honour. Let us not just see the seed but the glorious body that you have prepared for us in Jesus. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
