The Mystery of God’s Will: The wisdom of the redeemed in Christ

Passage: Ephesians 1:7-10

Big Idea: By God’s grace, ignorant trespassers are both redeemed to God through Christ’s blood and made wise to know the mystery of God’s will, the finale of all things in Christ.

1. The cost of our trespassing

2. The grace of God makes the ignorant wise

3. The finale of all things in Christ

Last week, our family went to something an HSC Showcase presentation at school.

It was an opportunity for many HSC students to show various projects that they’ve been preparing as part of their Year 12 assessment.

Our family went specifically to support Ezekiel, who performed a wonderful Jazz piano piece. Well done Ezekiel!

Others have prepared short films.

One was a short film about a murder mystery.

The student who produced it got his friends involved, added sound effects, and had a simple and yet engaging storyline.

In the short film, as one friend gets murdered after another, the audience keeps guessing – who’s going to be next? Who’s the killer?

At the end of the five minutes film, there were only the killer and the other last person in the house.

Will the victim escape? Will he be murdered?

We won’t know because that’s when the film ended.

At the film’s finale, the mystery of the murderer was finally revealed, but we were still left wondering whether he would strike his last victim.

Will he or won’t he? The mystery remains…

It’s a great way to finish the film; the student had done a great job bringing us into his story and helping us find out solve the mystery.

While not many of us (thankfully) won’t be solving real murder mysteries in our lives, we do still face many mysteries that we try to solve.

It might be the mystery of an uncertain future, not knowing where your will work next.

It might be the mystery of an unresolved family tension, not knowing how to respond to the person the next time you see them.

We want to know what we can do to solve these mysteries, if they can be solved at all.

And as Christians, we know that God knows everything and so we want to ask him what his will is.

We want to ask him how do we solve these mysteries in life.

What must we do to know the mystery of God’s will.

Last week, we began our sermon series on The Mystery of God’s will.

We looked at the Father’s Choice in Christ.

Today, we will continue by looking at the wisdom of the redeemed in Christ.

And so please open your Bible to Ephesians 1, beginning from verse 7.

We’ll see three things:

1. The cost of our trespassing

2. The grace of God makes the ignorant wise

3. The finale of all things in Christ

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Father, by your Spirit, please help us now to understand the mystery of your will, as revealed in your Son our Lord Jesus Christ, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

1. The cost of our trespassing

Please look with me at Ephesians 1:7, “In him we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of our trespasses, according to the riches of his grace”.

The Bible often describes sin as a trespass.

What’s a trespass?

A trespass happens when there’s a sign that says ‘No Trespassing’ because it’s private property, but we step on it anyway.

We trespass when we step over a boundary that we shouldn’t cross.

God has put very clear boundaries in his laws.

Do you remember how Jesus answered a scribe asked him which is the most important command?

“Love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, with all your mind and with all your strength” (Mark 12:29)

We’re to love God with every part of our being; God demands all of our love, from all aspects of our lives.

That means a lot of things, but one thing it definitely means is that we’re to worship no one else.

There’s an explicit command that says “Do not worship idols”

And Jesus continues by saying, “The second is, “Love your neighbour as yourself.”

Again this means a lot of things, but it will involve not harming your neighbour and not stealing from your neighbour.

There are commands from God about these as well.

But, you know what? We’ve crossed these boundaries; we trespassed.

We have not loved God with all of our being and we have not love our neighbours as ourselves.

And like all trespassers, we tend to minimise it.

We either deny that we’ve done anything wrong, or we treat it as if it’s not a big deal.

There are other times, when recognise that what we did was very bad, that we’ve committed a terrible trespass and we want to do something about it, to make it up.

We felt guilty about the trespass and we want to do something about it.

What is the real cost of our trespass?

Look with me at verse seven, “ In him we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of our trespasses, according to the riches of his grace.”

What’s the real cost of our trespasses? It’s the lifeblood of Jesus.

Our trespass against God cannot be ignored; they cannot be brushed off.

However, we can’t pay for it for what in the universe is worth trespassing God’s law?

What in the universe is worth our disobedience against God?

Is it wealth? Or making money?

Of maybe it’s our family? Is our family worth our disobedience against God?

If wealth and family are worth disobeying God for, then they are your idols, for they take God’s place in your life.

But these idols are not worthy.

In fac, nothing in all universe is worth disobeying God for, if God is indeed the God of all.

There’s nothing big enough in the universe to say that it’s important enough to trespass God’s laws for.

And so, what’s the cost of our trespass against God?

It has to be something bigger than the universe, since nothing in the universe is worth trespassing God for.

It’s nothing that you and I can ever afford, it’s nothing that all the wealth of this world, or this universe can afford.

What’s the cost of our trespasss against God? It’s the blood of Jesus, the Son of God.

Only the death Jesus, the death of someone who perfectly obeyed God, is worthy to pay for the cost of our trespass.

For the blood of Jesus is of greater worth than everything in this world, this universe, can put together.

If your trust in Jesus, God, out of the riches of his grace, has paid for your trespass in His Son.

In Jesus, God forgives our sins, God forgives our trespasses.

That’s what it means to redeem.

God redeems us by paying the great cost of our trespass against him by the blood of Jesus.

We are redeemed to God, we’re brought back to him.

That’s the summary of the good news of Jesus.

“In him we have redemption (brought back to God) through his blood (Jesus’ sacrifice on the cross), the forgiveness of our trespasses, according to the riches of his grace.”

It’s all God’s glorious grace!

The famous English writer, Marghanita Laski, was no friend of Christianity, but before she died in 1988 she said, “What I envy most about you Christians is your forgiveness. I have nobody to forgive me.”

Christians, you do have someone who forgives you!

The one who forgives you is none other than God, who forgives you in his Son Jesus Christ.

And God forgives you out of his glorious grace in Jesus Christ.

2. The grace of God makes the ignorant wise

This grace of God in Jesus redeems and forgives.

It also makes you wise.

Look with me at verse 8, “that he richly poured out on us with all wisdom and understanding.”

Ignorant people trespasses, they break laws because they either don’t know the rule or they don’t understand the consequences.

Before knowing Jesus, we were ignorant fools who trespass God’s laws because we didn’t know what he wanted or we didn’t care about the consequences of disobeying God.

But when we accepted who Jesus is and what Jesus has done by his death and resurrection, God also makes us wise.

When we accepted what God has done in Jesus, he pours out his grace on us with all wisdom and understanding.

That is, when we accept what God has done for us in Jesus, not only are we redeemed and forgiven, but we’re also given all wisdom and understanding.

Our forgiveness in Jesus and our wisdom in Jesus is tightly linked.

For when we are truly redeemed by God and forgiven by God, we are saying that we recognise the significance of who Jesus truly is.

There’s a sense in which someone who doesn’t believe in Jesus can know facts about him.

However, we can’t truly know who He is until we have been saved by him.

We can only truly know him when we accept what He has done for us.

And again, it’s all according to God’s glorious grace.

It’s by God’s grace that we accept what Jesus has done; it’s by God’s grace that we have the wisdom to understand how costly our trespasses are, it’s by God’s grace that we have the wisdom to realise how dangerously close we were to death and judgement.

It’s by God’s grace that we receive all wisdom and understanding.

If you want to know the mystery of God’s will, you must first be forgiven by God, you must first be saved through the blood of Jesus.

Only those redeemed and forgiven by God can know God’s will.

That’s why we can’t study the Bible as if it’s merely a text book.

To be sure, the Bible is a big book, with many parts in it that are hard to understand.

That’s why we have sermons and KOG on Sundays, Growth groups, and books to help us understand it.

We need help to understand the Bible.

But the Bible can never be understood merely as a text book.

The Bible can only be understood by God’s grace, by God’s blessings.

Only God can show us the true meaning of His Book.

And the first step to understand God’s will as God tells it in His Book, the Bible, is to turn to Jesus.

The first step is to be redeemed to God in Jesus, to have our trespasses forgiven in Jesus.

We can only understand God’s will if we are restored to him, if we’re brought back to him.

And this can happen only through the blood of Christ.

The Gospel of Jesus Christ is basis and foundation for understanding God’s will.

You will never understand God’s will if you are not redeemed in Christ.

However, to those who have accepted the Gospel of Jesus Christ, who have been redeemed and forgiven by God in Jesus, God wants them to know the mystery of his will.

God wants them to know that the finale of all things is in Christ.

3. The finale of all things in Christ

Look with me at verse 9-10, “He made known to us the mystery of his will, according to his good pleasure that he purposed in Christ as a plan for the right time—to bring everything together in Christ, both things in heaven and things on earth in him.”

What is the mystery of God’s will?

At the right time, God will bring all things, all things, together in Christ, both in heaven and on earth.

That’s the finale of all things.

The finale of all things is in Christ.

Unlike the murder mystery story that ended on a cliff-hanger, this finale will be it.

The finale of all things is in Jesus Christ.

God has already planned the finale of the world from the very beginning.

The creator of time and space has already arranged how history will run its course.

Everything in heaven and on earth, will ultimately find their place in Jesus Christ.

At the right time, God will bring everything together in Christ.

That’s God’s plan; that’s the mystery of God’s will.

At the heart of God’s will is Jesus.

Jesus is the singular focal point of all reality.

Jesus is the focus of God.

Everything will find their place in him.

If world history is a movie, then Jesus is the Protagonist; Jesus will be the main actor in God’s story.

We see a glimpse of what that looks like in those whom God has redeemed and forgiven in Christ.

We were once trespassers of God, enemies of God, but now we’ve been reconciled to God.

We’re now brought together as one people, united in Jesus Christ to God.

Those who are in Christ, those who are in Christ’s Church, is a glimpse of what will happen in the ultimate finale.

All things will be brought together in Christ.

Everyone will one day bow before Christ.

That’s why only those of us who were once trespassers but has now accepted Jesus can understand God’s mystery.

We’ve experienced what it means to be brought to God in Christ; we are living it!

One day, we’ll see it on a far bigger scale – everything in heaven and on earth will be brought to Jesus.

As Christians, we worship Jesus now but on that day, everything and everyone will worship Him.

However, that doesn’t mean everyone will be saved.

Everything will be brought under Christ, but only those who have accepted who Jesus is and what he’s done will share God’s joy on that great and final day.

Only those redeemed to God, by the blood of Jesus, will celebrate the grand finale on the Last Day.

If you have not been redeemed to God by then, you will still be a trespasser on that day.

You will be trespassing on God’s property, and you will be pay the cost.

On that day you will realise that nothing you have now, nothing you do now, is worth the cost of trespassing God’s laws.

Jesus is Alpha and the Omega, the A and the Z, the Beginning and the End,

Jesus is the One in whom God will bring everything and everyone together.

That’s the mystery of God’s will.

How do we know the mystery of God’s will?

If you want to know the mystery of God’s will, believe in Jesus and be saved by Jesus.

It’s only as we receive the riches of God’s grace in Jesus that we are redeemed and made wise, knowing that we’re heading into our finale in Christ.

Without Christ, we remain in our sins and trespasses, ignorant of the impending danger of God’s judgement at the finale of Christ.

And so, remember, to know the mystery of God’s will, you must be first be redeemed by Jesus’ blood.

God has revealed his will – he wants to save you in Jesus!

He wants to redeem you, forgive you in Jesus.

If you’re not redeemed and forgiven in Jesus, you will never understand his will.

But if you are redeemed and forgiven in Jesus, you will have the wisdom to understand His will and you will follow him, even if it seems like foolishness to th e rest of the world.

Many year ago, a Sydney Anglican couple that I know had the opportunity to go oversea to start a new Christian ministry in south Africa.

It will be very hard work; it will be dangerous work.

Their youngest son just so happened doing Year 12 that year, like my son Ezekiel.

It was one of the most important year in their son’s life!

Are they really going to leave his son in Australia and be a missionary?

They recognise that Year 12 HSC was important, and their youngest son is important, but their son is not the most important person in their lives.

Jesus is.

As a result, the couple went and spent a number of years in South Africa, making good, lasting changes in Anglican Church in South Africa.

The son studied and got into merchant banking.

However, he decided to study theology, just like his parents and he got a theological doctorate.

In fact, he’s now the Principal of a Theological College in Singapore, teaching students about the mystery of God in the Person of Jesus.

If would’ve been a hard decision all t hose years ago for the couple.

But as people redeemed by Jesus, they have the wisdom to recognise that Jesus is the most important Person.

He’s at the heart of God’s mystery.

It’s to Jesus that they give their life, their all.

Here are three questions for us to think about this week:

1. What are some trespasses we are most vulnerable to?

2. How does our life show that we have received both the forgiveness of God and the wisdom of God?

3. Is there a mystery in your life that you’re struggling with now? How does it fit in the mystery of God’s will as revealed in Jesus?

Friends, we are forgiven, we’re made wise by God’s glorious grace in Christ.

What must we do to know the mystery of God’s will?

It’s not something we do, but something that God’s done for us.

For to know the mystery of God’s will, you must be first redeemed by Jesus’ blood.

Let’s pray,

“Dear Lord, thank you for the grace that you’ve lavished on us in the Person of Jesus Christ. By the blood of Jesus, your only Son, please forgive our trespasses. By the grace you have for us in him, please give us all wisdom and understanding, that we might understand that Jesus is at the heart of the mystery of your will, that all things will come together in him. Please open our hearts and minds so that in all that we say and do, we will give Jesus our life, our all, so that Jesus, along with you and the Holy Spirit, may be glorified. We pray this in Jesus’ name, Amen.”
