Young Adults (19–30)

Meeting the God Who Speaks, While We Wait

Luke 1:1–25 · Sunday 20 September 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

For young adults, 19–30 · from Sunday’s sermon, preached by Jerry Zhu

Big Idea: God has not forgotten you. He speaks in history, he speaks into the impossible, and he works in the silence — and his word will be fulfilled in its proper time.

Aim:

Group members can say why Christianity invites examination rather than fearing it.

Each person names one thing they believe God can do in general but not for them.

Each person leaves with three names to pray for and one place to serve or invite someone.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • What is the longest you have waited on something — a grad job application, a visa, a diagnosis, a text back? What did the waiting do to you?
  • Your feed is full of people whose lives look like they are going somewhere. What does scrolling do to you when yours feels stuck?

Read

Luke 1:1–25 (CSB)

1 Many have undertaken to compile a narrative about the events that have been fulfilled among us, 2 just as the original eyewitnesses and servants of the word handed them down to us. 3 So it also seemed good to me, since I have carefully investigated everything from the very first, to write to you in orderly sequence, most honorable Theophilus, 4 so that you may know the certainty of the things about which you have been instructed.

5 In the days of King Herod of Judea, there was a priest of Abijah’s division named Zechariah. His wife was from the daughters of Aaron, and her name was Elizabeth. 6 Both were righteous in God’s sight, living without blame according to all the commands and requirements of the Lord. 7 But they had no children because Elizabeth could not conceive, and both of them were well along in years.

8 When his division was on duty and he was serving as priest before God, 9 it happened that he was chosen by lot, according to the custom of the priesthood, to enter the sanctuary of the Lord and burn incense. 10 At the hour of incense the whole assembly of the people was praying outside. 11 An angel of the Lord appeared to him, standing to the right of the altar of incense. 12 When Zechariah saw him, he was terrified and overcome with fear.

13 But the angel said to him, “Do not be afraid, Zechariah, because your prayer has been heard. Your wife Elizabeth will bear you a son, and you will name him John. 14 There will be joy and delight for you, and many will rejoice at his birth. 15 For he will be great in the sight of the Lord and will never drink wine or beer. He will be filled with the Holy Spirit while still in his mother’s womb. 16 He will turn many of the children of Israel to the Lord their God. 17 And he will go before him in the spirit and power of Elijah, to turn the hearts of fathers to their children, and the disobedient to the understanding of the righteous, to make ready for the Lord a prepared people.”

18 “How can I know this?” Zechariah asked the angel. “For I am an old man, and my wife is well along in years.”

19 The angel answered him, “I am Gabriel, who stands in the presence of God, and I was sent to speak to you and tell you this good news. 20 Now listen. You will become silent and unable to speak until the day these things take place, because you did not believe my words, which will be fulfilled in their proper time.”

21 Meanwhile, the people were waiting for Zechariah and were amazed that he stayed so long in the sanctuary. 22 When he did come out, he could not speak to them. Then they realized that he had seen a vision in the sanctuary. He was making signs to them and remained speechless. 23 When the days of his ministry were completed, he went back home.

24 After these days his wife Elizabeth conceived and kept herself in seclusion for five months. She said, 25 “The Lord has done this for me. He has looked with favor in these days to take away my disgrace among the people.”

Context

Between Malachi and the opening of Luke, several centuries passed in which God gave no revelation through a prophet to be recorded in Scripture. God’s people waited — under Rome, under Herod, inside a religion of mere forms. Luke, a Gentile doctor and Paul’s travelling companion, is not writing religious literature but doing historical investigation, and he says so. Then, in the temple, God opens his mouth again.

Discuss

  1. Luke gives three pieces of evidence for what he writes (vv. 1–3). What are they, and why does each one matter?
  2. Luke writes so that Theophilus “may know the certainty” (v. 4). When your faith wobbles at 1am, do you go looking for evidence or for a feeling? What would going back to the facts actually look like?
  3. Why does it matter that Luke dates this “in the days of King Herod” (v. 5) rather than in a golden age? What does that say to a generation told the world is falling apart?
  4. Zechariah and Elizabeth are called righteous and blameless — and they have no children (vv. 6–7). What does that do to the idea that if you follow God properly, life goes well?
  5. The angel says “your prayer has been heard” (v. 13) — past tense. What have you stopped praying for because you decided it was never going to happen?
  6. Zechariah asked for a son; God gave him a prophet to prepare the way for the Lord (vv. 14–17). If God gave you the job, the visa, the relationship you are asking for — what would he be giving it to you for?
  7. Zechariah believed in God but could not believe God’s promise in his own situation (v. 18). Where is that gap for you — the habit you can’t shake, the family member who will never believe, the sense that God’s promises are for other people?
  8. Gabriel gives silence as the sign (v. 20), and Elizabeth answers with “he has looked with favor” (v. 25). How can nine months of silence be grace? What would a grace of silence look like with your phone this week?

Live It Out

Our KOG rhythm is 3 · 2 · 1 · 0.

  • Pray for 3 people you know who aren’t Christians yet — housemates, colleagues, people in your group chat — by name.
  • Connect with 2 people at church outside your own age bracket or language.
  • Invite 1 of your three to church, or to the next young adults gathering.
  • 0 left unloved — who is on the edge of your group and never gets messaged?

This week: Pick the one prayer you gave up on and pray it every day this week. And put your phone face down for ten minutes a day — notifications off, nothing to scroll — and let one promise of God take root instead.

Memory verse: “Do not be afraid, Zechariah, because your prayer has been heard.” (Luke 1:13, CSB)

Pray

  • Thank God that he does not ask for blind belief — that Luke invites us to examine it and it holds.
  • Pray by name for the three people each of you is praying towards Christ — that God’s kindness would lead them to repentance.
  • Pray for the thing each of you has stopped praying for — that God’s word would be fulfilled in its proper time, and that we would wait without going numb.

Leader’s Notes

Joyfully point to Jesus. Some here are still working out what they believe, and in a call-out culture doubt can feel shameful — so make room for real questions. Luke himself invites examination; that is the whole point of verses 1 to 4. Question 7 gets close to the bone: go first with your own example, and never corner anyone. Watch for the person whose answer to question 5 is their parents’ marriage or their own mental health — stay with them rather than moving on.

Question helps

  • Q1 — Many had already compiled a narrative, so Luke was not inventing in a vacuum; the original eyewitnesses and servants of the word, trained to remember a rabbi’s words word for word, so this is testimony within thirty years, not legend from centuries later; and Luke’s own careful investigation, written in an orderly sequence.
  • Q2 — Luke’s purpose is not to make people feel good but to make them know for certain. The answer to a faith near collapse is not to wrestle inside your emotions but to go back to the facts: examine, read, talk with older believers.
  • Q3 — Josephus records Herod killing his wife Mariamne, three sons, his mother-in-law and his brother-in-law; Augustus said it was better to be Herod’s pig than his son. God raised up Moses in Egypt, Samuel in the judges, Daniel in Babylon, John and Jesus under Rome.
  • Q4 — Righteous does not mean flawless but faithful, godly and obedient. Childlessness was a deep disgrace, assumed to be punishment. Godliness does not mean the absence of pain. In the eternal record God remembers “the righteous”, not “successful people”.
  • Q5 — Notice the tense: not will be heard, not is heard now, but has been heard. The prayer Zechariah prayed years earlier had been in God’s ears all along. God delays not because he is not listening but because he is preparing a greater blessing.
  • Q6 — He asked for a son; God gave a prophet. He asked for a happy family; God gave the renewal of a nation. John’s task was to prepare, not to complete, pointing to one greater than himself. God’s blessing is there to make us a blessing.
  • Q7 — The sermon’s own list: we believe God can part the Red Sea but not sort out my marriage; that he raised Jesus but not that he can free me of that habit; that he made the universe but not that he can make someone my age bear fruit.
  • Q8 — Muteness reads like punishment; theologically it is grace. It is protection (no tongue for doubt, complaint or unbelief) and preparation (nine months to think, pray and wait — and his first word afterwards was praise). Elizabeth adds faith without evidence: God’s favour had been there all along.
