Nursing Home Talks

The Sinner in the Mirror

Romans 3:9–20 · 26 July 2026

Big Idea: The law of God shows us who we truly are — all of us under sin, not one of us righteous — so that every mouth is stopped before God, and every heart is driven to Jesus Christ.

Beginning: a good kind of fear

Can you remember a time when you were truly frightened?

Most of us here have lived long enough to know real fear — a telephone call in the middle of the night, or a doctor’s room and the word you did not want to hear.

For me it was a day many years ago, when I took my little daughter into a very large toy shop. I told her to stay close. She wandered off, and in a moment she was gone. I ran up and down those aisles calling her name. It was only five minutes, but it was the longest five minutes of my life. In the end I stood at the door and prayed — and out she came, running to me.

There are four ways we answer fear: we fight it, we flee it, we freeze in front of it, or we fawn — we try to please the very thing that threatens us. We do not enjoy fear. But fear is a gift. It keeps us alive.

Our passage today ends with a line that sounds very strange to us: “There is no fear of God before their eyes.” In the Bible, that is the worst thing that can be said about anyone.

So, three questions. Why is it good to fear God? What does that fear look like? And how should we answer it?

Let us pray. “Father, open our ears and our hearts to your word today, that we may know what it means to fear you. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

1. There is no one righteous — not even one (vv. 9–12)

Paul has already shown that those outside Israel stood guilty before a holy God, and that those inside Israel, who had the law, stood guilty too. Now verse nine: “What then? Are we any better off? Not at all! For we have already charged that both Jews and Greeks are all under sin.”

Then he quotes the Old Testament: “There is no one righteous, not even one. There is no one who understands; there is no one who seeks God. All have turned away… There is no one who does what is good, not even one.”

Notice the centre of that quotation — the heart of it. “There is no one who seeks God.” That is what righteousness is. Not a tidy record. Not a respectable life. Seeking God, wanting what he wants. And left to ourselves, none of us does it.

Now here is the wonderful thing. Some of you have loved the Lord Jesus for sixty, seventy, eighty years. You did not choose him out of your own goodness; left to yourself you would have turned away. That you sought him at all is a miracle — God moved your heart, and God holds you still.

And that miracle has not stopped. If your heart still stirs at the name of Jesus, if you still whisper his name when the night is long, that is not habit. That is the living God at work in you today, as truly as he was sixty years ago.

2. The law exposes us — throat, tongue, feet and eyes (vv. 13–18)

When the doctor comes to see you, what is often the first thing he says? “Open your mouth and say ahh.” The mouth tells him what is going on inside.

Paul makes the same examination. Verses thirteen to eighteen: “Their throat is an open grave; they deceive with their tongues. Vipers’ venom is under their lips. Their mouth is full of cursing and bitterness. Their feet are swift to shed blood… and the path of peace they have not known. There is no fear of God before their eyes.”

An open grave — not empty, but occupied, and unclean. And we know it is true, don’t we? A bitter word about the woman down the corridor. A sharp word to the nurse who was slow. A complaint about the family who have not visited for a fortnight. Words that leave a room colder than they found it.

Then the feet, swift to shed blood. You may say, “I can hardly walk to the window; that is not about me.” But Paul is not describing legs. He is describing hearts that run to anger. A heart can run a very long way while the body sits perfectly still.

Then the diagnosis: “There is no fear of God before their eyes.” Every symptom traces back to that. We are not unrighteous for want of education or opportunity or health. We are unrighteous because we have not feared God. As Proverbs says, “The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge.”

And here the gospel meets us. Jesus knew exactly what it is to fear God. In the garden, facing the wrath our sins deserved, he was so distressed that his sweat fell like great drops of blood. He was afraid. And still he said, “Not my will, but yours be done.” The righteous Son of God took the judgement that belonged to us, to bring us home to God.

So let the gospel do its quiet work on your tongue. When the tea is cold and the night was long and the sharp word is ready — stop. Choose silence over bitterness. And where you can, choose blessing: a kind word to the one who sits alone, a thank you to the one who dressed you this morning. That is no small ministry. It is the way of peace this world does not know.

3. Every mouth is silent before the God who speaks (vv. 19–20)

Verse nineteen: the law speaks “so that every mouth may be shut and the whole world may become subject to God’s judgment.”

Every mouth shut. Picture a courtroom where the evidence has all been read out and there is nothing left to say. No excuse, no appeal, no comparing ourselves favourably with the person in the next chair. On that day the arguing stops.

Verse twenty: “no one will be justified in his sight by the works of the law, because the knowledge of sin comes through the law.” One old writer put it beautifully: it is the straight edge of the law that shows us how crooked we are. But that is mercy, not cruelty. If we never knew we were crooked, we would never come to the one who can make us straight.

So how do we answer the fear of God? Not by fighting him — that is an ant against the ocean. Not by fleeing — where would we go? Not by freezing and leaving it for another day, when none of us is promised another day. Not by fawning with pious words we do not mean, as though God could not read the heart.

There is only one answer. We fall on our knees — and if the knees will not bend any more, the heart still can — and we cry out, “God, be merciful to me, a sinner.” And he is. He always is, for Christ’s sake.

Conclusion: the mirror that tells the truth

Think of God’s word as a mirror — the only mirror that never flatters and never lies. It shows you your bitterness plainly. And in the very same glass it shows you the wonder, the grace and the patience of God in Jesus Christ.

So look into it often. If your eyes are tired, let someone read it to you. Let the psalms you learned as a child come back to you in the quiet hours; they are still God’s word, and they are still working.

And know this: your church has not forgotten you. We pray for you by name, we thank God for you, and we long to see you. Your faith is not in retirement. When you pray for your children and grandchildren, for this home, for those who care for you — that is frontline work in the kingdom of God, and heaven takes it seriously even when the world does not notice.

You are not a burden. You are beloved saints of God, still bearing fruit in old age, kept by the same grace that first found you, and being made ready to see the face of the Lord Jesus.

Something to think about

1. Where am I still unwilling to let God’s word show me the sinner I really am?

2. What do my words — about this place, about my family, about the people who care for me — reveal about my heart?

3. What would it take for my mouth to fall silent before God today, so that I can simply listen to his words of grace?

Let us pray

“Our Father in heaven, thank you for your word, which tells us the truth about ourselves and the truth about you. In it we see our own crookedness, our bitterness, our turning away — and we see your patience with us, year after year after year. Father, forgive us. Thank you that the Lord Jesus feared you perfectly and bore your judgement in our place. Thank you that you have kept your hand upon your people through long lives, through illness and loss and waiting. Keep us to the end. Give us kind tongues and quiet hearts. Be merciful to any here who do not yet know you, and bring them home to Jesus while there is still time. Comfort those we love who are far away, and those who care for us here. And when our mouths at last fall silent, let it be to see the face of our Redeemer, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
