Faith in a disbelieving world

Passage: John 11.45_57

Big Idea: God is sovereign in bringing salvation and unity in Christ, even in man’s evil, disbelieving schemes.

Disbelief vs Unbelief

God is sovereign despite man’s disbelief

Have a confident faith as a child of God

Over the past week, a news story has taken over most of the Australian media.

It’s the story of Essendon’s new CEO, Andrew Thorburn.

Thorburn was appointed to be the CEO of an Australian Football Club called Essendon.

Previously, he’s been the CEO of National Australian Bank.

When news of his appointment as CEO of Essendon, there was a large public outcry.

Why? Because he’s also on the board of a Christian Church, City on a Hill.

It’s a Church the Bible.

Because it’s a Church that taught the Bible, it angered many people.

As a result, Andrew Thorburn resigned from his position in less than 24 hours.

He didn’t do anything wrong; he didn’t do anything illegal. How could he? He had less than 24 hours in the job!

He resigned because the public would not accept that he belongs to a Church that taught the Bible.

In a personal statement, Thorburn wrote, “… today it became clear to me that my personal Christian faith is not tolerated or permitted in the public square.”

He was appallingly treated by the public and by those in power.

I have often given examples of how Christians in other countries had been unjustly mistreated by those in power, even here in this pulpit.

This is now happening here, in 21st Century Australia.

This is a very public case, but by no means the only case.

There are many people around Australia, even around our own local community, who face enormous pressure to publicly identify as a Christian.

In fact, I think most of us live, study, work in an environment where most people aren’t Christians.

In most cases, we’re the minority.

Should we make ourselves known as Christians?

Should I be worried about being publicly known as a Christian?

If you have your Bible, please open up to John 11, beginning from verse 45.

In today’s passage, we’ll see three things to help us think about what it means to be a Christian in a disbelieving world:

Disbelief vs Unbelief

God is sovereign despite man’s disbelief

Have a confident faith as a child of God

Before we begin, let’s pray.

“Dear Lord Jesus, we are a people of little faith. Please help us in our unbelief. By your Spirit, open our eyes and our ears that we may believe in what you are saying to us today in your word, so that Father will be glorified. Amen.”

Disbelief vs Unbelief

Open up with me to John 11:45-46,

“Therefore, many of the Jews who came to Mary and saw what he did believed in him. But some of them went to the Pharisees and told them what Jesus had done.”

Over the past few weeks, we’ve seen what Jesus did.

He raised Lazarus from the dead.

These people here saw what Jesus did; no one denied it.

They believed it happened, that Jesus did in fact raise Lazarus from the dead.

But some went to the Pharisees.

Is it because they didn’t believe that Jesus raised the dead?

No, they did believe it, but they think that the Pharisees would be worried about it.

Look with me at verses 47-48,

“So the chief priests and the Pharisees convened the Sanhedrin and were saying, “What are we going to do since this man is doing many signs? If we let him go on like this, everyone will believe in him, and the Romans will come and take away both our place and our nation.”

The chief priests and the Pharisees didn’t deny Jesus raising Lazarus from the dead either.

In fact, they believed that he did many signs.

And they were worried because they saw Jesus as a threat.

For them, the problem was not the fact that Jesus could raise the dead.

They accept the fact, but they refused to believe the truth about Jesus.

For them it’s a problem of disbelief rather than unbelief.

What’s the difference?

When I was in Bible College, I wrote a paper on belief in the Gospel of John.

When I got the marks back, the marker made a very interesting comment?

Commenting on a sentence I wrote, he said, “Do you mean disbelief or unbelief?”

What’s the difference?

Unbelief means the absence of belief.

You don’t believe in something because you don’t know much about it.

Most people around the world who haven’t heard about Jesus would have an unbelief.

However, disbelief goes a little deeper.

Disbelief is to know about something but then choose to reject it.

It’s an intentional rejection that something is true.

Many people who grew up in the West, who would have heard the gospel, would have a disbelief.

Of course, it’s a very general statement; I’m sure there are people in the West who have not heard of Jesus and have an unbelief.

But, generally speaking, many have at least heard about Jesus, sometimes true things, sometimes wrong things, and they choose disbelief.

The Pharisees and the Priests here know the facts about the Bible and they have heard the facts about Jesus, but they refuse to believe.

They choose disbelief.

Why? It’s partly because they saw Jesus as a threat.

You see, the Sanhedrin that they convened is a ruling body over the country of Israel.

The Romans were officially ruling over the Jews at that time, but they choose to allow some of the Jews themselves to form the Sanhedrin in order to look after the legal and the religious affairs of the Jews.

As Jesus continues to do these signs, the Sanhedrin was afraid that more and more people would see Jesus as the Messiah, the Chosen One of God.

The popular belief among the people was that the Messiah would free them from the Roman oppression.

If Jesus is God’s chosen Messiah, and if the people embraced him as God’s Chosen Messiah, then the Sanhedrin would be in trouble.

Why? Because the Jews would rally under the Messiah to fight against the Romans, the Romans would respond with swift and violent force, and the Sanhedrin can kiss their power and their privileges goodbye.

And so, yes, the Sanhedrin was worried.

Despite knowing the truth about Jesus, they choose to disbelieve Jesus.

They were more concerned about their self-preservation than to admit to the truth about Jesus.

Is that still true today? Absolutely!

People today, whether they’re in the East or the West, though they know the truth about Jesus, would choose to disbelieve Jesus.

They choose to disbelieve Jesus because they would rather have the pleasures and the powers of this world.

They know about Jesus but they refuse to believe; They disbelieve.

I remember a young man that I taught many years ago.

He was a bright young man, he grew up in a Church and taught Sunday School.

When I asked him what he wanted to achieve when he grew up, he said he wanted to own a house in Mosmon, one of the wealthiest suburbs in Sydney.

Soon, unsurprisingly, his life was beginning to go astray.

I tried to speak to him but he chose to leave the Church.

I haven’t seen him since.

Here was a young man who knew about Jesus, who taught about Jesus but who ultimately disbelieved because he would rather have the pleasures and powers of this world.

Sadly, he wasn’t the only young man to fall away.

Many young and old, men and women, chose disbelief and rejected Jesus.

This is the society we find ourselves today.

We live in a society full of people who actively, intentionally disbelieve Jesus.

Powerful leaders, powerful lobby groups, actively seek out Christians in order to shame us, with little justification other than the fact that we’re Christians.

Shouldn’t we be worried to be a Christian publicly?

2. God is sovereign despite man’s disbelief

No, we’re not to worry because God is sovereign, in control, despite man’s unbelief.

Look with me at verses 49-50, “One of them, Caiaphas, who was high priest that year, said to them, “You know nothing at all! You’re not considering that it is to your advantage that one man should die for the people rather than the whole nation perish.”

Caiaphas the High Priest dismissed the worries of his fellow priests and said what seemed to be the most obvious thing.

Sacrifice the one, save the thousands.

It’s a no-brainer.

Now, note that he’s not saying that Jesus should die for saying or doing anything wrong.

He’s not even saying the miracles were a hoax.

He probably believed that Jesus did raise the dead but it didn’t matter.

But it’s better that Jesus to die, than for the whole nation to die.

It didn’t matter that Jesus was innocent, he had to die, in order to keep the nation safe.

Of course, being in the Sanhedrin, he wanted Jesus to die because the rise of Jesus would potentially be a threat to his job.

But it would’ve sounded bad if saving his job was the reason he gave to kill Jesus.

So, he dressed it up a little bit, “It’s better for one man to die than for the nation to die”

But there’s more happening behind the scenes, even more than what Caiaphas knew.

Look with me at verses 51-52, “He did not say this on his own, but being high priest that year he prophesied that Jesus was going to die for the nation, and not for the nation only, but also to unite the scattered children of God.”

Like many parts of the Bible, we see a dramatic irony at play.

It’s a little like this.

Take a look at this picture:

A doctor was saying to his patient: “The treatment has been rather successful. There has been a visible reduction in the size of the brain tumor.”

True, but there was something else happening.

What is it?

The patient’s head also reduced visibly.

While what the Doctor said was true, unbeknown to the Doctor, a far more worrying symptom is happening to the patient!

What the High Priest Caiaphas said might be true, from a certain point of view.

It might to the Sanhedrin’s advantage for one man to die instead of the nation.

But unbeknown to the High Priest, something far greater would result with the death of Jesus.

From a political point of view, any security brought about by the death of Jesus would be very short lived.

In fact, we know from history that Caiaphas himself would be sacked by the Romans six years later.

The death of Jesus would bring about eternal security.

Jesus would die for the nation, so that they would have eternal, everlasting life.

Jesus has died for the nation, so that they would not die.

This is what Jesus promised earlier in the chapter.

It’s what I encouraged us to remember back in John 11:25-26.

Jesus is the resurrection and the life. The one who believes in Jesus, even if he dies, will live. Everyone who lives and believes in Jesus will never die.

For that to happen, Jesus must die for them.

We die because of our sins against God.

God’s judgment against our sin is death.

Jesus, the Perfect, Sinless One, died in our place so that whoever believes in him might live.

If a person living in the land of Israel believe that, then they would be saved.

What Caiaphas said was quite true.

What he said was a prophecy; what he said was from God.

More than that, the life that Jesus brings is not just for the Jewish person who believes, but for all the scattered children of God, rather they are Jewish or not.

Whether you’re a Jew, or an Australian, or Chinese, or Indian, or Korean, or Malaysian, or Singaporean, (have I missed any in this congregation?)

Or whether you’re combination or none of these, the Good news of Jesus us for you.

God’s children are scattered around the world.

God has already chosen them before the beginning of the world as his children.

But they haven’t heard the good news and they haven’t believed in Jesus yet.

It’s our job to take the gospel to them, that they might believe.

That’s why Christians tell the good news of Jesus to everyone, whether we do it locally here or abroad.

We do it because Jesus died for God’s nation and because he died to unite the scattered children of God.

Caiaphas might have thought that he had hatched an evil scheme to destroy Jesus,

but even the very words that he used to condemn Jesus were from God.

God was, is and always be in absolute and sovereign control.

One example where we see God’s sovereignty in display is in the person of Saul.

Saul hated Christians.

He chased after Christians, and he actively helped to get them killed.

This resulted in Christians fleeing from Jerusalem and travelled to other places where they continued to share the gospel.

In fact, more people came to believe in Jesus as a result.

Instead of extinguishing the flame, Saul’s active pursuit of Christians actually fanned the flame.

God’s sovereignty at work!

Of course, later on, even Saul became a Christian.

He, too, travelled to many different countries and planted many Churches.

Though Paul used to breathe our slander against Christians, God used him to write at least 13 out of the 27 letters of the New Testament.

Listen to how Paul describes what God has done in his life:

“I give thanks to Christ Jesus our Lord who has strengthened me, because he considered me faithful, appointing me to the ministry—even though I was formerly a blasphemer, a persecutor, and an arrogant man. But I received mercy because I acted out of ignorance in unbelief, and the grace of our Lord overflowed, along with the faith and love that are in Christ Jesus” 1 Tim 1:12-14

God was sovereign and gracious, despite Paul’s previous hatred towards Christians

3. Have a confident faith as a child of God

After Caiaphas’ speech, the Sanhedrin started plotting on how they would kill Jesus.

Asa result, Jesus couldn’t walk openly among Jews anymore but stayed in Ephraim, in the countryside, near the wilderness.

Why couldn’t he walk among the Jews?

Look with me at verse 57, “The chief priests and the Pharisees had given orders that if anyone knew where he was, he should report it so that they could arrest him.”

There were many Jews in Jerusalem, many who would’ve received the Pharisees’ order.

The whole city would potentially be full of spies and informants who would report to the Pharisees about Jesus.

The busy city of Jerusalem was not safe for Jesus.

Was Jesus worried? No.

It wasn’t the right time yet.

Jesus was waiting for God’s signal; It’ll come very soon.

In fact, we’ll see it in the next Chapter. Make sure you come back over the next few weeks to find out!

What does passage tell us about being a public Christian.

There are at least two things:

No evil scheme of man can thwart God’s plan of salvation.

It simply cannot be done.

God’s plan of salvation cannot be stopped and so we don’t need to worry.

We can have confidence that God is in control even when we’re surrounded by a sea of disbelief against Christ.

I mentioned earlier about the Church that Andrew Thornburn is a part of, the church City on a Hill.

There’s right now, even this morning, a lot of pressure against the Church.

We should be concerned for them, and we should pray for them.

At the same time, we need to remember that God is in control.

No scheme of man can thwart God’s plan.

No government, no lobby groups, no football club, not even a majority of our community, can stop God’s plan of salvation.

If you believe in Jesus, then you are with Jesus, the one who made you, the Shepherd who died for you, the one who is Life.

Trust him, have confidence in him.

Nothing, no one, can take you or anyone who belongs to him, out of his hands.

Unite with the scattered children of God.

Jesus died to unite the scattered children of God.

What’s our Church’s vision statement?

English: We come together to build and to nurture a nation which lives and breathes the gospel of God.

Chinese: 蒙神恩召，传讲耶稣, 活出福音, 代代相传

God has called us to gather together.

It’s important and vital that we come together, united, as one.

Older Christians, Christ died for you to unite you to the younger Christians.

Talk to them.

English speaking Christians, Christ died for you to unite you to the Chinese speaking ones.

Talk with them and work with them.

And the same works the other way around as well.

Younger Christians, talk to the older Christians; Chinese brothers and sisters, talk and work with the English speaking ones.

I thank God that you’re here today, united in Christ.

Don’t underestimate the importance of this gathering here today.

Jesus Christ has died so that you can be gathered, united here, together, in his Church.

Don’t follow the ways of the Pharisees and the Priests.

The Pharisees, the Priests, and the High Priest Caiaphas in Jesus’ days would rather have the powers and pleasures of this world than to believe in Jesus.

Christ died for the nation of Israel so that those who believe in him might be saved.

Christ died also for us, his scattered children of God outside of the nation of Israel so that we might saved.

He died to unite the scattered children of God.

That’s why we gather, we meet as one together, on Sunday mornings, as God’s people, united by Christ.

Do not let your desire for power or pleasure or anything else stop you from gathering with God’s people.

As well as those of here at Church today, there are still many who aren’t here yet.

We have room for many more!

Thank you for all those who have been sharing the gospel with people over the past year.

God has blessed us with bringing new people into his kingdom over the past year.

Yesterday, I met with the Parish Council, the wardens and other leaders to give God thanks and to think about how to reach even more people for Christ.

Over the coming months there are many opportunities to bring the gospel of Jesus to many people.

We’ll hear about a new one later this morning.

Who can you invite over the next month?

Pray for them! Invite them!

Next week is the Anniversary and a great opportunity to invite someone.

I’ve already invited three families over the past week. How about you?

Is God being outsmarted by man’s schemes? Absolutely not.

Should I be worried about being a Christian publicly?

You might be met with disapproval, you might be targeted or even cancelled, but there’s no need to worry.

God is sovereign in bringing salvation and unity in Christ, even in man’s evil, disbelieving schemes.

And so, friends, be confident in Christ your Saviour and unite with the scattered children of God.

I’m thankful for the older brothers and sisters who come to Church this morning.

There are so many reasons for them not to come.

It’s wet, it’s cold, it’s difficult.

It’ll be easier just to watch from home.

I’m also very thankful for the families with young children who come.

It’s hard to get the kids to wake up, it’s hard to get them ready on time.

It’ll also be easier for you to watch from home.

But you are here, brothers and sisters, because you know that’s why Christ has died.

Jesus Christ has died to make this happen.

He has died to unite the scattered children of God.

For those of us who are watching online, for those of us who only come when we have time, my plea for you is to gather with God’s people.

I know that it’s not always easy but good things seldom are.

Christ has died to make the Church happen.

Whenever you can, at the very least on a Sunday morning, unite with the scattered children of God.

Be confident in Christ your Saviour and unite with the scattered children of God.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What in my life will tempt me to disbelieve Jesus, actively turning away from Jesus? (Is it power? Is it pleasure? Or a loss of them?)

Am I confident about Jesus, even when I’m in the minority?

Jesus has died to unite the scattered children of God. Do I share his concern for them?

Friends, Jesus has died, one for the many.

Be confident in Christ your Saviour and unite with the scattered children of God.
