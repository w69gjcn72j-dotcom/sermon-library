Intro: Giving up your rights

We live in a world obsessed with our rights.

In Australia, we emphasise on the rights of individual citizens.

You’ve probably have seen reports of people emphasising on their rights not to wear masks and their rights on not getting vaccinated.

It’s my body; it’s my rights.

In other countries, we see an emphasis on the rights of those in authority.

There are countries where the parents will have absolute rights over who their children marry,

and government will have absolute rights over what can be reported.

Both as individuals and as those in authority, we want our rights.

We know our rights; we argue for our rights; we fight for our rights.

We don’t want to be weaklings who give up on our rights; we want to be strong!

But here, in 1 Corinthians, the Bible tells us to do something utterly counter-intuitive.

The Bible here tells the strong Christians to give up our rights for a weaker brother.

It tells those who are more knowledgeable, more learned, to give up their rights for those less knowledgeable.

Rather than fighting for our rights, the Bible tells us there are times when it’s better to give up our rights.

That’s what we’ll be thinking through in today’s passage in 1 Corinthians 8.

What right would I give up for a weaker, less learned brother or sister?

We are going to see three things:

If you have your Bible, please turn with me to 1 Corinthians 8.

Knowledge puffs up but love builds up

Firstly, we’ll see that knowledge puffs up but love builds up

The Corinthians had probably written an earlier letter to Paul and asked him what he thoughts about food sacrificed to idols.

Corinth was an ancient city that had temples to other gods.

Often, people would eat meat and other food at the temple.

The food they had there would’ve been first offered as a sacrifice to the idol.

They would then have the food afterwards.

That was the normal practice of the Corinthian society.

When some of the Corinthians became Christians, they had to decide whether they should still eat the food that had been sacrificed to idols.

Some thought that it was fine; but others thought that it was inappropriate.

And so, they’ve sent a letter to Paul for clarification.

Their question would be something like this: “Paul, it’s alright to have meat sacrificed to an idol, isn’t it?”

Paul replies in verse 8:1,

(Read out – Peter, please type verse 1)

Those who wrote to Paul probably said, “We have good Christian knowledge, we understand Christian things.

And we’re pretty sure it’s right to eat food sacrificed to idols, even though our weaker, more ignorant brothers might not think the same.”

Paul doesn’t dispute their knowledge, but he does question whether they have love.

Knowledge puffs up but love builds up.

This reminds me of the balloon men that we often see on the side of the streets, usually next to caryards.

Have you seen them?

They have a little fan next to them which blows them up real high.

They draw a lot of attention to themselves very quickly.

They puff up very quickly, in a matter of seconds.

Compare that with the buildings that are coming up around Church.

They had to lay the foundation first and then, brick by brick, the builders build the units on top of the foundation.

They take a lot longer but unlike the balloon man, they are built to last.

If we have knowledge alone, without love, then it’s a knowledge that puffs up, like the balloon man.

A knowledgeable but loveless person only draws attention to himself or herself.

But a person who is knowledgeable and loving builds other up, they want the best for the other person.

Love builds relationship; love builds a community.

That’s why in our mission statement, we say that we nurture and we build a nation that lives and breathes the gospel of Jesus Christ.

That’s what God has come to do; he’s come to build a kingdom.

Being knowledgeable, even if your knowledge is correct, is not enough.

Look with me at verse 2:

(Read out – Peter, please type verse 2)

The person who is proud of his or her knowledge, the Mr and Mrs Know-it-all’s, don’t know as they ought to know.

They might compare their knowledge with other people, even other Christians, and take pride in how much they know.

But knowledge that puffs up is a shallow knowledge; it’s a knowledge that doesn’t know how much it doesn’t know.

As a commentator once said, “Knowledge is proud that it has learnt so much. Wisdom is humble that it knows no more.”

Knowledge that puffs up maybe true knowledge, but it doesn’t go deep enough.

As we dive deeper into God’s word, we’ll see that as important as it is to know God, what’s more important is that we’re known by God.

Look with me at verse 3:

(Read out – Peter, please type verse 3)

Far more important than our knowledge of God is God’s knowledge of us.

The Christian theologian Richard Hays once said, “what counts is not so much our knowledge of God as God’s knowledge of us. That is the syntax of salvation.”

Knowing that God knows us completely and perfectly and he still loves us is the stuff of salvation.

On most modern phones, they have a feature that makes you look better when you take a photo of yourself.

It’s called a filter and it makes you look better than you are in real life.

Sometimes, it might be just a whitening of your skin, or smoothing out your complexion.

Sometimes, it seriously transforms you into someone else.

In English, to love someone ‘warts and all’ is to love the person completely, even the person’s warts and all.

To love someone warts and all is to love them even when they don’t have filters.

We can filter our lives all we want but we’re filled with warts and all; we’re full of faults and scars, full of weaknesses and imperfections.

But God knows us, and he loves us, warts and all.

A line from a song that we sing describes it so well: You see the depths of my hearts and you love me the same, you’re an Amazing God.

That’s our amazing God; that’s our gracious God.

He knows us and he still loves us.

And we respond to him by loving him.

Once we know that God knows us and loves us, we want to know and love other people.

This is the loving knowledge that builds up.

It’s a knowledge that loves people rather than condemning them.

It’s a knowledge that inspires us to love people, warts and all, because that’s what God does.

And so, Paul wants us to have this mindset to begin with.

Yes, knowledge is important but make sure you have a knowledge that’s based on God’s knowledge of us.

Make sure you have a knowledge that loves.

Paul then takes us to the knowledge that the Corinthians probably referred to in their discussion about food being sacrificed to idols.

It’s the knowledge of God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ

Knowledge of God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ

Look with me at verse 4,

(Read out – Peter, please type verses 4)

An idol is an object that people worship other than God.

Paul affirms with the Corinthians that an idol is nothing in the world.

There aren’t real gods that are in competition with the real God.

There is no God but one.

There is only one real God.

There may be many so-called gods and so-called lords in the world.

But there is only one real one.

We see this in today’s world too, don’t we?

In a few weeks’ time, there will be a census done in Australia.

A census is done to take a look at what Australia is like in the year 2021.

The last time we had a census was back in 2016.

There were at least twenty different religions that people in Australia identify with.

There are many so-called gods and so-called lords in Australia.

This isn’t a modern phenomenon.

In the Ancient world, there would’ve been many Greek gods and Roman gods to choose from.

People were even encouraged to worship the Head of the Government, namely, the emperor.

It was controversial for the Christian in ancient world to claim that there was one God.

It’s still controversial for Christians today to claim that there is only one God.

But to the Christian, there is always only one God and only one Lord.

All things are from him: he’s our Creator.

He’s made everything.

And we exist for him: he’s our destination.

He’s where we’re going.

In the middle of a Christian’s understanding of who God is, is Christ himself.

We believe in one Lord, Jesus Christ.

All things are through him: All things happen through him.

Nothing exist apart from him.

We exist through him: He’s the purpose for our existence.

We, as a Church, as a people, exist only because of Jesus.

Here we see a parallel of ideas between God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

All things are from him (the Father) and all things are through him (Jesus).

Everything in this world, in this universe are from God and through Jesus.

Nothing in this world exist and nothing in this world can continue to exist apart from God the Father and the Lord Jesus.

More than that, we exist for him (the Father) and we exist through him (the Lord Jesus).

The destiny of where everything is heading is God the Father and the Lord Jesus,

And Christians, as people belonging to God the Father and the Lord Jesus,

Are in the midst of it all.

We belong to the new kingdom that is to come.

We belong to the new world.

In this new world, there’s God the Father, there’s the Lord Jesus and there’s us.

WE are caught up in the world to come.

Some people think of the Church as a niche, little community.

It’s a small community with a quaint (old) view of the world.

It’s like a little club, that sings old songs, does traditional things.

Basically, a museum, an artefact from the past.

But that’s not how the Bible sees the Church.

The very existence of the Church points to something that’s not only happened in the past,

We point to the future.

Our very existence points to a time beyond ours.

Despite the trials that the Church has faced over the past two thousand years, we stand firm, pointing to the gospel.

Pointing to the plan that God has for the whole world.

Pointing to the plan that God has for the whole human race, a plan which centres on the Lord Jesus Christ.

We’re not just a monument to what God has done in the past in the Lord Jesus.

We’re like a timer that points people to see what God is about to do in the future in the Lord Jesus.

And our presence reminds people that every day is a day closer to meeting God the Father and the Lord Jesus.

That’s our job in the present: we point people to our God and our Lord.

We point people to what God did in Jesus and we point people to what God will do in Jesus.

That’s the wonderful knowledge we have of God.

Idols are nothing but everything comes from God the Father and we exist for God the Father.

That idols are nothing but everything is through the Lord Jesus and we exist through the Lord Jesus.

It’s with this wonderful knowledge in mind that we come back to the question about idols.

When it comes to food being offered to idols, Christians must be willing to give up our entitlements even when we’re right.

Give up your entitlements, even when you’re right.

Paul has made it quite clear that there is one God, the Father and that there is one Lord, Jesus Christ.

And that idols are nothing.

But not everyone has this knowledge; not everyone understands the implication of this.

Coming back to the issue of eating food sacrificed to idols,

he points out to the Corinthians that some have been so used to associating eating food sacrificed to idols with idol worship,

that they can’t separate them.

For them, to eat food sacrificed to idols is to participate in idol worship.

They go together.

Even when they became Christians, they can’t dissociate eating food sacrificed to idols from idol worship.

Whenever they see someone eating food that was sacrificed to idols, they see idol worship.

That’s not saying that the Corinthian Christians who eat the food are actually participating in idol worship.

But to these Christian observers, perhaps young, less learned Christian observers, these ‘stronger’ Christians appear to be participating in idol worship.

Here in multi-cultural Sydney, we have many difference venues of worship, including many temples.

One of the largest Buddhist temples in the Southern Hemisphere is in Wollongong, the Nan Tien Temple.

They actually have a branch here in Kogarah, literally just down the road from us.

As well as Buddhist temples, there are Hindi temples in Sydney as well.

There was one near where I used to live called the Sydney Murugan Temple, in Mays Hill.

When I did some searches on there, you often see people recommending the food in the temple.

Can Christians just go there for the food without participating in worship?

In your heart, you might just go there for the food.

You might even be able to explain from the Bible that you believe that there is one God and that you don’t believe in idols.

But to a new believer, especially one who’s been converted from Buddhism or Hinduism, seeing a Christian eat food from the temple might prove too much for them.

Seeing a Christian eating food might be seen as too much of an association with worship.

When a young, less learned Christian sees a stronger Christian eat at a temple, they will be tempted to do the same, and end up with a corrupted worship.

While it’s true that food doesn’t bring us closer to God and we’re not worse off if we don’t eat, or better off if we do it,

Paul wants us to be careful.

Look with me at verse 9-10,

(Read out – Peter, please type verses 9-10)

Notice that Paul’s not telling the weak Christians to grow up.

Rather, he’s telling the stronger Christians to not be a stumbling block to the weak.

How can the strong be a stumbling block to the weak?

When the weak Christian see a Christian who’s supposed to be more mature eating food sacrificed to an idol, thinking that they’re Ok with idol worship,

They too, will think that it’s ok to participate in idol worship.

It’s an act that damages their idea of worship.

It will lead them to a false worship that allows for worship of multiple gods.

Look with me at verse 11-12

(Read out – Peter, please type verses 11-12)

Jesus loved this weaker brother or sister and had died for them.

Would you now destroy them by what you know, even if it’s right?

Would you wound their conscience by getting them to think that it’s alright to worship other gods?

To lead a weaker Christian into sin is not only a sin against the weaker brother or sister, it’s a sin against Christ himself.

Now some people will go on the defensive and say that it’s not their fault that the weaker Christians got it wrong.

It’s the weaker Christians’ fault.

They need to be taught the right thing; the problem is with them.

But no, Paul says the problem is with those who aren’t sensitive to the weaker Christians.

Look with me at verse 13,

(Read out – Peter, please type verse 13)

Paul would rather not eat meat if it causes a weaker brother or sister to fall.

Christ died for the person; he or she is precious to Christ.

If they are precious to Christ, your Lord, they should be precious to you as well.

For someone who truly knows God, or better, known by God, they would rather give up their entitlements than to see their brother or sister fall away.

For some of us who still have family members who worship other gods, we might come to exactly the same place as the Corinthian Christians.

We might also be presented with food, delicious food, offered to us at a restaurant or a home,

Food that has been offered to an idol.

And there might be times when younger Christians, or even non-Christians, looking to see whether you’ll eat it.

They do it to see whether you’ll join in the worship.

Even though you can demonstrate from the Bible that it’s well within your rights to eat it,

You might choose not to eat it, if it means that there’ll be any misunderstanding.

You won’t eat it if it’s going to cause your brother to fall.

You are entitled to eat it, but you choose to limit your freedom, you choose to surrender your rights so that the weaker brother or sister won’t fall.

That’s what we ought to do as a community known by God.

As a people known by God, we need to love God and we need to love our brother or sister, for whom Christ died.

We need to give up our entitlements for the sake of our brother or sister.

For the sake of Christ’s love for our brother or sister, we give up, we gladly give up, our entitlements.

What does this mean for us?

Give up your rights, so your brothers and sisters won’t fall.

Give up your rights, so your brothers and sisters won’t fall.

Whatever enjoyment you might have, if it’s going to lead to a brother or sister to fall, it’s not worth it.

Many years ago, a group of Bible College students in America went out to have pizza and beer.

Among them was an international student called George from India.

George had a scholarship from India and came to learn about the Bible so that he can go back to serve the church in India.

On that night, the American students encouraged George to drink a lot of beer and they had a fun time.

The American students went on to become ministers in Churches.

However, sadly, things didn’t turn out as well for George.

In time, George became addicted to alcohol and didn’t return to India.

Instead, he worked on the streets of an American city with barely enough money to but his next drink.

In God’s mercy, he eventually brought George back and he became a minister.

But the American Bible students had become a stumbling block for George.

If they had given up their right to enjoy pizza and beer and be more sensitive to George, things would turn out very different for George.

How about us?

What are we willing to give up for our weaker brother or sister?

Maybe it’s giving up and losing face in an argument.

Maybe it’s losing a night of enjoyment over food and drinks.

Maybe it’s losing the pleasure from a card game or computer game that we enjoy.

Maybe, it’s even losing a right, an entitlement, that you can rightly demonstrate is yours.

What do you need to give up for your weaker brother or sister?

For the love of Christ, for his love for your brothers and sisters, give up your rights so that your brothers and sisters won’t fall.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Do I have knowledge that puffs up or do I have love that builds up?

When was the last time I fought so hard for my rights that I potentially damaged a brother or sister?

What rights am I willing to give up so that a weaker brother or sister won’t fall?

Friends, brothers and sisters, for the love of Christ, for his love for your brothers and sisters, give up your rights so that your brothers and sisters won’t fall.
