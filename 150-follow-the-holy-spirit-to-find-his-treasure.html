Come on down children and youth!

Over the past few weeks, we had very windy days around here, haven’t we?

What happens on a windy day?

Yes, leaves fly around on a windy day.

What do you do when leaves fly around?

Has anyone tried to catch a leaf on a windy day?

You know what, on a windy day, when I’m walking on the streets, and leaves are flying, I would sometimes try to catch them.

It just so happens that I got some leaves, and I’ve got a leaf blower.

We’re going to blow one leaf; let’s see who can catch it. Ready?

Well done!

Who found it hard? Who found it easy?

Who controls where the wind was going?

Who controls where the real wind, out there in the world is going?

You know what? Jesus tells us that God the Holy Spirit is like the wind.

Just as the wind blows where it pleases, so does the Holy Spirit.

We can’t control the Holy Spirit, just like we can’t control the wind.

If the Holy Spirit wants someone to become a Christian, it’s completely within the Holy Spirit’s control and power; we can’t make a person a Christian.

But does that mean that there’s nothing we can do in the meantime?

Does that mean that we just sit down and don’t do anything?

No, we want to see more people coming to know Jesus.

Jesus tells us to tell people about him.

But if the Holy Spirit is like the wind, making people Christians whenever he wants, and Jesus tells us to go and make disciples, where should we go?

Where do you go to witness the next conversion to Jesus Christ?

The Breath of God sends the preacher to the outcast treasurer

Look with me at Acts chapter 8, verse 26, “An angel of the Lord spoke to Philip: “Get up and go south to the road that goes down from Jerusalem to Gaza.” (This is the desert road.)”

Philip was in Samaria when an angel of the Lord spoke with him.

Last week, we read that Philip was having a great ministry in Samaria.

Many people were believing in Jesus through Philip’s words and miracles done in Jesus’ name.

The apostles Peter and John came from Jerusalem to confirm that the Samaritans were genuine believers.

The spread of the good news started in Jerusalem and has now spread to Samaria, the next step in God’s plan to bring the gospel to the nations.

However, in the middle of this powerful and fruitful ministry, God did something unexpected.

He told Philip to leave and go to go down south, pass Jerusalem and onto a desert road.

Here’s a map which showed where Philip travelled.

It’s the green arrow that goes from Jerusalem to Samaria, then back down pass Jerusalem towards Gaza.

That was probably not what we would’ve done.

We probably would’ve wanted Philip to stay in Samaria, capitalize on the momentum he has there, so that he can make Samaria into an even bigger Church.

But the Holy Spirit has a different plan; he wants Philip to go down on a deserted road, with far fewer people, which probably also meant far higher risks.

Despite not knowing why he was going on a desert road and leaving the success of Samaria behind, Philip obeyed the Holy Spirit and went down the road to Gaza.

In verse 27, we read that along the way, he met an “Ethiopian man, a eunuch and high official of Candace, queen of the Ethiopians, who was in charge of her entire treasury. He had come to worship in Jerusalem.”

This Ethiopian man was probably not from the modern-day Ethiopia, but further north, probably in modern day Sudan (see map).

He was an official of the Queen of Ethiopia, the treasurer in charge of all the Queen’s treasury.

He was highly regarded in his own country but almost certainly treated like an outcast in Jerusalem.

For he would’ve been dark-skinned, a Gentile.

More than that he was a eunuch, a man who was made unable to have children.

According to God’s laws, he wasn’t allowed to worship God in God’s presence, both as a Gentile and as a eunuch.

In Jerusalem, he would’ve worshipped God in the Court of the Gentiles.

He would’ve had to worship God from a distance.

It would be as if we welcome everyone to Church except for people who have dark skin or have some physical disability.

They can stay at the glass door, but not come to the main part of the Church itself.

This Ethiopian eunuch was an outsider, an outcast who weren’t one of God’s people, but he wanted to learn more about and worship this God of the Jews.

He was serious about worshipping this God and started to read the Book of Isaiah to know this God better.

At the same time, this very God whom Philip was reading about told Philip to join the Chariot.

This chariot was probably not the ones you see speeding around race-tracks or into battle fields.

It was probably oxen driven and not horse driven, and so, Philip could simply walk next to it.

Philip was in the middle of something big in Samaria.

He was there as the gospel took a significant leap forward as part of God’s plan to reach the world.

Why did God take Philip out of Samaria and focus on one outcast? Why did God pay so much attention to this Ethiopian eunuch?

I can think of at least two reasons.

Firstly, each individual soul is precious to God.

Over 155 years ago, when this Church was opened to the public, there probably wasn’t a long term 155 year goal.

They probably didn’t think that 155 years later, the Church would look like this.

They probably didn’t have 10 year goals either.

However, our first members, the first wardens, saw that there was a need for a church to be planted here.

And so, the first wardens, two of whom were immigrants, asked for the land to be granted with the express purpose to build a Church.

They recognised all the way back in 1869 that people in Kogarah needed to hear God’s word.

Here’s where we fit in the Family tree of the Anglican Churches of Sydney.

St Philp’s York Street was one of the first Churches in Australia.

It then sent people to start St Peter’s at St Peter’s.

And people from St Peter’s sent people to Kogarah to start St Paul’s Kogarah.

And after us, come several other churches, including San Souci, Arncliffe, Hurstville and even Sutherland.

Someone might have a carefully thought-out plan of how we can keep expanding Churches throughout Sydney.

They might had done the research and got all the finance in order.

But as important as strategies are, we must follow the Holy Spirit’s lead.

If the Holy Spirit to go where there’s hardly any people, where there will be hardly any opportunities, then that’s where we need to go for that’s where the Spirit leads.

In the case of Philip, the Holy Spirit didn’t bring crowds of people to Christ.

The Holy Spirit wanted this one outcast, this Ethiopian eunuch to be saved.

That’s how much God cares for each of our souls.

Unlike many of us, God sees the preciousness of each soul as having more worth than all the buildings and all the cities in the world.

That’s what God wants, what God desires, and so that’s where he leads his church.

He heads his Church to save souls.

Second, God uses souls to save souls.

God could’ve given eunuch a vision or a dream.

God could’ve written the gospel message in the sky.

He could’ve even made the oxen that was carrying the eunuch’s chariot talk if he wanted to (he did it through donkeys before).

However, God uses believers to save unbelievers.

As God’s people, we’re God’s chosen means to save.

God uses us his people, to save, whether we’re paid to do so or not, whether we have planned to speak to people or not.

Each soul is precious to God, God uses souls to save souls and thirdly, God plans and predestines the salvation of souls.

God is the one who is in absolute control of our destinies.

He sends believers to unbelievers, and he prepares unbelievers for believers to hear the good news of Jesus and be saved.

God the Holy Spirit He told Philip using supernatural means.

But God moves the eunuch in what seems like far more natural means.

Even though it’s natural, it’s just a glorious, for God has worked in the Eunuch’s life so that at that precise moment Philip would meet him on the desert road, in the middle of nowhere, even as while the Eunuch reading the Bible.

Both Philip and the eunuch were exactly where God wanted them to be whether they knew it (as in Philip’s case) or not (as in the Eunuch’s case)

That’s how many of us have come to faith, isn’t it?

God has put the Christian whom he’s sent at exactly to right place, so that we can hear and believe the gospel.

The preacher Simon Manchester told the story about a man used to smoke, drink a lot of alcohol, swear, and who would always mock Christians.

He lost his job and had to find a new one in the insurance industry.

As someone selling insurance, he had to go with someone else and go to knock from door to door to sell insurance.

The person who went with this man was Ernie.

Ernie was a Christian and over time, share the gospel with this man.

Over time, this man became a Christian and sent his son David Cook to a camp, where he became a Christian.

David Cook himself would one day become the Principal of the Sydney Missionary and Bible College, and was one of the most influential preachers in Australia for a very long time.

Where is God sending us, as individuals and as a Church?

Does he want us to keep reaching out to the people here in Kogarah?

Does he want us to plant more Churches, like other members of Kogarah before us?

We must always ask these questions because each soul is important to God and he sends us to save the lost souls of Kogarah and beyond.

The outcast treasurer meets the Son of God in the word of God

Philip ran up to the Ethiopian’s chariot and asked, ‘Do you understand what you’re reading?”

This high ranked Ethiopian official humbly replied, “How can I, unless someone guides me?”

He was such a contrast with Simon Magus from last week.

Simon wanted to be great, Eunuch was humble.

Simon was popular in Samaria, but the Eunuch was an outcast in Jerusalem.

Simon was happy to be called the Great Power of God but the Eunuch wasn’t even named (being known as a Eunuch is not something you associate power with)

Simon’s focus was on being amazed by Philip’s miracles, but the Eunuch simply wanted to read Bible.

Simon was a fake but the Eunuch was genuine.

What was the Eunuch trying to understand?

He was trying to understand a passage from Isaiah 53.

Look with me at verses 32-33, “the Scripture passage he was reading was this:

He was led like a sheep to the slaughter, and as a lamb is silent before its shearer, so he does not open his mouth.

In his humiliation justice was denied him. Who will describe his generation?

For his life is taken from the earth.”

The eunuch then asks, who is this person who’s like a sheep?

The prophet or someone else?

And beginning from that very passage, Philip told the Eunuch about the good news of Jesus.

This passage was about Jesus; Jesus was like the sheep in the passage.

A Christian by the name of Samuel Carson made this observation about sheep.

Pigs squeal, but the sheep is normally quiet.

Jesus, as he was going to an unfair trial that would sentence him to death, was also quiet.

He didn’t protest, he didn’t shout at his enemies.

Instead, he was quiet.

But unlike sheep, who didn’t know what was happening, Jesus knew exactly what was happening.

Jesus, the Lamb of God, knew that he was going to the cross to take away the sin of the world.

He knew that he would face the full extent of God’s anger against our sin.

He was afraid to face God’s anger on our behalf and prayed in the Garden of Gethsemane the night before for God his Father to take this cup, this cross, away.

But Jesus trusted God and said to God, “Not my will, but yours be done”.

Jesus died to save those who would trust in his name.

All those who trust him won’t have to face the judgement of God.

If Philip and the Ethiopian eunuch read further, they would’ve read a message that was almost specifically for the Eunuch.

Look with me at Isaiah 56:3-5, “No foreigner who has joined himself to the LORD should say, “The LORD will exclude me from his people,” and the eunuch should not say, “Look, I am a dried-up tree.” For the LORD says this: “For the eunuchs who keep my Sabbaths, and choose what pleases me, and hold firmly to my covenant, I will give them, in my house and within my walls, a memorial and a name better than sons and daughters. I will give each of them an everlasting name”

While it might be true that the Eunuch was not worthy to come into God’s temple in Jerusalem, in Jesus, even the Eunuch will be accepted fully by God.

Even though the eunuch would have no descendants of his own, there’s everlasting hope for the Eunuch, because Jesus, the Lamb of God was slain, suffered, died and had risen for people like the Ethiopian eunuch

To be in Christ, the Ethiopian eunuch needs to repent and trust in Jesus.

That’s the good news that Philip would’ve shared.

That’s good news for the Ethiopian eunuch, the outcast treasurer, but it’s also good news for every outcast.

It’s good news for the single man, the single women, the single fathers and the single mothers.

It’s good news for those rejected for their looks, or their status, or their intellect, or their language.

It’s good news if you’re the most unpopular and misunderstood person in your school, and it’s also good news if you’re the most popular but also the most insecure person in your school.

Have you ever felt like an outsider or an outcast?

Then this gospel is for you!

Despite all of our failings, all of our weaknesses, all of our pride, when we come humbly before Jesus, we can be forgiven.

How did this Ethiopian eunuch come to meet Jesus?

The Eunuch met Jesus in his word.

The eunuch didn’t see Jesus physically, but He read about him in Old Testament and had the gospel explained to him through Philip.

God shares his good news about Jesus through his word and through people explaining the word.

The great evangelist John Chapman used to say, “If God rings you on the telephone, do not put your head in the microwave.”

When God calls you through the Bible, as he’s chosen to, then put your head in the Bible.

If you already believe, if you have already put your trust in God, then read God’s word, every day.

This is not an option for Christians.

You may choose to read a lot, or read a little, just read something from the Bible every day!

God saves people through his word the Bible.

Read it so that you’re prepared to share the good news of Jesus from God’s word when God’s placed you with an unbeliever in your life.

Read the bible every day.

Read what Annie sends on WeChat, read the passage that Range’s sent on WeChat.

Use Our Daily Bread.

I know some people who made a habit of reading the Bible before breakfast.

Their motto is No Bible, No Breakfast.

For the Bible is more important than breakfast, more important than brushing your teeth, more important than sleep, for it’s in the Bible that we hear God speak with us,

And It’s through the Bible that we meet Jesus.

The newly baptized treasurer rejoices in the treasure of God

After hearing the message about Jesus, the Ethiopian eunuch came to a pool of water and asked, “Look, there’s water. What would keep me from being baptized?”

He must have been thinking about getting baptized as Philip was sharing the gospel with him.

The eunuch ordered the chariot to stop, got down to the water with Philip and was baptized by Philip in the water.

When the eunuch came up, God had already taken Philip away to preach in another town.

The Eunuch then went on his way rejoicing, praising God!

He was happy because he’s found the greatest treasure in the whole wide world.

He had found peace with God in Jesus Christ.

When we had our last exhibition back when we were at the young age of 150, we went through a lot of old photos of the Church.

One of these photos was this one.

It was hidden in one of our archives.

It’s a stunning photograph of the front of the Church.

When I had a closer look at it, I realized it was taken and developed by Max Dupain, one of the most famous photographers in Australia’s history.

One of the most famous photos from Max Dupain was the Sun baker; it’s a black and white photo with a man lying face down on the sand.

I looked on the internet and a photograph that Max Dupain took costs $15,000.

I don’t know how much our photo is, but it’s a treasure of a photo.

When you come into Church, you’ll see a big photograph on the side.

That’s Max Dupain as well, though that’s a copy, which we bought for about $200.00

As precious as this photo may be, it’s nothing compared with the greatest treasure of all.

The greatest treasure we can have is knowing Jesus Christ as our Lord, King, and Saviour.

It’s knowing that even though we’re outcasts, God has accepted you, loved us and in Christ, we have a precious relationship with the God who made everything.

It’s the treasure that gives real joy.

That’s the joy that we want people coming to St Paul’s Kogarah to have.

While I’m happy for you to enjoy the music, the food, the people and even the preaching, my greatest hope is that it’s ultimately Jesus that brings you joy.

If we as a Church only want people to enjoy our food, music, programmes, and even the sermon, but not Jesus, then we have failed miserably as a Church.

Jesus must remain the ultimate reason for our joy.

After your time at Church, my hope is that you won’t think about how good the service was but think about who good Jesus is.

How have you treasured Jesus even more because you came to Church today?

To know him is to know real joy.

The Eunuch made that choice to believe and to follow Jesus and he was filled with joy.

When someone finds joy in Jesus, even God rejoices.

For the conversion of souls is what brings God joy.

God rejoices and all heaven with him, when the lost is found, when someone becomes a Christian.

Conversion brings joy to the one who’s converted from being a non-believer to being a believer.

Conversion brings joy to the one who has shared the good news with the new believer.

And conversion brings joy to the Ultimate Converter, God himself.

Conversion is a wonderful thing to see!

This brings us back to the first question: Where do you go to witness the next conversion to Christ?

Where do we go to see this joy in conversion? Can we know where the next conversion is?

Remember what I said earlier? The Holy Spirit is like the wind.

It’s the Spirit who determines when someone makes conversion happens.

So how do we witness the next conversion to Christ?

Follow the Spirit’s lead.

How do we follow the Spirit’s lead? Study the Holy Spirit.

When I flew the leaf earlier, the youth and the children were following the path of the wind as it blows the leaf.

As those who are Christians, as those who are born of the Spirit, let’s study where the Spirit goes, let’s follow him.

How do you study the Holy Spirit? By reading the Bible.

We read in Ephesians that the Bible, the Word of God, is the Sword of the Spirit.

It’s the Holy Spirit himself who inspired people to write the Bible.

If we want to follow the Spirit’s lead, if we want to study the Spirit’s lead, we must read the words inspired by him.

We must read the Bible.

In the television series, Shogun, there’s an amazing dialogue between two characters.

In the series, the lord Toranaga faced many trials, overcame many unfair treatments, overcame incredible odds.

His disloyal and defeated servant Yabushige, asks his lord a question, “How does it feel to shape the wind to your will?”

That is, how was Toganaga able to control so many factors that are seemingly outside normal human control?

To which Toranaga answered, “I don’t control the wind. I only study it.”

That’s what we need to do as well.

We don’t study the wind to become Shogun (General (将军))

Rather, we study the Wind of God, the Holy Spirit of God, so that we know where he wants us to go to see his work of conversion.

To study the Holy Spirit, we need to study the Word of God.

You can’t separate one from the other.

The more you study the Word, the more you’ll be aware of where the Spirit is going, where he’s leading.

Don’t just read God’s word half-heartedly.

Go deeper by going to Growth groups and by going to the School of Christian living.

Go deep by memorising God’s word; keep God’s word in your heart!

Where do you go to witness the next conversion to Christ?

Follow the Spirit’s lead.

When you get there, you might be the one the Holy Spirit uses to convert someone to Christ.

When you see someone trust in Jesus, you’ll witness for yourself the joy that comes from knowing and having Jesus as your greatest treasure.

You’ll hear yourself laughing with joy because they have found the same treasure that you have.

And you’ll being to understand why your heavenly Father is filled with joy in the conversion of one single sinner.

And so, friends, Study the Word and the Breath of God, so that we may treasure and rejoice in the Son of God

One of the great joys that we have last week is that Henry has put his trust in Jesus.

God the Holy Spirit has converted him from a non-believer to be a believer.

It’s such a joy to see Henry now having this treasure that we have in Christ.

That’s what we want to see more of at our Church.

For our church’s vision is this: We gather to build and to nurture a nation that lives and breathes the gospel of Jesus Christ.

We want to see 100 families coming together to serve at Church.

I thank God that God has so grown us that we need more rooms on Sundays!

Our goal is to not only have adults filling up our Church with Growth groups that study the Bible but we want to have our KOG kids fill up at least three classrooms in the coming years.

We want to see at least 10 people coming to our prayer meetings regularly so that we can ask God to show us his will.

We want to study the Wind of God, so that we will know where he is leading us.

We want the Holy Spirit to lead us so that more people can be converted and saved.

Friends, Study the Word and the Breath of God, so that we may treasure and rejoice in the Son of God

How does reading the Bible and praying help us to be clearer about where the Holy Spirit leads us?

How much joy does the conversion of someone at Church give us compared with what else happens around Church and around our lives?

Where is the Holy Spirit leading our Church in the next 10,50,100 years? (see question 1 before answering)

Study the Word and the Breath of God, so that we may treasure and rejoice in the Son of God.
