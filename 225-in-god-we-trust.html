Here’s an American coin.

On this coin and on almost every coin and note in America are these words, “In God we trust”.

In 1861, a few years before our Church was built, the Reverend Mark R Watkinson of Ridleyville, Pennsylvania petitioned the Treasury Department to add the statement ‘In God we trust’ on the coins, so that they won’t be like the non-believers who don’t know God.

Over time, the United States Congress adopted the motto and it’s now the national motto of the United States.

“In God we trust”

In the 19th Century, it’s probably safe to assume that the God referred to on the coin is the God of the Bible.

It’s in the Bible’s God that we trust.

But now in the 21st Century, we’re not so sure whether the Americans would say that they trust in the same God.

Today, there are many religions in America, from the gods of the indigenous Americans to the gods of the immigrants to the God of the Bible.

Indeed, even money itself may be like a god of some sort.

It’s in money, in this god, that people have placed their trust, their confidence.

Some people, full of money, are full of confidence, perhaps with even more than a rift of arrogance.

Where does you your confidence lie?

Today, we’ll be looking at what the Bible says about money.

The Bible actually has a lot to say about money.

One author has counted 2,350 verses in the bible talking about money.

Even Jesus himself is said to use 15 percent of his recorded words on the topic of money.

We won’t be looking at every verse in the Bible that talks about money.

However, there are a few that we’ll be looking at and it will be great if you have your Bible with you.

Today, we’re going to look at three things:

Financial security

God’s jealousy

Be Hungry!

Before we look at what the Bible says about money, let’s pray,

“Our heavenly Father, we thank you for your word. And we thank you for your gifts. Please help us now to understand how to view your gifts to us as we study your word. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”

Financial Security

What comes to your mind when you think about money?

Some of us might think about the happiness that money brings.

For example, we might be happy with a $1000,

but we’ll be happier with $10000.

Others might think of money as a value of that person.

I remember that I was driving a good friend’s mother back home a long time ago.

We were talking for a while and then suddenly she asked, “How much do you earn?”

I was a bit startled by it and said whatever that I was earning at that time, which wasn’t much.

She didn’t seem too impressed.

She was judging who I was, valuing me, as it were, by how much money I earned.

I really hope that none of us here value people this way.

But for most of us, money is about security.

Most of us feel that we need money in order to have security.

Or to put it another way, we feel extremely vulnerable or insecure if we don’t have money.

My Grandmother use to always tell me to carry some money with me,

just in case I knock and break something as cheap as glass (as in what you drink from).

Can you imagine that?

Breaking something as cheap as a glass and can’t repay it!

I now have the same concern for my children

Would my children have enough money when they go out or come back from school?

What if they accidentally knock down someone’s glass of water and break it?

Would they have money for the damages?

Wow, I sound just like my Grandmother!

Knocking down glasses is probably not your biggest concern.

We have far greater concerns.

Will I have enough money to raise my children?

Will I have enough money for retirement?

Can I afford to pay for my mortgage?

Very often, you’ll hear friends, uncles, aunties, parents, even children offering financial advice on these questions.

They’ll tell you their experience with investments, they’ll talk about what they wished they had invested in.

It’s always fascinating listening to hear what people think about investments.

Now imagine that a good friend of your parents has come to you for some financial advice.

The woman’s husband died six years ago.

She says, “I’m now down to my last twenty dollars. That’s all I have left.

There’s nothing left in the cupboards.

But I feel that God want me to put it in the offertory plate at the front of the Church.

What do you think?”

What would we say?

We probably say something like this, “That’s very generous, aunty, but God wants use to use your common sense.

He wants you to take good care of yourself.

I’m sure he appreciates what you’re doing but he wants you to be sensible.

He won’t rain down food from heaven and so you keep that $20, just in case something happens, you know, for your security.”

And another person comes up.

He’s a small business owner who had a bumper crop in his vegetable gardens on his large property.

It’s so good that he’s planning to have some major renovation work done so that he’ll have more storage space for the excess crop.

He hopes to go into semi-retirement after the renovation as the income should be fairly steady from then on.

He asks you what do you think?

And you tell him, “That’s good business thinking.

You’ve worked hard all your life. You deserve it!

It’s now for you to take it easy and spend time away.

You’ve been busy all your life and haven’t had time for your family.

And so, take it easy; spend time with your kids, with your grandkids.

Enjoy life!”

That’s probably how we respond, right?

I’m sure I’ve heard versions like that many, many times.

But Jesus’ responses to these two situations would be very different.

If you’re on this side of the room, go to Luke 12:19-20 and see Jesus’ answer to the rich farmer.

If you’re at home, go to Luke 12:19-20.

What would Jesus’ answer be?

Jesus’ advice would’ve been very different to ours.

Jesus commends the poor widow in trusting God with all that she has, and Jesus condemns the rich farmer for being a fool for storing up treasures for himself rather than trusting God with his life.

Why is Jesus’ financial advice so different from ours?

God’s jealousy

It’s because God is a jealous God.

Throughout the Old Testament, we see God as the King of His people, Israel.

God ought to be his people’s confidence, his people’s hope, his people’s security.

However, when other nations threaten Israel, they would often seek alliances with other stronger nations.

With these nations, they can defend against any incoming threats, or so they thought.

It seemed like the sensible thing to do.

However, the prophets saw this for what it was: it was unfaithfulness to God.

It’s to trust in the stronger nations rather than on the strength of their God.

Listen to how Isaiah spoke against Israel when they sought Egypt’s help:

“Woe to those who go down to Egypt for help

and who depend on horses!

They trust in the abundance of chariots

and in the large number of horsemen.

They do not look to the Holy One of Israel,

and they do not seek the Lord. “ Isaiah 31:1

Instead of trusting the one and powerful God, they relied on the might of the Egyptians.

This is what idolatry is: it’s trusting in something else as your security over God.

That’s why God gets jealous over what Israel did.

They were trusting Egypt when they ought to be trusting him!

It’s a misplaced trust!

It’s like a meme or a picture that I’ve seen around internet a number of times.

It shows an Asian son studying hard because the parents wanted their son to be a Doctor.

The Asian son studies hard and eventually becomes a Doctor.

But when the Asian parents ask the son for medical advice and the son gives it to them, they wouldn’t believe him.

Instead, they would rather believe something their friends sent them on Wechat.

It seems to be a common experience among Asian children!

Many Asian parents have a misplaced trust in social media rather than medical science!

But what’s worse, many of us have a misplaced trust in money rather than trusting God.

By placing our trust in our money, by seeking security in our finance, we fall into a form of false worship, which is really a form of idolatry.

It’s a misplaced trust in money rather than God.

When we trust our riches, when we put our confidence in the security that money can bring us, we make money our god.

It’s idolatry!

And so, trusting in our financial security makes God jealous, because we are trusting in our riches, rather than trusting him.

This is in contrast with what we’ve read today in the First Letter to Timothy, chapter 6.

If you have your Bible there, please open up to 1 Timothy 6, beginning from verse 6, “6 But godliness with contentment is great gain. 7 For we brought nothing into the world, and we can take nothing out. 8 If we have food and clothing, we will be content with these.”

We bring nothing into the world, and we’ll bring nothing out of it.

We can buy things and add things to our lives during our short time here but they in and of themselves don’t give us life.

It’s good to have the essentials like food and clothing.

We need them as part of life, but we don’t need much more.

We’re content with these.

Indeed, if in our short time on earth, we have godliness, that is, we have a life that’s been made right with God, and we have contentment, then, we’ve gained greatly in life.

However, on the other hand, we’ll lose out on life if we give up godliness with contentment for the love of money.

Look with me at verse 9-10, “9 But those who want to be rich fall into temptation, a trap, and many foolish and harmful desires, which plunge people into ruin and destruction. 10 For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil, and by craving it, some have wandered away from the faith and pierced themselves with many griefs.”

If your heart’s deepest desire is to be rich, you will fall into a temptation, you will fall into a trap.

People’s lives will be ruined and destroyed.

Why? Because the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil.

A root is the part of the plant that stays below the surface.

We can’t see the roots, but plants, flowers and trees grow from these roots.

That’s what the love of money is like.

The love of money is a root that we can’t see on the surface.

However, we can see the fruit of the love of money.

The fruit that the love of money produces are all kinds of evil.

Here’s the second question for today.

What kinds of evils can the love of money produce in us?

Again, talk with the person next to you.

If you’re at home, text your answer to us via Wechat or Whatsapp.

What kind of evils can the love of money produce in us? You have twenty seconds.

The love of money in us can produce all kinds of evils from us.

And it’s a love that never satisfies nor never it be ever satisfied.

John D. Rockefeller was at one time the world's richest man.

He is often regarded as the richest person in history.

Rockefeller was once asked, "How much money is enough?" He said, "One dollar more."

That sounds familiar, doesn’t it? Just one more dollar, and it will be enough.

For the money lover, it’s never enough; money will never satisfy.

The satisfaction that wealth promises is illusive.

The love of money never satisfies nor will the love of money itself ever be satisfied.

The love of money will one day ultimately destroy anyone who loves it.

Rather than being consumed with the love of money, God tells us to run to Jesus who both fills us and makes us hungry.

Be both filled and hungry

Jesus is the One who satisfies!

Last Monday, my family and I went to Jenny’s sister’s home for a family get-together.

We had a wonderful time!

And the food! There was so much food!

We had sausages, roast beef, lamb cutlets, roast duck; we even had Hong Kong style Tea.

It was delicious and filling.

We were so full that we didn’t have dinner that day.

We were satisfied.

The Bible tells us that God was pleased to have all his fullness live in Jesus (Col 1:19).

If you have Jesus, you have all of God’s fullness living in you.

If you have the fullness of God, then you’re completely filled.

You’ll be more spiritually more satisfied than the epic Easter Monday meal that I had at Jenny’s sister’s!

Those who have Jesus will have godliness, they will be right with God and they will have contentment.

Jesus himself has said,

“Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest.“ (Matthew 11:28)

You will be satisfied because Jesus, and Jesus alone, truly satisfies.

At the same time, even as you’re satisfied, you’ll have a hunger.

Jesus says in Matthew 5:6, “Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they will be filled.”

Those who have been satisfied by Jesus will have the same hunger that Jesus has.

Jesus hungers and thirsts for righteousness; those who belong to him will also have a hunger and a thirst for righteousness.

Look with me at Matthew 6:32-33.

Jesus has just taught his disciples not to worry about what they eat or what they drink or what they wear.

Why not?

‘For the Gentiles eagerly seek all these things, and your heavenly Father knows that you need them. 33 But seek first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness, and all these things will be provided for you.’

There is only one thing that God’s people run after.

God’s people will seek God’s kingdom and his righteousness above everything else.

To seek his kingdom and his righteousness is to want to see God rule in everything.

It’s about wanting to see God’s will done on earth as it is in heaven.

It’s about wanting to see people obey and worship God as their king.

In the New Testament, we see that Jesus has come to be the King of God’s kingdom.

To seek first God’s kingdom and his righteousness is place Jesus above everything.

It’s seeking those who are still lost because Jesus himself has come to seek and to save the lost (Luke 19:10)

Someone had once said that telling the good news about Jesus is like a beggar telling another beggar where to find food.

We’ve been blessed by God; we’ve found satisfaction in Jesus.

And now we want other people to find their satisfaction in Jesus also.

That’s why we do what we do here at St Paul’s.

We spend a lot of our resources on reaching to the children, reaching those living around us, reaching our friends, because we want to tell them where to find food that truly satisfies.

We want them to know Jesus.

And so, we come to Church on Sundays and we come to Growth Groups and Bible studies so that we can be both filled and be more hungry at the same time.

We come to find out what it means to be filled with Christ, to find our true satisfaction in him.

And we do that by hearing from God’s word and learning about who Christ is.

We also come to find out how we can fill our hunger and thirst for righteousness.

We want to see God’s kingdom grow among us here at Church and around us here in Kogarah and beyond.

We have a hunger to grow to be more like Christ.

We have a hunger to love our neighbours and point them to Christ.

Just as evil is the fruit of the love of money, the hunger and thirst for righteousness is the fruit of all those who have the love of Christ.

If we know that Christ loves us and that we love Christ,

If we have our satisfaction in Christ,

Then we will have a hunger for righteousness.

If we don’t have a hunger for righteousness, if we don’t care for our growth or the salvation of others,

then chances are we haven’t been satisfied in Christ, and that we don’t have the love of Christ.

One litmus test to see whether we are filled with Christ and have a hunger for righteousness is the use of our money.

Do we love Christ and give his money (remember it’s his money after all) for the growth of his kingdom?

Or do we hoard his money and spend it on a never-ending cycle of self-gratification?

Do we use his money to help other beggars find bread that truly last?

Friends, our security, both yours and mine, are not in money but in Christ and in Christ alone.

The way we use our money will show whether Jesus is truly our king or not.

So, where does this leave us?

Be filled in Christ, use his money for his glory.

I thank God that a while ago, Tim Fong, one of our Parish Councilors applied for a grant and we got it!

It was a grant to help us with reaching our multi-ethnic community with the news about our Mobile Community Pantry.

As part of the condition of the grant, we have to give an account of how the money was used.

The money’s not to be used for a holiday, or the purchase of a car or even repairs in the Church.

The purpose of the grant is for the Mobile Community Pantry and we’ll have to give an account for it.

God’s given you his money for a reason.

How will we give an account of what he’s given us?

Will we say that we’ve used 99% of it on ourselves and 1% on God?

Will we say that we had to spend all of God’s money on our house and none for him?

Or will we say that we’ve given so that others will come to know Jesus too?

In my family, we have the practice of reviewing our income and giving the appropriate amount for God’s use.

If we have an income, we’ll give part of it to Church.

If our income increased, we’ll increase our giving to Church.

We thank God that though sometimes our budget might be stretched but we have still been able to give regularly for God’s work.

We give because we want to be accountable to God for what he’s given us.

It’s his money; we just want to use it as he would have us.

And so, friends, be filled with Christ and use his money for his glory.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Do I have a love of money?

What financial advice would Jesus give me?

How do I use God’s money to help other beggars find Christ?

Friends, don’t look to money for security and satisfaction.

The love of money will lead you to an abyss that never satisfies and will produce in you all kinds of evil.

Rather, be filled with the love of Christ; find satisfaction in him.

Use his money for this glory.
