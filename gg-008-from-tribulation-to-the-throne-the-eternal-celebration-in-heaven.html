Home Groups (adults)

From Tribulation to the Throne: The Eternal Celebration in Heaven

Revelation 7:9–17 · Sunday 30 August 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

For adult home groups, following the Sunday sermon on Revelation 7:9–17 at the 9:15am service

Big Idea: The tribulation you are in is real, but it is not the end — the throne is. Because the Lamb has already washed you white in his own blood, you are already counted in the crowd no one can number, and God himself will shelter you and wipe away every tear.

Aim: By the end of the evening the group will

1. Name the tribulation you are actually in, and hold it next to the promise that the throne, not the trouble, is the end point.

2. Receive the white robe again as washed in the Lamb’s blood, not kept clean by good behaviour.

3. Take one step across a cultural, generational or class line inside our church family this week.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • Think of the largest gathering you have been part of. What did it feel like to be one face in that crowd: lost, or carried along?
  • When life has been hard, what have you wanted from other Christians — an explanation, a fix, or company?

Read

9 After this I looked, and there was a vast multitude from every nation, tribe, people, and language, which no one could number, standing before the throne and before the Lamb. They were robed in white with palm branches in their hands.

10 And they cried out in a loud voice: Salvation belongs to our God, who is seated on the throne, and to the Lamb!

11 All the angels stood around the throne, and along with the elders and the four living creatures they fell facedown before the throne and worshiped God,

12 saying, Amen! Blessing and glory and wisdom and thanksgiving and honor and power and strength be to our God forever and ever. Amen.

13 Then one of the elders asked me, “Who are these people robed in white, and where did they come from?”

14 I said to him, “Sir, you know.” Then he told me: These are the ones coming out of the great tribulation. They washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb.

15 For this reason they are before the throne of God, and they serve him day and night in his temple. The one seated on the throne will shelter them:

16 They will no longer hunger; they will no longer thirst; the sun will no longer strike them, nor will any scorching heat.

17 For the Lamb who is at the center of the throne will shepherd them; he will guide them to springs of the waters of life, and God will wipe away every tear from their eyes.

Revelation 7:9–17, Christian Standard Bible

Context

John is an old man exiled on Patmos for his testimony to Jesus, while back on the mainland Rome hunts the church. Into that darkness God opens a door in heaven and shows him how the story ends — not a puzzle to decode, but a picture painted to keep frightened Christians walking. Chapter 7 has just sealed 144,000 from the tribes of Israel: complete, but limited. Now John looks again, and the picture bursts its banks.

Discuss

  1. v.9 — Compare this crowd with the 144,000 of verses 1—8. What has changed, and what four categories does John use?
  2. v.9 — They are standing, robed in white, holding palm branches. A sinner could not stand before a holy God. What has already happened to them?
  3. v.10 — What is the difference between saying “I got saved” and “salvation belongs to God”? Which one runs your everyday thinking?
  4. vv.11–12 — The highest created beings fall facedown. What does that expose about the energy we spend making ourselves great — at work, at home, even at church?
  5. vv.13–14a — John answers, “Sir, you know.” What does maturity look like here — and in a group when someone asks what none of us can answer?
  6. v.14b — Blood stains a garment red; it does not bleach it. Where does your cleanness come from — and how does that differ from how you feel on a bad week?
  7. vv.15–16 — List everything God does for his people here. Which do you most need to hear this week, and why?
  8. v.17 — It does not say there will be no tears; it says God will wipe them away. Why does that matter for how we sit with a grieving friend?

Live It Out

Our church rhythm is 3 · 2 · 1 · 0. Put it to work this week:

  • Pray for 3 people in your street, your workplace or your family who are not Christians yet — by name, daily.
  • Connect with 2 of them properly. A meal, a phone call, a real conversation — not a wave across the fence.
  • Invite 1 of them. Our Father’s Day Celebration, “A Father’s Invitation”, is Sunday 6 September. Bring a friend, and bring a father.
  • 0 left unloved. At morning tea on Sunday, find the person who is standing on their own — and go and stand with them.

Challenge for the week

Cross one line this week. The crowd of verse 9 is from every nation, tribe, people and language, and Ephesians 2 says the dividing wall is already down. Pick one person in our church family whose language, culture or generation is not yours, and take one step: sit with them, ask a real question, invite them to your table.

Memory verse

“God will wipe away every tear from their eyes.” — Revelation 7:17

Pray

  • Thank the Lamb that your robe was made white by his blood, not your record, and ask him to take the shame you keep picking up.
  • Pray by name for your three friends who are not Christians yet — that God’s kindness would lead them to repentance, and that you would get one chance to point one of them to Jesus.
  • Bring the tribulation you named tonight — illness, a broken relationship, a crushing job, loneliness — and ask God to hold you until you come out of it.

Leader’s Notes

Adults here carry real weight and are practised at not showing it. Do not rush the honesty, and resist the urge to fix what someone names — the passage does not fix it either, it gives it an end point.

Guests and not-yet-Christians may be present. Point joyfully to Jesus; never corner anyone. Everyone in that crowd stands because of the Lamb, not their record.

Brief answers to the discussion questions

Q1. The 144,000 (12 x 12 x 1,000) pictures completeness: God’s Old Testament people, sealed. Now it bursts — a crowd no one can number, from every nation, tribe, people and language.

Q2. Standing means their sin is dealt with. White is purity, victory and resurrection glory, and verse 14 says they did not bleach the robes themselves. Palms are victory and joy (Leviticus 23:40).

Q3. Salvation belongs to God. The crowd does not say “we won” but “God has won, and we were included.” Most of our anxiety comes from reversing that.

Q4. We work at making ourselves great; heaven’s worship makes God great. Verse 12’s sevenfold praise — seven meaning completeness — runs forever: a state, not a mood.

Q5. Maturity is knowing what you do not know. The proud say “I know”; John says “Lord, you know.”

Q6. “Made them white” means being bleached — done to them. Christ’s death is your acquittal (1 John 1:7), and the cleansing is finished; repentance restores intimacy, it does not re-apply.

Q7. He shelters them with his own tabernacle, ends hunger, thirst and scorching heat (Isaiah 49:10), and lets them serve without burning out.

Q8. It takes grief seriously. God does not delete the tears, he wipes them away himself. So the word to a grieving friend is not “don’t cry” but “God has counted every one.”
