The Gift above every other gift

Passage: Ephesians 1:20-23

God powerfully gave the Risen and Exalted Jesus to the church, the fullness of Jesus

God powerfully to raised and exalted Jesus

God gives the exalted Jesus to the Church

The Church is the fullness of Jesus

I’m very thankful to the many generous people who give to our Church over the years.

A lot of people might think that we get some sort of funding from a big Church headquarters somewhere, but we don’t.

This Church can function, because God has put it in the hearts of his people to give generously to the Church.

That’s why we have the offertory at the end of the service; that’s why some, if not most, of our committed members give electronically.

Yes, we do get some money from rent, but that’s money that want to use to keep the property standing and working.

The offertory we give is for what we’re doing right now; it’s for the Church’s ministries.

And I thank God for those of us who give generously.

While most people give financially, others give various gifts to the Church.

Sometimes, it might give their time, others might give newly bought cleaning equipment.

However, sometimes, we get gifts that are, shall we say, questionable.

Sometimes, the Church receives old furniture from people.

Some are good, but most are in very poor condition; so much so that we generally have to get rid of them at the next garbage collection.

When you look at what some people give to the church, you just have to ask, is that really what they think of the Church?

A place where you dispose of your unwanted furniture, passing them off as ‘gifts’ to the Church?

It’s an unacceptably low view of the Church.

In contrast to these, we’re going to see what God gives to the Church.

We’re going to see the best gift that God could ever give, the Gift that is above every other gift.

What God gives shows the incredibly high view he has of the Church.

So please open your Bible to Ephesians 1:20-23.

We are going to see three things:

God powerfully raised and exalted Jesus

God gives the exalted Jesus to the Church

The Church is the fullness of Jesus

Before we do, let’s pray, “Father, thank you for the grace that you’ve given us in your Son our Lord Jesus Christ. By your Spirit, please open the eyes of our hearts, that we might see glimpses of the riches of your glory, for we pray in Jesus’ name, Amen.”

1. God powerfully to raised and exalted Jesus

Last week, we read in Ephesians 1:18-19 about how the Apostle Paul prays for God to open the eyes of the believers by the Spirit of Wisdom and Revelation,

so that they can see the hope of God’s calling, the wealth of his glorious riches in the saints and the immeasurably great power toward us who believe.

In today’s passage, from verses 20-23, Paul focuses on the last point about the great power God has for us.

Look with me at verses 20-21, “He exercised this power in Christ by raising him from the dead and seating him at his right hand in the heavens  —  far above every ruler and authority, power and dominion, and every title given, not only in this age but also in the one to come.”

What does God’s power look like?

By God’s power, he raised Jesus from the dead and seated him at His Right Hand in the heavens.

By God’s power, God raised and exalted Jesus to a place of incredible honour and power.

To show the power that they had, fathers in the Roman times have a right called the patria potestas

That is, the father has the right to dispose of his child or servant, that is to kill them, for they belonged to him.

It’s the power to take away life, a power that would’ve brought fear to many households, especially if the father was cruel and unloving.

However, the power to take away life is not all that powerful compared with the power that creates and makes life.

As living things, fathers and mothers can pass their life on to their children, but they can’t make life.

We can’t make life out of nothing.

After thousands of years, with all of our scientific breakthroughs, after sending people to the moon, after making atomic bombs that can destroy thousands of lives, we still can’t make one single life.

It takes far more power, more than any power that we possess, to make life.

But that’s what God did.

God created life; he made every single living thing that you see.

Not only does God make life, but he also has the power to give life to what was once dead.

For God raised Jesus from the dead.

What God did in raising Jesus is far more powerful than anything anyone has ever done in history.

God raised Jesus from the dead, never to die again.

And by that same power, God exalted Jesus to his right hand, the place of God’s favour.

Look with me at Psalm 110:1, “This is the declaration of the Lord to my Lord: “Sit at my right hand until I make your enemies your footstool.”

This Psalm describes the place of honour that the Lord God has said to King David’s Lord,

And how David’s Lord will have the victory over his enemies.

It was a prophecy that King David had made, by the leading of the Holy Spirit.

David’s Lord, of course, is Jesus himself.

Jesus himself referred to this in his trial, as recorded in Matthew 26:64.

We’ll start from Matthew 26:63, ‘But Jesus kept silent. The high priest said to him, “I charge you under oath by the living God: Tell us if you are the Messiah, the Son of God.” “You have said it,” Jesus told him. “But I tell you, in the future you will see the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power and coming on the clouds of heaven.”’

After Jesus died on the cross, God raised him from the dead and exalted him the highest place.

As the New Testament Scholar Peter O’Brien has said so clearly, “The Resurrection proclaims "He lives — and that for ever"; the Exaltation proclaims "He reigns — and that for ever'".

Jesus has completed the mission God has sent him to do; it’s done, it’s over.

After his mission, God the Father has given Jesus a seat next to him in heaven.

That’s where Jesus is.

That’s why there’s no tomb where you go to see the remains of Jesus.

For Jesus is living, reigning, at God’s right hand in heaven.

He’s not a memory; he’s not a vague ideal.

Jesus is now in heaven at God’s right hand, far above every ruler and authority, power and dominion.

His name is higher than any name that can be named, both now and in the age to come.

The ruler, authority, power, and dominion are references to angelic forces and demons who rebelled against God.

There are powerful spiritual forces rebelling against God and they instil fear and horror across many cultures.

For some of us in Asian cultures, we might have heard stories from uncles and aunties about seeing dirty things.

They’re not talking about sexually dirty things but spiritually dirty things.

It might be a dark feeling that they feel when they go to certain places.

The Bible speaks of spiritually dirty things.

There are many accounts in the Bible of Jesus casting out demons, who were spiritually dirty or unclean.

These spiritually unclean beings want to make you afraid of them.

But you don’t need to be afraid of them if you are a Christian, for you have Jesus, the one whom God powerfully raised and exalted.

You shouldn’t be afraid of them; they should be afraid of you!

For if you believe in Jesus, then you show them that the Kingdom of God is near, which means that their end is near.

And so, if you are a Christian, do not engage in any dark spiritual activities, like speaking with the dead.

At the same time, do not fear any spiritual forces, powers or dominions, for the Risen and Exalted Jesus, is far greater!

Thank God for his great power!

Thank God for raising and exalting Jesus above all powers and authorities.

In Jesus, you see the immeasurable power of God at work.

2. God gives the exalted Jesus to the Church

God then does something extraordinary, something mind-blowing after he raised and exalted Jesus by his immeasurably great power.

He gives Jesus to the Church!

Look with me at verse 22, “And he subjected everything under his feet and appointed him as head over everything for the church,”

God has put everything under the feet of Jesus.

It’s another reference to the Psalms, this time from Psalm 8:6,

We’ll start from verse 5, “You made him little less than God and crowned him with glory and honor. You made him ruler over the works of your hands; you put everything under his feet”.

The Psalm was originally talking about the place of honour God has given to mankind.

However, the psalm finds its ultimate fulfilment in the Person of Jesus.

God has placed everything under the feet of Jesus.

There is no one, nothing, above Jesus; everything is placed under his feet.

And God gives Jesus as head over everything to the Church.

God gave Jesus to the Church.

Last week, I invited the Young Adults group to my place to have Bible study and then watch the women’s FIFA semi-finals between Australia and England.

On the night, all eyes were on Sam Kerr, the Australian player who’s considered by many as one of the best forwards of all time and one of the greatest female soccer player of her generation.

Her ranking in the game FIFA 23 is No.1

And it’s great that Australia has her on our team!

However, despite scoring an incredible goal, Australia lost to England.

Having Sam Kerr on our team is great, but having Jesus on our team is infinitely better.

God gave His No.1 to the Church.

God gave the Risen and Exalted Jesus, the One who is head over everything and everyone, to the Church!

No one compares with Jesus.

For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son.

Who did he give his Son to?

To the Church!

If you are the Church, God has given Jesus to you.

You might ask, “But there are so many Churches! To which Church did God give Jesus to?”

It’s not a particular church, nor is it a particular denomination.

Rather it’s the Church that’s gathered around Jesus in the heavens.

Look with me at Ephesians 2:6, “He also raised us up with him and seated us with him in the heavens in Christ Jesus,”

Hien will share more about that with us next week.

For now, it’s important to remember that the word ‘Church’ means a gathering.

If you believe in Jesus, then no matter which church you go to, by faith, you are meeting with others who also believe in Jesus in the heavens.

That’s where this Church in Ephesians 1:22 is; it’s in the heavens.

It’s a Church that you come to by faith.

We can’t see it, but we’re there in the church in the heavens with Jesus, along with all the saints around the world, and all the saints of the past, and all the saints in the future.

But what we have here today, at this Church in St Paul’s Kogarah, is a reflection of what’s actually happening in heaven.

Those who are in the heavenly Church will long to be in the local Church, because this is as close to what we have to the Church in heaven.

As the Church, we receive God’s greatest gift; we receive the Lord Jesus.

That’s how God see the Church; he has such a high view of the Church that He gives the Church His Son Jesus.

Jesus is ruler of all, head of all, but he’s especially the ruler, especially the head of the Church.

There is a bond between Jesus and the Church that is far superior to Jesus’ relationship with the rest of the world, to the rest of creation.

For God raised up Christ, exalted Christ, made him as head of all things and gave him to the Church.

How much does God love the Church?

This hymn describes it well:

From heaven he came and sought her, To be his holy bride; With his own blood he bought her, And for her life he died. (主從天上來尋她，作主聖潔新婦， 甘願流自己寶血，捨身將她買贖。)

Can you see how precious the Church is to Jesus?

That’s how much Jesus loves the Church. That’s how much God loves the Church. That’s how we ought to love the Church, if we have known the love that God has for the Church.

The Church is so loved by God, and if we have truly understood this love, it will truly humble us.

Why us? Why is the church so favoured by God?

There’s nothing we can possibly do to deserve Jesus.

There’s nothing we can do to properly repay God for Jesus.

We will turn away from any arrogance, any boasts and instead come to God in a state of humble adoration.

3. The Church is the fullness of Jesus

When the Church receives Jesus, we receive Jesus as our head.

Look with me at verse 23, where the Apostle Paul describes the Church, “Which is his body, the fullness of the one who fills all things in every way.”

The Church is the fullness of Jesus, the One who fills all things in every way.

How is the Church the fullness of Jesus?

To help us think about it, I want to see if you can solve his puzzle:

A man was about to die, so he called his three sons.

He gave them equal money, and asked them to fill up their living room entirely with something.

Whichever son was successful would receive their father’s entire inheritance.

The first son bought sticks, and tried to fill the room entirely before he ran out.

The second son bought straw, but once again, he ran out before entirely filling the entire room.

The third son bought only two things, and was able to successfully fill the entire room and received the inheritance.

What did the third son buy, and how did he fill the room?

No looking at the sermon script!

(Matches and a candle)

A single light can fill a room of darkness.

Likewise, the Church is called to be filled with the light and glory of Christ.

Jesus is the head of the Church, his body.

As his body, we obey him; we go where he leads us.

And as we do, we live out his glory, we reflect his glory.

We show the world how good Jesus is by the way we live and by the words we speak.

We become a place where they see the Gospel and where they hear the Gospel.

We will be a people who talks about Jesus and live according to Jesus’ way all the time.

A while ago, I was walking by the school and some of the After School Care children called out to me.

One of the other children, who don’t come to the centre asked whether I’m the guy who talks about Jesus.

Yeah, that’s me! What a great honour!

And that’s what we’re on about, we’re the Church that talks about Jesus.

As the Church, we shine out the glory of Jesus.

Jesus is the Light of the World.

And as his people, he calls us to be the light of the world.

We are to shine His light to the world.

As the Church, we are to be so filled with faith, hope and love of Jesus that we can’t help but talk about Jesus, we can’t help but to live according to Jesus, who is the Way the Truth and the Life.

The Church is filled with Jesus, it’s the fullness of Jesus.

The Church is in fact a preview of what the new reality will look like in the future.

One day, when Christ returns, he will fill everything in the heaven and earth in every way.

On that day, the glory of Jesus will fill all things in every way.

On that day, everyone will talk about Jesus.

Some will talk about Jesus with great happiness and joy as they’ve always done in the Church, and will look forward to spending eternity with Jesus.

Others, including every dominion and power, every spiritual power, every human that has ever rebelled against Jesus, will do so reluctantly, knowing that they will be sent to eternal judgement.

On that day, which group will you end up in?

For those of us who believe in Jesus, remember that this great power that God used to raise and exalt Jesus, is the same power that is at work in us also.

It’s by that same power that we live, suffer and die as a Christian.

It’s also by that same power that we will rise and be seated next to Christ as Christians.

People will tell you that the Church is dying, a matter of time, on the decline. Rumour of the death of the Church is greatly exaggerated.

Though the Church has been threatened time and time again, from the powers without and from our weaknesses from within, God has kept the Church going, by his immeasurably great power.

After two thousand years of persecution, the Church remains.

The fact that we’re still here today is a testimony of God’s power in us.

And so, don’t be afraid to shine the light of Christ.

The darkness will hate it but it will also bring light to those who want to escape the darkness.

If you don’t shine your light of Christ, the glory of Christ, how will they see Jesus? St Paul’s Kogarah, Church, you are the fullness of Christ, the light of the world.

Be filled with Christ!

What gifts has the Church received?

We’ve received many, but the greatest gift is the One the Church received from God himself.

We have received Jesus Christ, the risen, exalted One whom God has placed as head over all things.

We are the Church, the body of Jesus and the fullness of Jesus.

Be filled to the fullness of Jesus, God’s gift to the Church.

Be filled with Jesus, be full of Jesus, let the words of Jesus live in our hearts and guide us in all that we say and do, so that the world, when they see what we do in Jesus’ name, will give glory to the Father in heaven.

Pray that you will be filled with Jesus.

When you talk with other Christians, encourage them to talk about Jesus.

When you are alone, think about Jesus.

When you’re at Church or other Christian gatherings, sing about Jesus.

Live and breathe the gospel of Jesus Christ!

Be filled to the fullness of Jesus, God’s gift to the Church.

Here are three questions for us to think about this week:

1. How does knowing that Jesus is exalted to God’s place of honour help us think how to think about the powers of the world (both political and spiritual)?

2. How would our view of the Church change once we recognise that God has given Jesus the head over everything to the Church?

3. How will you show the fullness of Christ this week?

St Paul’s, be filled to the fullness of Jesus, God’s gift to the Church.

Let’s pray, “Father, by your Spirit of Wisdom, please open the eyes of our hearts so that we may see the immeasurably great power you have towards us who believe. Open our eyes, that we might see the glory of Jesus Christ. We praise you for raising Jesus from the dead and for exalting him to the highest place. We thank you that you’ve given him to the Church. We pray that your Church here at St Paul’s Kogarah will be a light in Kogarah, shining the light of Jesus. We pray that we will follow Jesus our head and that Jesus will fill us with his glory as we proclaim his name. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
