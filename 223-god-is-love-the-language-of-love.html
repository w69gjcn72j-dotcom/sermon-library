God is love - The Language of Love

Passage: 2 Timothy 3.10-17

Big Idea: We know that in his Beloved Son, God loves us with his faithful love through his letters of love, the Holy Scripture.

God speaks words of faithful love

God loves faithfully through the Word, His Beloved Son

Love God faithfully according to his words of love

What is love? How do you define love?

Last year, a group of psychologists got together at a conference organised by a group called the Love Consortium (it’s a real thing!) to try to work out what love is.

There were experts who said that love is an attitude towards something that you love.

It can be a passionate attraction or a compassionate kindness to the person whom you love.

Other disagree and say that love is not an attitude but a moment.

You love only when there’s a connection between you and someone else.

Of course, the Love Consortium is not the first group of people to think about what love is.

People from all cultures throughout history have asked what love is.

When I was growing up, there was a song by the band Foreigner called, “I want to know what love is. I want you to show me.”

I’m sure you can think of many other songs about love that you know of.

Love is something that we think we know, but it’s something that’s very hard to define.

What is love?

Well, that’s what we’ll be looking at over the next five weeks.

In fact, we’ll be spending extra time thinking about what love is at our Church camp.

If you haven’t booked yet, there is still space.

Don’t miss out on it!

What is love? The Bible has plenty to say about love.

In particular, the Bible has a lot to say about God’s love.

What’s God’s love like? If I love God, what’s my love for God like?

Today, we’re going to see three things about love

God speaks words of faithful love

God loves faithfully through the Word, His Beloved Son

Love God faithfully according to his words of love

Let’s pray before we look at what the Bible says love is.

“Father, you love us with an everlasting love. Please open our hearts to your word, that we may receive your love into our lives, for Jesus’s sake. Amen.”

1. God speaks words of faithful love

“The heavens proclaim his righteousness; all the peoples see his glory.” Psalm 97:7

The God that Christians believe in is infinite.

Whatever our minds can think of, God is bigger than that, for our minds our finite, but God is infinite.

He doesn’t need anything, and he certainly doesn’t need us.

God could’ve happily left us alone on our little planet, but he hasn’t.

God knows us and he wants us to know him.

In fact, that’s one of the reasons he’s made us in His image.

The Bible tells us in its first chapter that we humans, both men and women, were made in God’s image.

As the image of God, God has given us the unique ability to communicate with Him.

No other creatures can relate to and communicate with God like human beings can.

That’s because God is personal by nature and, as images of God, so we, also, are personal by nature.

God is not an inanimate being, a cosmic machine that follows as set of rules.

No, God is a person; he is relational and can have relationships with other persons.

That’s who he made us to be, persons who can communicate with him.

But how do we communicate with God? How do we understand God?

We understand God as he reveals to us, as he unveils himself to us.

Take this photo, for instance.

It’s a photo of Paolo.

If you haven’t met Paolo yet, make sure you say Hi to him after Church.

For those of us who don’t know Paolo yet, what can you tell me about him from this photo?

It’s a photo of him as an alien from Asgard, the home of the fictional hero, Thor.

It’s from Thor Ragnorok. Has anyone seen it? Make sure you look out for Paolo next time.

But what’s the real Paolo like?

Paolo is Italian, makes great Italian food, and has become a Christian two Sundays ago!

Praise God!

You can’t tell much about Paolo just by looking at his photo.

You only know more about Paolo if he shares with you some things about him.

Similarly, we can’t know God unless God reveals himself to us.

We won’t know God unless God tells us about himself.

And that’s what God’s been doing ever since the beginning.

The infinite God, with infinite wisdom and infinite power, chose to communicate with us finite beings using our finite words.

We can’t go up and communicate at God’s level, but God can come down and communicate at ours.

He’s been doing that ever since the time of Adam and Eve.

Over the past few weeks, we’ve seen God speaking to Noah and giving him instructions about the upcoming flood.

God told them his thoughts and God gave them commands.

More than that, God gave them promises.

Here’s one that he gave to Abraham, in Genesis 12:1-3,

“The Lord said to Abram:

Go from your land, your relatives,

and your father’s house to the land that I will show you.

I will make you into a great nation, I will bless you,

I will make your name great, and you will be a blessing.

I will bless those who bless you, I will curse anyone who treats you with contempt, and all the peoples on earth will be blessed through you.”

It was a magnificent promise!

There was no record that Abraham did anything special to deserve God’s promise.

God gave a special promise to Abraham and his descendants.

And God kept his promise to Abraham; Abraham’s descendants grew in number.

Eventually, after four hundred years, they became a nation.

God then spoke to his people through the prophet Moses.

They weren’t naturally smarter or more capable than anyone else.

They were simply people that God wanted to speak with.

After this, God gave them formal commands, and these were written down in the Book of the Law.

Through the Book of the Law, the Israelites could remember what God had already said to them.

For over one thousand years after that, God continued to speak to his people through prophets.

Many of these prophets spoke and sometimes their words were written down and recorded in the Bible.

And so, many of these prophets began their message by saying, “Thus saith the Lord.”

This is what God is saying!

These words of God, these written records of God’s message, were love letters from God to his people.

It’s through these words that God’s people come to know that God loves them.

And throughout the centuries, God’s people have found hope and comfort in God’s love letters to them.

They’ve grown to love God’s words.

Here’s how the Psalmist described God’s word, “Let your faithful love come to me, Lord, your salvation, as you promised.

Then I can answer the one who taunts me, for I trust in your word.” Psalm 119:41-42

As well as enjoying God’s words, God’s people have a responsibility to respond well to God’s word.

Psalm 95:7-8, “For he is our God, and we are the people of his pasture,

the sheep under his care. Today, if you hear his voice: Do not harden your hearts as at Meribah, as on that day at Massah in the wilderness where your ancestors tested me; they tried me, though they had seen what I did.”

When we hear God’s word, we need to allow God’s word to enter our hearts.

These psalms, written as responses to God’s word, became part of God’s word themselves.

They were written as songs of love responding to God’s word of love.

Over time, God’s people recognised that even these responses to God’s word in the psalms were themselves inspired by God.

God teaches us how to respond to his words.

And for thousands of years, God’s people have been singing these hymns, these songs, which points us back to the love of God.

These songs remind us that you can trust God and his love for us.

These songs remind us that God’s faithful love is better than life (Psalm 63:3).

These psalms are our psalms, these songs are our songs, because we too, can say that God’s kindness is better than life.

We say these words, we sing these words because they are true.

The Scripture, the Bible, is full of letters of God’s love that God has sent over thousands of years.

But have we opened these?

2. God loves faithfully despite our faithlessness

God has sent us many letters of his love, but often we don’t open these letters.

We just let them sit on our shelves.

And even if we do open them, we often don’t listen.

It’s a little like this.

Over the past few years, many people have these tiny Bluetooth headphones that they put in their ears.

They’re so small that you often you don’t see them.

And so, sometimes I would try to talk to someone, not realising that they had their headphones in their ears the whole time.

I still remember there was a time when old people put hearing aids in their ears to help them listen to people.

Now, I see young people putting headphones in their ear which stops them from hearing other people!

And that’s what we’re like.

God speaks to us constantly, but we choose to ignore him.

It’s as if we deliberately choose to put our fingers in our ears so that we don’t hear from God.

We’ve been going against God’s word since the beginning.

Adam and Eve deliberately went against God’s command when they ate the forbidden fruit.

The great Abraham himself, very soon after God gave him the great promises, deliberately lied and put his wife in a vulnerable position, in order to save himself.

Israel, as a nation, constantly sinned against God.

Even when God was giving his commands through Moses, they were making an idol and worshipping that thing instead of God.

King David, who was one of the greatest king in Israel’s history, who wrote many of the psalms, sinned against God by committing adultery and murder, even when he was King.

God’s people, in the northern kingdom of Israel and the southern kingdom of Judah, failed God, again and again.

This is how the Bible summarised their downfall, in 2 Kings 17:12-14,

“They served idols, although the Lord had told them, “You must not do this.” Still, the Lord warned Israel and Judah through every prophet and every seer, saying, “Turn from your evil ways and keep my commands and statutes according to the whole law I commanded your ancestors and sent to you through my servants the prophets. But they would not listen.”

They ignored God’s words and so were constantly under God’s judgement.

This is Israel, a tiny nation compared with all the nations around it.

They didn’t have magnificent buildings like those of the ancient Egyptians or the ancient Babylonians or even the ancient Chinese.

Many of their magnificent buildings were destroyed.

As a nation, they’ve been attacked and destroyed many times.

They’ve experienced the horror of genocide more than once.

And yet, they came back each time; they’re still here!

Their very presence is a testimony of God’s faithful love!

God kept his word to Abraham and continued to bless the world through his descendants.

Though people had been unfaithful, God remained faithful.

Even when all hope was lost, even when their cities were destroyed, God remained faithful.

As a people, their greatest gift to the world isn’t a building or a culture, as good as these things may be.

Their greatest gift to the world, to us, is God’s word; their greatest gift to us the Scripture, the Bible.

They kept a record of God’s promises and had shown how God had kept his promises despite their faithlessness.

This treasure is greater than the great palaces and the temples.

Pyramids, and palaces will fade away but God’s word stands forever.

Here is what the theologian Gerald Bray said about the relationship between the Jews and the Bible:

“The truth is that apart from that word, which we now possess in the form of the Bible, Israel would never have survived. The Bible is Israel's only legacy to the rest of the human race and the measure of its greatness.”

But as great as God’s promises to Israel were, God’s focus is not on the nation of Israel itself, but on one particular person from the nation of Israel.

That person is none other than Jesus Christ.

Despite our faithlessness, God sent his Son, his Beloved one (Mark 1:11) to save us.

The Bible tells us that Jesus is the Word of God (John 1:1) and the exact expression of God’s nature (Heb 1:3).

He is the Image of the invisible God Col 1:15.

When we see Him see God; when we hear Jesus we hear God.

God’s focus is on Jesus and he wants us to focus on Jesus too.

Everything in God’s letter of love points to Jesus; everything in the Bible finds their fulfilment in him.

When you see Jesus, you will see God’s love for us.

Do you want to see the proof of God’s love?

Look with me at Romans 5:8, “But God proves his own love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.”

God proved his love to us when Christ died for sinners, when Christ died for us.

Do you want to know what God’s love is?

Look with me at 1 John 4:10

“Love consists in this: not that we loved God, but that he loved us and sent his Son to be the atoning sacrifice for our sins.”

When we want to see what God’s love is like, we must see it through Jesus Christ, the Word of God.

Every letter of God’s love points to Jesus.

Listen to how Jesus himself describes love in John 15:13, “No one has greater love than this: to lay down his life for his friends.”

Jesus has laid down his life for his friends; Jesus gave up his life so that his friends can live.

The love that Christ has for us is a true and faithful love.

Despite how unlovely we are, God still loves us, Christ still died for us.

This love that God has for us is not a fleeting feeling, it’s not a fling.

God loves us with a faithful love.

God keeps loving us despite the hardships, despite the suffering and despite the pain.

God’s love is shown through his faithfulness, as he keeps the promises that he’s made.

God’s love is shown supremely through Jesus.

As the hymn goes, “O Love that will not let me go, I rest my weary soul in thee.”

God’s love won’t let me go, God’s love won’t let you go.

That’s the assurance of those who trusts in God’s Beloved Son, the Lord Jesus.

It’s a comfort in a world full of broken promises.

In a world of faithlessness, God remains faithful to those who are his.

God keeps his promises made through his letters of love.

3. Love God faithfully according to his words of love

How do we respond to God’s faithful love?

We love God faithfully according to his words of love.

Open up with me to 2 Timothy 3:1-2,

“But know this: Hard times will come in the last days. For people will be lovers of self, lovers of money, boastful, proud, demeaning, disobedient to parents, ungrateful, unholy,” and the list goes on.

In the last days, in these days, people will live lives contrary to God’s word.

People will be lovers of themselves rather than lovers of God.

People will look out for their own interests first rather than asking for what pleases God first.

But Timothy knew what it meant to follow Jesus Christ.

He followed Paul as Paul followed Christ.

Timothy has seen firsthand Paul’s faithfulness despite the persecutions and the sufferings.

Paul himself has said, “all who want to live a godly life in Christ Jesus will be persecuted.”

There are no exception.

People will hate you because you love Jesus.

When people persecute us, it should not come as a surprise.

If you follow Jesus, it will happen.

And it will be hard.

How do we keep loving Jesus Christ when things get tough?

Look with me at 2 Timothy 3:14, “But as for you, continue in what you have learned and firmly believed. You know those who taught you.”

Timothy has been taught by Paul, his mother Continue in what you have learnt. Paul, his mother Eunice and his grandmother Lois.

These three would’ve been wonderful examples of faithfulness to young Timothy.

Even more importantly, these three would’ve taught Timothy Scripture.

Verse 15, “and you know that from infancy you have known the sacred Scriptures, which are able to give you wisdom for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus.”

Since Timothy was an infant, he was learning from the Bible.

Both Grandmother Lois and Mother Eunice knew that the most important thing, the most important treasure they can give Timothy is the treasure of God’s word.

There’s nothing more precious they or we can give to our children.

That’s why we make it our priority to teach our children the in KOG kids, KOG youth, KOG worx.

We make it our priority to teach the Bible in our schools, for these are our children’s formative years.

Not only do we teach them through lessons; we teach them through our actions.

We want them to imitate us as we imitate Christ.

And we imitate Christ as we read from the Scripture; we imitate Christ as we read about him in the Bible.

It is the Scripture, the Bible, that makes us wise for salvation in Christ Jesus. It’s through the Scripture that you know that God is love.

It’s through Scripture that you learn how to respond to God’s love.

Jesus himself has said that no one has greater love than this: to lay down his life for his friends.

How do we know we’re Jesus’ friends?

Jesus himself has said, “You are my friends if you do what I command you.”

You know that Jesus sees you as a friend, you know that Jesus died for you, when you do what he commanded.

To obey Jesus is the right way to respond to his love!

We don’t love God in any old way that we want.

Because love is not just a feeling, love is not just knowledge.

To love God is to obey him even when, especially when, things are hard.

To love God is to be faithful to him, to trust him, even though we’re tempted to love ourselves.

That’s love.

We don’t know how to love, but God himself has shown us how to love him.

God has shown us how to love in his letters of love.

Over this coming week, there will a conference called GAFCON IV (four) in in the African country of Rwanda.

Anglican leaders from all over world will meet to talk about what it means to love God and what it means to love one another.

I went to GAFCON III (three) five years ago when it was held in Jerusalem.

This year, GAFCON will have something very important to discuss.

Earlier this year, the Church of England allowed its members to bless something that God condemns.

The ministers there were authorised to bless same sex unions.

They believe that love is what you want it to be.

However, that’s not love.

An older word is a better description, the word lust.

To lust after something is to desire something that’s forbidden.

And so, it’s more proper to say that their ministers are authorised to bless lust, something that God has strictly forbidden.

Not all Anglican Churches in England are like that.

There are quite a few Anglican churches in England, who believes God’s word, who remains faithful, who has decided to stop their partnership with the Church of England.

And it’s not only them.

There are many Anglican Churches throughout the world, including Sydney, who stand by the word of God.

Many of our bishops will be there.

They didn’t come to this decision lightly; there will be a great cost to the faithful Christians.

As we’ve seen over the past decades, faithful Churches have lost Church buildings, they have lost all their money, they’ve lost their homes, they’ve lost their superannuation.

However, they would rather remain faithful to God’s word than to follow the lust of the world.

That’s what genuine love looks like.

It’s loving God according to his letters of love.

Please pray for this conference over the coming week!

And don’t just pray, read God’s word as well.

Treasure God’s word; read God’s word as a letter from someone who loves you.

Read it as the words of someone whom you love.

As you read the Bible, apply it in your life.

Love God faithfully by living according to his words of love.

How do you know God loves you? Read the Scripture, they are his letters of love to you!

How do you know you love God? Read the Scripture, live faithfully according to his letters of love to you.

He says these things to you because he loves you.

If you know that He loves you, you will live according to what He says. Why wouldn’t you?

And so, friends, “Love God faithfully, according to God’s faithful love in Jesus”.

Read about God’s faithful love for you in Jesus as you read the Scripture.

Scripture is profitable for teaching, rebuking, correcting and for training in righteousness.

That is, Scripture is good and profitable to teach us what it means to walk on the right path to God in Jesus.

Scripture is good and profitable to rebuke us when we stray from the right path.

Scripture is good and profitable to correct us to help us get back on the right path.

Scripture is good and profitable to help us stay on the right path.

As you read Scripture this week, as you read your Bible, think about what it’s teaching you, what it’s rebuking in you, what it’s correcting in you and how it is training you.

As you let Scripture work in your life, you will be complete, equip for every good work.

You will live a life of faithfulness that shows what it means to truly love God.

Friends, “Love God faithfully, according to God’s faithful love in Jesus”

Here are the three questions for this week:

1. Do I receive the word of God as letters of love from God?

2. Have I given God heart-felt thanks for his faithful love for me lately?

3. How have I responded to God’s faithful love with obedient and faithful love?

“Love God faithfully, according to God’s faithful love in Jesus”
