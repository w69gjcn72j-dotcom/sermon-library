Boldly enter and gather

Hebrews 10:19-25

Big Idea: Since Jesus is our great high priest, we boldly draw near to God, not giving up on gathering in faith, hope and love.

We have boldness because we have Jesus

We draw near to God together

We don’t give up gathering

When I was in year 10, I had to go to work experience.

I had chosen to work in a music shop and was getting ready for the interview.

I’ve never been to an interview before and so I dressed quite casually for it.

So I just wore my T-shirt and my tracksuit pants (hey, it was the 90’s)

My father, who was about to drive me there, said that I dressed too casually.

It’s a job interview! I had to at least tried to look that I’ve prepared!

And so, I got changed into something more formal for the interview.

At the end, I was got the placement!

When we’re about to meet someone important we’re about to meet, we want to make sure that we’re well prepared.

We dress appropriately and we behave accordingly

What happens when we come to meet God?

How do we prepare to meet God?

What it will be like to meet God?

In today’s passage, we’re going to see what it means to meet God.

We’ll be looking at Hebrews 10, beginning from verse 19 and we’ll see three things:

We have boldness because we have Jesus

We draw near to God together

We don’t give up gathering

Before we look into it, let’s pray.

“Father, please prepare our hearts now as we hear from your words. By your Spirit, draw us near as we come to you. We pray this in Jesus’ name, Amen.”

We have boldness because we have Jesus

Firstly, we have boldness because we have Jesus.

Please open up your Bible with me to Hebrews 10, beginning from verse 19,

(Yi, please type in verse 19)

We can come boldly into the sanctuary, that is, the heavenly sanctuary, where God lives, because of Jesus.

In the Bible, God told the Israelites to worship him in the temple in Jerusalem.

Deep in the temple is the sanctuary, the Most Holy Place, the Holy of holies, which symbolises where God lives.

The High Priest can enter the Most Holy Place once a year to make a sacrifice to God.

Here in Hebrews, the author is telling us that because of Jesus, we, the followers of Jesus, can boldly enter the real sanctuary.

Unlike the High Priests of old, we’re not entering a copy of the Sanctuary;

We’re entering boldly in the real thing, the heavenly sanctuary.

Enter with boldness? Doesn’t that sound a bit odd?

Shouldn’t we enter the heavenly sanctuary with less boldness and more humility?

Why are we told to enter with boldness?

Here, the boldness is not talking about trusting in how good we are.

Rather, like everywhere else in the Bible, the boldness is trusting in what Jesus has achieved.

It’s a bit like this.

I have here the key to the Church.

I use this key to unlock the door this morning to open up the Church.

I can confidently come to the Church door, not because I’m clever at opening doors but because I have the key that works.

If I forget the key, I would be locked outside.

My confidence in opening the church’s door does not depend on how clever I am, but on how reliable the key is.

If the key works, it will open the door.

In a similar way, our confidence to enter into heaven doesn’t depend on how clever we are, or how good we are.

Rather, it depends on how reliable the blood of Jesus is.

The Bible tells us that Jesus’ death, as symbolised by his blood, is the new and living way by which we enter into the very presence of God.

Jesus’ blood is the key to enter into heaven!

It’s by the death of Jesus that we can come before the throne room of God!

Now, we need to be careful here to not confuse humility with doubt, nor should we confuse boldness with pride.

A false humility is one which thinks that we should doubt our standing before God, even if we do trust in Jesus.

Someone who has false humility will say, “Yes, I believe in Jesus but I don’t know if I’m good enough to enter heaven.”

It’s a false humility because it’s a belief that still says that entry into heaven depends on how good you are.

That’s a false humility.

No, true humility is one which depends absolutely on how good Jesus is.

It says, “Yes, I believe in Jesus and because of his sacrificial death, I am absolutely certain that I can enter heaven, that I can boldly come before God.”

There’s no place for pride here.

The humble person takes no pride in what he’s done.

Rather, he trusts completely in what Jesus has done.

Because of what Jesus has done, because of Jesus’ sacrifice, he can boldly, with great confidence, enter into the very presence of God.

More than that, he has confidence to enter into God’s presence because Jesus is not only the sacrifice but the priest who offers the sacrifice.

Look with me at verse 21. (Yi, please type in verse 21)

Jesus is the Great High Priest who has offered himself as the sacrifice.

Jesus is the Great High Priest over the house of God.

He’s the one who will lead us and take us into God’s presence.

In fact, Jesus is the true Worship Leader in God’s House.

In our Church, Jesus is the true Worship Leader.

The Song leader is wonderful, the service leader is great, but they are not the Worship Leader.

The true Worship Leader, the Great High Priest over God’s house is none other than Jesus Christ.

It’s Jesus who leads us in worship; it’s Jesus who leads us into God’s presence.

Since Jesus is our sacrifice and since Jesus is our Great High Priest, our Worship Leader,

We have boldness to enter into the very presence of God.

A good friend of mine who has this boldness is Greg.

Greg is a keen walker.

He loves to do bushwalks and often takes walks in suburbs and cities all around Sydney.

But even more than walking, Greg loves to talk about Jesus.

Greg would walk around places like Parramatta or the north shores to talk about Jesus.

He came to Kogarah a few nights ago for a meeting with me but came two hours earlier so that he can walk around Kogarah and talked to people about Jesus.

He’s bold not because he’s necessarily an outgoing person but because he has confidence in the blood of Jesus.

Friends, be bold because of Jesus.

Be bold to come into God’s presence because of Jesus.

If you’re not a Christian yet and you’re not sure whether you will enter into God’s presence, please come and either see or message me afterwards and I’d love to speak with you.

My prayer is that all of us, whether we’re here or online, will have the boldness to come to God, a boldness that comes from having Jesus.

We draw near to God together

As we come before God, we do so together.

Look with me at verse 22, (Yi, please type in verse 22)

We draw near to God together.

The Bible’s command is to “let us” (plural) draw near.

In these verses, the Bible tells us to do three things as we come to God.

We are to come to God, assured in faith.

We are to come to God, holding to hope.

We are to come to God, provoking love.

And we’re to come to God, together.

Living in a place like Sydney, we’re used to being individualistic.

We don’t want to share with others; we want to win the rat race.

Earlier this week, I read about a race for some mentally handicapped boys. One boy, Andrew, was much faster than the others.

He was metres ahead of the others and almost at the finish line, when he saw his friend fell.

Even though everyone was yelling for Andrew to keep running, he stopped, went back, and helped his friend get up.

Together they finished in last place.

Unlike many of us, he would rather end up last with a friend than to leave his friend behind.

Sometimes, our handicapped friends can teach us more about humanity than many of our able-bodied friends.

And not just our able-bodied friends, but also some of our able-bodied Christians too.

As we’ve seen over the past few weeks in our series in 1 Corinthians,

some Christians who should’ve known better can be individualistic as well.

Rather than loving and caring others, they only think about what benefits them.

This extends to their relationship with God as well.

As long as they’re right with God, as long as they are going to heaven, then that’s fine.

But that’s not fine.

The Bible tells us time and time again that together, we are a people of God.

God wants us to come before him, united together, as one people of God.

In light of what Jesus has done for us, God wants us to come before him, assured in faith.

Look with me at verse 22 ((Yi, please type in verse 22)

We are to come to God in full assurance of faith.

We can be absolutely sure that as the people of God, we will meet God together.

How can we be sure?

Because we’ve been made clean from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.

Jesus has cleansed us and so qualified us to enter the most holy place, into the very presence of God.

We can now have an unhindered, unblemished relationship with God in Jesus

When we believed what Jesus has done for us, God has given us a new heart, a clean heart.

We used to have a sinful, wicked, divided heart but when we believe in Jesus, he gives us an clean, loyal, undivided heart.

In Jesus, God’s given us a true heart so that we can have full assurance of faith.

We come before God in assured faith.

We also come before God, holding to hope.

Look with me at verse 23 (Yi, please type in verse 23)

We hold on to the hope that we have, without wavering.

God is faithful!

God’s said that he’s forgiven us in Jesus.

God’s said that he’ll give us a new heart.

God’s said it; it’s done!

He can be trusted.

When all hope seems lost, hold on to the hope that is yours in Jesus Christ.

You can trust him.

We come before God in assured faith, holding to hope and provoking love.

Look with me at verse 24 (Yi, please type in verse 24)

God wants us to watch out for one another and to provoke one another to love and good works.

We need to stir up people to do good works.

We don’t want people just to sit back; we want them to take action to love other people.

We’ll provoke them lovingly to love other people.

And it won’t always be comfortable.

In fact, it’s almost certainly going to be a little awkward, even a little confronting.

That’s because most of us don’t like people telling us to change.

We think that we’re pretty good where we are.

But God doesn’t want us to stay where we are.

He wants us to increase our love for one another; he wants us to do more good deeds.

Yes, you might be loving, and you might already be doing good deeds.

How can you be better? How can you be more loving?

When we come to Church, it should be second nature for us to provoke one another, lovingly, to love and good deeds.

What does this look like in our Church?

A great place for us to provoke one another to love and good deeds is in Growth Groups (also known as Bible studies)

In Growth Groups, we gather to study God’s word and to encourage one another to be love more and to do more good deeds.

While we can do that in the service, many of us find it easier to share and talk in small groups.

In small groups, we can draw near to God by faith together in prayer,

we can encourage one another to use their gifts from God for God’s purposes,

And we remind each other to tightly hold on to the hope that we have in Christ.

Joining Growth groups is a wonderful way to help us grow together in Christ.

If you’re not in one regularly, then can I encourage you to join one.

We want you and your group to come before God, assured in faith, holding on to hope and provoking one another to love.

Can you think of someone in your life who loved you enough to share his or her concern for you in Jesus?

Someone who’s not just interested in his or her own life but loved you enough to spend time with you to encourage you and to provoke you to love more?

Who has loved you enough to provoke you to love?

Think of someone and perhaps send me a short message of how that person has provoked you to love Jesus and his people more.

You can message me via whatsapp, wechat or Zoom.

You have twenty seconds.

Someone has loved you enough to provoke you to love more.

Be that person to someone else.

Show active love and encourage them to hold on to the hope we have in Jesus.

Draw near in prayer to God with them and for them.

As God’s people, we have confidence because we have Christ.

We draw near to God together in faith, hope and love.

And we don’t give up fathering.

We don’t give up gathering

We do not give up gathering.

That’s probably a controversial thing to say in a world like ours.

There are times when one case comes to our city and suddenly the whole city is told to not gather in large numbers.

And of course, it’s an extraordinary time and we should listen to our leaders.

But here, the Bible tells us about the danger of not meeting together.

Look with me at verse 25, (Yi, please type in verse 25)

It’s harmful to stop meeting together.

It’s a bad habit.

One of the most annoying habits we see on our roads is when cars change lanes without signalling.

When I drive and I see someone change lanes without signalling, I say “No signal pal”

Not only is it annoying habit; it can be dangerous as well.

If you don’t signal, the risk of you crashing into another car is a lot higher.

Not signalling in a car is a bad habit.

Not gathering together as God’s people is also a bad habit to get into.

When you don’t gather, you stop drawing near to God with other people in faith.

You stop confessing your hope in Jesus together.

You stop provoking one another to love and good deeds.

Of course, I know that in COVID situations, there will be times when we can’t gather by law.

There will be times when our Doctors will advise not to gather.

During those times, we must work extra hard to love one another,

But whenever you get the opportunity to gather, make the most of it!

When we don’t make the most of the opportunity to gather, the risk of crashing our faith is a lot higher.

The word that’s translated “neglecting” is a very strong word in the original language.

It carries the idea of abandoning or forsaking.

It was the word used by Jesus when he was on the cross, “My God, My God, why have you forsaken me?”

The Bible here tells us not to forsake, not to give up, on gathering together.

Don’t give up one coming to meet with God with the rest of God’s people.

Like most bad habits, you don’t see its effects at first.

But over time, you will see the effect of the bad habit on the person.

Don’t fall into the bad habit of negligence.

Don’t neglect to gather together.

Instead, keep encouraging one another, and all the more as you see the Day approaching.

The Day is the Judgement Day.

It’s a Day when Jesus will come again to judge the living and the dead.

On that Day, he’ll take all those who have trusted him, who had hoped in him to be with him forever.

On that Day, there’ll also be those who’ll be rejected by him because they have rejected him in their life.

These are the ones who had fallen away, some of whom would’ve giving in to the bad habit of not gathering.

Don’t give in to this bad habit.

Gather with God’s people.

Gather as we come before God in faith, hope and love.

When I was in my previous church in St John’s Cathedral, Parramatta, we had a radio ministry.

A radio station would broadcast our service to thousands of people around Sydney.

One of our listener was from the southern part of Sydney, about one and half hours drive away from Parramatta.

He was too old and physically not able to come but would listen to us every Sunday on the radio.

However, every year, on the Sunday closest to his birthday, he would make the journey to come to St John’s.

That was his Birthday wish.

He wanted to come to Church for his birthday.

To be able to draw near to God and meet with God’s people was his birthday present!

If he could, he would come every Sunday!

He would love to be able to do what you’re doing now!

Friends, I’m very thankful for all those who have made an effort to come and gather every Sunday.

I’m especially thankful for all those who put in extra effort, despite the the added difficulties that COVID brings.

And I’m also thankful for those who though couldn’t make it physically on Sundays, are still doing the best they could by joining us online.

For those of us who may be tempted to fall into the bad habit of neglect to meet up, please resist it.

Do your uttermost to meet up as regularly as you can.

If you’ve been coming to Church regularly twice a month, increase it to three times a month.

If you’ve been coming to Growth Groups once a month, increase it to twice a month.

And if you do come regularly, take the challenge to be here early.

As I heard someone said recently, you don’t ever come on time; you’re either early or late.

Give up the habit of being late.

Be like the old radio listener; see Church like a birthday present as we enter boldly into God’s presence by the blood of Jesus,

And as we gather as his people.

Friends, since Jesus is our great high priest, we boldly draw near to God, not giving up on gathering in faith, hope and love.

And so, what does this mean for us?

As those who trust in Jesus, we boldly enter into God’s presence, as we faithfully gather with God’s family.

When you come to Church, come with an expectation that you’re about to meet with God.

That’s what we’ve come to do, every Sunday.

When we come together as God’s people, under the leadership of Christ, we get a taste of what heaven is like.

This is a glimpse of what meeting God is like.

Of course, it’s only a glimpse.

What you see here at Church is like the trailer of a movie.

The trailer is glimpses of the movie that’s released before the actual movie is released.

In the trailer, you see parts of the action, parts of the story, but not quite the full movie.

If you want to see the full movie, you have to wait until the movie comes out.

Our Church is not perfect but it is a glimpse of heaven.

Though far from perfect, you’ll see evidence of God’s grace, God’s faithfulness, God’s love in the lives of the members.

One day, you’ll see the church in all its beauty on the day Jesus comes back.

Until then, come to Church, don’t fall into the bad habit of not gathering.

Instead, boldly enter into God’s presence, as we faithfully gather with God’s family.

Here are the three questions for this week.

Today, we have heard that as God’s people, we’re to gather in love, faith and hope.

And so, imagine Jesus asking you these three questions”

Do you trust me? Faith

Will you wait for me? Hope

Do you love me? Love

May we be a Church that trusts Jesus, hopes in Jesus and love Jesus as we boldly enter into God’s presence, faithfully gathering with God’s family.

Let’s pray,

“Father, thank you for the love you’ve shown us in the Lord Jesus. Please forgive us when we’ve doubted you and had given up on gathering together. By your Spirit, revive us, make us see with delight the joy that is ours in Christ. When we gather as the Church, please open our eyes that we might see the Church as you see her, the beautiful bride of Christ. Please give us the assurance of faith, please give us an unwavering hope, please give us a love that provokes even more love and good deeds. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
