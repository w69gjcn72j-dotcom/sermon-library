How do you respond to sirens?

In my children’s old school, they have different sirens for different situations.

For example, when a siren goes like this………., it means there’s a fire and everyone has to go to the designated area.

If the siren goes like this…….it means there’s a lockdown and everyone has to stay inside.

It’s vital that everyone gives the right response!

If you hear the siren for fire but think that it’s a lockdown, what happens? You’ll leave the safety of your classroom and head into danger.

If you hear the siren for lockdown and thought that it was fire, what happens? You’ll be waiting for danger to come to you, in the form of fire.

It’s vital to respond appropriately to the sirens.

Later on this year, we will be having a drill (a practice) of how what we’ll need to do if an emergency happens at Church.

We have the practices so that when we hear the signal, we’ll know how to respond.

In the Bible, the resurrection of Jesus is like a siren.

It signals that a new chapter in God’s timing for the world has begun.

How should we respond to it?

What’s an appropriate response to the siren of resurrection?

Please turn to the notes section of your booklet and if you do take notes, here are the three headings for how we ought to respond to the siren of resurrection:

A confused response to the risen Christ

The risen Christ responds and commands

Our mission to obey the risen Christ

Before we begin, let’s pray, ‘Father, may the words of our mouths and the meditations of our hearts be acceptable in your sight, our Lord, our Redeemer. Amen.’

a) A confused response to the resurrection

Look with me at verse 16, ‘The eleven disciples traveled to Galilee, to the mountain where Jesus had directed them.’

They went back to Galilee, where Jesus’ ministry began.

This was the place where Jesus began to disciple them.

When they got to Galilee, they saw the risen Jesus.

Look at their response, ‘17 When they saw him, they worshiped, but some doubted.”

The “they” in this verse refers to the brothers that Jesus referred to back in verse 10.

When they see Jesus, they finally understand that Jesus is who he said he is.

He is the Christ, the Son of God.

He is the One who rose from the grave.

For the first time in the Gospel of Matthew, the disciples bows down and worship Jesus.

They give him the honour that is due to him.

They worship him as they would God.

But what does it mean that some of them doubted?

It probably refers to a group of brothers, who were also with those who worshipped Jesus but who still weren’t sure about who Jesus was.

The word doubt here doesn’t mean that they didn’t believe in Jesus.

The word means that they were uncertain and that they had a sense of hesitation.

After all, this is a new experience for them.

They have never seen anything like this.

Sure, they’ve seen Jesus healed people, they’ve seen Jesus raised people from the dead.

But Jesus’ resurrection takes it to a whole new level.

The people who were healed by Jesus, even raised by Jesus, lived a normal life and would eventually die again.

But Jesus’ resurrection is the destruction of death.

Jesus’ rose to never die again.

This has never happened before.

Until Jesus, no one has risen from the dead who would never die again.

And so, it’s very understandable that some of them doubted.

In fact, I think that the Bible here is very helpful to show us that some people had doubted.

The Bible shows us the reality of how people respond to the resurrection of Jesus.

Because it’s not just the people in Jesus’ days who had difficulties believing the resurrection;

People throughout the centuries, including many people today, still have a lot of trouble with the resurrection of Jesus.

It’s as little learning to ski for the first time.

The first time I learnt to ski was when I was in year 6.

Back then, we didn’t ski in the snow; we skied on a grassy plain, in a sports camp in Narabeen, on the northern side of Sydney.

I was told that to turn to the left, I needed to drop your right shoulder toward the tip of my right ski, while increasing the pressure of my right ski.

If I do that, on my right, my skis will turn to the left.

It’s counter-intuitive; it doesn’t seem to make sense at first.

But if you have a go and put pressure on your right ski, your skis will turn left.

How about that?

Even so, I had only very limited success with skiing over the past thirty years.

If you want more help with skiing, talk to my children!

That’s a little like what the resurrection of Jesus is like.

When you first hear it, it doesn’t make sense straight away.

The resurrection of Jesus is a new experience unlike anything the universe has ever seen.

And so, it makes sense for the followers of Jesus to be a little uncertain, even a little confused on what they need to do about it.

What do we do when we have doubts about the resurrection? Or doubt about the Bible in general?

When you have doubt about Christianity, don’t take it immediately as the opposite of faith.

The opposite of faith is not doubt but deliberate and intentional unbelief.

The Christian pastor Tim Keller writes:

“A faith without some doubts is like a human body without any antibodies in it. People who blithely go through life too busy or indifferent to ask hard questions about why they believe as they do will find themselves defenceless against either the experience of tragedy or the probing questions of a smart sceptic. A person’s faith can collapse almost overnight if she has failed over the years to listen patiently to her own doubts, which should only be discarded after long reflection.”

When you have doubts, don’t panic but also, don’t brush it aside.

Be honest with God!

Come to Jesus and come to the Bible and spend time searching for the answers.

When God’s people have doubts, God doesn’t just tell them that they’re wrong.

Rather, God lovingly shows them his evidence and reassures them.

As some of the disciples worship Jesus while others stand a little confused before Jesus, Jesus, the risen Christ responds with reassurance and a command.

b) The risen Christ reassures and commands

Look with me at verse 18,

‘Jesus came near and said to them, “All authority has been given to me in heaven and on earth.’

Firstly, Jesus gives them a reassuring response.

Jesus tells the disciples that he is now the Lord of Heaven and Earth.

All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to him.

He is not only the King of the Jews; he is the King of everything and everyone.

As the King of everything, as the King of Heaven and Earth, he gives his disciple a command.

Look with me at verse 19-20, “Go, therefore, and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe everything I have commanded you.”

The main command that Jesus is giving there is to make disciples.

This is the central idea of the command.

Everything else that Jesus tells the disciples to do revolve around this one command.

Make disciples!

That’s Jesus mission for the disciples: make disciples.

They’re to be disciple-making disciples.

The disciples of Jesus are to make more disciples of Jesus.

Now, some people think that it takes a special Christian to be a disciple of Jesus.

They think that while most of us can be Christians, only the more holy ones, only a selected few can be disciples.

But that’s not what Jesus is saying.

Jesus is not telling his disciples to make two classes of Christians, the general Christian type and the elite disciple type.

Jesus is commanding his disciples, every one of them, to make more disciples.

To be a disciple of Jesus is not to be a special, selected class of Christians.

All Christians are called to be disciples; all Christians are called to be followers of Jesus.

All Christians are called to deny themselves, take up their cross and follow Jesus.

If you’re not willing to be a disciple, you’re not willing to follow Jesus.

If you want to follow Jesus, then you’re committing yourself to be a disciple of Jesus.

What does it mean to be a disciple of Jesus?

It means following and obeying Jesus and here, Jesus is commanding his disciples to make disciples.

To be a disciple of Jesus is to make disciples of Jesus.

How do we make disciples?

Jesus tells us to make disciples of all nations.

We’re to tell people from different countries and different cultures the good news about Jesus.

We are to bring the good news of Jesus to the old and to the young.

We are to bring the good news of Jesus to our friends, to people like us and to people who are unlike us, even to the people who hate us.

That’s who the Good News of Jesus is for.

It’s for people of all nations.

That’s the target group for the disciples: they are to make disciples of all nations.

But how do we do it?

Jesus tells us that to make disciples of all nations, we need to do three things.

Firstly, we are to go.

We need to face outward.

We are to go to them.

We are not to sit and wait for them to come to us.

We are to go to them.

That’s why last year, we’ve reached an additional sixty-six families on weekdays on top of the families who are coming here on Sundays.

We go out to those who are in schools, in nursing homes, in our Children Centre to tell them about Jesus.

We go out and make disciples

Secondly, we are to baptize.

We need to face upward to God.

We want people who have accepted what Jesus had done for them to be baptized in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Spirit.

We want them to commit themselves to the God of the Bible.

That’s what we did last week, when six people from both the 9:30am Chinese and the 11:15am Generations service were baptized at Doll’s Point.

It was a wonderful celebration of what God has done in the lives of these six brothers and sisters in Christ!

We face upward and we make disciples baptising them in the name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit.

Thirdly, we teach them to obey everything that Jesus has commanded.

We face inward and we help each other to obey everything Jesus has commanded.

In our Church, we teach them through preaching, Growth Groups, KOG Youth, KOG Junior, KOG Infants and special classes like Christianity Explored and Baptism classes.

We don’t just want people to stop at conversion.

We want everyone who come to grow in their knowledge of Jesus and to grow to be more like Him, day by day.

And as we do all these things, making disciples of all nations, facing outward to the world, facing upward as we baptise them in the name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, facing inward as we teach them everything Jesus had commanded,

We look upward again at Christ, who gives us a reassuring promise, “I am with you always, to the end of the age.”

That’s the assurance that the disciples of Jesus can have, Jesus is with us always.

It’s an impossible task to make anyone into a disciple of Christ, if Jesus Christ is not with us.

It’s impossible to tell people to follow Christ, if our eyes are not centred on Jesus Christ.

And so as we face outward, as we face inward, we must always face upward, to ward Jesus Christ.

What does this mean for us here at St Paul’s?

c) Our mission to obey the Risen Christ

At St Paul’s, we recognise that our mission is to obey the risen Christ.

That’s what St Paul’s is for.

Our Church, St Paul’s Kogarah, like every other genuine Church, is to be a gathering where we make disciples of Jesus Christ.

That’s what every Christian believer in the Lord Jesus Christ is called to do.

And as a Church that follows Jesus Christ, that’s what we’re called to do.

That’s our mission.

We’re to make disciples of all nations and we’ll do that as we’ll face outward and go to the world, we’ll face inward as we seek to grow in Christ and we’ll face upward as we look towards Christ.

And this year, I am happy to announce that our Church will be participating in the John 3:16 mission.

You might have seen the banner in front of our Church ‘What is 3:16’

As you drive past the 41 churches in the Georges River region, from Kogarah all the way to Liverpool, you’ll see many Anglican Churches with the same banner.

Our aim is to reach the 1.2 million people in our area.

Our Bishop, the Bishop Peter Lin, has put down this mission for every Anglican Christian in the region.

The mission is twofold:

Firstly, for every parishioner in the region to memorise John 3:16

Let’s say this verse together:

“For God loved the world in this way: He gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who believes in him will not perish but have eternal life.”

Do you think you can memorise this verse this year?

I know you can!

Secondly, for every parishioner to pray for the opportunity to share it at least once in 2020.

To help us with that, I’ve already printed this verse on our Gospel cards.

If you already have a gospel card, you can share it with others.

Later this year, we’ll have training to help us share this verse with others.

Wouldn’t it be great if everyone in our region can memorise John 3:16 and share it with others?

We want to face outward and take the Gospel out!

To help us with our mission to reach our region, we’ll have Moore College Students coming at the end of March to reach our community.

Towards the end of April, we’ll be working in partnership with the Anglican Church at Brighton-Le-Sands to start a new Kids’ Club in the holidays.

I’ve spoken with the Rector there the Rev Steve Bryan over the past few months and we’re very excited about what’s in store!

Over the next few weeks, we’ll be working on our website to improve our engagement with the wider world.

Did you know that over the past 28 days, our Church had over 18,000 hits on the internet?

Over the past month, many people had gone through the digital front door of our Church.

We want to make sure that it’s welcoming so that they’ll want to turn up in person one day!

As well as facing outward, we need to face inward.

My hope is that all of us will be able to grow in the way we read the Bible both here at Church and at home.

My aim for this year is to finish reading the Bible in Chinese.

I’ve started last year and I should be able to finish it later on this year.

How about you?

What’s your goal for Bible reading this year?

As well as Bible reading, I hope that we can help each other to grow in our obedience to Jesus.

That’s why I hope that as many people as possible can join at least one Growth group / Bible study this year.

As far as I can tell, there are over 80% of the people in our Church who are either going to a Growth Group or a Bible study regularly.

That’s great but I hope that this will increase over the year as I think being in a Growth Group is a great way for us to help each other be accountable to grow in Jesus.

As well as outward and inward, we must always face upward.

We need to always lift our eyes to Jesus, knowing that as we make disciples in his name, he is with us always.

Here are our three questions for this week:

Whose salvation am I praying for now?

In what area do I need to obey Christ more this year?

Who can help me to obey Christ more this year?
