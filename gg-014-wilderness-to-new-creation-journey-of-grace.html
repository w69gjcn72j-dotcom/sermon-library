Home Groups (adults)

Wilderness to New Creation: Journey of Grace

Luke 3:1–38 · Sunday 11 October 2026 (157th Anniversary Sunday) · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

For adult home groups and midweek fellowships · Sermon by Rev Jerry Zhu

Big Idea: God’s word still comes to people who will listen in the wilderness; it turns us round to bear real fruit, and it grafts flawed people like us into Jesus’ genealogy of grace — the new humanity of the Father’s beloved Son.

Aim: by the end of the study, group members will

see that God’s word comes to people willing to listen, and plan “wilderness time”;

name one concrete “fruit of repentance” for work, home or street;

rest their identity in Christ, not in heritage or performance.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

1. Connect (10 min)

  • St Paul’s has just turned 157. What’s one thing you’re grateful to have inherited from family or church — and one thing you’d rather not pass on?
  • Where does the “noise” in your week come from — emails, news, the family group chat? When did you last have real quiet?

2. Read: Luke 3:1–38 (CSB)

1 In the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar, while Pontius Pilate was governor of Judea, Herod was tetrarch of Galilee, his brother Philip tetrarch of the region of Iturea and Trachonitis, and Lysanias tetrarch of Abilene, 2 during the high priesthood of Annas and Caiaphas, God’s word came to John the son of Zechariah in the wilderness. 3 He went into all the vicinity of the Jordan, proclaiming a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins, 4 as it is written in the book of the words of the prophet Isaiah:

A voice of one crying out in the wilderness: Prepare the way for the Lord; make his paths straight! 5 Every valley will be filled, and every mountain and hill will be made low; the crooked will become straight, the rough ways smooth, 6 and everyone will see the salvation of God.

7 He then said to the crowds who came out to be baptized by him, “Brood of vipers! Who warned you to flee from the coming wrath? 8 Therefore produce fruit consistent with repentance. And don’t start saying to yourselves, ‘We have Abraham as our father,’ for I tell you that God is able to raise up children for Abraham from these stones. 9 The ax is already at the root of the trees. Therefore, every tree that doesn’t produce good fruit will be cut down and thrown into the fire.”

10 “What then should we do?” the crowds were asking him. 11 He replied to them, “The one who has two shirts must share with someone who has none, and the one who has food must do the same.” 12 Tax collectors also came to be baptized, and they asked him, “Teacher, what should we do?” 13 He told them, “Don’t collect any more than what you have been authorized.” 14 Some soldiers also questioned him, “What should we do?” He said to them, “Don’t take money from anyone by force or false accusation, and be satisfied with your wages.”

15 Now the people were waiting expectantly, and all of them were questioning in their hearts whether John might be the Messiah. 16 John answered them all, “I baptize you with water, but one who is more powerful than I am is coming. I am not worthy to untie the strap of his sandals. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire. 17 His winnowing shovel is in his hand to clear his threshing floor and gather the wheat into his barn, but the chaff he will burn with fire that never goes out.” 18 Then, along with many other exhortations, he proclaimed good news to the people. 19 But when John rebuked Herod the tetrarch because of Herodias, his brother’s wife, and all the evil things he had done, 20 Herod added this to everything else — he locked up John in prison.

21 When all the people were baptized, Jesus also was baptized. As he was praying, heaven opened, 22 and the Holy Spirit descended on him in a physical appearance like a dove. And a voice came from heaven: “You are my beloved Son; with you I am well-pleased.”

23 As he began his ministry, Jesus was about thirty years old and was thought to be the son of Joseph, son of Heli ... [vv.24–37: through David, Abraham and Noah] ... 38 son of Enos, son of Seth, son of Adam, son of God.

3. Context

Luke dates John’s ministry (around AD 28–29) by naming seven men who held all the political and religious power of the day — then says God’s word came to John in the wilderness, the forerunner promised in Isaiah 40. Luke then traces Jesus’ family line not just to Abraham (as Matthew does) but all the way back to Adam, “son of God”: Jesus is the Saviour of all humanity, the start of a new creation.

4. Discuss

  1. (vv.1–2) Luke lists seven powerful men, then says “God’s word came to John ... in the wilderness.” What is Luke telling us about where God’s history is really written?
  2. (vv.3–6) Repentance is a change of mind and direction, not just an apology or guilty feeling. How do Isaiah’s pictures help us see that?
  3. (vv.7–9) Why was “We have Abraham as our father” so dangerous? What are our versions — “I grew up in church”, “my family has always been Anglican”, “I’ve been at St Paul’s for thirty years”?
  4. (vv.10–14) John’s fruit is ordinary: shirts, food, honest invoices, fair pay. What would it look like at your work, in your budget or with neighbours? Where do we rename sin — “I’m just being honest”, “righteous anger”, “sharing a prayer point”?
  5. (vv.15–18) How does John point away from himself to Jesus? Why is that “good news” (v.18)?
  6. (vv.21–22) Jesus had no sin, yet he lined up with sinners to be baptised (compare Isaiah 53:12). What does that say to “experienced believers” who quietly feel they don’t need to repent?
  7. (v.22) Heaven opened “as he was praying.” In Christ the Father says “beloved” to us too. How might that change how you handle pressure to perform — at work, as a parent, or serving at church?
  8. (vv.23–38) Why does Luke trace Jesus to Adam? The line includes Jacob, Judah and David — hardly heroes. What does a “genealogy of grace” say about our 157 years, and what will we hand on?

5. Live It Out

KOG 3·2·1·0 — pray for 3, connect with 2, invite 1, 0 left unloved.

  • Pray for 3 — three people at work, in your street or in your extended family who don’t yet know Jesus. Pray by name daily.
  • Connect with 2 — a coffee, meal or walk with two of them this week.
  • Invite 1 — invite one of your three to the Christmas concert on 13 December (we are praying for 120+ people), to church or to the Discover Course.
  • 0 left unloved — at morning tea, find the person on the edge: new, alone or drifting.

This week’s challenge: 15 minutes of “wilderness time” daily: phone in another room, read Luke 3–4 and pray. Then do one concrete fruit from vv.10–14.

Memory verse: “You are my beloved Son; with you I am well-pleased.” (Luke 3:22)

6. Pray

  • Thank God for 157 years of faithfulness through flawed people. Confess where inherited religion has replaced living faith.
  • Ask for quiet, listening hearts and courage to bear real fruit.
  • Pray by name for your 3 — that they would hear the Father’s welcome in Jesus.

7. Leader’s Notes

Framing. Joyfully point to Jesus. Assume some in the room aren’t yet Christians, or are unsure. God’s word comes to ordinary people who listen, and Jesus stands with sinners — good news for them. Guests may be present; never corner anyone.

Q1–2 — God’s history is not written by those on thrones but by those who hear his word in the wilderness. The wilderness is a place set apart from the clamour of the world. Repentance means a change of mind and a shifting of target: turning from our own path to God’s, not merely saying “I was wrong”.

Q3 — Bloodline, tradition and religious identity can’t replace personal repentance and trust in Christ. A long history is not “spiritual insurance” but “spiritual responsibility”.

Q4 — Repentance shows up in money, power and how we treat people in need. The fruit flows from grace, not willpower: money and power stop being our security.

Q5 — John points away from himself to the One who is coming, who will baptise with the Holy Spirit and fire — and the “good news” (v.18) is that Jesus comes.

Q6–7 — Jesus’ baptism follows the logic of the incarnation and foreshadows the cross: he was “numbered with the transgressors” (Isa 53:12). Heaven opened as he prayed; the Father’s words echo Psalm 2:7 and Isaiah 42:1. Our identity rests on who Jesus is, not on what we do.

Q8 — Matthew traces Jesus to Abraham (Israel’s Messiah); Luke to Adam (Saviour of all people, the “last Adam”, 1 Cor 15:45). The first Adam failed in the garden; the last Adam triumphed in the wilderness (Luke 4:1–13). (Luke’s line runs through David’s son Nathan, not Solomon.) In Christ we are a new creation, not a patched-up old Adam.

Our vision. “From faith to faith”: faith is passed on from one generation to the next, but it is only real when each person turns to Christ for themselves. Home groups are the engine of our growth towards 75 households by the end of 2026 (we are at 50), on the way to 500 households by the end of 2036. Our four goals for December: the Discover Course (5 people), the Christmas concert on 13 December (120+ people), “Welcome to the Family” (4 households), and the hall dress rehearsal (set up and pack down). Ask each member to pray for 3 and invite 1 — to the concert or the Discover Course — and to help welcome new households and serve on the day. What disciples will we leave for the next 157 years?
