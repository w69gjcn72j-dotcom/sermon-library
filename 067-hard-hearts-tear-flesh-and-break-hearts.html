Hard hearts tear flesh and break hearts

Passage: Matthew 19:1-12

Though hard hearts tear apart what God has joined together, Christ tenderly mends the broken-hearted.

Are divorces possible, lawful or permissible?

Hard hearts tear apart what God’s joined together

Broken hearts are tenderly mended by Christ

Intro:

Earlier in our sermon series, I talked about how good it is to see marriages that have lasted for years.

I love how couples like Herbert Fisher and Zelmyra Fisher have stayed together for 86 years and 290 days,

And how Cleovis and Arwilda Whiteside are still together and at 102 and 98!

At the same time, I’m saddened to hear the divorces and break-ups of couples, whether they’ve been together for a few years or for decades.

Some of these couples were model couples in Hollywood.

They were together even when many couples come together and fall apart all around them.

They told stories about what love is through their films as well as through their personal lives.

They looked lovely together, for years; they spoke lovingly of each other, for years.

They’ve got lovely homes, and lovely families.

What could possibly lead them to divorce?

Of course, it’s not just those who are rich and famous who go through divorces.

All kinds of couples, even Christian couples, have gone through divorces.

I think all of us here, whether directly or indirectly, will know someone who has gone through divorce.

What leads to divorces and what has God done about it?

That’s what we’ll be looking at today.

Before we continue, I want to say to all those who have been personally affected by divorce that God loves you; he loves you so much.

There may be parts of today’s talk that might be difficult and I’ll understand if you need to take a break.

However, can I encourage you to keep turning to our Lord ; pray to him in the middle of the talk if you need to, because he cares for you.

Jesus loves you, and we love you too.

As we look into what Jesus says about divorce, let’s pray,

“Father of all mercies, please open the eyes of our hearts, that we might repent of our hard-heartedness, and turn to you for forgiveness and grace. We pray that your Spirit will move and shape our hearts now, that our hearts might be more like that of our Lord Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

Today’s passage will be in two parts, and we’ll have a third as a response to the passage.

The three parts are:

1. Are divorces possible, lawful, or permissible?

2. Hard hearts tear apart what God’s joined together

3. Broken hearts are tenderly mended by Christ

We’ll be spending most of our time in Matthew chapter 19.

So please open your Bible to Matthew 19.

1. Are divorces possible, lawful, or permissible?

As we look into chapter 19, we’ll be asking ourselves three questions:

Are divorces possible?

Are divorces lawful?

Are divorces permissible?

Matthew chapter 19 begins with Jesus having left Galilee and was travelling to the region of Judea.

Some of the Pharisees went to Jesus to test him and asked, “Is it lawful for a man to divorce his wife on any reason?”

The Pharisees weren’t asking out of concern for the divorced or even a concern for what God teaches.

They were setting up a test, a trap really, for Jesus.

You see, at Jesus’ time, there were two Jewish schools of thoughts on the topic of divorce.

The School of Shammai only allowed adultery as the only ground that justified divorce.

But the School of Hillel saw the ground as much wider.

According to the School of Hillel, almost anything could be ground for divorce.

A wife might have cooked the dinner that wasn’t nice and it was enough ground for the husband to divorce his wife.

Or a husband could find a prettier looking woman than his wife and it would be enough ground for divorce.

And so, will Jesus side with Shammai which only see adultery as the ground for divorce, or will Jesus side with Hillel which sees almost anything as ground for divorce?

More than that, the last time that someone spoke about a divorce was Jesus’ cousin, John the Baptist.

John spoke against King Herod Antipas’ divorce and ended up having his head chopped off.

And so, what will Jesus say?

Look with me at verse 4, “Haven’t you read,” he replied, “that he who created them in the beginning made them male and female, and he also said, ‘For this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two will become one flesh’?”

Jesus has gone back to the very beginning, when God made human beings, when God made them male and female.

Throughout this sermon series, I referred to the seven stars in the Bible that points to Jesus.

This is Star 1, God is the Creator and we’re the creatures.

When God created us male and female, he created marriage to be the powerful union between husband and wife.

He created us to be one flesh.

The two has now become one flesh, one unit.

God made the marriage bond between the man and the woman to be stronger than that between the Parents and the child.

The bond between parents and a child is strong but the bond between the husband and the wife is to be stronger.

It’s a very strong bond.

But is it possible for this bond to break?

Look with me at verse 6, “So they are no longer two, but one flesh. Therefore, what God has joined together, let no one separate.”

God has joined them together, it’s a very strong bond, and so let no one separate.

Man is not supposed to tear the marriage bond apart, and Jesus commands man not to do it.

The implication is that it’s possible to break the bond

For if it’s not possible for man to break the bond, why would Jesus command it?

And so, this bond, ordained by God, stronger than the bond between father and son, between mother and daughter, can be torn apart by man.

That is, marriage is to be the lifelong bond between husband and wife but it is possible for man to tear what God has joined together.

This is Star 2.

God is the Holy One but we as sinners have rejected his rule.

The Pharisees know that it’s possible for man to tear the marriage bond apart.

And so, their question is not whether it’s possible, but on what ground is divorce lawful.

When divorce does happen, what does God’s law say about it?

Please open your Bible with me to Deuteronomy 24:1-3, “If a man marries a woman, but she becomes displeasing to him because he finds something indecent about her, he may write her a divorce certificate, hand it to her, and send her away from his house. 2 If after leaving his house she goes and becomes another man’s wife, 3 and the second man hates her, writes her a divorce certificate, hands it to her, and sends her away from his house or if he dies, 4 the first husband who sent her away may not marry her again after she has been defiled, because that would be detestable to the LORD. You must not bring guilt on the land the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance.”

Even in ancient Israel, when they should’ve known that marriage is the lifelong union between one man and one woman,

The Israelites still treated marriage with contempt.

And so, some of the men would simply just tell the wives to leave, or they would abandon their wives.

However, this puts the women in a very vulnerable position because in that society, they wouldn’t be able to find any work on their own.

Almost all women in that society depend on men’s work to survive.

And if they marry another man, the first husband might come back and say that she’s committed adultery.

He might also come back and take any properties that she’s acquired since he sent her away.

Worse, if the second husband doesn’t like her, he might send her back to her first husband.

She would’ve been treated like property.

And so, to protect the women, Moses allowed the men to write certificates of divorce so that it’s clear that the first marriage bond is broken and the woman is free to marry somebody else.

It’s sad to hear how some women were treated like objects in ancient Israel.

It’s sad to know that in some places, in many places around the world still, even here in Australia, some women are still treated as objects.

It’s treating a human being, a beautiful creature made in the very image of God, as mere possession, like other things that you own.

It’s dehumanising; it shows contempt for its maker.

Any form of dehumanisation must not take in our community, especially in the sacred space of marriage.

There is no excuse to dehumanise any spouse in the marriage.

Husbands cannot treat wives as their possessions, as their slaves and wives cannot treat husbands as their possessions, as their slaves either.

Sadly, I’ve seen husbands who are so possessive of their wives that they control all of their wives’ friendships, all of their social media accounts and all of their time.

I’ve also seen wives taking full control over their husbands, speaking over them in public, publicly mocking them as weak and useless.

There is no place for dehumanising oppression in a marriage, whether it’s done explicitly or not.

For God has a special concern for the oppressed, even those who have been oppressed in the privacy of their own homes.

It was in light of the protection of women that God has permitted divorce in ancient Israel.

God permitted it, but it wasn’t a law in the sense that God wanted the divorce to happen.

It was permitted, not because divorces give any pleasure to God.

It was permitted because it’s a recognition of the hardness of the human heart.

Hard hearts tear apart what God’s joined together

For hard hearts lead to divorce.

Look with me at verses 7-8, “Why then,” they asked him, “did Moses command us to give divorce papers and to send her away?” He told them, “Moses permitted you to divorce your wives because of the hardness of your hearts, but it was not like that from the beginning”

What’s a hard heart?

Physically, a hard heart is one where many of its blood vessels have been hardened by fatty tissues.

If there’s enough fatty tissue in the heart to block a blood vessel supplying blood to the heart itself, it can lead to a heart attack.

I haven’t had it, but I was told that for some people, it’s like having a truck coming on your chest.

In a marriage, divorce is the heart attack, and the cause is a hard heart.

Marriage was designed by God to be a place of safety and love.

Marriage was designed by God to last a life-time.

However, sin hardens the heart and rots the one flesh that God has created.

When hard heartedness sets in, for one or both of the spouses, it sickens the marriage.

It tears the marriage apart.

If the marriage bond between the husband and the wife is one flesh, then divorce is like tearing the body apart.

Is it possible? Yes, but it’s deeply painful.

Why would anyone do it? It’s because our hearts are hard.

What do hard hearts look like?

Look with me at verse 9, “I tell you, whoever divorces his wife, except for sexual immorality, and marries another commits adultery.”

Hard heart – sexual immorality.

A hard heart shows itself in its sexual immorality.

God requires spouses to be faithful to one another, in all aspects of life.

This is especially the case when it comes to sexual faithfulness.

God has designed sexual intimacy to be enjoyed in the safety of a faithful marriage and nowhere else.

When someone violates that trust by having sexual relationships outside of that marriage, it is a betrayal of trust.

That person has broken the marriage vow, tearing apart what God has joined together. When a spouse commits sexual immorality and engages in sexual practices with someone other than their spouse, they undermine their marriage covenant.

This is the sin of adultery.

Hard heart – committing adultery in the heart.

While committing adultery is engaging in sexual practices outside of marriage, it’s bigger than the physical act itself.

A hard heart will show itself by committing adultery in the heart.

Listen to what Jesus says in Matthew 5:28, “But I tell you, everyone who looks at a woman lustfully has already committed adultery with her in his heart.”

Jesus says that even when your heart think of someone inappropriately in a sexual way, you commit adultery.

For being faithful to your spouse is not just faithful to her in your body, but in your hearts and in your minds as well.

Don’t commit adultery in your hearts!

Don’t harden your heart!

Be faithful to your spouse in both your action and in your thoughts.

Hard heart – happiness.

A third way that shows the hardness of our hearts is to think that my life is all about my happiness.

I have seen and heard people who were thinking about getting a divorce telling me, “Don’t you want me to be happy?”

Have you ever heard that?

Don’t you know that I’ll be happier if I divorce him?

Don’t you know that I’ll be happier if I’m single? Don’t you know that I’ll be happier if I’m with someone else?

It’s actually a terrifying thing to hear, because you’re telling me that your happiness more important to you than your faithfulness.

You’re telling me that happiness more important than God’s word.

It’s terrifying to hear because you’re telling me that happiness is your idol and that your heart is so hardened, that you will treat your spouse as a thing that must be removed because he stops you from being happy.

You treat marriage like a game and your spouse like a toy, whose sole function is to make you happy.

It is dehumanisation disguised as a pursuit for happiness.

Here we see Star 3 at work: God is the Judge, we are the judged.

The cause of divorces are our hard hearts.

Divorces are public acknowledgement of hard hearts.

Jesus focuses on the implication of what happens after divorce has taken place.

In both Luke’s account and Marks’ account of the teaching, Jesus says that he who divorces his wife and marries another commits adultery. (Luke 16:18, Mark 2:11).

When someone divorces and then remarry, they commit adultery.

In many cases of divorces, often, but not always, a person divorces so that they can remarry.

There are those who are already involved romantically with someone other than their spouse and so they divorce so that they can remarry their new love interest.

That’s clearly a hardness of the heart.

They divorce in order to remarry; they commit the sin of adultery.

In Matthew 19, we see Jesus says that when someone divorces his wife, except for sexual immorality, and marries another, he commits adultery.

Jesus is here not giving sexual immorality as a legitimate reason for divorce.

However, he is saying that that when sexual immorality has already been committed, then that person has already committed adultery.

If the wife has already committed sexual immorality, that is engaging in sexual intimacy outside of marriage, then the implication is that the man may divorce his wife and he is not committing adultery.

The same applies if it’s the man who has committed sexual immoraility.

The wife can divorce the husband without committing adultery.

However, this is not a law for a spouse to automatically divorce.

Remember, divorce is the heart attack, the issue is still the hard heart.

A hard-hearted husband will automatically blame the wife who has committed adultery and ask for a divorce, but a tender-hearted husband will seek reconciliation first.

It’s not easy! I’ve seen a case where the man who had committed adultery and caused great pain, great pain, to the wife.

But rather than divorcing him, she forgave him when he repented.

However, if the person who had committed adultery insists on having it her way, then the husband does not commit adultery by divorcing his wife.

Sadly, I’ve seen this too, when a wife refused to repent and the marriage ended up in divorce.

Just briefly, there’s one more scenario where the Bible see divorce can happen in a believer’s life.

Look with me at 1 Corinthians 7:15, “But if the unbeliever leaves, let him leave. A brother or a sister is not bound in such cases. God has called you to live in peace.”

If a Christian woman has an unbelieving husband and if he chose to leave, the Christian woman can let the man leave, that is, to go ahead with the divorce he has initiated.

She does not commit adultery by agreeing with what the husband has decided to do.

As Sydney Anglicans these are the two options that we’re formally presented when divorcees come and ask us whether they can remarry.

Divorcees can remarry if their divorce was due to the sexual immorality of their former spouse, or that their unbelieving spouse has left them.

In our tradition, divorcees must apply for a special permission from the local bishop before they can remarry.

One question that has come up is what if a woman has gone suffered domestic abuse?

Can a woman leave the husband if they have been suffering domestic abuse at home?

As I mentioned earlier, dehumanisation has no place in marriage and domestic abuse is a terrible form of dehumanisation.

If you are in danger, look for safety; it’s not a sin to find safety.

We will never encourage you to be in a position of harm and we will do all that we can to protect you.

If you know someone in a domestic abuse situation, then please come to me or Jenny afterwards.

Broken hearts are tenderly mended by Christ

If all we know of the world ends at Star 3, if all we know are the consequences of our hard heartedness and the judgement for our wrongs,

Then the world would be a terribly hopeless place to live in.

But thanks be to God, as Christians, we know that can come before Jesus Christ, the one who tenderly mends our broken hearts.

Please look with me at Psalm 147:3, “He heals the brokenhearted and bandages their wounds.”

We live in a fallen world, where divorces can cause a lot of pain and damage.

But we thank God that we can always come to Jesus, who heals the broken-hearted.

In Star 4, we learn that Jesus is our Saviour and if we trust in him, we’re saved.

He saved us by dying on the cross for our sins.

We have a God who would rather die than to live without us.

In Jesus we have a God who suffered both the physical pain of being on the cross and the spiritual pain of carrying the sins of the world.

On the cross, Jesus cried out to God, “My God, my God, why have you forsaken me.”

Jesus knew firsthand the pain of being abandoned, left utterly alone.

And he responds to this abandonment by praying, lamenting before God.

In his prayers, Jesus presented his grief, his pain, and his plea to God.

Jesus prays not only because prayer is important, but because prayer is effective.

Look with me at Hebrews 5:7, “During his earthly life, he offered prayers and appeals with loud cries and tears to the one who was able to save him from death, and he was heard because of his reverence.”

On the cross, Jesus cried out to God the Father to save him from death and he was heard.

We know that Jesus was heard because of Star 5; we know he was heard because he rose from the dead.

And those in him will one day rise also.

When we cry out from our hearts, when we tell God what grieves our hearts, God hears.

And as we do come before him, we are reminded from Star 6 that we are a new people, empowered by His Own Holy Spirit, to find strength to face each day.

We can do it on our own; God has sent His own Spirit to help us.

And not only has he given His Holy Spirit individually; He has given His holy Spirit to His Church, the fellowship of believers.

We’re there to support one another.

There will be people here at church who can help and support you.

There are brothers and sisters who point us to Star 7, reminding us that our attention ultimately mustn’t be on our own happiness, our marriages or our singleness.

Ultimately, our eyes must focus on Jesus, God’s focus on the Last day.

Our eyes must be on Jesus, our Hope, on that very last day.

As well as Churches, there are also Christian organisations like Anglicare or Firstlight Care for those of us who prefer Chinese, who can help us think through issues around divorce.

You’re not alone!

And so, pray for those in marriages.

But also pray for those who have gone through divorces.

It can be very hard and isolated time.

Be sensitive and be honest with them.

If someone is already divorced, acknowledge the divorce but also provide practical support.

I’ve got a very helpful book that I received recently called ‘Renew: An Australian Guide for Christian women survivors of domestic abuse.’

It’s a good resource to help women and men think through how we can face those suffering domestic abuse.

I’ll be ordering them on behalf of the Church.

If you would like a free copy, please let me know.

Earlier this year, we ran a course to help us think and identify domestic abuse.

I’m looking at running the course again next year; please let me or Range know if you would like to sign up for the next one.

And so, why are there divorces in our world? It’s because we have hard hearts.

We have hard hearts towards those who closest to us.

We have hard hearts who worships the idol of our own happiness.

May our Lord be merciful and melt our hearts of stone and give us a heart of flesh instead.

And may our Lord heal those among us whose hearts have been broken by hard hearts, that we might find comfort and joy in Jesus Christ once again.

But long before you go down the path of divorce, work hard to prevent it.

Actively focus on your spouse; love them in private and praise them in public.

If you’re married, don’t let your eyes wander from your spouse; don’t pursue the idol of your own happiness.

Don’t harden your hearts and break the hearts of others; instead, though our hearts may be made of stone, ask God to melt both your heart and the heart of your spouse.

For those of us whose hearts are broken, find your healing in Jesus, the One who mends broken hearts.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What are some of your examples of being hard-hearted, especially in relation to your spouse (if you’re married)?

What does repentance for your hard heartedness look like?

How will you bring the gospel of Jesus to the broken hearted this week?

Don’t use your hard hearts to break hearts.

Ask God to soften both your heart and the heart of your spouse.

And for those of us who have broken hearts, come to Jesus for mending and healing.

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you for Jesus, who has loved us with the most selfless and glorious love, who loves us from his heart. Please forgive us for our hard heartedness. We’ve seen and have experienced the terror that hard-heartedness can bring and yet out hearts remain stubbornly hard. Please forgive us. Melt our hearts of stone, so that we may repent and come to your throne for forgiveness. We pray especially for those whose hearts have been broken by hard hearts. Father, please send your Spirit of healing among us, that we may be healed. Let our broken hearts be healed, so that we might rise up and rejoice with great gladness. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”
