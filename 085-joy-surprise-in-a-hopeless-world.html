A very popular American children story is called the How the Grinch who stole Christmas.

Has anyone heard of it?

In the story, there was a creature called the Grinch who lived alone on a mountain, away from everyone else.

As Christmas time approaches, he saw all the townspeople celebrating Christmas and having a lot of fun.

He got really annoyed by them and decided to dress up as Santa Claus but instead of giving presents, he stole the presents.

He just wanted to ruin Christmas for everyone.

He hated Christmas and he hated people who were happy at Christmas time.

Hopefully, you haven’t met someone like the Grinch, but you probably have met someone who’s had enough with Christmases.

For them, Christmas is just a time to celebrate the end of the year.

For a lot of business owners, Christmas is a headache because they can’t find anyone for work.

For some larger families, Christmas is a time of trying to organise big family lunches and dinners, which takes time and money.

For some of us, even Christians, we might ask ourselves, why should we get excited about Christmas, again?

Over the next month, as Christmas approaches, we’ll be looking at the theme of joy.

And today we’ll be looking how Christmas is a time of surprising joy in a hopeless world.

If you have your companion booklet, please open up to this page if you want to write down notes from today’s sermon.

Leaps of joy in a once-hopeless womb

Earlier in this chapter, the Bible tells us that Elizabeth and her husband Zechariah were very old.

We were also told that they didn’t have any children.

However, one day, the angel Gabriel appeared to Zechariah and told him that he and Elizabeth would have a son.

Elizabeth became pregnant, just as the angel’s said, and stayed at home for five months.

Meanwhile, the angel Gabriel visited another woman, a relative of Elizabeth, by the name of Mary.

Unlike Elizabeth, Mary was young and she wasn’t married.

The angel Gabriel told Mary that she, too, would have a Son.

When Mary asked how this could be since she was a virgin, Gabriel responded by saying that the Holy Spirit would overshadow her.

It’s by the creative power of the Holy Spirit that Mary would give birth to a Son.

This Son would be called the Son of God.

Gabriel then told Mary about Elizabeth and her pregnancy.

After Gabriel left her, Mary hurried to the hill country of Judea, to the town where Elizabeth was staying.

Now, hardly anyone would know about Elizabeth’s pregnancy because the Bible says that she was living in seclusion for five months.

However in the sixth month, Mary turns up at Elizabeth’s door and greets her, “Hi, Elizabeth, it’s me, Mary.”

Before Elizabeth could respond, something strange happens!

Look with me at verse 41, “When Elizabeth heard Mary’s greeting, the baby leaped inside her, and Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Spirit.”

Before Elizabeth could answer the door, the baby inside Elizabeth leaped inside her to reply.

I’ve never felt anything like it, but my wife Jenny had.

I won’t tell you which one, but when one of my children was still inside Jenny’ womb, she would move when she’s alone with Jenny.

But when I was around, she would stop.

And so, I spoke to the baby while she was still inside Jenny, “This is Daddy.

I’m going to count to three, and you will move.

One…two…”

Before I get to three, she starts to move!

Well for one thing, at least I know she can count!

When Mary says Hi to Elizabeth, the baby inside her, who will one day be John the Baptist, leaped inside Elizabeth’s womb.

Kids (and adults), when was the last time you leaped and jumped?

Come on, stand up and show me that you can jump.

I’ll jump too. Ready? Jump!

Why do we jump?

Well, most of the time, especially when we’re kids, we jump when we’re happy.

Baby John the Baptist was happy inside of Elizabeth and he jumped.

Why? Baby Jesus was around.

Jesus would have just started to grow inside Mary’s womb; his life on earth has just begun as his body was slowly being formed inside Mary’s womb.

As soon as Jesus was around, even as a tiny, fetus inside Mary, Baby John John the Baptist knew, even when he was in his mother’s womb.

For when the angel Gabriel appeared to John the Baptist’s father, Zechariah, he told him that Baby John will be filled with the Holy Spirit even while he is in his mother’s womb.

And that John the Baptist will prepare God’s people for the Lord.

However, who would’ve thought that John the Baptist will start pointing people to Jesus before he was born?

The first person that John the Baptist tells about Jesus was his mum, Elizabeth, even while he was in Elizabeth’s womb

Elizabeth herself was filled with the Holy Spirit.

What does a Spirit-filled Elizabeth do? Four things:

Firstly, she speaks the truth about Jesus.

Look with me at verse 42, “Then she exclaimed with a loud cry, “Blessed are you among women, and your child will be blessed!”

To be blessed is to be approved by God.

Elizabeth proclaims that Mary is blessed, and that the Baby inside her is blessed.

Second, Elizabeth speaks with humility.

Verse 43, “How could this happen to me, that the mother of my Lord should come to me?”

Elizabeth doesn’t deserve to have this experience of meeting the mother of her Lord.

She humbly accepts this honour of meeting the mother of her Lord.

Third, she recognises the joy from the Lord.

She wasn’t hiding this from anyone; she was exclaiming all these words in a loud voice.

For many of us, when we’re excited we shout and we get loud.

Elizabeth recognises that the baby’s presence inside Mary’s womb is a cause for great joy.

She also recognizes that this joy is in her baby also; she felt Baby John’s leap of joy inside her.

Elizabeth had the discernment, the insight, to see the joy of the Lord.

And fourthly, she recognises that trust in God and being blessed by God goes together.

Verse 45, “Blessed is she who has believed that the Lord would fulfill what he has spoken to her!”

Someone who trusts in God is blessed.

It’s a joy to know that God’s dependable, that whatever he said, he will do.

God had spoken to Mary, that she will carry a son who will be called the Son of God.

Mary probably couldn’t feel any physical difference so early in her pregnancy and what the angel said was so extraordinary that it might still feel a little unreal to Mary.

But Elizabeth assures Mary, “Blessed is she who has believed that the Lord would fulfill what he has spoken to her!”

Elizabeth herself had to learn to trust God.

She once had a hopeless womb, but now there’s a living, active baby, little baby John, filled with the Holy Spirit, leaping for joy inside of her.

And now she encourages Mary to do the same: Trust that the Lord would fulfil what he said, and you will be blessed.

When you’re blessed, you know that God looks upon you with approval, you’ll be filled with joy.

Christmas time is a busy time of the year.

There are Christmas parties, end of year dinners, family get together and so on where there’ll be a lot of fun and joy.

But as good as these are, that’s not the joy that Elizabeth and Baby John felt.

They weren’t happy because Mary the cousin came over for a family dinner. They were happy, even leapt for joy in Baby John’s case, because God the Holy Spirit pointed out to them that inside this young girl, inside Mary, was the Saviour of the world.

Inside this young, most probably, teenage girl, is the hope of the world.

This little baby inside Mary is God’s promise coming true.

It’s the Holy Spirit who gives Elizabeth and Baby John the Baptist joy.

Some of us might be feeling weary and tired as we approach Christmas.

We might be tired of the year that we just went through, tired of the relationships we have with people, or simply tired of the repetitive and hard life we’re living.

If that’s you, then can I invite you to let the Holy Spirit show you who Jesus is.

Let the Holy Spirit show you what a tremendous and humbling privilege it is to be in the presence of Jesus himself.

If you are a Christian, and you’re not joyful in the Lord, let the Holy Spirit point you back to Jesus.

You can’t manipulate the Spirit to give you joy.

You can’t manufacture this joy on your own.

It’s a joy that we pray for as we come before Jesus and hear his voice in God’s word, the Bible.

When the Holy Spirit is inside of you, and you hear God’s voice, you will get excited, you might even leap for joy.

Tomorrow, a group of University students will come to our Church here in Kogarah with the Power to Change Christian group to tell people about our Christmas events and hopefully to tell people about Jesus.

They came last year, and they were filled with joy.

Some of them were nervous to begin with, but by the time they came back, they were filled with joy.

How about you?

Will you leap for joy at the presence of Jesus? Do you get excited at the sound of Jesus’ name?

Find that joy that comes from trusting in God and his promises in Jesus.

Magnify the Lord in a once-hopeless world

Mary’s blessed because she trusted in God and his promises.

And she responds with a poem, or I think what’s more likely, a song.

Traditionally, this song is called the Magnifcat, or the song of Mary.

Come to Luke chapter 1 verse 46 with me,

“And Mary said:

My soul magnifies the Lord,

and my spirit rejoices in God my Savior,

because he has looked with favor

on the humble condition of his servant.”

The word translated ‘magnified’ here is ‘megalumen’ in Greek.

Today, we use the word lumen to describe how bright something is.

For example, this projector here is 5500 lumen; it’s pretty bright.

When Mary says she wants to Megalumen the Lord, she’s saying she wants to shine mega light, a lot of light, on the Lord.

Just as Baby John leapt at the presence of Jesus, Mary wants her soul, her deepest being to show the magnificence and the glory of the Lord.

She wants her spirit to rejoice in him.

She has five reasons to magnify and to rejoice in the Lord:

Firstly, the mighty and holy Lord looks upon her with favour.

From the second part of verse 48 onwards, “Surely, from now on all generations

will call me blessed,

because the Mighty One

has done great things for me,

and his name is holy. “

She’s only a servant girl; she’s no one special

But now, all generations will call her blessed.

She had received extraordinary favour from the Lord, that she doesn’t deserve.

Second, she magnifies the Lord because the Lord is merciful to those who fear him, who trust and tremble at his word.

Third, she rejoices in the Lord because the Lord has done mighty deeds with his arm.

The language of God doing mighty deeds with his arm is often associated with how God rescues his people from oppression.

Here, Mary associates God’s mighty deed with how he scatters those who are proud.

The proud are those who think they don’t need to hear God’s word; they think of themselves as people who have no need to obey God’s word.

They might not say they don’t need God, but their intention was enough for God to act against them.

The intention of the proud was offensive to God, and he responds to their pride by scattering them.

Fourthly, God reverses the order of the world.

Instead of the proud ruling, God brings them down.

Those mighty on their thrones, those proud in hearts, those who don’t trust God, God topples from their thrones.

Rather than the proud, God exalts and lifts high the lowly.

Instead of the rich getting the good things God satisfies the hungry with good things.

But those who are rich, God sends away empty.

And fifthly, Mary magnifies God because God is faithful and keeps his word, keeps his promises to his servant Israel, and to Abraham and to his descendants.

And so, Mary’s soul magnifies the Lord because he looks upon Mary with favour, because the Lord is merciful, because the Lord is mighty, because the Lord reverses the order of the world, scattering the proud, exalting the humble, and because the Lord’s word can be trusted.

That’s Mary’s Magnificat; that’s Mary’s song.

Forty years ago, at around Christmas time, many pop singers around the world got together to record a song to help the poor in Africa.

It’s called Live aid and they wrote this song, “Do they know it’s Christmas time at all?”

Here are some of the words, “At Christmas time, it's hard but while you're having fun, there's a world outside your window, and it's a world of dread and fear, Where a kiss of love can kill you, And there's death in every tear.”

They were not very hopeful about the world back in 1984.

And now, in 2024, the world doesn’t seem that hopeful either.

Of course, 2024 is certainly a good year for some, but it’s not a great year for many around the world.

Major wars and conflicts from 2023 seemed to have escalated in 2024.

Some of us might be struggling with the cost of daily living, while others flaunt their wealth.

A lot has changed in the past forty years, but the world is still as hopeless as it was back then.

But unlike the Live aid singers, we don’t try to fix this hopeless world by ourselves.

We magnify the Lord and point people to the One, the Only One, who can save the world.

We point people to Jesus.

God made a promise to heal the world of sin and death.

His promise is fulfilled in Jesus, for only Jesus can heal the world.

One of the concerns that many people in our community have seen in our world, both inside and outside of Church,

Is the growth in the anxiety and frustration of our youth.

In Australia, 1 in 10 people aged 18–25 experience anxiety in any 12-month period.

1 in 10 young people see the world and they are anxious about it.

They’re anxious about how they can live from day to day and they’re anxious about their future.

As Christians, we want to show our youth that there is light in this dark world, that there is hope in all the hopelessness.

We can’t take all their anxiety away, and we can’t fix the world to make it stress free, but we can point to the One who has come to save it.

We can magnify the One who is trustworthy, who will be with us, even if we walk through the shadow of the valley of death.

We can point them to Jesus, the friend who is there always for those who trusts in him.

God has answered our prayers after many years, more than ten, and has opened up the way for us to share the gospel there. Praise God!

We had also spoken with the Principal at St George Girls and we’re now working to improve the enrolment process so that more students can join SRE.

It’s looking positive! Please continue to pray for this.

Currently, we have around 220 students in SRE classes across four schools and by God’s grace, we’ll have more next year when Kogarah High opens SRE classes.

Our Church is part of a board that currently employs Nick Hadges as a part time SRE teacher.

He, together with other SRE teachers, are doing a great job in our area.

Will you join us to support Nick and his work?

Will you help us to magnify the Lord in Kogarah, particularly among the youth?

Trust the Lord who unfailingly surprised a once-hopeless people

Each of the characters in today’s passage was surprised by the joy that God brings.

Elizabeth was surprised with the joy of meeting the mother of her Lord.

Mary was surprised with the joy of knowing the Baby growing inside of her will be called the Son of the Most High God.

Even Baby John the Baptist leapt with joy in Elizabeth’s womb in the presence of Jesus.

There were all unexpected; they were all joyful surprises from God.

They are surprises but they aren’t random surprises.

The coming of Jesus is the fulfilment of God’s promises; God made a promise and Jesus came.

God’s unfailingly reliable but he’s not predictable.

God will keep his promises, but he might do it in ways that we would have never expected.

He’s the God who unfailingly surprises us with good things that he’s promised.

Back in 1996, a film came out called Mission Impossible.

Has anyone seen it?

It’s about an agent called Ethan Hunt who was given impossible missions to complete.

Except of course, he does complete them.

In fact, there’s been now seven Mission Impossible films since the first one.

The eighth one is coming out next year.

Who knows, maybe they finally realise that the missions are not that impossible and they just might call the last one Mission Hard, but Possible.

Because that’s what we’ll be expecting as an audience: even though it looks impossible, Ethan Hunt will complete his mission, should he choose to accept it.

Our God is far, far more reliable than Ethan Hunt!

God has never failed to keep his promises.

God’s unfailingly faithful, but he’s never boring.

He will surprise us every now and then with blessings that we haven’t thought possible.

But he’ll do it to glorify and magnify Jesus.

Do we see God’s acts of power with eyes of faith?

He does it, and it’s always a joy to see how God works in our lives.

Time and time again, we see incredible coincidences that seem quite impossible.

They are loving reminders of his sovereign power.

When those who trust in God see it, it brings a ray of sunshine into our day.

Next year, we’ll have a new staff member on our team: Peter Chen.

We’ve been looking around for someone who can speak Chinese and English fluently for years.

I didn’t look for Peter specifically but he found us.

But more than that, even though I only met him and Hanna a few months ago, we have so many mutual friends between us.

I officiated a wedding that Hanna was playing music at.

My long time family friend, Uncle Zion, is a good friend of Hanna’s Dad.

Over the past week, I met three separate people who also know Peter.

Peter and Hanna share a lot of networks with us, and whenever I meet a mutual friend that both our families know, they are lovely reminders that God is in control.

They are reminders that God is faithful, but never predictable.

This will be the biggest investment in any staff since I started here.

Jenny and I, along with many in our Church are looking forward to working with them!

Should we get excited about Christmas, again?

Yes! We should be like baby John, leaping with joy at the presence of Jesus. Don’t ever stop rejoicing in Jesus!

We should be like Elizabeth, filled with the Spirit to proclaim to others about Jesus.

We should be like Mary, who magnifies the Lord with her soul – pointing people to Jesus.

With eyes of faith, let’s look for the surprises that God unfailingly gives to those whom he loves in Jesus.

By faith, see how he has faithfully is carried you through the trials of life.

That’s why no Christian should ever be bored about Christmas.

Christians aren’t Grinches, who gets tired or even hates Christmas, because true, genuine Christians can’t help themselves but magnify and rejoice in Jesus.

And so, Souls of St Paul’s Kogarah, rejoice in and magnify our God, for he has unfailingly surprised the hopeless world with Jesus’ coming.
