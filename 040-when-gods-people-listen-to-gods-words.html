When God’s people listen to God’s words

Passage: Nehemiah 8

Listen closely to God’s law and you’ll weep, rejoice and obey the Saviour.

Listen closely to God’s law

Weep and rejoice at God’s law

Swiftly obey the Saviour according to God’s law

Almost ten years ago, an artist by the name of KC Green drew this comic:

The dog is in the middle of a fire.

He has a cup of coffee in front of him.

He smiles and says, “This is Fine”.

Have you seen it?

Since then, many people have used this to talk about how people often respond to disasters.

They deny the severity of what’s happening and say, “This is fine”.

Do you know people like that?

People often don’t deny what’s happening because they don’t want to face how bad things are

We don’t want to face them because they can lead to sadness and sorrow.

Often, we don’t want to face the bad things in life, the disasters, the disappointments, because we don’t know how to face the sorrow that comes with them.

However, denying these things only delay the inevitable.

Sooner or later, we must face the difficulties in life.

Sooner or later, we must face our sorrow.

How do we face our sorrow?

Where do we find the strength to overcome our sorrow?

There can be many reasons why we become sorrowful, and we don’t have a silver bullet answer that will answer every kind of sorrow that you’ll face.

However, in today’s passage, we will meet a nation filled with sorrow.

And we’ll see how they were led from sorrow to joy.

If you have your Bible, please open up with me to Nehemiah 8:1

In today’s passage, we’ll see three things:

Listen closely to God’s law

Weep and rejoice at God’s law

Swiftly obey the Saviour according to God’s law

Before we look into it, let’s pray,

“Father by your Spirit, please let your word penetrate our head, sink into our hearts and move our hands in active and joyful obedience. We pray this in Jesus’ name, Amen.”

Listen closely to God’s law

Look with me at Nehemiah 8:1, “all the people gathered together at the square in front of the Water Gate. They asked the scribe Ezra to bring the book of the law of Moses that the Lord had given Israel.”

The Water Gate was probably a commonly recognised place.

It would probably be like our Town Hall, which is basically at the heart of our city.

All the people, all the men and women, all those who could listen with understanding, gathered together and asked Ezra the Priest to bring out the book of the law of Moses that God’s given to Israel.

All the people gathered together because they wanted to hear from God’s word.

Many years ago, we took our family to see KungFu Panda.

It was Ezekiel’s first ever movie at the cinemas.

He was very excited to see it.

We got into the theatre and watched the screen light up.

In Australia when you watch movies, you don’t get the movie straight away.

Instead, you get many, many ads.

After a few minutes of ads, Ezekiel said in a loud voice, “Where’s Kungfu Panda?”

He said what many of us were thinking!

Just like a little boy who wanted to start watching his movie, the people of Israel were keen to hear from God’s word.

They had a thirst to hear God’s word.

In particular, they wanted to hear from the Book of the Law of Moses, most likely the Pentateuch (摩西五經) – Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy.

Though it was an ancient book by their time (it would’ve been over 1000 years old), they were still keen to hear it because they know that it’s the Word of God.

And so they stood there from daybreak until noon, six, possibly up to seven hours, listening closely to God’s word.

Ezra the Scribe read God’s word to them from a high wooden platform that was especially made for this purpose.

Along with him stood 13 others who were probably there to help with Ezra going through the long Bible reading.

And the people stood up while God’s word was being read.

They showed a deep and reverent respect for God’s word.

Ezra began by blessing the Lord, probably in the form of prayer.

The people responded by lifting their hands, as you would expect to do if you were praying in many cultures and cried out, Amen, amen!

And then they would kneel low and worship the Lord with their face to the ground.

Their whole posture was one of worship.

That was how they worshipped God.

The Levite would explain God’s word to them and translate God’s word to them, most probably from Hebrew to Aramaic, so that the people could understand God’s word.

For them, to listen to God’s word and to help each other understand God’s word was how they worshipped God.

To listen closely and to understand God’s word was their act of worship.

That’s what we should be expecting when we go to any gathering of Christian worship.

We should expect God’s word to be read to us and explained to us.

And that’s what we do here at this Church.

Men and women, boys and girls, from different cultures, speaking different languages, gather around to hear God’s word and to hear it explained.

And like the Israelites, we also have translators who help all of us understand God’s word.

We also have people who help us understand God’s word.

That’s why we have preachers, Growth Group leaders, KOG junior Leaders, KOG Youth leaders, Young adults leaders.

As God’s people we want to have a thirst for God’s word and we want to be filled with God’s word.

That’s our act of worship – to hear and to understand God’s word.

What can we do to cultivate a thirst for God’s word?

Talk with the person next to you.

Send in your answer via Whatsapp or Wechat.

What can we do to cultivate a thirst for God’s word?

You have twenty seconds.

Great answers!

What would happen when we do hear and understand Gods’ word?

We’ll weep and we’ll rejoice.

Weep and rejoice at God’s law

That’s what happened to the Israelites: They wept and they rejoiced at God’s law.

Look with me at 8:9, ‘Nehemiah the governor, Ezra the priest and scribe, and the Levites who were instructing the people said to all of them, “This day is holy to the Lord your God. Do not mourn or weep.” For all the people were weeping as they heard the words of the law.’

When the people heard God’s law read and explained, they started to cry.

They were filled with sorrow and crying when they heard God’s word.

It wasn’t just a sniffle; it was outright weeping.

Nehemiah, Ezra and the Levites had to tell them to stop!

They wept because they understood that they have failed God’s law.

And not just them, but generations of them, from the time of their ancestors until now.

God’s law would’ve clearly stated what would happen if the God’s people disobeyed him.

The people would be punished and exiled from their own land.

And that’s exactly what happened to the Israelites.

They were living with the consequences of their sin, and they knew that deserved it.

In the comedy movie called Monty Python and the Holy Grail, there was a scene where King Arthur was fighting the Black Knight.

In the sword fight, King Arthur hacked off both the Knight’s arms.

Arthur was quite convince that he won but the Knight insisted on fighting and said, “it’s but a flesh wound” even though both arms were hacked off.

It wasn’t just a flesh wound but a potentially fatal wound.

When the Israelites heard God’s law, they realised they didn’t just have a flesh wound but a fatal wound.

When they heard God’s law, they knew that they were rightly under God’s judgement, and they wept in response.

That’s what we must expect when we come to hear God’s word.

God’s word exposes us for who we are.

God’s word shows the darkness inside each one of us.

Hebrews 4:12 tells us that, ‘For the word of God is living and effective and sharper than any double-edged sword, penetrating as far as the separation of soul and spirit, joints and marrow. It is able to judge the thoughts and intentions of the heart.’

If someone has listened to God’s word correctly, they would be saddened by their sin before God.

Indeed, I would much rather you feel saddened by God’s word than to feel nothing at all!

Because when you’re saddened by it, I know you’ve heard it.

Whenever we heard God’s word, be prepared to weep.

However, if we only weep when we hear God’s word, then we haven’t heard the full message.

As you come before God in God’s word, you’re not only coming before the Holy God.

You’re also coming before your Saviour, your Rescuer.

In God’s law, as well as condemnation for our sin, we also see God’s salvation of us from our sin.

Though the Israelites were disobeying God time and time again in the wilderness for over forty years but God kept his promise and took the children of the Israelites into the Promised Land.

Though the Israelites had rejected God time and time again even when they were in the Promised Land and eventually were exiled by God,

God had brought them back to the Promised Land.

And so, while it’s right to weep at our sins, we need to remember the salvation that God has brought about for his people.

Look with me at verse 10, “Then he said to them, “Go and eat what is rich, drink what is sweet, and send portions to those who have nothing prepared, since today is holy to our Lord. Do not grieve, because the joy of the Lord is your strength.”

They’re to eat what is fat, drink what is sweet and send food parcels to those who haven’t prepared.

This is the day to celebrate the Holy God who saves, because the joy of the Lord is your strength.

What is the joy of the Lord? What makes God happy?

It gives the Lord great joy when we trust in his him and what he’s done for us.

The Bible tells us that heaven rejoice when a sinner turns back to God.

Heaven, God’s throne, rejoices, when a person is saved.

Laughter fills the heavens when a person is saved.

That’s what gives God joy; Salvation brings joy to the Lord.

When the Israelites returned from exile into the promised land, thirsting for God’s word, hearing and understanding God’s word, it gives God great joy.

In response, the Israelites were to celebrate what God has done with a great feast.

On this side of the cross, we know that God rejoices when God’s people, saved by trusting in his Son our Lord Jesus Christ, living under God’s rule in God’s kingdom.

That’s the joy of the Lord.

And whatever gives the Lord joy ought to give us joy.

Not only does seeking the joy of the Lord give us joy but seeking the joy of the Lord will also give us strength.

Our sin will bring us sorrow but God’s salvation will bring us joy because we know that in Christ, God has overcome our sin.

And since our sin has been overcome, we have joy that overcomes any sorrow caused by sin.

Our sins may be great but our Saviour is greater still.

Being confident of the salvation Jesus has done for us will give us the joy and the strength to overcome the sorrow and the heartache that sin brings.

The joy of the Lord is your strength.

I know that some of us are weary by sin.

Some of us may feel the sorrow that sin brings.

I know that I felt it from time to time, perhaps more often over the past two years.

But whenever I hear that someone has become a Christian, it gives me great joy.

Whenever I hear someone who have understood the words of Jesus and joyfully making costly decision for his glory, it gives me great joy.

For they would rather be happy in the Lord than be happy with the world.

That’s the joy of the Lord; it’s the joy of seeing God’s will be done, seeing people saved for Christ’s glory.

It’s a joy that empowers us to continue to keep serving the Lord.

It’s a joy that God shares with those who are his.

What saddens you, as well as what gives you joy, reveals who you really are.

Those who have understood God’s law will be sorrowful for their sins.

Those who have understood God’s law and it’s fulfilment in the salvation of Jesus will find joy in the Lord.

If we have the joy of the Lord, we will swiftly obey the Saviour according to God’s Law.

Swiftly obey the Saviour according to God’s law

Look with me at verse 14, “They found written in the law how the Lord had commanded through Moses that the Israelites should dwell in shelters during the festival of the seventh month.”

They read God’s law and came across feast called the Feast of the Booths.

It’s a feast that was held from the 15th day to the 22nd day of the seventh month to celebrate God’s provision of the Israelites in the wilderness on their way to the Promised Land.

Each household would gather branches from olive, wild olive, myrtle, palm, and other leafy trees in order to build a shelter to live in for seven days.

I still remember that a few years ago, a number of us have gone away to camp for the first time.

I can’t believe it’s more than two years ago now!

It was great to be away for a few days, living in our tents.

But we were also very glad to be able to come back home and sleep in our comfortable beds!

As soon as the Israelites found out about the Feast of the Booths in the Book of the Law, they decided to celebrate it straight away.

It would’ve took a lot of effort on their part.

It would’ve been uncomfortable but they did it straight away because they knew that that’s what God wanted them to do.

They were in the seventh month and the Feast of the Booth was supposed to happen in the seventh month and so they didn’t hesitate but obeyed straight away.

Like the Israelites, we, too, are on a journey to the Promised Land.

We know that this home that we have here on earth is temporary and that our permanent home is heaven.

We might live on this earth for a few decades, ten, if we’re blessed.

But we’ll live in heaven for thousands upon thousands of decades.

The time we spend on earth is temporary.

And while we don’t necessary build tents from branches to remind ourselves that we’re pilgrims on a journey,

We do remind one another that we are on this journey together when we come to Church.

When we come together as Church, we remind ourselves that our home is in heaven and that together, we’re on our journey home.

More than that, we remember that our God has tabernacled, and built his tent among us.

The apostle John tells us in John 1:14, that “The Word became flesh and dwelt (literally tabernacled or built a tent) among us. We observed his glory, the glory as the one and only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth.”

Jesus lived among us for a short time and is now preparing a permanent home for us in heaven.

When the Israelites read about the Feast of the Booths in the law, they obeyed it straight away.

Jesus is greater than the Book of the Law for in Jesus, we see the fulfilment of the Book of the law.

Will we obey Jesus as quickly as the Israelites obeyed the Book of the Law?

The story is told of the devil briefing his junior devils. “Who is going to come up with a good strategy to stop the enemy winning people into his kingdom?” he asked. One devil piped up, “I'm going to tell them there is no God"

‘That’s not a very good strategy,’ replied the devil, ‘because there is masses of evidence that there is a God. Very few people will really believe you. Anyone else got any ideas?”

Someone else said, ‘I’m going to tell them there's no judgment.'

‘That's a better strategy,’ replied the devil. 'But actually men and women know there is accountability. They know that actions have consequences. And so it's still not a very good strategy. Anyone else got any ideas?”

And then one demon piped up, 'I'm going to tell them that there's no hurry!’

'Ah,’ said the devil, ‘that is exactly what you ought to do. That is the perfect strategy.’

If you know the Lord Jesus’ will, if you know God’s will, then don’t hesitate.

Do it!

Let the joy of the Lord empower you to do his will.

If you know that what you do will bring Jesus, joy, do it.

If you know that what you say might lead someone from the sorrow of sin to the joy of salvation, speak it.

Be swift to do God’s will.

How do you know God’s will? When we listen closely to God’s word.

Listen closely to God’s word.

You will weep because of your sins but you will also rejoice because of the Lord’s salvation.

As you rejoice in the Lord’s salvation, you will find strength to do his will.

It’s only as you find your joy in the Lord’s salvation that you will find strength to overcome your sorrow.

What does this mean for us?

The Joy of the Lord is your strength.

Let the joy of the Lord, the joy that his salvation brings, be our strength always. Jesus can bring joy to a lost brother, a lost sister.

Jesus can bring joy to a lost father, a lost son.

Long for the joy that comes with the Lord’s salvation.

Keep praying for their salvation.

And work hard to understand God’s word.

It won’t be easy and it won’t always be comfortable.

Listen closely to it.

You will find it to be a source of great sadness as you ponder over your sin, but you also find in it a place of great joy.

If God has so willed and if he has enabled you, help others understand it.

Don’t hesitate to bring joy to the Lord, for it’s his joy that will bring you joy.

It’s his joy that will bring you strength.

For the joy of the Lord is your strength.

Here are the three questions for this week:

How will I listen more closely to God’s word this week?

Do I have the joy of the Lord?

Is there anything that God wants me to do that I am hesitating to do?

Friends, may the joy of the Lord be our strength, always.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you for your word. We thank you that in it, we see your commands and your salvation. Most of all, we thank you that in your word we meet Jesus, our Saviour. Father by your Spirit, please instil your joy in us. Let what gives you joy, give us joy. Let the source of your joy be the source of our joy. Let your joy be our strength. And, strengthened by your joy, may we do your will, gladly, without hesitation. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
