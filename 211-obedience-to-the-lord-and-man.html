Obedience and leadership at home

Please open your Bible with me to Ephesians 6:1, let’s say the verse together, in both languages, “Children, obey your parents in the Lord, because this is right.”

This command from the Lord flows from what the Apostle Paul has been saying in 5:1.

It’s the verse on your companion booklet.

What’s the verse? “Therefore, be imitators of God, as dearly loved children.”

The Bible tells us to be imitators of God and it lists out different ways that we can do it.

We are to live as God’s holy people, not living as sexually immoral or impure people.

That’s what we focussed on last week.

Last week, we read from verses 22-33 about what it means to live as husbands and wives,

How wives are to submit to their husbands as to their Lord and how husbands are to love his wife as himself.

Paul’s message was for both husbands and wives.

Presumably, some of the first people to ever hear these words would have been husbands and wives, sitting together in the Church, listening to Paul’s letter read out.

As well as husbands and wives, Paul also expected some children in the audience.

Paul expected children to be there too, in the Church, when the Epistle (Letter) to the Ephesians was read out.

These children would most probably be still living with their parents, as the words ‘bring them up’ in verse 4 implies that they still needed to be looked after.

However, they were old enough to hear and respond to this command:

“Children, obey your parents.”

There are many cultures in the world who would agree with this command it the sentence stops here.

“Children, obey your parents.”

Why? Well children should be grateful to their parents.

Some of us will know this very famous Chinese poem, (translate back to English)

“慈母手中線，遊子身上衣。 臨行密密縫，意恐遲遲歸。 誰言寸草心，報得三春暉。”

Your Mum is like the sun, how can you repay her?

Of course, you should obey her (and your Dad too!)

It’s not just in the Chinese culture that obedience to parents is expected.

I spoke with an older lady, whose husband, also quite old, had to obey his Tunisian father, who’s very old to do things that he would rather not do.

But is that what the Bible is talking about?

Well, no, because the whole command is this: “Children, obey your parents in the Lord, because this is right.”

Children in the Church, believers who are children, should obey their parents in the Lord.

Obeying parents in the Lord is right.

Obey your parents in the Lord is right because you obey Jesus.

That is, because you believe in Jesus, you obey your parents.

It is in the context of obeying Jesus that you obey your parents.

And so, this rules out anything that your parents might command you that’s against the Lord Jesus.

If your parents tell you to do something that will make you disobey Christ, don’t do it.

However, for everything else, obey your parents.

If you obey Jesus, you will obey your parents, for this is right; it’s the right thing for something who believes in Jesus to do.

We’re also commanded to honour our father and mother.

To honour someone means giving them respect and support them through physical and often financial means.

The Bible says that this is the first commandment with a promise: that it many go well with you and that you may have a long life in the land.

It’s based on what God has promised the Israelites in the ten commandments in Exodus 20:12, “Honor your father and your mother so that you may have a long life in the land that the LORD your God is giving you.”

When God gave this commandment to the Israelites in the wilderness, he was referring to the land of Canaan, the Promised Land.

God has promised to give the land of Canaan to Abraham’s descendants, the Israelites.

If they honour their parents, God promised that they will live long in the Promised Land.

Does this apply to us?

Well, since we were not the Israelites in the wilderness and since God hasn’t promised to give us the land of Canaan, this promise doesn’t apply to us in the same way.

However, as believers in Jesus, all promises are now fulfilled in the Lord Jesus.

In Jesus, we look forward to the inheritance that’s ours in Jesus Christ.

Since Jesus will inherit everything and will be the Master, the King, of the new heaven and new earth,

We can look forward to receiving that promise of the new earth, the new land.

That’s the future for all who believe in Jesus.

In the meantime, there is an echo of that reality here.

When we obey our parents in the Lord, when we honour them in the Lord, generally speaking, we can God to fulfil his promise of us living well and enjoying long life in this land.

This promise is not only for the Israelites living in Israel, but for all believers in every land.

As the passage is not talking explicitly about the future inheritance of Christians, and Christians don’t just live long but live forever in the new heaven and new earth,

It seems to make more sense to take this as a promise to live well, in this land, generally speaking.

Now, I have to emphasise that this is generally speaking.

There are very obedient children who don’t get to live long nor do they get to live well.

Vice versa, there are disobedient children who indulge their lives and get to live to a very old age.

However, like the many sayings of Proverbs in the Old Testament, generally speaking, God will be faithful in keeping his promise to those who obey their parents in the Lord.

There are echoes of God’s promise of the new heaven and new earth in his promise that comes to those who obey their parents.

Why will those who obey their parents live long and live well?

It’s partly due to this little secret that many parents know but many children don’t.

Children, can I tell you a secret?

Your parents want what’s best for you.

Your parents want to see you thrive; they want to see you soar!

They want you to live long lives in the land.

Kids, did you know about this secret? I hope mine do!

Again, this applies generally; sadly, not every parent want what’s best for their child, but generally, most do.

They want to show their children how to live well and how to live long.

They want to teach their children from their own personal experiences, both from their victories and their defeats.

And so, children, listen to your parents, obey them in the Lord.

If your parents are Christians, they’ve lived through childhood and some of them even lived through Christian childhood.

They can teach you what it means to live in the Lord; obey your parents in the Lord.

Children, I know that it’s sometimes hard to obey your parents.

It can be embarrassing at times and it can be hard to keep practising your piano, keep doing your homework and keep doing your chores when your parents asked you to do these.

But keep at it, just as you would obey Jesus.

How should you obey Jesus? You should obey him and love him with all that you are, with all of your heart, all of your soul, all of your mind and all of your strength.

It’s out of your obedience to Jesus that you obey your parents.

What about parents?

Look with me at verse 4, let’s say it together, “Fathers, don’t stir up anger in your children, but bring them up in the training and instruction of the Lord.”

Fathers, your children are not your property.

They are gifts from God, precious, given to you for a short time.

They may get on your nerves sometimes, and there will be times when it’s appropriate for you to discipline your children.

But don’t stir up their anger due to your pride and arrogance.

Don’t humiliate them, don’t treat them unfairly and don’t show favouritism.

Don’t make it even harder for them to obey you.

Don’t burn the bridges in your anger and then be surprised that they’ve burnt their end of the bridge.

That’s not godly.

Sadly, I’ve seen this too many time, in Chinese culture, in Englis-speaking culture and in other cultures too.

Instead, bring them up in the Lord; nurture them in the Lord.

Fathers, show Christ-centred leadership in the way you treat your children.

Do they frustrate you? Yes, and it’s to be expected.

Only a fool of a father would think that children had already got everything together.

They are children and they’re still learning.

Spend time training and instructing them.

They will get it wrong sometimes, they will make foolish decisions; show them how it’s done according to the instructions of the Lord.

Show them how it’s done according to God’s word, the Bible.

Treat your children in light of how your heavenly Father treats you.

Treat them with gentleness, patience and kindness.

Nurture and train them so that they can thrive and survive in this world.

Teach them, physical survival skills, driving skills, most importantly, teach them what it means to live with integrity, a life that is worthy of the calling we’ve received from the Lord Jesus.

Earlier this week, I spoke with Nick Hadges, the Scripture Teacher we’ve been supporting.

Nick teaches the Bible at James Cook High and he told me about a young student who became Christian one and a half years ago.

This boy (let’s call him Ed) has as father who was violent.

So violent, in fact that he was sentenced to jail.

He was also known to be a religious man (a religion that’s anti-Christian).

However, Ed wanted to share the gospel of Jesus with his father, because he wants his dad to know the Heavenly Father.

He doesn’t agree with what his earthly father has done and he cannot obey some of the things that his father commands.

But he loves his father and wants to honour him by introducing him to the true God, the Father of our Lord Jesus.

It’s a very, very hard thing to do, but Ed does it, he keeps at it, because he loves his father.

That’s what honouring our parents in the Lord looks like.

Children, how are you obeying your parents in the Lord?

Fathers how are you nurturing and training your children (with mother’s help) in the LORD?

Wouldn’t it be good to see that at Church?

That’s one of the great things we have as an Intergenerational Church.

We help one another to obey, and we help one another to nurture and to train.

Obedience and leadership at work

The Bible then takes us from the home to the office.

As well as children and parents, Paul expected slaves to be in the Church.

Let’s say verse 5 together, “Slaves, obey your human masters with fear and trembling, in the sincerity of your heart, as you would Christ.”

The Bible does something quite unique here.

It addresses slaves as people in their own rights, ethically responsible for their own decisions.

That’s unique in the ancient world because in ancient Rome, slaves are treated as possessions, and they were not free to do what they wanted.

But slaves who have believed in Jesus are set free from sin, the most terrible master of all.

Jesus has come to save them from being nobodies to being dearly loved children of God.

And they are now in the Church, sharing equal standings with freed people as well as masters, because they have all been saved by Christ.

And so, even though they are still slaves to their own human masters, they serve as slaves who have been set free from sin by Jesus.

Being freed by Jesus doesn’t mean that they are free to do whatever they want.

Instead, they are to serve their human masters with fear and trembling.

The phrase, “fear and trembling” is an interesting phrase because it’s often used to refer to God’s presence.

That’s what we read in Philippians 2:12, together, “Therefore, my dear friends, just as you have always obeyed, so now, not only in my presence but even more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling.”

Why? “13 For it is God who is working in you both to will and to work according to his good purpose.”

God’s working in them, that’s why they should work out their salvation with fear and trembling.

And when they work for their earthly masters, they, too are to be working with fear and trembling.

When they work for their boss, they have to work with the same attitude as they would with the Lord himself, that is, with fear and trembling.

In the ancient world, there are many reasons for slaves to serve with trembling and fear.

The slaves had no union to protect them.

There were no fair trading laws.

Is that why they’re serving with fear and trembling?

No! They serve with fear and trembling not because they didn’t have protection, but because they were serving Christ.

Friends, if you are serving the Church in a ministry, you are serving the living God; how can you not serve with fear and trembling?

Do you even realise who you’re serving?

When you serve in the KOG children ministries, you are to serve with fear and trembling, serving Christ!

When you are up here praying, you ought to prepare with fear and trembling, because you are serving Christ.

I’m actually always more worried for people who are over confident in their own abilities to serve than those who serve with fear, especially if it’s the first time they serve.

The overconfident often come to serve with little or no preparation, no practices, no humility.

These are the ones who may be clever but often fall away after a while.

But those who takes serving seriously, who prepares well, who always comes to serve with humility, who might be a little nervous – these are the ones who recognise that they are serving the Lord.

These are the ones who serve the Lord sincerely, from the heart.

They serve sincerely, not while they were being watched.

They don’t serve just to please people, but they serve from the heart.

They are serving the Lord, and not merely just serving the people, even when they serve the people.

When customers look at you serving at counters, when your colleagues see you serving on your computer, they should see how you’re serving God.

And if your God is important to you, you will have a good attitude and produce quality work.

You will serve with a good attitude.

People shouldn’t need to nag you to work, and people should recognise you for your integrity.

You mean what you say because your Lord Jesus says, “Let your yes be yes and your no, no.”

You serve with a good, conscientious, attitude, because you want your business, your company and your boss to succeed.

How about you? Can you be trusted at your work?

What would your boss see if they happen to come and see you when you’re not watching?

Friends, if you’re a worker, give it your best.

In your workplace, you ought to be known as hard-working and honest workers.

That’s what we should be known for, because when we serve our human masters, we serve, we obey, as we would Christ.

If we serve poorly, if we don’t serve with a good attitude, if we’re only working when our boss is around, then we reflect poorly in Jesus.

We’ll be terrible witnesses of Jesus.

Sadly I’ve seen this in many employees before, employees who work far less than what they were paid for.

Some serve well, but only when their boss is watching.

Their boss will think they are doing a good job, but they, will only get the job half done, because they don’t work when the boss is not working.

Of course, some serve badly even when bosses are watching.

However, every now and then, there are people who serve well, because they are wise servants who know that their Boss is always there.

Not their human Boss but the Big Boss, the True Boss, the Lord Jesus.

Over the years, I’ve trained some student ministers and they are some of the hardest working people I’ve ever known.

They put in far more hours than I asked of them, because ultimately they weren’t serving me by Jesus.

I thank God for them all.

As well as servants, there were masters in the same congregation.

Let’s say verse 9 together, “And masters, treat your slaves the same way, without threatening them, because you know that both their Master and yours is in heaven, and there is no favoritism with him.”

Masters are to treat their slaves in the same way.

That doesn’t mean that they are to serve their slaves but to make sure that they themselves are a model employee.

If you’re the boss, you set the standard.

You work with a good attitude, you work with sincerity of heart, you work with integrity, because you’re serving the Lord as well.

You are a fellow servant with your Christian employee.

And if you’re going to be a good boss, you have to work harder than those under your care because you have to look after them, making sure that they are treated fairly and kindly, as your Lord would have you.

You don’t threaten them to obey your commands, but you lead with a Christ-centred leadership.

Jesus Christ is your Lord, and Jesus Christ is their Lord; you’re both answerable to Jesus.

There’s no favouritism with him.

As the boss, you have authority over your employee and you have a responsibility for your employee.

And you must exercise your power responsibly.

You don’t use manipulation and you don’t lead for your own personal gain.

Neither should you abandon any form of power structure, letting employees do whatever they want.

Not having any for of power structure is not helpful for them, and it’s not helpful for your work environment.

It’s because you are responsible for them, you are responsible for your workplace – you have the job of keeping it in order.

You use your authority to help your employees thrive.

You keep your workplace going under the lordship of Christ.

Christians should be the hardest working, most sincere, workers, because they are serving the Lord not men.

They serve the Lord in all areas of their life, including their workplace.

Christians should also be the bosses who are themselves models of what their workers aspire to be.

They are to treat their workers with integrity and fairness, exercising Christ-centred leadership over those who work for you.

It’s hard to be a good boss, but you do it, because you are serving the LORD along with these fellow servants of the LORD.

3. Promises and rewards for the obedient

Though we don’t deserve it, God gives promises and rewards for the obedient.

If you obey your parents, and you honour them, God gives you the promise of living a good and long life.

God also gives this promise to workers, verse 8.

Talking about Christian workers, the Bible says that they should serve with a good attitude, “knowing that whatever good each one does, slave or free, he will receive this back from the Lord.”

What do you now, in your life, in how you obey your parents in their home, in how you work at your desk on Mondays to Fridays, all matters to God.

God will reward you accordingly.

If you do well, if you do the good work of obeying your parents in the Lord and obeying your human masters as to the Lord, God will reward you!

You work hard in your life, not in order to be saved.

God is not going to save you because you have obeyed your parents and worked hard as a Doctor.

It’s by grace that we’re saved.

We’re going to see children who haven’t always obeyed their parents and doctors who aren’t the most hard-working.

If it salvation was by works, none of us would be in heaven for none of us is ever good enough.

If we have to work for our salvation, then it will not be the good news.

The good news of Jesus is that we are saved by faith.

And because we’re saved by faith, we want to obey our parents in the Lord.

Because we’re saved by faith, we want to teach our children in the Lord.

Because we’re saved, we want to serve our employers well.

Because we’re serving the Lord and we want to treat our employees well.

We do all these things in the Lord and for the Lord.

Our Lord sees what we do for his glory, even when no one is looking, even when it’s hard, even when there’s no one to thank or congratulate you.

Our Master see even in the midst of own personal and spiritual opposition.

And our Master promises to reward.

Martin Luther has this wonderful phrase, “When a father washes diapers, he may be ridiculed by some … but God … is smiling—not because that father is washing diapers, but because he is doing so in Christian faith” (“The Estate of Marriage,” Luther's Works, vol 45).

No father really wants to change nappies; no one really want to be there when you change nappies.

No one see you removing the smelly nappies and put the clean nappies back on.

Your wife might thank you, but your baby probably won’t.

When done in faith, even changing nappies is pleasing to God.

What would God say about the way you lived your life on that last and final day?

Would it have been a life of obedience? To your parents? To your boss?

Would it have been a life of Christ-centred leadership over those under your care?

May God help us in our weaknesses.

May he help us to live a life in the Lord Jesus, at work and at home.

And so, friends, as children and workers, fathers and bosses, we obey the Lord by our obedience to man and by our Christ-centred leadership of man.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Children and workers, how do your parents and bosses want you to obey them?

Fathers and bosses, how are you leading them under the gaze of your Heavenly Father and your Master?

How does the promise of God’s reward help us in our obedience to the man (whether they are parents or bosses)?

Children and workers, fathers and bosses, obey the Lord by our obedience to man and by our Christ-centred leadership of man.

Let’s pray, “Lord Jesus, you are glorious and gracious, powerful and patient. Please forgive the stubbornness of our hearts for our unwillingness to give you our whole heart. Please help us see your glory even more, so that we will come before you in obedience. And in that obedience, may we obey our parents, may we obey our bosses. May we obey them not because of our culture or because of we want to please men. Rather, may we obey them because we obey you. And may those of us who lead do so with Christ-centred leadership, knowing that you have given our children and our employees only for a short time. May they thrive under our care, that they may bring glory to you, to the Father and to the Holy Spirit, one God, who reigns forever and ever, Amen.”
