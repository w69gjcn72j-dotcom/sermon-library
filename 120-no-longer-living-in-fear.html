One of Australia’s favourite singers is a man by the name of John Farnham (Farnsy!)

55 years ago, all the way back in 1967, he had a hit song called, “Sadie, the Cleaning Lady”.

Anyone know that song? Maybe not, you’re all too young!

One of his most famous songs is “You’re the Voice”

Many people around Australia know the song; it’s so well-known that it’s almost like an unofficial national anthem.

The words of the chorus are quite striking:

“You're the voice, try and understand it Make a noise and make it clear Oh, whoa We're not gonna sit in silence We're not gonna live with fear Oh, whoa”

Some of us have heard of it.

The song was written as a response to the rising threat of nuclear war back in the 1980’s.

There were protests on the streets against nuclear weapons.

They didn’t want to live in fear and so they shouted in the streets to make their voices heard.

It’s now been almost 40 years since the song was written.

Sadly, the threat of nuclear war is still here.

Even as we speak, people are still in silence and living in fear.

Some live in silence and in fear because of the threat of nuclear war or just the threat of wars in general.

Others are living in fear because they live under oppressive governments or a toxic work environment.

There may be some of us sitting here today living in fear that the rest of us don’t know about.

In today’s passage, we’re told, “Do not be afraid”.

How do we do that? How do we stop living in fear?

If you have your Bible, please open up to John 12, beginning from verse 12.

We’re going to see three things:

A misplaced hope in political power

A fulfilled hope in the Coming King

Shout and testify King Jesus

Before we begin, let’s pray,

“Father, by your Spirit, please help us see the glory of your Son our Lord Jesus Christ, Amen.”

A misplaced hope in political power

Look with me at John 12:12, “The next day, when the large crowd that had come to the festival heard that Jesus was coming to Jerusalem,”

Which day of the week is this?

It’s Sunday; Christians traditionally celebrate this day by calling it Palm Sunday.

The festival they were celebrating was the Passover.

It’s the festival that celebrates God saving the Israelites from the Egyptians.

Ancient historians have said that up to 2.7 million people would be in Jerusalem around the time of Jesus to celebrate the Passover.

The large crowd were very excited because they heard that Jesus was coming.

Some of them would’ve seen or heard about what Jesus did recently in the town of Bethany, which was….

Raising Lazarus back from the dead.

And so, many of them took palm branches and waved them about as Jesus was coming in.

The palm branch was a national symbol for the Jews around the time of Jesus.

And so, they were celebrating the coming of Jesus as someone significant for the Jewish people.

It’s a bit like this symbol here.

What is it? It’s a Boomerang.

It’s a national symbol for Australia.

It’s been around in Australia for 10,000 years.

In fact, the word Boomerang is an European adaptation of the word, “bunmariny” – a Dharug term spoken by the First nation people here, in the Georges River area.

t’s a national symbol for Australia.

The palm branch was a powerful national symbol for the Jews.

They shouted, “Hosanna”, which means, “God saves”.

What did they need to be saved from?

For the average Jew shouting on the street that day, the answer was obvious.

They needed to be saved from the Roman oppression.

They had been living in fear of the Romans for many years.

And so they shouted, using the quote from Psalm 118, “Hosanna!

Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord”

When the Psalmist first wrote the psalm, it was written as a welcome from Jerusalem to all those coming towards the city.

The full verse in the Psalm reads, “He who comes in the name of the LORD is blessed. From the house of the LORD we bless you.”

Over time, the Psalm was used to welcome the one who comes in the name of the Lord to rule in the name of the Lord.

And so, the crowd shouted, “Hosanna! Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord —the King of Israel!”

The Jews had a puppet ruler, who was put there by the Romans.

No, the people wanted a true king, a political king with political power.

There’s a strong political tone here, isn’t there.

As people with no political power, living under oppression, they felt that they had no freedom, no peace.

They wanted freedom from their oppressors, and they hoped that Jesus would give it to them.

And that sounds right, doesn’t it?

If you live in political oppression, you live in the fear of being discriminated against, you live in the fear of losing your job, you may even live in the fear of losing your life.

But once you have political power, everything will be alright.

You’ll have stability and you’ll have peace.

That’s what the Jews were looking for! They were looking for a new political ruler in Jesus.

They wanted to be a people, a nation, living under the rule of Jesus.

Wouldn’t that be great?

And we’ve seen this throughout history.

The early Christians were terribly oppressed by the Romans and unbelievers for hundreds of years.

Under God, Christianity grew and had a massive influence on the Roman world.

Eventually, Charlamagne, a man who identified himself as a Christian, was crowned the Emperor.

He was the ruler of most of Europe.

His official title was, “Charles, most serene Augustus, crowned by God, great and pacific emperor, governing the Roman empire.”

Rather than being politically oppressed, Christians found themselves to be in favour with the emperor.

Sounds like a great thing, right?

Christians, with political power, leading, ruling over the empire.

However, soon after, there were many who called themselves Christians rising through the ranks.

They called themselves Christians not because they were followers of Christ.

They called themselves Christians in order to get political power.

They weren’t interested in following Jesus’ teaching on love, humility, and service.

They identified themselves as Christians in order to rule, to oppress, to steal and to kill.

Sadly, the Church itself became contaminated with people who were there to seek power, and not to seek Christ himself.

And this continues today.

There are people who believes that peace lies in having political power.

What they seek first of all is power and they use the name of Jesus only as a tool.

Power is their end game, their end goal; calling themselves Christians is their means of getting it.

That’s what the Jews were doing at that time.

They welcomed Jesus as the King of Israel, but they didn’t want to follow him.

Because what they wanted wasn’t Jesus himself, but the political power that they thought he was going to give them.

A fulfilled hope in the Coming King

Surprisingly, Jesus didn’t stop them; he didn’t rebuke them.

He found a young donkey and sat on it and continued his way into Jerusalem.

This, too, is a highly political act.

Look with me at verses 14-15, “Jesus found a young donkey and sat on it, just as it is written: Do not be afraid, Daughter Zion. Look, your King is coming, sitting on a donkey’s colt.”

Jesus deliberately chose to enter Jerusalem by riding on a donkey to fulfil not just one but two prophecies.

In Isaiah 40:9 we read,

“Zion, herald of good news, go up on a high mountain. Jerusalem, herald of good news, raise your voice loudly. Raise it, do not be afraid! Say to the cities of Judah, “Here is your God!”

And in Zechariah 9:9 we read,

“Rejoice greatly, Daughter Zion! Shout in triumph, Daughter Jerusalem! Look, your King is coming to you; he is righteous and victorious, humble and riding on a donkey, on a colt, the foal of a donkey”

Jesus is basically saying to the Jews, “I am the King that the prophets have foretold”.

The writer of John goes one step further, “Do not be afraid, raise your voice, for Jesus, who is God, is coming to you.”

It’s a powerful symbol of peace.

Jesus didn’t come on a war horse, ready to lead the Jewish people into a political battle against the Romans.

No, he rode on a donkey, righteous, victorious and humble.

If it happened today, it would have been Jesus coming in a humble hatchback rather than a military tank.

Who drives a hatchback here?

Yes, it’s as if Jesus is coming to the city of Jerusalem in your car, than one of the best tanks in the world, the T-14 Armata from Russia.

Why would Jesus come on a humble donkey rather than a powerful warhorse?

It’s because it’s a done deal; He is in no danger of defeat.

He is so powerful that he doesn’t need the protection of a tank to defend himself or the missiles from a tank to attach his enemies.

He’s here, victory is assured; He will bring peace.

He will bring peace, but it won’t be done through political power.

Look with me at Zechariah 9:10; it’s the verse following the quote we read earlier,

“I will cut off the chariot from Ephraim and the horse from Jerusalem. The bow of war will be removed, and he will proclaim peace to the nations. His dominion will extend from sea to sea, from the Euphrates River to the ends of the earth.

Under the rule of King Jesus, there won’t be any more need for weapons.

The peace will be absolute.

And the extent of his rule will be extensive; his dominion will extend to the ends of the earth.

How will he bring about this peace, this freedom?

Look with me at verse 11,

11 As for you, because of the blood of your covenant, I will release your prisoners from the waterless cistern.”

It’s by the blood of Jesus that he gives his people peace from fear and freedom from oppression.

The greatest and most crucial war that we have is not between one nation against another, nor is it between one ideology against another.

The greatest and most crucial war of all is the one between us and God.

As fallen human beings, we’ve been at war against God since the very first moment we disobeyed him.

Every time we disobeyed God, turning our backs on his commands, we show that we’re at war against God.

As long as we’re at war against God, there will be no peace.

But by dying in the place of sinners, Jesus offers these sinners true peace.

True peace begins when we have peace with God.

No weapons, no technology, none of our own cleverness, can bring us peace with God.

Only the blood of Jesus can make that happen.

There’s only one thing that can bring us peace, the blood of Jesus, shed on the cross.

It’s by his wounds that we are healed.

The land that we’re meeting on today originally belonged to the people of Eora nation.

Studies have suggested that they’ve been here for up to 50,000 years.

However, all that changed 235 years ago with the coming of the English settlement.

Many people of the Eora nation suffered as a result.

The suffering continued for many generations; for many of them, they had no peace.

They were living in fear.

And so, when a white person tries to share the Bible with them, it’s very, very hard for them to hear.

That’s because they hated the white people.

Rev Michael Duckett, pastor of Macarthur Indigenous Church, shared his experience as an indigenous minister, “I’ve seen men who hate white people – who’ve been traumatised and abused by them their whole lives – but when God comes into their lives, their hearts are changed. They know, ‘This is my brother now’, and they’ll sit with them. Only God can change the heart of a person... to love their neighbour as God has said.”

No white man, or any coloured man, can changed the heart of a person.

Only the blood of Jesus can do that.

Only the blood of Jesus can bring true peace, only the blood of Jesus can change our hearts.

The change is not always immediate, but it’s always a marvel to behold.

This is the new life; this is the change that King Jesus has come to give.

He has come to proclaim peace between God and man, peace that leads to love between fellow men, peace that Jesus makes by his blood, shed on the cross.

3. Shout and testify King Jesus

The disciples didn’t understand what was happening at first.

It wasn’t until after Jesus was glorified that they understood.

That is, it wasn’t until Jesus died, resurrected, ascended into heaven, and given his Holy Spirit to them that they understood what happened.

Looking back that the first Palm Sunday, they remembered the Scriptures.

They saw how Jesus fulfilled what the prophets have said.

They worked our who these things were happening to Jesus.

It was all part of God’s plan as foretold in the Old Testament.

In the meantime, there was a large crowd who had witnessed what Jesus did in Bethany, where he….

Raised Lazarus from the dead.

This crowd kept testifying about Jesus, telling people what they saw.

This leads to the making of another crowd.

This crowd heard what the first crowd said and wanted to come and see who this Jesus is.

It’s a case of spreading the news of Jesus by word of mouth.

In America, there’s a fast-food joint by the name of In-and-Out.

In-and-Out is known for their effective use of word-of-mouth advertising.

For example, In-and-Out are known to have secret menus.

These aren’t published officially on their menus or websites but they ‘leak’ the menu out.

To get to the items on the secret menu, you’ll need to hear it from a family or a friend.

And it works for them; everyone wants to get in on the secret!

As the word of Jesus continued to spread through the word of mouth by one crowd to another, the people wanting to come to Jesus continued to grow.

So much so that the Pharisees said to one another, “You see? You’ve accomplished nothing. Look, the world has gone after him!”

What does this mean for us?

Firstly, understand who Jesus is in light of what he did at the cross, the tomb, the ascension and the Old Testament.

These are fundamentals in understanding who Jesus is.

The disciples didn’t understand the significance of what happened on Palm Sunday until after Jesus was glorified, until after Jesus’ death, resurrection and ascension.

They didn’t understand the significance of what Jesus did until they remembered the Old Testament prophecies about Jesus.

Over the years, I’ve seen many people get Jesus wrong when they don’t see him through his death, his resurrection, his ascension and what the Old Testament had said about him.

On top of all these, we need God’s help to understand who Jesus is.

And so, before you read the Bible, pray.

In your Growth Groups, in KOG Youth and KOG Junior, your leaders will help you think through the significance of Jesus’ death, Jesus’ resurrection, Jesus’ ascension and what the Old Testament had said about Jesus.

There’s so much to learn about Jesus, keep working on it!

Second, don’t be afraid but shout out his praise.

For those of us who understood the significance of Jesus’ death and resurrection, we’re in an even better position to shout praises to God than those who welcomed him on Palm Sunday.

We know him not as our political king but as the far superior, far more glorious King who brings us true peace.

Don’t live in fear because your King is near.

And because the King is near, we shout in praise.

Don’t hold back your praises, sing loudly.

Sing louder than the musicians.

Praise Jesus your King with all of your might because he’s worthy of all our praise.

Thirdly, testify who Jesus.

Tell the story of Jesus to others.

The news of Jesus spread by word of mouth back then, it still spreads by word of mouth today.

Share your story of how Jesus saved you.

Tell your families and friends why you follow Jesus.

Let the crowds know the peace he’s won for you.

Don’t be afraid to share your story.

Julius Palmer was a Christian who believed the Bible in the Middle Ages.

He strongly opposed the teaching of the Roman Catholic Church and was arrested and condemned for his views.

On the morning of July 16, 1556, he was publicly burned at the stake with his friends.

As flames were coming all around them, they began to sing a song from Psalm 31.

They were singing, “Be strong and take courage.”

Though they were surrounded by flames, they remembered the Bible and they sang praises to God.

They had every reason to fear, but they’re reminded that they didn’t need to, for the King is near.

And because the King is near, they sing to praise him, even as they suffer for him.

Which Bible verse will you remember in your time of trial? What song will you sing when you’re afraid?

Friends, Stop living in fear, but shout in praise, for King Jesus has come to proclaim peace.

When you do face fear, remember Jesus, the King of Peace, who’s come to bring you peace.

Sing Hosanna, in times of peace and in times of fear, for your King is near.

How do we stop living in fear?

Do we fear tomorrow? The King is near, he holds tomorrow in his hands.

Do we fear rejection? Though your friends forsake you, though your parents forget you, Your King will never abandon you.

Do you fear loss? The King owns everything. Whatever you lose now is nothing compared with the life that he gives.

The King is near.

How do we stop living in fear?

Memorise Bible verses so that they can help you praise God during your trials.

Last week, we heard how ‘Xiao Mung’ memorised Philippians 4:7-8.

That’s a great passage to remember; I’ve printed the passage for you to take home.

How do you stop living in fear?

Memorise songs of praise that gives glory to Jesus.

The crowds sang, presumably from memory, when Jesus came to Jerusalem.

Julius Palmer and his friends sang from memory as they were burning at the stake.

Often, when we sing at Church, I would try to see if I can memorise the words of the song.

For songs I know very well, I would close my eyes and sing from memory.

For me, those moments often become times of prayer especially when those songs are written as prayers to God.
