Passage: Isaiah 53

Big Idea: Jesus took the punishment for our peace with God

Hearing and seeing but not believing

Punished for our peace

The righteous servant justifies

I can hardly believe it, but we’ve been in and out of COVID lockdown for two years!

Many families have waited for the situation to stabilise before taking their families away on a holiday.

And it seems that now’s the time! It’s now time for a quick family get away, maybe down south to Tasmania or up north to Queensland.

It’s time for some peace and quiet, away from the big smoke of our city to the gentle, peaceful countryside or to other capital cities.

The problem is that everyone else is thinking the same thing.

This weekend, Sydney Airport is expected to have 80,000 people travelling interstate.

The airport is stretched to the limit as it seeks to accommodate the travellers, while having 20% less staff due to COVID.

Here’s a picture of the airport at 4:26am on Wednesday morning.

It’s already beginning to be filled with people!

Before the travellers can find peace and quiet at their destination, they need to get through the stress of travelling through the airport first!

We all look forward to enjoying peace and quiet, don’t we?

Even those of us who seek exciting adventures on our holidays, eventually look for the peace and satisfaction that come afterwards.

Peace and satisfaction: they are two things that are hard to find in our world.

I don’t really need to tell you that the whole world has been living with the anxiety that comes from being in a pandemic over the past two years.

There are countries around the world right now that are going through wars, countries like Ukraine, Yemen, and Myanmar, along with many more.

In our own country, we’ve seen the anxiety that comes from rising waters due to the rain over the past few weeks.

Is there anywhere in the world where we can truly find peace at this point in time?

Where can I go to find true, satisfying peace?

In the fifty-third chapter of the Book Of Isaiah, we will see where we can true, satisfying peace.

We’ll see three things in this chapter:

Hearing and seeing but not believing

Punished for our peace

The righteous servant justifies

If you have your Bible there, it will be great if you can open up to Isaiah 53, beginning from verse 1.

Hearing and seeing but not believing

“Who has believed what we have heard? And to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?”

The Arm of the Lord is a reference in the Bible referring to God’s might in saving his people from their enemies.

The kids these days might refer to their powers as their guns, their muscles.

It’s the power of God on display.

Here, the phrase the Arm of the Lord actually refers to a person.

Look with me at verses 2-3,

“He grew up before him like a young plant and like a root out of dry ground. He didn’t have an impressive form or majesty that we should look at him, no appearance that we should desire him.

He was despised and rejected by men, a man of suffering who knew what sickness was. He was like someone people turned away from; he was despised, and we didn’t value him.”

We would expect that the Arm of the Lord will be a mighty person, who will do something spectacular to save God’s people.

Perhaps a team of mighty people, forming a super strong kingdom or government to fight against the enemies.

But God has done something unexpected, something that’s hardly believable.

This mighty person, this Arm of the Lord, will grow like an insignificant young plant, and have nothing impressive about him.

He will be despised and rejected by men; he will be a man of suffering.

He’s not someone that we’ll naturally turn to for rescue.

I still remember a few years ago, a lady by the name of Susan Boyle walked up on stage on a show called British Got Talent.

She was cheerful but otherwise seemed very ordinary.

Even the judges weren’t impressed by her appearance.

But when she opened her mouth and sang “I dreamed a dream”, all their jaws dropped.

She had a wonderful and beautiful voice.

Since then, she’s sold more than 25 million copies of her albums.

At first glance, she wasn’t someone we naturally assume would be able to seriously sing.

However, when the right moment came, she shows that she can sing beautifully.

Likewise, at first glance, the Arm of the Lord seems to be a weak and unable to save.

But at God’s timing, at the right moment, God will reveal the true power of the Arm of the Lord.

The Person that is the Arm of the Lord might be right in front of us, but unless the Lord reveals his true identity to us, we won’t be able to see Him as He truly is.

We’ll only see him as a weak, despised, sickly reject of a man.

And that’s how people saw Jesus.

When they looked at Jesus, they only saw someone who was weak on the outside.

Religious teacher? Sure. Miracle worker? Maybe.

But they didn’t believe that he was who he claimed himself to be.

He said the He was the Son of God, who came to save the world!

Who would believe that?

When the religious teachers and political leaders of Jesus’ day saw Jesus’ influence continue to grow, they hatched a plan against him.

On a Friday morning, the First Good Friday Morning, two thousand years ago, they held an unfair trial and charged him unjustly.

He was sentenced to death on the same day.

The soldiers spat on Jesus, mocked him and twisted a crown of thorns for Jesus to wear.

They despised and rejected Jesus, the Arm of the Lord, just as the prophet Isaiah said they would.

These people might have heard about Jesus’ teachings but they had not understood.

They might have even seen Jesus’ miracles, but they didn’t believe.

How could this wretched, beat-up man be who he the King and Saviour that he said he was?

He was about to be executed on the cross, for crying out loud!

They have heard but they have not believed.

Christians who follow in Jesus’ footsteps have likewise been heard but they have not been believed.

They have shown convincing historical evidence about what Jesus has said and done.

Almost all reputable scholars would agree that the Bible documents we have are good historical records that have been passed faithfully over many centuries.

Millions of millions of Christians throughout the centuries have testified that Jesus is the King and Saviour.

Even so, Christians are despised and rejected for our beliefs in Jesus.

Our world has heard about Jesus, but it has not believed.

What are the consequences to believing in Jesus? What are the consequences for not believing in Jesus?

If we do believe in Jesus, we will have peace.

Because that’s why Jesus came to us.

He came to be punished for our peace.

Punished for our peace

There is a purpose to Jesus’ suffering; he came purposely to suffer for our peace.

Look with me at verses 4-5, “Yet he himself bore our sicknesses, and he carried our pains; but we in turn regarded him stricken, struck down by God, and afflicted. 5 But he was pierced because of our rebellion, crushed because of our iniquities; punishment for our peace was on him, and we are healed by his wounds.”

Jesus, the mighty Arm of the Lord, suffered for our sake; he was pierced on the cross for our rebellion against God.

He was struck down by God purposely, for us.

Jordan and Blake were brothers who were travelling with the Mum, Donna.

They were driving in the rain and somehow got caught in the path of torrents.

Their car was stuck, and they were moments from being swept away.

When the rescuers came, young Jordan, who was thirteen years old at the time, told the rescuers, “Save my brother first”.

And so, the rescuers rescued the ten-year-old Blake.

But they couldn’t come back in time to save Jordan and his mother Donna.

Sadly, Jordan and Donna were swept to their deaths.

Jordan purposely, sacrificially, chose to stay behind so that his little brother could be saved.

Jesus, the Arm of the Lord, suffered for a purpose.

Jesus suffered for us; he was crushed for us.

He was wounded for us; he died for us.

It’s us who have sinned against God; it’s us who have rebelled against God.

Instead of worshipping God as God, we worshipped something or someone else.

To worship something mean to place it as the most important thing in our lives.

It might be our wealth, or happiness, or family.

We might just simply worship ourselves as the most important thing in our lives.

But when we do that, we rebel against God; we set up a rival god in His place.

The Chinese have a saying, “認賊作父” To claim the thief as your father.

That’s what we’ve done! We’ve claimed a thing as God; we’ve given the honour that is due to God to another.

It’s evil and we all do it.

Look with me at verse 6, “We all went astray like sheep; we all have turned to our own way; and the Lord has punished him for the iniquity of us all.”

We stray like sheep but the Lord has punished him, Jesus, for the iniquity, the sin, of us all.

This is what Jesus did when he died on the cross – He was punished by God for our sins.

That’s how the Apostle Peter understood it.

Listen to how he describes what Jesus did on the cross, using the very words of the prophet Isaiah:

“He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree; so that, having died to sins, we might live for righteousness. By his wounds you have been healed. 25 For you were like sheep going astray, but you have now returned to the Shepherd and Overseer of your souls.” 1 Peter 2:24-25.

It’s by his wounds that Christians are healed.

On the cross, Jesus was wounded, Jesus died,

It’s because of what Jesus did on the cross that Christians can have true peace.

It’s a peace that the world cannot give, and the world cannot take away, not now, not ever.

This is a peace that comes because Jesus the righteous servant justifies sinners like us.

The righteous servant justifies

Look with me at verses 10-11, “Yet the Lord was pleased to crush him severely. When you make him a guilt offering, he will see his seed, he will prolong his days, and by his hand, the Lord’s pleasure will be accomplished. 11 After his anguish, he will see light and be satisfied. By his knowledge, my righteous servant will justify many, and he will carry their iniquities.”

Though the religious and the political leaders of the day, treated Jesus unjustly; though the soldiers mocked Jesus mercilessly,

it was all within God’s good pleasure and will.

Though the suffering of Jesus was severe, though Jesus died from it, it wasn’t the end.

After his anguish, Jesus, the Arm of the Lord, will see the light and be satisfied.

There is hope beyond the suffering.

Suffering and death will not be the end of this servant.

He will be the righteous servant, the one who justifies many, the one who will make the unrighteous right, the unjust just.

He justifies them by carrying their iniquities, to pay for what they couldn’t pay themselves.

He paid for them with his own perfect, righteous life.

Jesus does a great exchange

The reward for his righteous life is eternal peace with God.

The punishment for our rebellious life is eternal, severe crushing from God.

But when he died for us, Jesus exchanged his righteous life for our unrighteousness, his peace for our punishment.

When he died on the cross, his exchange was complete; his mission was accomplished.

Richard Knight was left England on a mission last week.

He went with two other friends from the Church on a 28 hour journey to Poland to help the refugees from Ukraine.

They went with a bus load full of aid for the refugees from the Church.

But more than giving aid, they were going to help with the Ukrainian refugees to find somewhere safe to stay until the war was over.

And so, on the journey back, they took eight Ukrainians back.

One of the passengers was a little girl called Hope, born on the day the war began.

Hope and her family are now safe in Dublin.

Richard’s mission was accomplished.

Jesus has accomplished his mission of salvation by justifying rebellions sheepish people like us who go astray.

Like the little girl Hope, there was nothing we could do to save ourselves; we were powerless to save ourselves.

We needed Jesus to come to save us, to rescue us.

After being nailed to the cross for about three hours, Jesus said, “It is finished.”

The mission is accomplished.

He has taken our sins and paid for the punishment that we deserved by dying on the cross.

Our sins are paid for; it’s all over.

God’s demand for our rebellion were met in Jesus.

But Jesus didn’t stay dead; after three days, Jesus rose from the dead, triumphant over death to prove that he’s truly the Lord we can trust.

Jesus was satisfied when he rose from the grave, knowing that God the Father is satisfied with him.

This satisfaction from God the Father is the foundation of what it means for us to have true peace.

We can only have peace when God is at peace with us.

As long as we rebel against God, as long as we war against God, God is not at peace with us, nor are we at peace towards God.

However, when Jesus paid the price on the cross for our rebellion against God, God is satisfied.

God is satisfied with Jesus, and if we are with Jesus, God is satisfied with us.

Our satisfaction, our peace, then, ultimately lies in whether we know Jesus Christ.

We will have true peace when we believe in Jesus Christ.

Today, somewhere around the world, even as we have our service here this morning, Christians will die.

But Christian will die knowing that the One they face, the One who Judges the Living and the Dead is Jesus, their Lord, the One whom they have believed in.

And so even then, even when we face death, we will not be afraid.

On 9 February, a young woman by the name of Brooklyn Salisbury posted on Facebook and Instagram, “I have good news!”

Less than one month later, she died.

What was her good news?

It’s not because there was a cure for her illness; she knew she was dying.

The good news that she shared with her 248,000 followers was that we have hope in Jesus.

Listen to what she said, ““I’m sick. Soon to die. But so are you. I’m just doing it faster… My goal is to highlight God’s strength in our weakness (2 Cor 12:10-12, 11:30). His faithfulness in the midst of suffering. Our future hope in Jesus beyond death (1 Pet 1:3-9). He alone is eternally worthy, and I am only temporarily sick.”

Brooklyn had a month to live but she had peace, true peace, because she has Jesus.

Brooklyn has peace with God because she knows that Jesus took her punishment.

This peace with God is not just for Brooklyn but for all of us here, whether we’re here live in person or watching from home.

Jesus took the punishment for our peace with God.

That’s the good news.

To use a phrase that’s been used many Christian bumper stickers over the years, “Know Jesus, know peace. No Jesus, no peace.”

Knowing Jesus, believing Jesus, is the only way to have peace with God.

That’s the good news.

You’ve heard it, do you believe it?
