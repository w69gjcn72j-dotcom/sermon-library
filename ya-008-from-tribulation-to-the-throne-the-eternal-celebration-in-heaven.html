Young Adults (19–30)

From Tribulation to the Throne: The Eternal Celebration in Heaven

Revelation 7:9–17 · Sunday 30 August 2026 · St Paul’s Anglican Church Kogarah

For young adults (19–30), following the Sunday sermon on Revelation 7:9–17 at the 9:15am service

Big Idea: The tribulation you are in is real, but it is not the end — the throne is. Because the Lamb has already washed you white in his own blood, you are already counted in the crowd no one can number, and God himself will shelter you and wipe away every tear.

Aim: By the end of the evening the group will

1. See that heaven’s crowd crosses every line your feed sorts people into — and let that change how you treat people now.

2. Stop performing for a standing you already have: the white robe was washed in the Lamb’s blood, not earned in your twenties.

3. Name the pressure you are actually under, next to a God who has counted every tear and will wipe them away himself.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • Scroll back through the last week of your camera roll or group chats. Who does it look like you belong to?
  • What are you quietly measuring yourself by right now — marks, salary, whether you are dating, how you compare to the person you graduated with?

Read

9 After this I looked, and there was a vast multitude from every nation, tribe, people, and language, which no one could number, standing before the throne and before the Lamb. They were robed in white with palm branches in their hands.

10 And they cried out in a loud voice: Salvation belongs to our God, who is seated on the throne, and to the Lamb!

11 All the angels stood around the throne, and along with the elders and the four living creatures they fell facedown before the throne and worshiped God,

12 saying, Amen! Blessing and glory and wisdom and thanksgiving and honor and power and strength be to our God forever and ever. Amen.

13 Then one of the elders asked me, “Who are these people robed in white, and where did they come from?”

14 I said to him, “Sir, you know.” Then he told me: These are the ones coming out of the great tribulation. They washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb.

15 For this reason they are before the throne of God, and they serve him day and night in his temple. The one seated on the throne will shelter them:

16 They will no longer hunger; they will no longer thirst; the sun will no longer strike them, nor will any scorching heat.

17 For the Lamb who is at the center of the throne will shepherd them; he will guide them to springs of the waters of life, and God will wipe away every tear from their eyes.

Revelation 7:9–17, Christian Standard Bible

Context

John is an old man exiled on Patmos for his testimony to Jesus, while back on the mainland Rome hunts the church. Into that darkness God opens a door in heaven and shows him how the story ends — not a puzzle to decode, but a picture painted to keep frightened Christians walking. Chapter 7 has just sealed 144,000 from the tribes of Israel: complete, but limited. Now John looks again, and the picture bursts its banks.

Discuss

  1. v.9 — Who is in this crowd, and what four categories does John use? Feeds and campuses sort people into “like us” and “not like us” — what does verse 9 do to that?
  2. v.9 — Standing, robed in white, holding palm branches. What has already happened to them — and what did they have to do to get in?
  3. v.10 — They shout that salvation belongs to God, not “we made it.” Where are you still narrating your own highlight reel?
  4. vv.11–12 — The angels put their faces on the ground. If worship is making God great rather than yourself, what does that do to how you turn up on Sunday, or how you serve?
  5. vv.13–14a — John says, “Sir, you know.” Call-out culture rewards sounding certain. What does it cost you to say “I don’t know” — and why is that maturity?
  6. v.14b — Blood does not bleach things white. So where does your cleanness come from — and what changes on the days you feel like a fraud?
  7. vv.15–16 — List everything God does for his people here. Which lands hardest on a share-house Wednesday when you are broke, tired and behind?
  8. v.17 — God wipes away every tear — it does not say the tears never happened. Why does that matter for sitting with a mate who is not okay?

Live It Out

Our church rhythm is 3 · 2 · 1 · 0. Make it concrete this week:

  • Pray for 3 people from uni, work or your share house who are not Christians yet — by name, every day.
  • Connect with 2 of them properly. Coffee, a gym session, a meal you actually cook. Not a reply to a story.
  • Invite 1 of them. Father’s Day Celebration, “A Father’s Invitation”, is Sunday 6 September — and if your dad is around, bring him too.
  • 0 left unloved. Someone in this room or on the edge of church is new, or has stopped coming. Message them tonight, before you forget.

Memory verse

“God will wipe away every tear from their eyes.” — Revelation 7:17

Pray

  • Thank Jesus that your standing was settled by his blood, not by how your twenties are going, and ask him to loosen whatever you measure yourself by.
  • Pray by name for your three friends who are not Christians yet — that God’s kindness would lead them to repentance, and that you would get one chance to point one of them to Jesus.
  • Bring what you are actually carrying — the degree, the job, family expectations, loneliness — and ask God to hold you until you come out of it.

Leader’s Notes

This age group is fluent in sounding fine. Give the room permission to be unimpressive, and do not solve what someone names — the passage gives it an end point instead.

Guests and not-yet-Christians are common here. Never corner anyone. Point joyfully to Jesus: everyone in that crowd stands because of the Lamb, not because they got their life together first.

Brief answers to the discussion questions

Q1. A crowd no one can number, from every nation, tribe, people and language. It breaks every barrier of race, culture, language and border; Ephesians 2:14, the wall is already down.

Q2. Standing means the sin question is settled; a sinner could not stand before a holy God. White is purity, victory and resurrection glory, washed in the Lamb’s blood (v.14).

Q3. Salvation belongs to God. “We made it” is the instinct behind most of our anxiety and comparison.

Q4. Worship joins something already in progress in heaven, not a performance. That replaces “did I lead that well” with “was God declared great.”

Q5. Maturity is knowing what you do not know. Pride says “I know”; John says “Lord, you know.”

Q6. “Made them white” means being bleached — done to them, not by them. Christ’s death is your acquittal (1 John 1:7), so you are not re-washed every time you fall.

Q7. Shelter (God’s own tabernacle over them), no hunger, thirst or scorching heat (Isaiah 49:10), and service without burnout.

Q8. The tears were real. God does not pretend them away; he stoops and wipes them himself. The job with a struggling mate is presence, not repair.
