The lightning fast and “swallowing” victory of the Lord Jesus

Intro:

When I was growing up there was this popular song called ‘When the going gets tough’.

Who knows that song?

You’re old, like me!

It has a very catchy phrase, “When the going gets tough, the tough get going”.

The idea is that when things get tough, the tough people, will keep going to get the job done.

That’s good, it’s good that the tough keeps going.

However, a good friend of mine changed the words from, “When the going gets tough, the tough get going” to

“When the going gets tough, the tough goes shopping”.

That is, rather than keep going, they give up and go shopping.

They give up.

While we want to be the tough who get going, we often are like the second group and give up instead.

That’s how some people treat their Christian faith.

They like being Christians when things are relatively smooth.

It’s generally easier to be a Christian when things are good.

But when going gets tough, when there is opposition, we might begin to ask ourselves, “Why bother?”

For some of us with young children, it’s already hard getting them to get out of bed on school days.

Do we really have to get them out of bed on Sunday as well?

Many of us have family members who aren’t Christians, and we might have very different views from each other.

Do we really want to stay with what the Bible teaches even the rest of the family doesn’t agree with us?

No matter where you are in your walk with Jesus, you will face challenges as a Christian, you will face difficulties.

Following Jesus is hard work.

Why should I follow Jesus when it’s so much work?

If you have ever asked that question before, you’re not alone.

In fact, the Corinthian Christians from almost two years ago probably asked the same question to Paul, even if it’s not in the exact words.

And we’re going to hear Paul’s answer to them in today’s passage.

If you have your Bible there, please open to 1 Corinthians 15, beginning from verse 45.

In this passage, we are going to see that Jesus is worth following, even if it means hard work.

We are going to see two things:

(Jesus is) The Last Adam who gives us the risen life

The lightning fast and “swallowing” victory of the Lord Jesus

Before we look into it let’s pray,

“Father, please fill our hearts by your Holy Spirit to enable us to hear and believer your words. As we hear from your words, please incline our hearts to and love follow Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

The Last Adam who gives us the risen life

Over the last two weeks, we’ve been thinking about the resurrection of Jesus and how Jesus is the Christian’s hope in this life, in death and in the life beyond death.

We’ve read last week that this life is like a dishonourable seed and that the risen life in Christ is the glorious body.

In today’s we’re going to see focus on Jesus again and what his resurrection means not just for Christians but for every man and woman, every boy and girl.

We’re going to see that he’s the Last Adam who give us the risen life.

Please open your Bible with me to 1 Corinthians 15, beginning from verse 45, “So it is written, The first man Adam became a living being; the last Adam became a life-giving spirit. 46 However, the spiritual is not first, but the natural, then the spiritual.”

Jesus is the last Adam who gives life.

The Apostle Paul wants us to think back again to the idea of creation.

You might remember last week, Paul used the different aspects of life in the creation account to describe what the risen life in the new creation is like.

In today’s passage, he contrasts the epitome, the essence, as it were, between the Old Creation and the New Creation.

The highlight of the old creation is Adam, the first man, made in the image of God.

The highlight of the new creation is Jesus, whom Paul calls the Last Adam.

According to Genesis 2, God created man by forming him from dust and breathed the breath of life into him, and the man became a living being.

Once God breathe his breath of life into the first Adam, he starts living.

However, the Last Adam doesn’t need God to breathe into him to live.

Jesus, the Last Adam, is a life-giving spirit.

He gives life to lifeless things; life flows out of the Last Adam.

It’s like what happened on one occasion in the time of Ezekiel the prophet in the Old Testament.

God brought the prophet Ezekiel to a valley full of dry bones and told him to prophesy to the bones.

God told Ezekiel to tell these dead bones to rise and live.

As Ezekiel was prophesying, the bones came together and started to form flesh.

Wind then came blowing in and breathed life into them.

That’s a glimpse of what Jesus the Last Adam would do.

The Last Adam would give life to the lifeless.

The Last Adam would give life to the dead and bring them back to life.

What the first Adam went through was natural: God breathed life into him and he lived.

What the Last Adam will bring will be spiritual: He himself will bring life, new, risen life, to others.

It’s a massive contrast!

It’s like the difference between a battery-powered toy and the sun.

The toy will move and respond as long as it has the battery in it.

When the battery dies, so does the toy.

But the sun has so much energy that it powers billions upon billions of lifeforms here on planet earth.

The power is incomparable.

Of course, Jesus has more life-giving power than even the sun.

For even the sun will one day run out of energy, even the sun will fade one day.

But not Jesus; he is the life-giving spirit who gives everlasting life.

Jesus is the Last Adam who is the life-giving spirit.

He is the Last Adam.

He is Humanity’s last hope; there is no one else like him.

Jesus is the Last Beginning for us; there is no more Adam after Him.

And what an incredible Last Adam we have in Jesus!

Being the life-giving spirit that he is, He is far, far superior to the first Adam.

Look with me at verses 47-48,

“he first man was from the earth, a man of dust; the second man is from heaven. 48 Like the man of dust, so are those who are of the dust; like the man of heaven, so are those who are of heaven.”

By default, every person who’s been born are like the first Adam.

Like Adam, we’re born of dust and like Adam, we’ll return to dust, we’ll die in hopelessness.

But now in the Last Adam, in the new Adam, we have a new hope.

For the Last Adam is of heaven, he belongs to heaven.

If we belong to the Last Adam, the Man of Heaven, we’ll be like him, we’ll go to heaven with him.

That’s the question we have to ask ourselves: Do we belong to the First Adam, the man of dust or the Last Adam, the Man of Heaven?

The Adam we belong to not only determines whether we’ll end up, it will also determine our relationship with God.

Look with me at verse 49, “And just as we have borne the image of the man of dust, we will also bear the image of the man of heaven.”

The first Adam had borne God’s image but now that image has been damaged.

However, in Jesus, the perfect image of God is restored.

Those who follow Jesus will now bear the image of Jesus, who is the perfect image of God.

That is, those who follow Jesus will reflect God in a far clearer, far superior way than anyone who’s trying to reflect God’s image without Jesus.

Christians who follow Jesus bears the image of the man of heaven, who is the perfect, untainted image of God.

A few weeks ago, a young man came to Church on a weekday to talk about some difficulties he’s been facing.

He told me that he sees God in everyone and he is frustrated when people don’t understand what he sees.

However, while I understand his frustration, I told him that we don’t see God in people but an image of God.

And more than that, what we see in others and what others see in us are broken images of God.

That’s where the frustration lies.

When we look at each other, we see each other through broken lenses for we, too are broken, just like the person we’re speaking to.

We are broken images of God, speaking to other broken images, through broken lenses.

But in Jesus, we see the perfect image of God and what it means to truly reflect God’s image.

As followers of Jesus, we follow Jesus imperfectly, but when we do follow him, we reflect a small glimpse of God’s restored image in Jesus.

For when we follow Jesus, we bear the image of the man of heaven,

And so, follow the Man of Heaven; follow Jesus!

He is the Last Adam – He is our Last Hope.

He is the Last hope for human to bear the image of God, to be restored to where God had created us to be.

And it’s only through the Last Adam that we have any hope of inheriting God’s kingdom.

For in Jesus, we see a lightning fast and a swallowing victory.

The lightning fast and “swallowing” victory of the Lord Jesus

Look with me at verses 50, “What I am saying, brothers and sisters, is this: Flesh and blood cannot inherit the kingdom of God, nor can corruption inherit incorruption.”

Flesh and blood cannot inherit God’s kingdom.

Those who belong to the First Adam cannot inherit God’s kingdom.

Left to ourselves, all of us who have flesh and blood, cannot inherit God’s Kingdom.

But there is hope for people like us.

Verse 51, “51 Listen, I am telling you a mystery: We will not all fall asleep, but we will all be changed, 52 in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised incorruptible, and we will be changed.”

For those who trust in Jesus, who follow the Man of heaven, we can look for a moment when we will all be changed.

It will happen as quickly, in a moment.

In the original language the word is atomos, where we get the word ‘atom’.

That is, in the smallest amount of time, in the twinkling of an eye, we’ll be changed.

It’ll be as quick as… a flash.

It’ll be gone in a blink,

When that happens, the trumpet shall sound!

It’ll be the announcement that the Lord Jesus is here!

It’s the announcement that victory is at hand!

It will all happen very quickly.

Look with me at verse 53, “For this corruptible body must be clothed with incorruptibility, and this mortal body must be clothed with immortality.”

We were originally naked in our flesh and blood; we were naked in our corruptible, mortal body.

But when Jesus comes, when the trumpet sounds, we will be clothed with incorruptibility, we’ll be clothed with immortality.

In our flesh, we’re naked with dishonour.

But when we’re raised from the dead, we’ll be honoured with a new body.

In our flesh, we’re vulnerable to decay and death.

However, in Jesus, even death is swallowed up.

Look with me at verses 54-55, “When this corruptible body is clothed with incorruptibility, and this mortal body is clothed with immortality, then the saying that is written will take place:

Death has been swallowed up in victory.

55 Where, death, is your victory?

Where, death, is your sting?

What a striking image: Death has been swallowed up.

What has been the most interesting thing that you’ve swallowed up or eaten over this past week?

It might be a special meal or an exotic delicacy of some sort?

What has been the most interesting thing that you’ve swallowed up or eaten over this past week?

Send your answer by whatsapp or wechat or Zoom.

You have twenty seconds!

In English, there’s a phrase that says, “I have them for breakfast.”

The meaning is that someone is so sure that they’ll win that they’re said to have their opponents for breakfast.

For example, someone might ask, “Will this football club win the match?”

The supporters of the football club might reply, “No worries, they’ll have the other team for breakfast!”

Or someone might say, “Will Sue be alright at the job presentation?”

To which Sue’s friend might reply, “She’s done presentations like this many times. She has them for breakfast!”

The idea is that the person will success to effortlessly that it’s like them having an easy, unhurried breakfast.

That’s how successful Jesus will be over death!

How will Jesus conquer death? He’ll swallow death up. He’ll conquer it like he’s having it for breakfast!

Jesus will have an easy victory over our greatest enemy, death.

Where is death’s victory? It’s not there because Jesus has won.

The sting of death is sin.

If you imagine death to be like a scorpion, then sin is like the sting of the scorpion.

Sin is where death strikes.

Usually, we think of death as the result of sin.

We die because we sin.

This is true but here, Paul turns the idea around.

Why do we sin?

We sin because we are dead.

Not dead physically, but dead spiritually.

Because we’re spiritually dead on the inside, we sin.

When we sin, it shows that we are spiritually dead.

Sin stings because when we sin, it shows that we’ve broken God’s laws.

That’s why the power of sin is the law; it shows us that we’re sinners.

We sin when we fail to keep God’s law and when we sin, it shows that we’re spiritually dead.

As spiritually dead beings, we head toward a future of being spiritually dead forever.

And so, we’re spiritually dead, we sin, and we head toward a future of being spiritually dead.

However, look with me at verse 57, “But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ!”

Thanks be to God indeed, who gives us the victory through Jesus.

For those who believe in Jesus, we also begin with being spiritually dead, we also sin

But now rather than facing a future of being spiritually dead, Jesus has swallowed up death so that we’ll have life.

We will one day rise from the dead and live with him forever!

What we have now, what we commonly call life, is but a seed.

What comes after is what God calls life.

What comes after is life that is truly life.

Jesus has swallowed up death for breakfast!

There is victory in him, forever.

Nothing else compares.

And so, in light of the life and the victory we have in Jesus, Paul ends with these words, in verse 58, “Therefore, my dear brothers and sisters, be steadfast, immovable, always excelling in the Lord’s work, because you know that your labour in the Lord is not in vain.”

Though we will face opposition as Christians, though life will be hard, be steadfast.

Be solid!

Remain immovable – don’t let the troubles and oppositions you face move you away from Christ.

Always excelling in the Lord’s work; don’t give God what your minimum effort.

Give God your maximum effort, excelling in his work.

For you know that your work in the Lord is not in vain!

Knowing that we serve the life-giving Lord is what keeps us going as Christians.

Knowing that Jesus eats death for breakfast means that we’ll get up on Sundays mornings and take ourselves and our kids to Church.

Knowing that Jesus will raise up on the last day with the trumpet sound will keep us following him, no matter what opposition we might face.

We’ll keep going because we know that what we do for the Lord is never in vain!

Over the last eighteen months, we had planned for so many things for Church.

We were going to have a mission, we were going to have a Kids’ Club in the holidays, we were going to have a Thanksgiving Dinner in March last year!

Even for the plans that we attempted, the amount of effort we put in does not always produce the result that we want to see.

Looking back, it looks like they were all planned and done in vain.

However, despite the appearance, all the things we’ve done in the Lord, from the sweeping the floor to leading from the front to teaching the Bible, when it’s done in faith, is never done in vain.

I remember many years ago when I was looking after a group of kids in my old Church.

There was no one really leading the kids’ ministry and teenagers would often be rostered on to look after them.

The kids were generally rowdy and noisy as they already had Sunday School and were just waiting for their parents to finish Church.

As one of the teenagers who looked after them, it’s easy to think that we were just wasting our times, just as we were wasting theirs.

Why bother?

Over time, I was convicted that we needed to look after them properly.

And so, we started something called Junior Worship for them.

It was a time to teach them to worship God using the gifts that God’s given them.

It was hard work, not only for me and Jenny but for all the kids and families involved.

Was it worth it?

Looking back at that little group, I thank God that two of them have now been serving as ministers in Churches around Sydney.

One of them has been actively involved in the band Cityalight for many years.

And many more have been serving in different Churches ever since.

We didn’t know it at the time, we didn’t know where our hard work will lead to, but God knows.

For our part, we needed to remain steadfast and immovable, excelling in the Lord’s work, for we know that our labour in the Lord is not in vain.

And so friends, whatever ministry you’re doing now, keep working hard at it, excel in it.

It’s not in vain!

Many of us here live busy lives with many different commitments.

However, which of the work that we do will have truly lasting effects?

In all of our work, which ones will end up like the Man of Dust, destined to be forgotten?

Which ones will end up being recognised by the Man of Heaven and celebrated by the angels in heaven?

In light of eternity, will our life’s work be in vain?

All of our life’s work, left to themselves, will be in vain.

All our life’s work, if it’s not done for Jesus’ glory, no matter how glamorous it is to the world, will be all for nothing.

However, if our life’s work is to do the Lord’s work, it will not be in vain.

If our life’s work is to do what the Lord has asked us to do, to trust him, to love him, to love our neighbours, to share the good news of Jesus with them,

It will never be in vain.

And so, friends, remain immovable in your labour in the Lord.

Jesus is our Last Adam and he is our Swallower of Death.

And so live for him, labour for him, be immovable in your labour in him.

What does this mean for us?

Live for Jesus, the Swallower of Death

Before the invention of the airplanes, everyone who came to Australia did so by boat.

On 4 August 1845, the boat Cataraqui was in Bass Strait, the stretch of water between Victoria and Tasmania.

The boat had been travelling from Liverpool, England since 20 April and had a total of 410 people.

As it was approaching Tasmania, it hit some of the rocks around Tasmania and began to sink.

Of the 410 people, only nine survived; 400 people died.

On that day, the sea swallowed up 400 people.

And death is like the sea, swallowing up everyone who’s been born from the time of the first Adam until now.

Everyone, whether you’re rich or poor, man or woman, educated or uneducated, will be swallowed by death.

But thanks be to God for Jesus has swallowed up death in victory.

Jesus is greater than even death, for Jesus has risen from the dead.

And so, what will you do with your life?

Will you live a life only to be swallowed up by death?

Or will you live for the Swallower of death?

Friends, don’t waste your time on the things of this earth.

Rather use your time, use your resources, to labour, to work hard, in the Lord.

Use your gifts at Church, excel in the gifts he’s given you.

Give him the honour whether you’re at Church or at school or at work.

Live for Jesus, the Swallower of Death.

Here are the three questions for this week:

Does my life look like the Man of Dust or the Man of Heaven?

Which of my work is done in vain? Which aren’t done in vain?

Will I be ready when Jesus comes in the twinkling of an eye?

Friends, when things get hard, when there is opposition, when we ask ourselves, “Why bother?”

Remember: Live for Jesus, the Swallower of Death.

Father, please forgive us when we’re tempted to move away from our service of you. Please make us immovable, always excelling in our service of the Lord Jesus. Please let us rejoice in the victory that we have in Jesus, the great Swallower of death, in whose name we pray, Amen.”
