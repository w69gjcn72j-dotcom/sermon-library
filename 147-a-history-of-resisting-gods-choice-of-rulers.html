In 1970, a farmer by the name of Leonard Casley had a dispute with the Western Australia government over his farm.

Leonard Calsey could physically harvest 4000 hectares of wheat, but the government only allowed him to harvest 40 hectares.

As a result, Leonard Calsey decided to secede himself and his farm from Australia.

That means, he doesn’t recognise the Western Australian government’s rule over him; he will set up his own rules.

In fact, he appointed himself to be “His Majesty Prince Leonard 1 of Hutt” and his farm would be his micronation.

He even issued his own currency, his own stamps and even his own passports (which no country, not even Australia, recognizes).

He did all that to resist the Western Australian government and also, to avoid paying taxes due to the Western Australian government.

When he died in 2019, he owed more than $2.5million to the Australian Tax Office, which his son inherited.

In 2020, the farm was sold off to pay the debt and Leonard Calsey’s micronation was no more.

Even though Leonard Calsey might have thought he had the authority to rule over his little micronation, he really didn’t.

He was merely resisting the government who actually had the authority to rule over him.

As Christians, we declare that Jesus is Lord.

We often say it so much that we forget what the word means.

The word Lord means Master, the One who rules, the One we obey.

Christians say that Jesus is not just Lord over Christians but Lord over all the earth, over everyone and over everything.

Jesus is the Lord of all!

There are those who will recognise Jesus as the rightful ruler of all and there are those who will resist Jesus as the rightful ruler.

Like the case of Leonard Calsey, we have a choice to make: do we recognise Jesus’ rule or do we resist Jesus’ rule?

How do we respond to the claim that Jesus is Lord?

Do we resist or do we recognise him as the ruler?

That’s what we’re going to explore in today’s passage.

So please open up with me to Acts 7, beginning from verse 1.

We’re going to see four things:

The God of glory chooses Father Abraham

The God of glory saves through brother Joseph

The God of glory rules through the exile Moses

Resisting or recognizing Jesus as God’s chosen saviour and ruler

Before we do, let’s pray, “Father, by your Spirit, please soften our hard hearts so that we might hear your word speak into our lives, that we might bring glory to Jesus, in whose name we pray, Amen.”

The God of glory chooses Father Abraham

Last week, we read that Stephen was charged by some of the religious leaders to speak falsely about Mose and God.

Look with me at Acts 6:13-14, “They also presented false witnesses who said, “This man never stops speaking against this holy place and the law 14 For we heard him say that this Jesus of Nazareth will destroy this place and change the customs that Moses handed down to us.”

Stephen was charged with speaking against the temple in Jerusalem, the heart of worship for many Jews.

This is the holy place where they come to worship God.

More than that, Stephen was charged with speaking against Moses’ customs.

Moses was one who passed God’s laws down to the Jews.

They were saying that Stephen and his message about Jesus was a threat to their temple and to the customs of Moses.

And so, the high priest asked Stephen, “Are these things true?”

Stephen began by pointing them back to the beginning of their nation.

He pointed them back at God’s calling of Abraham, some two thousand years before Stephen’s time.

Look with me at chapter 7 verse 2-3, ““Brothers and fathers,” he replied, “listen: The God of glory appeared to our father Abraham when he was in Mesopotamia, before he settled in Haran, 3 and said to him: Leave your country and relatives, and come to the land that I will show you.”

The history of the Jews started with God

It was God himself, the God of glory, who took the initiative to do the extraordinary work of making Abraham into the Father of the Jews.

It was the God of glory who appeared to Abraham, not in Jerusalem, but in ancient Mesopotamia, which is in modern day Iraq.

God told him to leave Mesopotamia, moved to different places and finally to settle in Canaan, the very place where Stephen and the Jews were standing on.

However, though God told him to move to Canaan, God didn’t give him the land, yet.

Abraham, didn’t even own the land he was standing on.

But God promised to give this land to him and his children as an inheritance.

And so, Abraham, landless and at that time, childless, depended on the promises of God and trusted that God will keep his promises.

In today’s terms, Abraham would be stateless.

A stateless person is a person who don’t belong to anywhere.

Today, there are at least 10 million stateless people in the world.

They don’t have nationality; no country would recognise them as their citizens.

They are like these children in Telipok, Sabah, Malaysia.

They are children of immigrants with no official records of their nationality and so they have no access to education.

They are stateless children.

God was telling Abraham to be stateless.

He had left his home country behind, and he would live the rest of his life as a stateless foreigner in Canaan.

He would eventually have a son in Canaan, and his family will continue to live in Canaan, but in verse 6, God said that one day, they would be strangers in a foreign country, and they would enslave and oppress Abraham’s descendants for four hundred years.

Abraham’s children would not only be stateless, but they would be slaves of a foreign nation, which was a lot worse than even being stateless.

However, that would not be the end, for God himself would judge the nation that made Abraham’s descendants slaves and they would come out and worship God.

God gave Abraham a glimpse into the next 500 years or so.

How would you respond?

You and your children would spend the next 500 years firstly living as stateless people then sold off to slavery.

Many of us would probably say to God, “No thanks”

But Abraham wasn’t like most of us.

He responded in obedience.

God gave Abraham the covenant of circumcision, which was a sign that they belonged to God.

Abraham accepted that life would be hard for him and his descendants, that theyu would be oppressed and treated unfairly.

However, Abraham knew that God would save them, and they would one day come back this place to worship God.

Abraham didn’t resist God but obeyed God instead because he recognised that God is the God of glory.

A lot of times, we struggle to obey God, don’t we?

We struggle not necessarily because what God is asking for is hard, but it’s because we don’t recognize that he is the God of glory.

We resist to obey God because we don’t recognise him as the one who rules.

Most of us will never be put in a position anywhere near Abraham’s position.

Most of us probably won’t be asked to be stateless and I don’t think any of us will even entertain the thought of our children and their children to live as slaves.

Even so, many of us still struggle to obey because unlike Abraham, we don’t recognize God is the God of glory, that He is the One who rules.

We fail to recognise that God is the One who has all history in the control of his hands, the One who rescues his people.

We might sing with our lips that Jesus is Lord, but do our choices say the same thing?

Frances Ridley Havergal was a Christian songwriter in the 1800’s who wrote the beautiful Christian Hymn, “Take My life and let it be, consecrated Lord to Thee.”

In one of the verses, she wrote the words, “Take my silver and my gold, Not a mite would I withhold”.

She asked Jesus to take all her belonging, all the money that belonged to her.

A few years after she wrote these beautiful words, she put it into practice.

She gave away almost all her wealth to the Church Missionary Society, also known as CMS, keeping only a few items for herself.

She sang devotion to Jesus and she lived out her devotion to Jesus, because she recognsied that Jesus is the rightful ruler of her life.

Wouldn’t it be good to sing our songs with the same devotion? With the same conviction?

Wouldn’t it be incredible to hear our songs sung not because they sound nice but because we mean what we sing, that Jesus is indeed the Lord of all?

It’s nice to sometimes hear harmony when we sing together at Church.

But what’s infinitely better is when we see there’s harmony between the words that we sing and the life that we live, because we truly recognise that Jesus is Lord.

The God of glory saves through brother Joseph

The God of glory chose Abraham and Abraham recognised the rightful rule of God in his life.

He passed on what he knew to his son Isaac.

Isaac in turn passed this on to Jacob and Jacob passed this onto his twelve sons.

These twelve great-grandsons of Abraham are called the Patriarchs.

They were the fathers of the twelve tribes of Israel.

Before they became fathers of their tribes, they were brothers who betrayed Joseph, one of their own.

Look with me at verse 9, “The patriarchs became jealous of Joseph and sold him into Egypt, but God was with him.”

Why were the brothers jealous of Joseph?

It was because their father Jacob gave him special attention because Joseph was the son of the wife Jacob loved.

It was also because God was revealing the future to Joseph through dreams.

In Joseph’s dreams, there was clear indication that one day Joseph would rise to be the ruler above the brothers.

The brothers didn’t like that and sold Joseph to slavery in Egypt.

The brothers committed human trafficking agaist their own brother out of jealousy.

I remember meeting an older Chinese Christian sister who was always full of joy.

However, one day, she shared with us about how her older sister sold her to strangers for money.

She still found it very hard to forgive her after all these years.

This Christian sister was clearly still hurt and bitter about what her sister did.

However, bitterness wouldn’t be the defining characteristic of Joseph’s life.

God was with him and gave him favour.

God even gave him favour and wisdom in the sight of Pharaoah, the ruler of Egypt.

Pharoah recognised that God’s favour and wisdom was with Joseph and so he appointed Joseph to rule over Egypt and even over Pharoah’s household.

Joseph then used his authority to save the Egyptians.

By God’s favour and timing, Joseph even saved the brothers who had sold him to Egypt in the first place!

The brothers initially rejected what God’s revealed to Joseph.

However, as things turned out, what God had revealed to Joseph in his dreams came true; the brothers bowed down to Joseph in recognition of his rule over them.

Despite the brothers’ rejection and cruelty, God made Joseph their ruler and saviour.

Their very lives depended on Joseph’s rule and Joseph’s mercy.

What happened to Joseph was a pattern of what’s to come.

Here’s the pattern:

God chosen a ruler.

But God’s people failed to recognize God’s chosen ruler and resist him instead.

Even so, God’s chosen ruler is eventually vindicated.

God’s chosen ruler will rule and will save, despite the initial resistance of God’s people.

In Joseph’s case:

God had chosen Joseph.

But the brothers failed to recognize Joseph as God’s chosen ruler and resisted him instead.

Even so, God’s chosen ruler Joseph was eventually vindicated.

As God’s chosen ruler, Joseph ruled and saved the brothers, despite their initial resistance.

The God of glory rules through the exile Moses

We see the same pattern happening in the life of Moses.

Look with me at verses 17-18, “As the time was approaching to fulfill the promise that God had made to Abraham, the people flourished and multiplied in Egypt, until a different king who did not know Joseph ruled over Egypt.”

God had promised that Abraham’s descendants would be strangers in a foreign country and they would be slaves for four hundred years.

The new Pharoah, who didn’t know Joseph, forced the Israelites to abandon infants outside to let them die.

He was committing mass infanticide, the killing of babies, on the Israelites.

There was one baby who escaped the infanticide; his name was Moses.

Why was Moses spared from the infanticide? It wasn’t because he was more clever and more worthy.

The reason the Bible gave was that he was beautiful in God’s sight.

It was out of God’s mercy that Moses was kept safe.

Pharoah’s daughter saw him in the river and eventually adopted him, almost certainly against the will of her father!

Moses was then educated in the schools for royalty in Egypt, learning all their wisdom, and he was powerful in speech and actions.

He was living as a prince of Egypt.

When he was forty years old, he visited his people, the Israelites, with the hope of helping them.

He saw one of his fellow Israelites who mistreated by an Egyptian and Moses responded by killing the Egyptian.

When he saw two Israelite men arguing the next day, he wanted to be the peace maker.

He wanted to help them.

Instead, he was rejected by the one who was doing the mistreating.

Look with me at verse 27, “But the one who was mistreating his neighbour pushed Moses aside, saying: Who appointed you a ruler and a judge over us? 28 Do you want to kill me, the same way you killed the Egyptian yesterday?”

Knowing that he wasn’t safe in Egypt anymore, Moses became an exile, living in Midian, where he fathered two sons.

Moses was a descendant of Abraham living away from the land that God promised Abraham,

Living away from the land of Egypt where he was living like a prince for 40 years,

Living away from his own people.

He stayed there forty years.

How old does that make Abraham?

Eighty.

At eighty years old, he was looking after the sheep when he saw a bush that was burning.

It was an angel of God appearing before him and Moses heard the voice of God spoke.

God said, “I am the God of your ancestors—the God of Abraham, of Isaac, and of Jacob.”

Moses met God, not in Abraham’s homeland, not in Egypt, the home of the Israelites for more than 400 years, but here in the wilderness of Midian.

It was in the wilderness that God called Moses.

God is not restricted to one place; God can engage with us from anywhere he chooses.

God had seen the oppression that his people were going through and God had heard their groaning.

Now is time for Moses to go back to Egypt.

The rejected prince, the exiled prince will now come back to be their ruler and saviour of God’s people.

Moses will continue to do this for another forty years.

How old would that make Moses by the time he’s finished? 120.

Again, in the life of Moses, you see the pattern:

God had chosen Moses.

But his fellow Israelites failed to recognize Moses as God’s chosen ruler and resisted him instead.

Even so, God’s chosen ruler Moses was eventually vindicated, after God appeared to him in the burning bush and after he led the Israelites out of Egypt.

As God’s chosen ruler, Moses ruled and saved the Israelites, despite their initial resistance.

God’s people constantly find themselves in trouble and danger and God keeps sending his saviour to them.

We see this repeated again and again throughout history.

The question is will they recognise God’s chosen ruler or will they resist him?

Resisting or recognizing Jesus as God’s chosen saviour and ruler

Why did Stephen tell all these stories about Abraham, Joseph and Moses?

Stephen recounted these stories in response to the charges that they made against Stephen.

They said that Stephen spoke against this place and the law.

They accused Stephen of talking about this Jesus who would destroy this place and change the custom Moses handed down to them.

However, Stephen was preparing a counter argument:

Long before Jerusalem, long before the law was given, God’s been sending his chosen rulers, rulers who were full of wisdom, full of God’s favour, rulers who were powerful in action and in speech.

God’s people failed to recognise these rulers time and time again.

Instead, they resisted these rulers whom God’s sent to save them.

That was the consistent pattern: They failed to recognize God’s chosen ruler and instead resisted to sit under his rule.

In time, God’s sent his Supreme Saviour, the Lord Jesus Christ

Will they now resist Jesus, just as their ancestors did before them?

Or will they recognise that he’s the One sent by the God of glory, the same God who had called Abraham, the same God who showed favour to Joseph, the same God who spoke to Moses in the burning bush?

Will the recognise that Jesus is the One sent by the God who is in charge of all history?

What Stephen is preparing us to see is that in Jesus, you see the same pattern as you see in Joseph and Moses.

God had chosen Jesus.

But the Israelites and the Gentiles (the non-Israelites) failed to recognize Jesus as God’s chosen ruler and resisted him instead by nailing him to the cross.

Even so, God’s chosen ruler Jesus was eventually vindicated, after God raised him from the dead.

As God’s chosen ruler, Jesus ruled and saved those who believed in him, despite their initial resistance.

And we can add one more point to Jesus:

As God’s chosen ruler, Jesus will rule heaven and earth absolutely despite the world’s initial resistance to him.

If we recognize Jesus as the ruler that he truly is, then he will have the final word on all things in our lives.

If something else has the final word, then that thing is what reigns supreme in our lives.

For example, Jesus wants you to love your brothers and sisters just as he loves us, but we don’t do it because we don’t like them.

If Jesus is supreme, you will love them even though you don’t like them, because that’s what Jesus commands.

You’ll do things for them, you’ll sacrifice for them, because Jesus reigns supreme in your heart.

If you don’t love them because they are not your type of people you like, then it’s you comfort that reigns supreme in your heart, not Jesus.

May God help us to let Jesus reign supreme in our hearts!

We can look at it from the other way, of course.

If you don’t recognize him as ruler, then you are resisting him.

God has chosen Jesus to rule everywhere, but when we don’t obey him, we’re resisting his rule.

When we resist him, we act like the jealous brothers, we act like the Israelite who oppressed his brother.

We resist the One whom God’s chosen as ruler and saviour.

We can resist Jesus actively by directly doing what he tells us not to do.

Or we can resist Jesus passively by not doing what he tells us to do.

If we are still resisting Jesus, then we will find ourselves in the hands of an angry God, for we have shown contempt for his one and only Son.

May God lead us away from resisting against Jesus!

Do we resist or do we recognize Jesus as the ruler?

If you are not a Christian yet, then my prayer for you is that you will recognize who Jesus is.

God has shown him as his chosen ruler and saviour.

Recognise him!

If you are a Christian, you will continue to feel the resistance in you to recognize Jesus as king.

Pray for the Spirit’s help to resist the resistance and to recognize the ruler.

Pray that you will not be just praying this for yourself, but also for those whom God’s placed around you.

Don’t resist Him!

And so, friends, don’t resist Jesus but recognize him as the one through whom God’s sovereignly chosen to be ruler and saviour.
