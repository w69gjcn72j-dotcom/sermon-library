Zeal for the Father's House

Sermon Title: Zeal for the Father’s House

Passage: John 2:12-25

Big Idea: Jesus is consumed with zeal for his Father’s House, the old Temple, which anticipates Jesus himself as the new Temple.

The Temple has turned into a shopping centre!

The Temple is “my Father’s House”

The Temple is Jesus’ Body

Gordon James Ramsay Jr. is a famous chef, restaurateur, television personality, writer, and former footballer.PPT

You have probably seen him once or twice on the television, yelling at someone he’s been training in the kitchen.

I have to admit I’m not a huge fan of him using foul language over and over again.

However, most of us would probably agree that he’s absolutely serious about people producing good food in the restaurant.

He wants to bring out the very best in chefs and he pushes them hard.

We see this in his own restaurants, which had been awarded a total of 16 Michelin stars.

The Michelin stars are how the best restaurants are rated.

The more Michelin stars, the better the restaurant.

Whatever you think about Gordon Ramsay, you have to say that he has a passion, a zeal to produce the best food.

He says, “I cook, I create, I’m incredibly excited by what I do.”PPT

He’s singularly driven by cooking.

Today, we’re going to look at Jesus, who’s also driven, but not for cooking.

Rather Jesus has a passion, a zeal to see God honoured and we’re going to see what happens when the worship of God is dishonoured.

Let’s pray, “Father, please help us to understand your word, feel the gravity of your teaching and to obediently do your will. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”

The Temple has turned into a shopping centre!PPT

Please open the Bible with me to John 2:13, “The Jewish Passover was near, and so Jesus went up to Jerusalem.”

The Passover happens once a year on the 14th Day of the Jewish month of Nisan.

It’s still being celebrated by many Jews today; this year, it’ll be on 8th April.

For thousands of years, the Jews celebrated the Passover to remember how God has saved the Israelites from the Land of Egypt,

when they were still slaves in Egypt.

Jesus and his disciples went up to Jerusalem to celebrate the festival.

They would’ve gone up the hill where Jerusalem was situated and walked into the temple.

The temple they saw was Herod’s Temple.

It was a magnificent building that was almost 500 metres long, 275 metres wide and 46 metres high.PPT

People from all over the Roman empire used to go and worship God there.

However, people couldn’t go in anywhere they choose.

Only the priests were allowed to go in the inner area of the Temple.PPT

Then only Jewish men were allowed in the Court of the Men and Jewish women in the Court of the women.

Non-Jews, people like most of us sitting here today, would only be allowed in the Court of the Gentiles.

The Jews were very concerned to keep the Temple ceremonially clean and so all Gentiles were kept in the outer court.

As Jesus and his disciples were walking towards the temple, they would’ve passed the Court of the Gentiles.PPT

It’s here that they found people selling oxen, sheep and doves.

They were sold because part of the worship requires animals to be sacrificed.

Rather than bringing in an animal, the worshippers can come into the temple and buy a sacrifice.

This is especially helpful for those travelling from far away.

Can you imagine how much hard work it will take to take a sheep through hundreds of kilometres, only to have it sacrificed at the temple?

It would’ve been so much more convenient for the travelling worshippers to buy the animal at the temple.

It would’ve saved so much time.

Jesus also saw another group at the temple: the money changers.

One of the things that every conscientious Jew would do as they come to the temple was to pay the temple tax.

Each Jewish male over twenty years old was required to pay half-shekel each.

These shekels must be of the approved Tyrian currency because only the Tyrian currency is acceptable in the Temple.

The reason is because other coins might have an offensive picture, like the pictures of Caesar’s head on it.

This would’ve been unacceptable in the Temple.

With so many people coming from different countries all over the Roman Empire, each with their own unique currency,

The money changers had the important job of changing the currency to the correct one for use in the temple.

Again, it’s a great convenience to have!

I remember actually going to a money exchange place in Jerusalem to exchange my American dollars to Israeli shekels. PPT

I searched for a place with good exchange rates and was relieved to have finally found one that had a good deal!

In Jesus’ days, having money changers on the Temple site would’ve made the lives of the travellers a lot easier.

It was very convenient to have the money exchanger there.

The temple was like a one-stop shop, all that they needed for worship was conveniently located on site.

It would’ve been the ancient equivalent of the Westfield Shopping Centre.

It’s a one-shop stop for all your supplies, in the Temple’s case, your supplies for sacrifices!

But what did Jesus do?

Look with me at verse 15, “After making a whip out of cords, he drove everyone out of the temple with their sheep and oxen. He also poured out the money changers’ coins and overturned the tables.”

He drove the sellers and the money exchangers out of the temple!

He told them, “Get these things out of here!”

Now there was no indication that these people were doing anything illegal, like overcharging the people for the oxen or having ridiculous exchange rates.

Rather, what they did was spiritually wrong.

They took up the places where the worshippers should’ve been.

If they had set up their shops in the Court of the Gentiles, then the Gentiles would’ve had no place to worship God at the temple.

because that’s the only place where they were allowed to be!

Sure, it was convenient for some people to get their animals and have their money exchanged.

But it was at the expense of others, who could not come to worship God.

And Jesus was angry about it.

They have turned God’s house, a house of worship, into a marketplace, a shopping centre.

They have trumped their convenience over worship.

And Jesus has come into God’s house to set things right.

The Bible talked about this in the Book of Malachi.

Open up with me to Malachi 3:1 and 3.PPT

“See, I am going to send my messenger, and he will clear the way before me. Then the Lord you seek will suddenly come to his temple, the Messenger of the covenant you delight in—see, he is coming,” says the Lord of Armies… 3 He will be like a refiner and purifier of silver; he will purify the sons of Levi and refine them like gold and silver. Then they will present offerings to the Lord in righteousness.”

Jesus has come suddenly into God’s temple and found that the worship was corrupted by people who treated it as a shopping centre.

And so, he began his refining work by driving out those who didn’t belong there.

Some people come to Church with the same consumerist mentality as a shopper.

They come to Church as they would a shopping centre.

What do you do in a shopping centre?

You go around, window browsing, choosing the produce you want, and make a purchase.

In the same way, some people go around to different Churches, choosing only to listen to teachings that they enjoy, like passages that talks about God’s love and God’s mercy,

And avoiding or even criticising passages that talk about judgement, repentance and obedience.

Others will only choose Churches based on their music and insult Churches that don’t play their type of music as happy-clappy or old-fashioned organ.

But that’s exactly the shopping centre mentality of Church that Jesus hates.

Because at the end of the day, it’s not about you.

It’s not about your convenience; it’s not about what you like.

When you come to Church, your focus should be on God.

You come here to see a glimpse of his glory as you hear from his word, as you sing his praises.

That’s what the Jews should’ve been focussing on instead of making, turning the temple into a marketplace.

That’s what made Jesus so angry: they’ve turned God’s temple into a marketplace.

The Temple is “my Father’s House”

As important as the temple is for worship, for Jesus, this place was something more.

It was his Father’s House.

Look with me at verse 16, “He told those who were selling doves, “Get these things out of here! Stop turning my Father’s house into a marketplace!”

Jesus loves the temple not only because it’s the place to worship God, but because it’s his Father’s House.

Jesus loves God the Father; he’s loved the Father before all things were made and he will love the Father for all eternity.

When people dishonour his Father’s House, they dishonour the Father.

When people cheapen his Father’s House by making it into a marketplace, they cheapen God’s honour.

When that happens, Jesus gets upset, because Jesus is concerned for God’s deeply concerned for God’s honour.

In the Bible, we call this concern for honour, zeal.

That’s what we see in verse 17, “And his disciples remembered that it is written: Zeal for your house will consume me.”

It’s a quote taken from Psalm 69:9 which reads, “because zeal for your house has consumed me, and the insults of those who insult you have fallen on me.

In an older translation, it reads, “For the zeal of thine house hath eaten me up”

It’s a very vivid picture of how important God’s honour was to the Psalmist.

In Psalm 69, the Psalmist was being mocked because he was so concerned for the honour of God’s name.

People were laughing at him because of the extent he went to in order to stand up for God’s name.

And the Person who has the most concern, the most zeal, for God’s honour, is none other than Jesus.

Jesus wanted people to have genuine, sincere worship, not the secondary, degraded worship that was happening in the Temple.

Jesus had a zeal to see people worship God, giving the honour that’s due to God.

J.C. Ryle, the famous English Bishop, wrote this about zeal in his book Practical Religion:

“Zeal in Christianity is a burning desire to please God, to do His will, and to advance His glory in the world in every possible way… They have a passion for one thing, and that one thing is to please God and to advance God’s glory. If they are consumed in the very burning of their passion for God, they don’t care-they are content. They feel that, like a candle, they were made to burn; and if they are consumed in the burning, then they have only done the work for which God has appointed them.”

Zeal is like a fire that burns, and the person who has zeal is like a candle that were made to burn.

They’re driven to honour God with a burning desire.

Jesus was driven by the zeal, a burning desire, to honour God

And he did something about it.

Jesus was driven by his zeal to drive all the animals, all the money changers out of his Father’s House.

It’s not enough for Jesus to have an opinion about what true worship is about.

The zeal that Jesus had for God’s honour drove him to action.

If a man comes into Church and burns the national flag, we’ll be outraged.

If the person next to us speaks badly about our wife or our husband, we’ll be furious.

How about God? How important is God’s honour to us?

Will you let someone cheapen the Church’s worship of God?

Will you drive out any obstacles, any sins, that dishonours God in the Church?

It’s a sad state of the Church when Christians are more concerned about their own honour, the honour of their family, rather than the honour of God.

But Christ Jesus would not sit idly by while God’s honour is cheapened.

Driven, consumed by his zeal for God’s honour, he did something about it.

My prayer is that we, too, will be moved by Jesus’ zeal for God’s honour.

Let’s start with honouring God in our own personal lives.

Do we have any sin in our lives that we need to drive out, so to speak?

Let that same zeal that Jesus had for God’s honour drive the sin out.

Pray for the same zeal that Jesus had; pray that God will give you the same zeal that Jesus had.

And it’s not just zeal to drive sins out; it’s also the zeal to be spiritually active.

It’s only by the Holy Spirit that God gives us that we can have the zeal to do what’s right in God’s eyes.

It’s only by His Holy Spirit that we have the zeal to read the Bible daily and to pray regularly.

It’s only by the zeal that comes with the Holy Spirit that we can actively seek the spiritual good of others and bring honour to God’s name.

And so, pray that God will give you the zeal.

But know that when you do have the zeal for God’s honour, you may be mocked.

This happened to the Psalmist back in his day.

And in Jesus’ days, he faced opposition for the zeal that he has for God’s honour.

The Temple is Jesus’ Body PPT

The Jews wanted to know who gave the authority for Jesus to do what he did!

How dared Jesus to have gone rogue and drive out all the animals and money changers.

To them, Jesus was obviously wrong!

And so they asked for a sign.

Look with me at verse 13, “So the Jews replied to him, “What sign will you show us for doing these things?”

If they had thought that Jesus was just a hooligan who wanted to cause trouble, they would’ve just had him arrested.

But it looked like they might had actually believed that Jesus was more than that.

Perhaps Jesus was a prophet from God?

In reply for their demand for a sign, Jesus answered, “Destroy this temple, and I will raise it up in three days.”

They were obviously thinking about the magnificent building that Herod had started building.

The building project started forty-six years before Jesus was there and would not be finished for another 36 years or so, until the year 63AD.

It would have been easier destroying the Harbour Bridge, the Opera House, the University of Sydney and then rebuilding all of them in three days. PPT

If Jesus could actually pull it off, then it would be a great sign, then it would show that he indeed had the authority to change the regulations inside the temple.

But the Jews weren’t about to destroy the temple just to see whether Jesus had the authority.

In time, the temple was destroyed, in 70AD, just seven years after it was completed, and it was never rebuilt. PPT

Jesus didn’t rebuild Herod’s Temple to show his authority because he wasn’t talking about that Temple.

He was talking about the temple of his body. PPT

A few years after this episode in John 2, Jesus would be sentenced to death and his body would be destroyed.

However, three days afterwards, Jesus rose from the dead.

Jesus raised his body from destruction.

This rising from the dead, this resurrection, was his sign,

In fact, it’s the ultimate sign.

It’s the ultimate sign because the resurrection shows that Jesus has authority over everything.

It shows that Jesus has authority over life, authority over death, authority over heaven, authority over hell, authority of bringing people to God and authority to judge everyone, the living and the dead.

It certainly shows that he has authority to bring about true worship in God’s temple.

With the resurrection, Jesus shows that his body, in fact, is the new temple.

He is the place where people go to in order to meet with God and to worship God.

There’s no need to go back to the old temple to worship God anymore.

Even if you want to, you can’t, because the temple is not there anymore.

If you want to worship God, you must come to Jesus.

It is in Jesus that you see God’s glory; it’s in Jesus and in Jesus alone that you worship God.

In order to understand the significance of who Jesus’ resurrection, the disciples needed to understand him in the framework of the resurrection, the Old Testament and Jesus’ own teaching.

Look with me at verse 22, “So when he was raised from the dead, his disciples remembered that he had said this, and they believed the Scripture and the statement Jesus had made.”

The disciples admit that it’s only after Jesus raised from the dead that they understood and believed Scripture, that is the Old Testament.

It’s only after the Jesus was raised that they understood and believed what Jesus has said.

We see the implications of what they have believed about Jesus in the teachings of the New Testament.

And so, for us to understand the significance of Jesus, we need to read Old Testament, which contains the promises leading up to Jesus,

We need to read the New Testament, which contains eyewitness accounts of Jesus as well as the disciples’ understanding application of the teachings of Jesus.

And we need to see this in the light of the resurrection of Jesus.

The death and resurrection of Jesus is the key to understanding how Jesus fits in the whole Bible.

You can’t understand the significance of Jesus’ death and resurrection without the Bible.

You can’t understand the Bible without believing the death and resurrection of Jesus.

They go together.

Finally, in the final verses of John chapter two, there’s an account about Jesus performing many signs at the Passover Festival.

Many people believed in the name of Jesus.

Interestingly, John notes, in verse 24, that “Jesus, however, would not entrust himself to them, since he knew them all 25 and because he did not need anyone to testify about man; for he himself knew what was in man.”

Even though a person might say with his mouth that he believes in Jesus, Jesus doesn’t trust him.

Jesus doesn’t believe in the numbers game.

His mission is not to see as many people as he can sitting in the pews in the Church.

Jesus’ concern is that they will have a burning zeal to honour God’s name and that they would truly worship him.

We’re going to explore what it means to truly worship him as we look into John over the next few weeks.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What distractions do you need to drive out?

Just as Jesus drove out the animals and the money exchangers, what distractions do you need to drive out so that you can focus on worshipping God?

Are you zealous for God’s honour? If so, what does your zeal look like?

Many people claim to have zeal for God, but they have little to show. Can you visualise what your zeal for God looks like?

How can you, under God, be consumed by your zeal for God? What would that look like?

Jesus was consumed by zeal for his Father’s House. Do you have the same zeal as Jesus?

Brothers and sisters, I don’t know about you, but I know that I don’t stand up for God’s honour as often as I should.

We need God to instil in us the zeal of Jesus so that we’ll have a longing for the Father’s honour.

Let’s pray that God will give us a burning zeal for his honour through the power of his Holy Spirit.

Let’s pray,

“Father, we have often prayed to you, hallowed be your name. We have often prayed for the honour of your name. And yet, Father, forgive us for the many times we have failed to honour your name. Forgive us for our cowardice for not standing up for your honour. By your Spirit, inflame in us a burning zeal to see you honoured. May we be like a candle, shining out the light of Christ. And if we’re burnt, then we have only done the work you have appointed us to do. We ask this for Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
