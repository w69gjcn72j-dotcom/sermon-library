Sermon Title: The Holiness of God: The Holy Spirit joins us to the Lord Jesus

Passage: 1 Corinthians 6:9-20

Additional Passages: Galatians 5:19-26, 1 Cor 9:25-27

Big Idea: God the Holy Spirit: Saints are joint to the Lord in one Spirit, so fruitfully glorify God with your bodies

Do we have any parents who brought their children to Church today?

I’m very thankful that my father and mother took me to Church since I was a baby.

For as long as I can remember, my parents had always told me to give God thanks for the food before I eat.

And so, they taught me this prayer (Cantonese): 谢谢天父, 赐我食物, 求主洁净, 保我平安, 奉主名求，誠心所願。

Basically it’s thanking God for the food and asking God to cleanse the food and give me peace.

When I was a boy, I would say that prayer before I put anything in my mouth.

It was just a part of life.

When I came to Australia, as I was about to eat my food, I would do the same thing.

But I would say the prayer really fast – in about two seconds.

My friends, most of whom weren’t Christians, didn’t know what I was doing.

Some of them thought that I was saying sorry to the food before I ate it!

“I am sorry I’m about to eat you!”

That’s how strange some of our Christian behaviours might seem to someone who’s not a Christian.

To someone who’s not a Christian, Christianity might be a way of thinking, a belief, that has little or nothing to do with real life.

However, to someone who’s genuinely a Christian, being a Christian has everything to do with real life.

Being a Christian involves our will, our feelings, our actions, our spirit and our body.

Being a Christian has everything to do with how we live.

When a Christian lives a godly life, we say that the person has piety, that is, the person lives a life that glorifies God.

Being a Christian is not simply a belief, a philosophy; it is a way of living.

Why should Christians glorify God in the way they live, in the way they use their bodies in their day to day life?

That’s what we’ll be exploring in today’s passage.

Please open up with me to 1 Corinthians Chapter 6: verses 9 to 20.

We’ll see three things:

The unrighteous made into saints by the Lord Jesus and the Spirit

The saints are joint to the Lord Jesus in one spirit

The saints fruitfully glorify God with their bodies

Why should a Christian glorify God with their bodies? What does it look like?

Before we look into it, let’s pray, “Father, thank you for giving us word the Bible. Please open our eyes and our ears, that by your Spirit, you will plant your word deep into our hearts, for Jesus’ sake, Amen.”

The unrighteous made into saints by the Lord Jesus and the Spirit

Over the past two weeks, we’ve been looking at what the Bible says about the Triune God.

As Christians, we believe in a God who is One God in Three Persons:

We believe in God the Father, God the Son (Jesus) and God the Holy Spirit.

When we were reading about God the Father, we learnt that: The incomparably holy God strengthens weary Christian saints who patiently wait for him.

When we were reading about God the Son we learnt that: Jesus’ perfect obedience to his Father’s will sanctifies us for perfect worship of the holy God.

Today, we will learn about God the Holy Spirit, and I hope that we’ll learn that “Saints are joint to the Lord in one Spirit, so fruitfully glorify God with your bodies.”

Many people in our society might have a vague idea about God, whether they’re religious or not.

Some people might even have some vague ideas of who Jesus is.

However, not many people might know much about the Holy Spirit?

According to the Bible, the Holy Spirit is not a thing, not a force, but a Person.

In fact, the Holy Spirit is the Third Person of the Trinity; he is fully God.

Before he left, Jesus promised that God the Father would send God the Holy Spirit in the name of God the Son.

Jesus, during his last days before going to the cross, prepared his disciples for the arrival of the Holy Spirit.

This happened after Jesus died, rose from the dead, ascended into heaven, on the fiftieth day since Jesus rose from the dead.

Jesus sent the Holy Spirit, His Spirit, so that we’ll believe in him.

In John chapter 3 verse 8, Jesus says, “The wind blows where it pleases, and you hear its sound, but you don’t know where it comes from or where it is going.a So it is with everyone born of the Spirit.”

The Holy Spirit is like the wind; you can’t control him.

But wherever he goes, he can make someone born again.

By the Holy Spirit, someone who was not a Christian can become a follower of Jesus.

Being a Christian is possible, only through the work of the Holy Spirit.

This is not just a change in lifestyle.

It is a spiritual, complete change in who you are as a person.

Look with me at today’s passage, in 1 Corinthians chapter 6 beginning from verse 9, “Don’t you know that the unrighteous will not inherit God’s kingdom? Do not be deceived: No sexually immoral people, idolaters, adulterers, or males who have sex with males, 10 no thieves, greedy people, drunkards, verbally abusive people, or swindlers will inherit God’s kingdom. 11 And some of you used to be like this.

You used to be like that, but now you are not that anymore.

Penny W was a woman in her mid 30’s who struggled with alcohol back in 1999.

She became a Christian in 1999 and was able to stop in 2001.

However, she picked up just one drink at a party and she was unable to stop in 2002.

She tried to stop her alcohol addiction with her own free will but it didn’t work.

She then started to go to a group where they started sharing some verses from the Bible with her.

She found a lot of support in that group and her faith in Jesus started to grow again.

Now, she’s helping others who struggle with addiction.

For many people who struggle with addiction, change is hard, and often seem impossible.

But change is hard not just for those with addiction.

Change is hard when what we want to change from is where we find out identity.

It’s hard when whatever it is that we want to change from is a vital part of who we are.

Go back and have a look at the list in 1 Corinthians chapter 6.

There were sexually immoral people, idolators, people who cheat on their spouse, homosexuals.

Most of these are sexual sins.

Then there are the thieves and the greedy, that is, those who want to take things that aren’t theirs.

There are drunks; there are verbally abusive people, that is people who spread rumours and lies.

You can ask them to change, they might even tell you they want to change, but they often won’t or can’t change their way.

Because they can’t and won’t change, they won’t inherit God’s kingdom since God is holy and his kingdom is only for those who are holy.

Now notice what the Bible calls them: “And some of you were like this”.

That’s what some of you were!

That was what the people who went to that Church used to be like.

They were not the morally elite of the society.

There were people in the Church who cheated on their wives, who had an unhealthy desire for money, who verbally abused people.

But some that’s what some of you were; they used to be like that.

But now, they’ve been changed.

They were washed, they were sanctified, they were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus and by the Spirit of our God.

God has changed them when they believed in Jesus and what he has done for them.

And God changed them by the Holy Spirit’s work inside of them.

It’s a fundamental change.

The cheat has no desire to leave his wife behind; the thief has no desire to steal.

They are now sanctified, saints in the Lord Jesus, by the work of the Holy Spirit.

And it’s not a private matter.

It’s something that they act out with their bodies.

Notice that all the activities that the Bible listed involves someone physically acting upon someone else.

You cheat on someone; you engage with sexually immoral activities with someone; you steal from someone.

The change is personal, but the effect of the change plays out in his day-to-day life

The change happens because the Holy Spirit is in the person.

Or to put it another way, if a person says that he’s become a Christian and nothing changes in his life, that is, if he keeps on cheating, if he keeps on stealing, then you have to ask whether there’s any genuine change inside the person.

Here’s a photo of my parents’ in law (Jenny’s parents).

They celebrated their 60th anniversary last Monday.

Jenny and her family prayed for Mr Wong for a very long time.

But God’s grace, Mr Wong became a Christian.

On the day he became a Christian, he stopped smoking.

Now, there are no verses in the Bible that tells him to stop smoking.

He just realised that his life is different now.

He belongs to Jesus, and he must live a life that glorifies Jesus.

One way that he chose to do that is to stop smoking.

That’s not the only way to show that your life is now different because you’re a Christian.

There are other changes that might happen in your life because you’re now a Christian.

But there must be a change in your behaviour, in your life’s direction, in the way you live, because the Holy Spirit has sanctified you, made you into a saint.

And through the holy Spirit, saints are joint to the Lord in one Spirit.

The saints are joint to the Lord Jesus in one spirit

The change that happens to people who believe in Jesus is more than just a change in the person’s will.

It’s even more than a change in the person’s heart.

It’s a change in the person’s spirit, the deepest part of his / her identity.

Look with me at verse 17, “But anyone joined to the Lord is one spirit with him.”

As a minister, I had officiated many church weddings.

In an Anglican wedding, the couple would exchange their vows, exchange their rings and then there’s a joining of hands.

I would then say what is probably one of the most important known sentences in the wedding service, “Those whom God has joined together let no one put asunder.”

That is, what God has joined together, let man not separate.

For they are now one unit, one life, one flesh.

In 1 Corinthians chapter 6 verse 17, we have a similar idea, but instead of one flesh, anyone joined to the Lord is one spirit.

Before one flesh, the couples were their own individual selves, but when they’re married, they’re one unit, one flesh.

Before we become believers, we’re individual selves, but when we’re joined to the Lord Jesus by his Spirit, we’re one Spirit.

As people joined to the Lord, we mustn’t just keep thinking about ourselves, focusing on our own rights and our own privileges.

We must think of who we are as people joined to the Lord.

That’s the thinking behind the passage, from verse 12 onwards, ‘“Everything is permissible for me,” but not everything is beneficial. “Everything is permissible for me,” but I will not be mastered by anything.’

Everything is permissible for me: I’m allowed to do anything I want.

I’m allowed to do anything, so you mustn’t stop me from doing anything that I want.

I’m allowed to do anything, so don’t tell me who I can or who I can’t sleep with.

I do what I want, when I want, with whom I want, and I don’t need your permission.

Does that sound familiar?

You probably heard it in one form or another, but it’s nothing new.

Self-centredness has been around for thousands of years.

But the Bible challenges our self-centredness by asking: is what you’re doing beneficial to you and to others?

You might be permitted to do it, it might even be legal, but is it beneficial?

The Bible commentator Thistleton commented that here is the “well-known paradox that if everyone claims unqualified autonomy, no one can be free, for everyone is threatened by the freedoms of the other.”

You can’t be completely free without infringing on the freedom of another person.

We’ve become so focussed on what I should be freed to do that we’ve become blind to the fact that we might be mastered by the very thing that we think will give us freedom.

Our desire to do whatever we think is permissible becomes an obsession.

We want our way so much, that we turn a blind eye on what is truly good and beneficial.

In fact, we might choose to turn a blind eye on what is good and beneficial because to do what is good and beneficial often means not getting to do what you want to do.

In his book, the Manual, Al Stewart says this: “Part of becoming a man rather than a boy is being able to see that long view – that our lives have purpose in the service of others, staring with our Creator.”

This is what separates the man from the boys, the woman from the girls: they put what is beneficial above their own rights.

Look with me at verse 13, ““Food is for the stomach and the stomach for food,” and God will do away with both of them.”

Someone might argue that we’re physical, living beings who have stomachs that need to be filled and there’s food out there for our stomach.

We have the right to eat all kinds of food for our physical needs.

And all these physical needs are temporary anyway.

Don’t we have the right to choose whoever we want to sleep with, since that satisfies our physical desire as well?

And does it really matter, since even our sexual needs are temporary anyway?

Sexual sins, however, are different from other sins.

Sexual sins refer to any sexual intimacy outside of marriage between one man and one woman.

This is what the Bible says about sexual sins, in the second half of verse 13, “However, the body is not for sexual immorality but for the Lord, and the Lord for the body.”

The body is for the Lord, and the Lord is for the body; they belong together.

Again, we hear echoes of the “one flesh” language coming in.

Your body matters and what you do with your body matters.

That’s why God raised the Lord Jesus up from the dead, because the body matters.

That’s why God will raise our bodies up from the dead, if we believe in Jesus, because our body matters.

Our bodies are like the limbs of Jesus; those who belong to Jesus are like his hands and his feet.

Our whole person, our body, our will and our spirit, all belong to Jesus; they are all parts of the body of Jesus.

The Bible rhetorically asks we should take a part of Christ’s body (that is, our physical body) and make it part of a prostitute.

Absolutely not! For do that will be to make some kind of monster out of Christ’s body?

Verse 16, “No! One flesh language in v16, “on’t you know that anyone joined to a prostitute is one body with her? For Scripture says, The two will become one flesh.”

The one flesh language comes back.

This quote about the two becoming one is a beautiful description of what God did in the very first marriage.

In the very first marriage, God’s put two bodies, two sets of limbs and formed one beautiful new body.

God’s design of marriage is beautiful; the couple now has the new identity one flesh.

But the Bible goes further, “But anyone joined to the Lord is one spirit with him.”

Those who are joined to the Lord is one spirit.

Your body, your spirit, your will, is joined to the Lord Jesus to form one body.

And this he does by his Holy Spirit.

We’re not one flesh, but one Spirit with the Lord.

It’s even more beautiful than the most beautiful earthly marriage.

It’s by the Holy Spirit that we come to know and serve the Lord Jesus.

It’s by the Holy Spirit that Jesus shows us that he loved us enough to die for us on the cross, to make us holy.

It’s the Holy Spirit himself who turns us to Jesus, who washes us, who cleanses us, to make us holy for Jesus.

Dave Jensen was born to a devout Christian family.

His father was the Bishop Peter Jensen, the former Archbishop of Sydney, and his mother was Christine.

They were both very kind, very Christ-loving parents to Dave.

Though Dave was baptised, he wasn’t a Christian.

He was a rebellious teenager who got drunk, who used drugs, who slept with many girls.

He even got a girl pregnant when he was a teenager.

He probably did everything that was on the list we read earlier.

Though he would’ve known many of the Bible stories, it wasn’t until he was 28, that he realised that Christianity was true.

Dave’s job is now telling people about Jesus.

He was broken but he’s now cleansed and sanctified.

There’s joy and hope in the way that he speaks, because he’s one Spirit with the Lord Jesus.

He’s joined to the Lord by the Holy Spirit.

Are you joint to the Lord in One Spirit?

How would you know?

If you are joined to the Lord in one Spirit, than the most important person in your world, and in all the world, is not you.

It’s not even your spouse, the person with whom you’re in one flesh with.

The most important person in the world and in your world is the person with whom you’re in One Spirit with.

If Jesus is the most important person, then you’re holy to him; you’re a saint.

And you want to glorify him fruitfully with your body.

The saints fruitfully glorify God with their bodies

Look with me at verse 18, “Flee sexual immorality! Every other sin a person commits is outside the body, but the person who is sexually immoral sins against his own body.”

Sins are bad and can be very damaging.

However, one sin that harms more than most is sexual sins.

When a person sins sexually, the impact on the body goes far deeper and is far more lasting than other sins.

Sometimes, you can see the physical effects of sin immediately; other times, the trauma sets in after many years.

It will affect the relationship with other people, including your spouse, or your future spouse of you’re not married yet.

But more importantly, sexual sins are spiritual sins.

Verse 19 to 20, “Don’t you know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you, whom you have from God? You are not your own, 20 for you were bought at a price. So glorify God with your body.”

You don’t own your body, God does.

You belong to God.

If a husband becomes intimate with a woman other than his wife, we call him a cheater, for he belongs exclusively to his wife with regards to sexual intimacy.

If we take ourselves and unite our body, our will, our spirit other than God himself, than we are cheaters, for every part of beings belong exclusively to God.

If you are a Christian, if the Holy Spirit lives in you, then you are a temple of the Holy Spirit.

That’s why the Church building is not the temple.

That’s why the Cathedral in the city not the temple.

God’s spirit is not in the building, but in men and women, and in boys and girls who believe in Jesus.

Don’t devote yourself to anyone else except for God.

Do not cheat on God.

Jesus, God the Son took a frail, mortal body, died on our behalf in order to save us.

We are alive, we’re saved, we have a body to move around in, only because of Jesus.

And so, let’s use our bodies for him, let’s use these bodies that he saved to glorify him, so that others will know how good He is.

We glorify Jesus in our bodies by being fruitful.

In Galatians 5:22-23, the Bible describes what being filled with the Holy Spirit would look like on the outside.

It’s like a beautiful, glorious fruit:

“But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, 23 gentleness, and self-control. The law is not against such things.”

Now these are not seven different fruits but one fruit.

Those who have the Holy Spirit should exhibit all the fruits of the Spirit.

I only want to focus on one as we conclude: It’s the part of the fruit called Self-Control.

Throughout our passage today, we’ve seen what happens when people don’t have self-control.

The person who does not have self-control is obsessed about what their rights, they fight for their right to do anything they want.

The person who has no self-control is obsessed about their right to be sexually intimate with whoever they want.

The person who has no self-control just let their whatever they feel or whatever they think take over.

The self-control that the Spirit produces in a person is not a stoic, self-determined, will power that wants full control of the situation.

Neither is it talking about no control, of course, and just simply letting your emotion be your master; self-control is not about following your heart.

True self-control is the willingness to submit to the Holy Spirit’s control.

It is to let the Spirit rule in your heart.

It’s not about being led by the will in your mind or the emotion in your hearts.

Self-control is the recognition and the application of joining to the Lord by the Holy Spirit; it’s to live as one Spirit with the Lord Jesus.

That’s how you glorify God with your bodies.

Hudson Taylor was a missionary who was responsible for leading hundreds of missionaries in the mid-19th century into China to tell people about Jesus.

Here’s how his son Fredrick Howard Taylor described him, “His son Fredrick Howard Taylor wrote in 1932,

Here was a man almost sixty years of age, bearing tremendous burdens, yet absolutely calm and untroubled. Oh, the pile of letters! Any one of which might contain news of death, of lack of funds, of riots or serious trouble. Yet all were opened, read and answered with the same tranquility — Christ his reason for peace, his power for calm. Dwelling in Christ, he drew upon His very being and resources. . . . And this he did by an attitude of faith as simple as it was continuous. Yet he was delightfully free and natural. I can find no words to describe it save the Scriptural expression “in God.” He was in God all the time and God in him. It was that true “abiding” of John fifteen.

Even in the hardship of being a missionary in China, he had peace, he had freedom, because he was joined to the Lord Jesus by the Holy Spirit.

Hudson Taylor’s identity was not defined by money, or my fame, or by even happiness itself.

His identity is in being joined to Jesus in one Spirit.

Why should a Christian glorify God with their bodies? What does it look like?

It is to be one in Spirit with the Lord; it is to let the Holy Spirit join you to the Lord.

And Jesus, the One whom you’re joint with in Spirit, wants to speak with you through his word. He wants to listen to you as you pray. Will you listen to him? Will you speak with him?

That’s what we do here at Church; as the temple of the Holy Spirit, we hear from him as we hear the Word and we speak to him as we pray.

If you want to find out more about what it is to know Jesus, please join us for our new people group afterwards.

Saints are joint to the Lord in one Spirit, so fruitfully glorify God with your bodies

Start with small steps, like giving thanks to God before food.

Make it a habit; every child and every adult can do that.

The next step might be to give regularly to Church and to missionaries.

To be intentional gracious in giving to ministries takes discipline and it helps us from being greedy.

Finally, glorify God with your bodies by physically going out there to invite people to Church.

KOGKids leaders have promised that if 10 new children come to KOGKids at the end of the year, one of the teachers will get a pie of their face.

True sacrifice right there.

That’s great to see. You know what, if we have 10 new children coming to KOGKids regularly, you can also pie me.

Let’s tell people about Jesus, whatever it takes!

Here are three questions for us to think about:

Why is an unholy and an unrighteous life unacceptable to all who call themselves Christians?

“Anyone joined to the Lord is one spirit with him” – does this describe your relationship with the Lord?

How have you glorified God with your body, especially in the area of self-control?

Friends, if you are a believe in Jesus, then you’re a saint.

Remember: Saints are joint to the Lord in one Spirit, so fruitfully glorify God with your bodies.
