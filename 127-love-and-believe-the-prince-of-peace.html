1. Peace at any price?

No matter where you’re from, no matter where you find yourself in human history, you will always find a universal desire for world peace.

Everyone wants world peace!

Little children want world peace, old grandparents want world peace, movie stars, musicians and politicians, all want world peace.

Our Prime Minister, Anthony Albanese was in Singapore, gave one of the most important talks in his role as a Prime Minister last night to talk about how to keep peace in our region.

It’s important for all nations in the region to work together to promote peace.

We’re so used to having peace here in Australia, aren’t we?

We’re extremely blessed to have peace in our country because we’re currently not in any major conflicts with other countries.

We have a really good defence system, probably one of the most best in the world; it’s called the sea!

As an island, we’re situated quite far away from other places, except for New Zealand, who is generally fairly friendly to us, unless it’s cricket or rugby.

Otherwise, we live in a fairly peaceful country, something we mustn’t take for granted.

There are many countries around the world right now who face the threats of war or internal conflicts, almost every day.

For example, the African country of Sudan has suffered three internal wars spanning more than 40 years of its 67 years as a nation.

The fighting started again in April 2023.

For many years, many countries in the West, including Australia, have received refugees from Sudan.

The people there are desperate for peace.

They will fight for peace, or they will flee to find peace somewhere else.

They want peace, no matter what it takes.

Of course, it’s not only at a national or international level that we want peace.

We want peace at a domestic level as well.

We want peace at home and in our neighbourhood.

Even in a relatively peaceful country like Australia, we still experience a lot of conflicts at the domestic level.

Over this past week, Jenny and I, along with many Australians, are saddened by a lot of violence done to children, sometimes by adults, other times by other children.

It’s horrible what one person can do to another.

It’s sad that sometimes these acts of violence can happen at home, where it should’ve been safe.

For many of us, our culture gives great priority to peace at home.

For the Chinese, there’s a saying, “家和万事兴。jiā hé wàn shì xīnɡ.”

If there’s peace at home, everything will go well.

And so, many Chinese aim for peace at home, no matter the cost.

And so, we want world peace, we want peace at home and we want inner peace.

We hear that from a lot of Eastern and Western literature now, about the need to have a healthy, mental well-being.

Our mental health is something that many of us often don’t take care good care of.

And so, we’re told, time and time again, to take some time off.

Give yourself a break; do whatever it takes to get inner peace.

We know that peace is important and peace is good; it’s something that often comes at a great price.

In fact, some people have said that we must achieve peace at any price.

The phrase, “Peace at any price”, is often associated with the former British Prime Minister, Neville Chamberlain.

As Germany grew in power in the 1930’s Great Britain and France wanted to respond by appeasing Germany as much as possible, trying to achieve peace at any price.

Great Britain and France even agreed to not stop Germany invading parts of southern Czechoslovakia, if it means he won’t invade the other parts of Czechoslovakia or other parts of Europe.

Chamberlain returned from the meeting feeling that peace was achieved with the compromise.

However, Hitler invaded the rest of Czechoslovakia, and continued in his conquest of other countries.

Chamberlain’s pursuit for peace at all costs led him, his country and other countries down the very tragic path of suffering and war.

Hitler, of course, and other dictators and warlords like him, will also say that he’s in pursuit of peace.

Hitler wanted peace, world peace, for himself and for his country, even it means at the great cost of others.

Peace for one person is very often oppression for another.

Though the oppressor might say there’s peace, the oppressed will say that it’s a lie.

The peace is false.

This is not new, of course.

In the time of the prophet Jeremiah, the people of God was described to be utterly wicked.

Listen to what God says of them in Jeremiah 6:13-14, “For from the least to the greatest of them, everyone is making profit dishonestly. From prophet to priest, everyone deals falsely. They have treated my people’s brokenness superficially, claiming, “Peace, peace,” when there is no peace.

The people of God, even the prophets and priest of God, were treating the brokenness and wickedness of the people as if it was nothing.

Their proclamation of peace was a lie.

God hated it for it’s not true peace.

What is true peace?

What does true peace look like?

2. The Son’s gift of peace and joy

Open your Bible with me to John 14, beginning from verse 27, Jesus says, “Peace I leave with you. My peace I give to you. I do not give to you as the world gives. Don’t let your heart be troubled or fearful.”

It was and still is a common Jewish custom to greet one another with the word, “Shalom” which means, “peace”.

Sometimes, the word Shalom is used to say goodbye to someone as well.

Here, when Jesus says Peace, Shalom, to his disciples, he’s saying his farewell.

Jesus said these words on the night before he died on the cross.

He was about to experience more pain and anguish than anyone else has ever experienced.

He was about to face the full force of God’s anger.

He was going to experience the very opposite of peace.

Elsewhere in the Bible, he was described to be sweating great drops of blood, not because he was afraid of death, but because of the full force of God’s wrath against sin.

It was not God’s anger against his sin, for he has none.

Rather it was God’s anger against the sins of the world.

And yet, his concern was not for himself but for his disciples.

His concern was that they will have peace, his peace.

In fact, He came to die on the cross so that the disciples might know peace.

Unlike the dictators, Jesus’ concern wasn’t for himself but for his disciples.

He didn’t want them to be troubled or fearful; instead, he wanted them to have his peace.

What is this peace that Jesus is offering his disciples?

Is it an inner peace that’s relaxed and at ease even if the whole world around them is burning.

Like this meme:

Jesus is not just talking about an inner, personal peace.

Rather, he’s talking about something much bigger, something bigger even then World peace.

Look with me at verse 28, “You have heard me tell you, ‘I am going away and I am coming to you.’ If you loved me, you would rejoice that I am going to the Father, because the Father is greater than I.”

Jesus’ peace is one that involved God the Father in heaven as well as us here on earth.

Jesus said he was going away – He will die on the cross very soon, in the next twenty four hours.

Jesus said he was coming to them – Jesus will rise from the dead after three days and will go back to the disciples.

More than that, after he spent forty days with the disciples, Jesus would go back to heaven.

Jesus is going to God the Father.

Jesus will see and share the glory of God and he will experience absolute peace in a way far beyond anything the world can offer or imagine.

It’s in this sense that the Father is greater than Jesus.

The Father is in heaven enjoying heavenly glory and peace and he’s sent Jesus into the world.

Jesus took on the nature of humanity in order to die and rise from the dead.

He did it in obedience to the Father and now he’s returning to the glory that he shared with the Father.

In heaven by the Father’s side, Jesus can enjoy absolute peace.

Heaven is the place where Jesus has absolute inner peace and absolute outer peace.

That’s the peace that Jesus is giving to his disciples.

The disciples followed Jesus; they were the first followers of Jesus.

Even these disciples, his first followers had difficulties understanding this peace.

Jesus tells them that it’s only for those who love and believe him who can understand this peace.

Firstly, they will understand this peace if they loved Jesus.

Jesus said, “If you loved me, you would rejoice that I am going to the Father”.

If the disciples had loved Jesus, they would rejoice, they would be happy that Jesus is going to the Father.

They should’ve been happy that Jesus was going to the Father’s side and will enjoy glory and peace.

But their sadness showed that they didn’t love Jesus as much as they should.

Their focus was still on themselves, and not on Jesus.

Look with me at verse 29, “I have told you now before it happens so that when it does happen you may believe.”

To love Jesus, they needed to believe Jesus.

And so, Jesus told them all these things before they happened so that hey would believe him.

What were they to believe about Jesus?

They needed to believe that the death and resurrection is the beginning of the new creation.

Jesus’ death was the price that he paid for our peace with God.

The greatest war we face as humanity is not World War 1, the Great War, or World War 2, as horrible as these wars were.

The greatest war that we face is the one between God and man.

There’s no peace within the hearts of man, or among nations of man, unless there is peace between God and man.

There is nothing we do that can stop the war; only Jesus, as the One who is fully God and fully Man, can pay the price for our peace with God.

There’s peace when we believe Jesus.

And when we truly believe Jesus, we will love Jesus.

We will love him for who He is, we will love him for what He’s done.

We love him because you know him.

For those who love and believe him, Jesus gives his peace.

It’s a peace that comes with great joy.

To believe in Jesus is not merely intellectual understanding but with the belief comes beautiful and emotional joy.

There’s joy that comes with Jesus’ peace.

And the way we love and believe him is based on how Jesus himself loves and obeys the Father.

Jesus is about to face the ruler of the world.

The Devil, the great enemy of God and his people, is coming for Jesus.

But Jesus does not fear the devil for he has no power over Jesus.

The cross might have appeared to be a triumph for the devil, the place when injustice and human sin wins.

Instead, it’s where Jesus shows the world that he loves the Father.

Throughout the Bible, we know that Jesus loves people, and the cross does show us that.

The cross shows us that Jesus’ love for people is sacrificial and glorious.

But here, Jesus’ focus is on his love for the Father.

Why did Jesus go to the cross? Because he loves the Father.

How does going to the cross shows that he loves the Father? Because He did as the Father commanded.

Jesus loved the Father and obeyed Him; He did it for the Father’s glory.

The cross is where God demonstrated his own love for us: While we were sinners, Christ died for us, Romans 5:8.

But the cross is also where Jesus showed us he loved the Father.

Jesus went to the cross to die for us to show that He loves the Father.

The cross is where we see that God loved the world and where we see Jesus loved the Father.

On that most dreadful cross stood the wonder of God’s love for us and Christ’s love for the Father!

On the cross Jesus has won peace for God and man.

On the cross and the resurrection afterwards, Jesus ushered in the new creation.

Do you believe it?

3. Love, believe and obey the God of Peace

There’s peace and joy for those who love and believe Jesus.

There’s peace and joy for those who follow how Jesus loves and obeys the Father.

If we don’t know how to love and believe Jesus, all we have to do is look at how he love and obeys the Father.

How Jesus loves and obeys the Father is how we’re to love and believe Jesus.

But even as we follow Jesus, we must always remember that our goal is not peace and joy.

You don’t love and believe Jesus if all you’re after is peace and joy.

These are great things, but they are not what you’re after the most.

The goal is to love and believe Jesus, the goal is to love and obey the God of peace.

Peace and joy are NOT the goals but the by-products of our pursuit for Jesus.

Can I have a volunteer?

Here’s _________ and his / her shadow.

Can you catch your shadow? Who do you think will win if you have a race with your shadow, if the light is always behind you?

Shadows might be beautiful, but they are only beautiful because the reality is glorious.

Both peace and joy are but the shadows that go with love and belief in Jesus, the Prince of Peace.

If we chase the shadow, if we chase after peace, we’ll never get it.

And yet, that’s what we spend most of our time on, isn’t it?

We pursue peace, or at least, our version of peace, in our world, in our home, in ourselves, but we don’t get it.

Jesus say, “pursue me instead. Follow me. Love me.”

Jesus has won the peace that the world so desperately need.

Love him, follow him, pursue him.

Run to him because He is the Son of God, who has come to save us from our sins.

Run to Him because he’s the glorious Son of God, perfect and good in every way.

Run to him, because He loves you far greater and far deeper, than anyone else who’s ever loved you.

Love him, believe him, run to him.

Dr Timothy Keller is one of the most influential Christian speaker and writer of our time.

Over the past few years, he was struggling with cancer and on the 19th May, he went to be with the Lord.

He was alone with Kathy, his wife of 48 years.

His son Michael tweeted that he kissed Kathy on the forehead and then breathed his last breath.

His final words were: “There is no downside for me leaving, not in the slightest.”

That’s the peace that Dr Keller had even as he faced death.

He had peace because he knew and loved Jesus.

He had peace because he was going to the Jesus and to God the Father.

He would want us to rejoice because he’s going to Jesus and the Father, where the peace is perfect.

There is no downside in his death.

Do you believe this? Do you believe Jesus?

Friends, as Christians, as followers of Jesus, we must be bold and tell the world that Jesus is the One to love and believe.

We must point them to the cross.

We must keep pointing them to the cross for on the cross, Jesus showed us what love and faith looks like

On the cross he showed his love for the Father and he showed the Father’s love for us.

And so, run to the cross, run to Jesus.

Love and believe him, and as a by-product, you’ll have peace and joy as well.

Why are peace and joy so elusive in a world that desperately wants them?

It’s because they don’t have Jesus.

Without Jesus, there can be no genuine peace, there can be no lasting joy.

You can call it what they will, but it’s not peace and joy.

Jesus has paid the price for our peace.

And so, Love and believe the Prince of Peace

Know him more by reading the Bible daily and praying to him.

We know and love our families and friends more when we listen and talk with them.

Listen and talk to Jesus by reading the Bible and praying to him.

I’ve been so encouraged to hear that many of our brothers and sisters I asked in our church are reading the Bible daily.

That’s great!

Find opportunities to love him by obeying what he says.

Tell someone in your family, in your Growth group this week how you have obeyed him.

Share with someone how you have peace with God, even when you’re facing the difficult times in your life.

Love and believe the Prince of Peace.

Here are the three questions for this week:

1. Where in your life do you think you need peace the most?

2. How are you modelling your way of loving Jesus after his love for the Father?

3. Are you running to Jesus or running after shadows?

Friends, don’t think that you can have peace and joy without Jesus.

Love and believe Jesus, the Prince of Peace.
