Why Are You Crying?

John 20:11-18 · 2020
Big IdeaThe risen Christ turns Weeping Mary into Messenger Mary.

“Why are you crying?”

Passage: John 20.11_18

Big Idea: The risen Christ turns Weeping Mary into Messenger Mary

Weeping Mary cries for Lost Jesus

The risen Christ finds Mary weeping

The risen Christ sends Messenger Mary

The Lord’s message to us:

Today, we’ll meet a woman in the Bible called Mary.

There are a number of women called Mary in the Bible.

There’s Mary, mother of Jesus.

There’s Mary of Bethany, sister of Martha.

Today, we’re going to meet a Mary in the Bible who’s neither of these.

She’s known as Mary Magdalene.

What do we know about Mary Magdalene?

The Bible tells us in Luke 8:2 that Mary Magdalene was one of the women who travelled with Jesus and his disciples and supported them.

Mary Magdalene was also a woman from whom Jesus drove our seven demons.

But most importantly, Mary Magdalene was listed explicitly as a witness of the death of Jesus in three of the gospels.

Even in the gospel where she wasn’t mentioned explicitly, Luke implicitly included her when he mentioned about the group of women who supported him from Galilee (Luke 23:49)

But in all four gospels, Mary Magdalene is listed explicitly by name in all four gospels as one of the earliest witnesses of the resurrection of Jesus.

Mary Magdalene was one of the first people in history to see the resurrected Jesus!

While there were other women in the other Gospel accounts, the Apostle John focuses on Mary.

Please open up your Bible with me to John chapter 20.

1. Weeping Mary cries for Lost Jesus

At the beginning of the chapter, we see Mary coming to the tomb and then realises that the stone had been removed.

She runs to the disciples and tells them what had happened.

After the disciples have left, she returns to the tomb, crying.

Look with me at John 20:11, “11 But Mary stood outside the tomb, crying. As she was crying, she stooped to look into the tomb.”

I don’t know about you, but when I read this passage and re-read this passage throughout this week, I keep coming back to this word, ‘crying’. (crying slide)

The Greek work translated as the English (Chinese) word for crying here appears eight times in the Gospel of John.

Four of them are used here, in John chapter 20.

Here, Mary’s crying outside the tomb of Jesus.

There were two angels where Jesus body laid and they asked her, “Woman, why are you crying?”

Can you imagine turning up at someone’s funeral and a person asks you, “why are you crying?”

It’ll be strange, wouldn’t it? (no picture slide)

It will be a strange thing to ask if this is a funeral and the people asking are just ordinary people.

But this is not a funeral and the people are who asking are anything but ordinary.

The Bible tells us that they are angels, dressed in white.

Angels are messengers from God, delivering God’s message to God’s people.

They’re powerful, spiritual beings.

Here, they’re not asking Mary out of curiosity.

They’re not asking because they didn’t know about Jesus’ death.

They asked Mary a question because they want Mary to think more deeply about what she’s doing.

Mary, why ARE you crying?

Why ARE you so upset?

Here’s Mary’s response, “Because they’ve taken away my Lord and I don’t know where they’ve put him.”

Mary’s crying because Jesus is lost.

Of course, Jesus’ body is not lost; Jesus’ risen!

And ironically, rather than being lost, Jesus, the risen Christ finds Mary Magdalene weeping outside his tomb.

2. The risen Christ finds Mary weeping

Look with me at verses 14-15, “Having said this, she turned around and saw Jesus standing there, but she did not know it was Jesus. “Woman,” Jesus said to her, “why are you crying? Who is it that you’re seeking?”

Jesus asks her the same question that the angels asked, why are you crying?

But Jesus goes one step further than the angels.

He asks, “who is that you’re seeking?”

Yes, of course, Mary’s seeking Jesus’ body.

But Jesus wants her to think a little deeper: who does she actually think she’s seeking?

A teacher who met a tragic end?

A miracle healer who couldn’t be rescued?

Or someone more?

Thinking that Jesus is a gardener, Mary asks whether it was him who took the body of Jesus away.

Her main concern is to find the lost, dead body of Jesus.

Jesus then reveals himself to her; he calls her by her name, “Mary”.

It’s a little bit like an episode of Undercover Boss.(undercover boss slide)

Have you seen it?

It’s a show where Bosses disguise themselves as one of the workers to get a better idea of what the workers really think about them.

Jesus, of course, isn’t disguising himself. (no picture)

However, in his risen state, he’s often not immediately recognised even by some of his closest friends.

In Mary’s case, Mary didn’t recognise who Jesus is until she hears his voice calling out to her, “Mary”.

It’s just like what Jesus said earlier in John, “The sheep follow him because they know his voice.”

Mary recognises Jesus’ voice; she recognises her Shepherd’s voice.

After Jesus calls out to Mary, Mary responds by saying ““Rabboni!”—which means “Teacher.”

At long last, she recognises that the person who is speaking to her is none other than Jesus, the very one whom she thought was lost.

What does the risen Lord Jesus have to say to Mary?

The risen Lord Jesus Christ wants Mary to deliver a message.

3. The risen Christ sends Messenger Mary

Look with me at verse 17, “Don’t cling to me,” Jesus told her, “since I have not yet ascended to the Father.”

After seeing the risen Jesus, perhaps Mary falls down before him and tries to hold on to him.

She doesn’t want to lose Jesus again!

But Jesus says that his journey is not over yet.

He’s risen from the dead; and now, he must ascend to the Father.

Jesus is now ascending to heaven, going back to his Father’s House.

He’s going there to prepare a place for those of us who trust in him (John 14:2)

So for now, Jesus will go and ascend to the Father.

But before he ascends, he wants Mary to deliver a message to his disciples.

Again, in verse 17, “But go to my brothers and tell them that I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God.”

Throughout the Gospel of John, Jesus emphasises that he relates to the Father like no one else.

And now, he invites his disciples to join his family.

He invites his disciples to call his Father, their Father.

More than that, this Father is also Jesus’ God and the disciples’ God.

Just as Jesus has obeyed God absolutely, the disciples are called to do the same.

Of course, there’s a sense in which our relationship to God the Father is not identical to Jesus’ relationship to the Father.

Jesus is uniquely the Son of God and no one, not even us, can ever replace Jesus.

But Jesus has opened up his relationship with the Father so that we, too, can relate to God as a son or a daughter relates to their Father.

The God of Jesus is the God of his believers.

The Father of Jesus is the Father of his believers.

This is the great message the Jesus has for the disciples.

This is the message that Jesus wants Mary to bring to his disciples.

And so Mary becomes a messenger of Jesus’ message.

Rather than weeping and crying over the lost body of Jesus, she now tells the disciples, “I have seen the Lord!”

And she tells them all the things that Jesus has said to her.

The Lord’s message to us

What message does our Lord have for us in today’s passage.

There are at least three things:

Let God ask us questions

When the angels and later on Jesus saw Mary crying, they asked her, ‘Why are you crying?’

Though Mary was loyal to Jesus, she believed Jesus was dead and his body was lost.

The question shows what Mary really believes about Jesus.

It shows her that she hasn’t really yet believed Jesus is Lord, the one who actually has conquered death.

Mary’s answer, though obvious, shows that she has underestimated Jesus.

What question is Jesus asking you today?

Here’s a simple one to begin with, imagine Jesus asks you this question, “Why haven’t you spend time with me today?”

It might be an obvious question with an obvious answer.

Life’s busy, I have to look after the children, I’ve got homework.

But listen to Jesus’ question again, “Why haven’t you spend time with me today?”

Jesus doesn’t ask out of curiosity; he asks because he wants you to probe deeper into your heart.

He wants you to honestly think about your relationship with him.

And so, as we read God’s word, we need to let God ask us questions.

What is God asking us today?

Second, Jesus is bigger than we think.

Like Mary, we often want to defend and protect Jesus.

And it is right to defend the Person and the teachings of Jesus when it’s under attack.

However, there’ll be times when we’ll be tempted to make it relevant and, in the process, make it say what the rest of the world says.

But Jesus is the risen Lord; he’s all-powerful.

There’s a sense in which he doesn’t need us to defend him.

He is the risen Lord and he can handle all the attacks, all the ridicules that the world can throw at him.

What we’re called to do is to be faithful to his word, to obey him and to tell the world about it.

Jesus is the one who changes life, not us.

Just as one of his sheep, Mary Magdalene, heard and recognised his voice, his sheep still recognises his voice today.

And finally, be a messenger for the Son.

Bring the word of Jesus to the world.

Let them know that if they trust in Jesus, Jesus’ God will be their God.

Jesus’ Father will be their Father.

We live in a godless society.

We live in a world that worships created things rather than the creator.

Also, many of us are now living in a fatherless society.

There are many children who have never known the love of a father in their home because their father is physically absent.

I suspect there are many more who don’t know their father’s love because these fathers are emotionally and spiritually absent.

While we want everyone to be godly, we have a drought of godly men, and in particular, godly fathers, in our society.

Christian men, if God has so blessed you as to give you a family, be a godly father.

Be an example of what it means to be a godly to your wife and to your children.

Show them what the kindness of God looks like, show them what the love of God looks like.

So that when you talk to them about God’s kindness and patience, they can say that they have a glimpse of what that looks like in the way you behave.

Jesus has ascended to his Father and our Father, to his God and our God.

Christian brothers, by God’s grace, show them a glimpse of our heavenly Father by becoming more like Jesus in our family.

Here are the three questions for this week:

What questions would God be asking me right now?

What will he ask me to help me grow in my relationship with him?

How am I underestimating Jesus?

What am I doing right now that shows I’m not trusting that Jesus is the all-powerful Lord?

How am I a messenger of God?

How is my life, in both words and deeds, a message to those around me?

Weeping Mary, who was crying because she thought she had lost Jesus’ body, became Messenger Mary, who was tasked to tell the good news of Jesus to his disciples.

May God turn our sorrow into joy as we tell his gospel to our families, our neighbours and our friends.

Let’s pray, “Father, thank you for your love for us in Jesus Christ. Thank you that you have raised him from the dead and has shown us that he’s the all-powerful Lord. Father, please forgive us for not obeying him as we should, forgive us for not trusting him as we should. Please open our hearts and our ears so that we can listen to the questions you have for us as we hear from your word. Make us your messengers of the gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ, in whose name we pray, Amen”
