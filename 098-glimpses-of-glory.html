Glimpses of Glory

John 2:1-12 · 2020
Big IdeaA glimpse of Jesus' glory inspires faith in his followers.

Sermon Title: Glory at Cana 在迦拿的荣耀

Passage: John 2:1-12

Big Idea: A glimpse of Jesus’ glory inspires faith in his followers.

A glimpse of the new dawn

A mother adjusts her glance

Believe and praise Jesus for his glory

Two weeks ago, Jenny and I celebrated our 19th wedding anniversary.

We had a fairly simple wedding and we thank God that everything went according to plan, generally speaking.

A few years later, we attended another wedding.

It was up in Springwood in the Blue Mountains.

It was a long way to travel but we got there.

In fact, everyone was there except for the bride, who was almost one and a half hours late.

The Bride finally came and we had the wedding.

After the wedding, we had a light dinner, which had now been left out in the open for a very long time.

The next morning, I felt very sick; I couldn’t go to work.

And I found out that I wasn’t the only one.

Many people who were at the wedding also had food poison.

It was a wedding disaster!

Today, we’re going to see a wedding that was heading towards disaster.

A glimpse of the new dawn PPT

In his introduction to the Gospel, the Apostle John states, in 1:12, that “The Word has become flesh and dwelt among us. We observed his glory, the glory of the one and only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth.”

The Word, here, is Jesus Christ and John says that he is a witness of the glory of Jesus Christ.

And throughout the letter, he records accounts after accounts of what he’s witnessed of Jesus.

Why? We see his purpose towards the end of the Gospel, in 20:31, “But these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name.”

John wrote his letter so that all of us who read his letter will believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God.

It’s for this reason that he records what’s known as the first sign of Jesus in the Gospel of John.

Please open up with me to John 2:1, “On the third day a wedding took place in Cana of Galilee. Jesus’s mother was there,”

On the third day? Which third day are we talking about?

It’s been a busy few days for Jesus and his followers.

If you have your news sheet there, now will be a good time to go to the notes page and write down what’s been happening in those few days.

Let’s go back to 1:19. On this day, a group was sent to ask who John was.

John says they should be more concerned about who’s coming after him, the one whose sandal strap John was unworthy to untie.

That’s Day One.

Day Two, John identifies Jesus as the one he was talking about.

He’s the Lamb of God who takes away the sins of the world.

Day Three, John tells two of his disciples who Jesus is and they start to follow Jesus

Day Four, the two disciples stay with Jesus the whole day.

Day Five, Jesus finds Philip and tells him to follow him. He also speaks to Nathanael who calls him the Son of God.

On the third day after that, that is, on Day Seven, there’s a small group of disciples who accompanies Jesus to a wedding in Cana of Galilee.

This is the only time in the Gospel of John where John gives a day-to-day account of what Jesus was doing.

Can you think of another time in the Bible where we have a day-to-day, week-long account?

The Creation!

In the creation account, God created everything in six days and he rested on the seventh.

Here, in the Gospel account, Jesus begins his ministry, gathering disciples in the first six days and attend a wedding on the seventh.

On the seventh day, Jesus celebrated at a wedding.

With the coming of Jesus, we have a new creation and it’s an event that’s worth celebrating!

It’s a subtle but masterful touch on preparing us to see that Jesus is coming to make all things new.

In fact, that’s what we’ll be looking at as we go through the next three chapters of John over the coming months.

Jesus has come to make all things new.

The Bible Commentator, C.H.Dodd, puts it like this:

“The three chapters present the replacement of the old purifications by wine of the kingdom of God, the old temple by the new in the risen Lord, an exposition of new birth for new creation, contrast between the water of Jacob’s well and the living water from Christ, and the worship of Jerusalem and Gerizim with worship in Spirit and in Truth”

That’s a preview of what we’re going to see over the next few months.

We’re going to see how Jesus begins to make all things new.

This is a new beginning not only for the Jews, but for all of us.

The seventh day of Jesus’ public ministry, at the Wedding at Cana, marks a new beginning in our story.

It’s truly the dawn of something new.

There are many moments in history where mankind has seen something new.PPT

For example, there was an article published in Cosmos on 19th Feb 2020 that says that researchers have found charred plant remains from a site dating back to 65,000 years ago in Mirarr country in western Arnhem Land, in Northern Territory.

That is, the Aboriginal people have discovered the Continent of Australia at least 65,000 years ago.

Since then, Europeans, Chinese and people of many other nations have discovered Australia and have called this place their home.

That’s a great discovery!

Other incredible discoveries that we’ve experienced as mankind include the technological advances that we’ve experienced.

With the advent of the mobile phone, we can communicate with our friends on the other side of the world with the tap of a button.

They could be hiking on the mountains, rowing on the seas, in the middle of a library, and I can still contact them.

I’ve used mobile phones for many years and I still find that incredible to believe.

As incredible as all these moments are, they pale in comparison with the significance of the Person of Jesus.

Jesus travelled only a relatively short distance, up and down modern-day Israel and Palestine, with a small group of followers.

But today, there are 2.18 billion people all over the world who identify themselves as followers of Jesus. PPT

The historian Philip Schaff described the overwhelming influence which Jesus had on the subsequent history and cultures of the world like this:

“This Jesus of Nazareth, without money and arms, conquered more millions than Alexander, Caesar, Mohammed, and Napoleon; without science…he shed more light on things human and divine than all philosophers and scholars combined; without the eloquence of schools, he spoke such words of life as were never spoken before or since, and produced effects which lie beyond the reach of orator or poet; without writing a single line, he set more pens in motion, and furnished themes for more sermons, orations, discussions, learned volumes, works of art, and songs of praise than the whole army of great men of ancient and modern times.”

Jesus has had an incredible impact on the history of mankind.

But even that’s only a glimpse of his glory.

John wants to show us, his readers, the glory of Jesus Christ.

As he writes his letter, he’ll take us through what Jesus’ done and taught and will ultimately take us to the place of glory, the crucifixion, the resurrection and the exaltation of Jesus Christ.

That’s what the Gospel of John is about; that’s what the whole Bible is about!

It’s to show us a glimpse of the glory of Jesus.

That’s what we need to do as we read the Bible and as we come to Church.

We need to ask ourselves: Where is Jesus’ glory?

How is Jesus glorified here?

And here in John 2, is where John begins his record of how Jesus reveals his glory to his disciples.

Here we see a mother’s request and how she adjusts her glance on her son. PPT

A mother adjusts her glance

Look with me at verses 2-3, “and 2 Jesus and his disciples were invited to the wedding as well. 3 When the wine ran out, Jesus’s mother told him, “They don’t have any wine.”

It was most probably a relative or a close family friend who invited Jesus and his disciples to the wedding.

Weddings in Jesus’ time was a week-long celebration and it was the groom’s responsibility to make sure there’s enough wine and food to go around.

It would’ve been very embarrassing for the groom and his family if either the food or the wine ran out, which was exactly what happened to this particular groom.

What does Jesus’ mother do when she finds out? She goes to Jesus and tells him.

She wants him to do something about it.

In the Gospel of John, the name of Jesus’ mother is not mentioned but from the other Gospels we know that it’s Mary.

Mary was a virgin when she had Jesus.

It wasn’t until after the birth of Jesus that Mary and Joseph became intimate and had other children.

We don’t hear from Joseph after the infancy account of Jesus and so we know that he’s probably died by the time Jesus began his ministry.

Jesus would’ve taken up Joseph’s trade and followed in his father’ footstep as a carpenter.

Jesus would’ve worked as a carpenter and provided for his mother and his siblings.

And so, Mary would’ve learnt to rely on Jesus, knowing that Jesus will provide.

She’s probably asked him to do things for her many, many time.

However, Jesus does not do respond as you might expect.

Verse 4, “What does that have to do with you and me, woman?” Jesus asked. “My hour has not yet come.”

Jesus’ response is not rude, but it’s not endearing either.

It’ll be like saying, “What do you and I have in common (about this thing we’re talking about)?

Why do you involve me?

It is no way around it; it’s a mild rebuke.

Why does Jesus give his mother a mild rebuke?

It’s because Jesus has come to do God’s will.

God’s will comes first and no human is going to set a different agenda for Jesus.

No human is going to manipulate or determine Jesus’ actions, not even his mother.

For Jesus, doing God’s will was priority.

This must have been hard for Mary to accept; after she must have asked Jesus to do things for her many times.

But as Jesus begins his ministry, God’s mission must come first.

Mary won’t get any special privileges or special favours for being the mother of Jesus.

Everyone must come to God by trusting in Jesus, even his mother.

After mildly rebuking her, Jesus gives the reason for his response, “My hour has not yet come.”

This is a theme that comes back constantly in the Gospel of John.

Jesus is very mindful that a time, his hour, will come and it’s not at the wedding at Cana.

There will be other times throughout the Gospel of John when Jesus mentions that it’s not yet his hour.

When the hour comes, Jesus will announce it and says that the hour has come for him to be glorified.

At that hour, at the right time, Jesus will be glorified.

As we read through the rest of the Gospel, we know the hour that he refers to is the hour of his crucifixion, followed by the Days of his Resurrection and Exaltation.

That’s the hour of glory that Jesus looks forward to.

Mary herself recognises that Jesus is the one in charge, not her, and so she tells the servants, “Do whatever he tells you.”

The gospels only have a limited record of Mary’s words but here is a great phrase, “Do whatever he tells you.”

Mary puts her trust in Jesus. Even though she was rebuked, she trusts that her Son will do what he needs to do in his own time.

Jesus then does go ahead and does something about the wine situation.PPT

Believe and praise Jesus for his glory

It just so happens that there are six stone jars nearby which are used for Jewish purification.

These are big jars, which can store up thirty gallons, or 150 litres.PPT

Six jars mean that together, they can fill up to 750 litres.

Listen to what Jesus tells the servants, in verse 7, “Fill the jars with water,” Jesus told them. So they filled them to the brim.”

And so they did; They now have plenty of water for ceremonial washing.

Listen carefully to what Jesus tells them in verse 8, “8 Then he said to them, “Now draw some out and take it to the headwaiter.” And they did.

Often, when we look at this verse, we think of the servants drawing some water from the jar, which had by now miraculously been changed to wine by Jesus.

However, the word translated ‘draw’ here almost always refer to drawing from the well. PPT

And so, what I think happened is this: the servants draw the water from the well, which had been changed miraculously into wine by Jesus.

Before this, the servants would draw water from the well, fill the jars with water for ceremonial washing.

After Jesus, the servants would draw water from the well, which had now been turned to wine, to celebrate the couples’ wedding.

It’s the sign of a new beginning.

By filling the jars with water, Jesus says that the time for ceremonial washing has come to an end.

By creating the new wine from water, Jesus is saying in effect that a new time has come.

It’s also a sign that reminds us of another time when God has changed water into something else.

Do you remember another time when God transformed water?

Yes, it was in Egypt.

To save his people from the land of Egypt, God sent 10 plagues against the people of Egypt.

The first one involves changing the waters of Egypt into blood.

It was a sign of Judgement.

With the coming of Jesus, God has also changed water into something else, but this time, to wine.

It’s a sign of celebration! God’s new work has begun.

This abundance of wine is also promised through the prophets.

This is what God promised through the prophet Amos, in Amos 9,

14 I will restore the fortunes of my people Israel.[d] They will rebuild and occupy ruined cities, plant vineyards and drink their wine, make gardens and eat their produce.

Drinking wine is a sign of celebration and when God’s time of restoration comes, there will be plenty of wine, plenty of celebration.

That doesn’t mean the Bible endorses drunkenness; there are many passage where the Bible speaks against drunkenness.

But here, wine is drunk sensibly to celebrate God’s glory.

Wine is here symbolises the joy that comes with Christ.

The headwaiter tastes the water that had been turned to wine and congratulates the groom on how good the last wine was.

Usually, the best wine will be served first and when the diners are a little tipsy, the waiters will serve the inferior wine.

But, it looks like the groom had switched and left the best wine for last, or so the headwaiter has thought

And so, he tells the groom how superior the last wine is.

However, he shouldn’t be congratulating the groom, who, as far as we know, had no idea what happened.

From experience, I can tell you that often, the groom does not know what happens on the wedding day!

Instead, the headwaiter should’ve been congratulating Jesus instead.

It’s Jesus who has changed the water into the wine.

The headwaiter praised the wrong person.

And it still happens today.

People still often praise the wrong person when they should’ve been praising God.

This happened a few weeks ago on the ABC news channel.PPT

Over the past few months, thousands of people across Australia along with many around the world prayed for God to send relief for the fire.

Many of us, including many people at Church, prayed for rain.

And God answered by sending us rain.

On 9th February, ABC had this heading on one of their news article: “Prayers answered as NSW rainfall extinguishes 74-day Currowan bushfire”.

That sounded pretty innocent enough.

The person who wrote this article may not even had been a Christian.

Our culture has been so steeped in Christian language that we might use it without actually acknowledging that it’s God.

Christians, of course, know that God has indeed answered our prayers.

But when that article came up, many people complained about it.

Thousands of comments have poured down over Twitter and Facebook, expressing anger at the ABC for daring to use the word, prayer.

Here’s what they said:

“Prayers have no place in journalism. #ThisisNotJournalism” ABC news… prayers had nothing to do with it. Please delete this offensive tweet. #FreedomFromReligion

There are people who hate to give God thanks for the rain.

They hate giving God thanks for anything.

Whereas the Headwaiter at Cana mistakenly congratulated the groom rather than Jesus,

Many people in our day would deliberately thank anything else, whether it be science, human ingenuity, political might, rather than giving thanks that rightly belong to Jesus.

But not Christians. As Christians, we know that our God is the Father who know how to give good gifts to His children.

And so, we thank God for all that he’s done for us.

We are thankful for science and ingenuity and good governance, but the one to whom we’re most thankful for must always be our God, the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit.

Why does one group see an event and thank God while another group sees the same event and doesn’t thank him? PPT

It has to do with whether they have faith in him.

Look with me at the last verse, verse 11, “Jesus did this, the first of his signs, in Cana of Galilee. He revealed his glory, and his disciples believed in him.”

Here, the miracle at Cana is described as a sign.

What Jesus did is a sign that God’s new creation has begun, that God is beginning to fulfil his promises made through the prophets.

All these signs are glimpses of Jesus’ glory.

Who had seen it? The headwaiter and the Groom did, but there’s no record of them thanking Jesus.

They merely experienced and enjoyed the wine.

The servants who had drawn the water from the well saw the miracle; they knew that Jesus had changed the water into wine.

But there’s no record of them thanking Jesus or of them following Jesus either.

However, the disciples saw what Jesus did and they recognised the importance of what Jesus did.

They had seen glimpses of his glory and they believed in him.

That’s what the sign of the miracle was designed to do – it’s meant to lead people to see a glimpse of Jesus’ glory and for them to believe in him.

One of my biggest concerns I have for our Church and for Churches everywhere is that people come and they don’t see the glory of Jesus.

They might come and do their religious duties, then they leave.

They might come, hear the words of the Bible and even speak the words of the Bible, but they don’t see the glory of Jesus.

I’ve seen people who had been involved in Church for many years, keen Christians who led Bible studies, taught children, even took places of responsibilities at Church and yet after a while become disillusioned and left the Church.

The reason, they haven’t seen the glory of Jesus.

How do I know they haven’t seen the glory of Jesus?

They get more excited about the achievements of the world, than they do the accomplishments of Jesus.

They are happier to see the glory of the world, than the glory of Jesus.

They have many priorities that they put ahead of the lordship of Jesus.

They glorify their family more than Jesus; they glorify their jobs above Jesus and they glorify themselves above Jesus.

Brothers and sisters, how do you glorify Jesus in your life? How do you make Jesus look good in your life?

To glorify Jesus, he needs to the most important person in your life, he needs to always be on your highest priority.

He is the Person that you will praise most often.

If you have seen but a glimpse of his glory, you can’t help but tell others how good he is.

If you have seen but a glimpse of his glory, you won’t have enough but you will want to see more of it.

And you’ll read through the Bible, seeking his glory in both the promises of the Old Testament and his fulfilment of them in the new.

My prayer is that all of us here will see more glimpses of the glory of Jesus.

And as we see his glory, we’ll do what the disciples do, we’ll believe in him, we’ll put our trust in him.

And we’ll also do what his mother does, telling others, “Do whatever he tells you to do.”

Here are the three questions for this week: PPT

What’s one thing about Jesus I can glorify in this week?

What’s one thing or one person that I’m tempted to put above Jesus?

What’s one thing I can tell another person about Jesus this week?

Let’s pray,

“Father, we thank you that you have revealed your one and only Son to us. We thank you that by faith, we have seen glimpses of Jesus. Please help us to fix our eyes firmly on him and his glory. Don’t let our eyes stray; don’t let our hearts wander. May Jesus so fill our vision that we can’t help but praise his name. For Jesus’ sake, Amen.”
