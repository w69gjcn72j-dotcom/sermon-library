Introduction

Thank God that we could worship together and receive God’s Word at the beginning of the year.

I like to preach the message from the book of Acts with you. We learn the book of Acts contains the witness of how the gospel of Christ could be spread throughout the World within the first century. However, before the disciples rushed to the global evangelistic actions, the Lord Jesus commands the disciples to stay in Jerusalem in order to wait for the gift of God’s promise, that is the coming of the Holy Spirit.

Why is the waiting for the coming of the Holy Spirit is so importance of the accomplishment of Jesus’ gospel mission to the world? Today I like to use some time to teach on the important doctrine of the Holy Spirit and the meaning of His mission.

What and Who is the Holy Spirit?

Once a comic strip depicts a young person listening intently to the pastor’s sermon. Suddenly his ears heard a term he didn’t understand. The boy turned to his mother and whispered to her that he knew the Father and the Son. “But who is the Holy Spearmint?” he asked.

More often, the Holy Spirit seemed to be the unknown, and unmentioned person of the Trinity.

Most Christians readily and warmly responded to the description of Jesus as the Son of God and this indicates a relational identity as a son-father relationship with which we are familiar. In addition, when in Christ, we learn to call God ‘Father’, that helps us to understand and respond to him as the one who governs, guides, provides for, guards and loves His children.

But the name ‘Holy Spirit or worse ‘Holy Ghost’, tends to convey a cold, remote and some how abstract image. After all, what is ‘Spirit’ or the older name Holy Ghost? This in fact caused many Christians express that the Holy Spirit is seen to be distant and impersonal by comparison with the Father and the Son.

Even though a lot of interest rise in and talk about the Holy Spirit in recent years, yet there are still a lot of confusion about the third person of the Trinity.

Our confusion moves in several directions.

Some Christians are uncertain about the Spirit’s identity. Exactly who is the Holy Spirit? Is the Spirit fully divine, or less than God? Is the Spirit fully personal or merely an impersonal force?

Other Christians are confused about the work of Holy Spirit. What does He do? And how and where is he at work? How can we be certain that it is the Holy Spirit who is active in, through, and around us?

So, let us first dealt with the identity of the Holy Spirit.

“I believe in the Holy Spirit.” Throughout the centuries these simple words from the Apostle’s Creed have capsulised the church’s belief about the third person of the Trinity. As Christians we have not only placed our trust in God the Father and Jesus the Son. We have also entrusted ourselves to the Holy Spirit. Like the Father and the Son, The Spirit is worthy of our trust and worship, because he is fully personal and fully divine.

But how do we know this? We can affirm this Trinitarian faith by tracing this historical faith through the Bible.

First, we trace the Holy Spirit in the Old Testament. He was God at work in the creation of the universe and in sustaining life on earth. According to the Genesis creation narrative, the Spirit was active when God called the universe into existence. In Genesis 1:2 mentioned the Spirit of God was hovering over the water, and then God spoke the series of creative words.

Above the work of creating the universe, the Spirit also participated in the creation of humankind. Adam became a living being when God breathed into Adam’s nostrils, that is when God gave to Adam His own animating Spirit (Gen. 2:7).

The Spirit is not only God at work originating life, He is also the power that sustain life (Ps. 104:29-30). All living creatures owe their existence to God’s Spirit. But this is especially true for humans. Indeed, when God recalls His spirit, life ceased (Gen. 6:3; Eccles. 12:7).

Second, the Holy Spirit was God’s power active in special ways in the lives of certain people. In Old Testament, the Spirit’s presence provided the resources necessary to complete a divinely ordained task. However, no one even the leaders of the kingdom could presume to process the Spirit permanently. And the Spirit’s endowment was limited to special people for particular purpose. Not everyone in Israel enjoyed direct access to God’s spirit. Rather, the people were dependent on their leaders and others God chose to mediate His presence among them.

The unsatisfying experience led God’s Old Testament people to direct their attention to the future. They eagerly anticipated a grand day when God’s Spirit would dwell permanently among them - and within them all.

These hopes were fuelled by the prophets who spoke of the future coming of the Messiah. Not only would the fullness of the Spirit on him (Isa. 42:1), but the Messiah would also pour out the Spirit on the people. When this glorious event occurred, all God’s people- from the greatest to the least- world know God and enjoy the fullness of the Spirit. (Jer. 31:34)

Jesus’ teaching on the ministry of the Holy Spirit

(John 14:15-17, 25-27, 15:26-27)

Now we proceed to the New Testament’s fulfilment. The most concentrated teaching in the Gospels on the ministry of the Holy Spirit is to be found in Jesus’s farewell discourse in John’s Gospel chapters 13 to 16.

Here the significance of the Spirit’s coming is announced by Jesus (Jn. 14: 25-27, 15:26-27). Jesus himself promised that he would give his disciples the full measure of the Spirit.

Following Jesus’s departure, his Father would send another “Counselor” to empower them for their mission (Jn. 14:16) The Greek word for counselor or advocate parakletos, which is a compound word. A compound of kaleo, to call, and para ‘alongside’ has a legal connotation. The Holy Spirit is the witness-advocate who testifies to Christ. He comes to teach the disciples, reminding them of the Lord’s instructions and guiding them into truth. This same Spirit would testify about Jesus to the world and assist the disciples in taking their stand as Christ’s witness (Jn. 15:26-27).

Acts of the Holy Spirit empower the church to mission

(Acts 1:4-8, 2:1-4)

Finally, we will see the relationship between the Holy Spirit and the church.

The book of Acts records the glorious fulfilment of the promise of pouring out of the Spirit upon all God’s people occurred at Pentecost (Acts 2:1-5). This event marked a milestone in the history of God’s activity in the salvation of humankind. At Pentecost, the Holy Spirit entered world in a unique way. And in so doing he inaugurated a new age, the age of fulfilment (1 Peter 1:10-12).

The outpouring of the Holy Spirit signalled the birth of the Spirit endowed, Spirit empowered and Spirit lead church. It started the age of the global mission of the church. Throughout this age, the Spirit would focus his work on the new community, the fellowship of the followers of Christ.

Since Pentecost, the presence of the Spirit was no longer limited to some leaders, it embraces all believers. It extends to every Christian in every nation and in every generation. The Holy Spirit enjoys a new identity as the mediator of the presence of the risen and exalted Jesus Christ within His community. The Spirit is the teacher, leader, and empower of the church.

He indwells the church on the Lord’s behalf.

He will guide the church to the future completion of the divine program. As such, the Holy Spirit is God at work bringing history to its ultimate goal. The Spirit will one day transform us into the likeness of Christ.

Conclusion

Today’s teaching about the Holy Spirit, the third person of the Trinity, is at the core of the Christian faith. The Spirit is leading our lives, for He guides us in our daily living. Far from being the privilege of certain leaders, the Holy Spirit is the inheritance of every child of God. When we repent our sin and accept Christ to be our Saviour and Lord, we have immediate access to the fullness of the Spirit in our lives.

He will lead us into clearer understanding of God’s words and empower us to be the witness of Christ. Let us commit our lives to God and connecting with Him in four areas.

Let God teach us through reading and listening His Words from the Bible and reflect on his words.

Let us walk close to God. As we receive understanding from Him, respond to God with the affection and needs of our hearts.

Let us pray more fervently to seek God’s power so that we can serve and bear witness for Christ.

Let us communicate with our Christian community. As we connect with others who share our faith in Christ, we experience mutual encouragement to love one another.

Let us testify our faith with our relatives and neighbours about Christ.

The Holy Spirit will lead us into His witnessing mission for Christ.
