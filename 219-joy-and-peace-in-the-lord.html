Joy and peace in the Lord

Passage: Philippians 4:1-9

Big Idea: The Lord Jesus is the secret to the Christian’s joy and peace.

Stand firm and united in the Lord (4:1-3)

Rejoice in the Lord (4:4)

The Lord is near - Be gracious! (4:5)

Don’t worry but pray to the Lord of peace (4:6-7)

Think well and follow my lead (4:8-9)

Since we’ve started to come back, I’ve had many wonderful opportunities to catch up with many of us here at Church.

We’ve had lunches and dinners and it’s great to share what’s on our hearts.

As our community live with the threat of COVID-19, we face a number of issues among us.

Some of us who are older are worried about our health.

Some of us who are married and have families are worried about our job security.

Some of us who are younger are worried about future job prospects.

Research has shown that our anxiety has affected even the youngest members of our community.

A survey was done on children aged between one and five in Queensland.

Nearly one in five children experienced symptoms of anxiety or depression during coronavirus restrictions earlier this year.

A four-year-old girl said her teddies and dolls were in trouble for not washing their hands properly or coughing into their elbow.

A three-year-old son was playing cars and one of the vehicles was an "ambulance taking all the dead people to hospital because of the virus".

As parents, we want the best for our children.

We want them to be happy.

But how can they be happy and worry-free in a world like ours?

In today’s passage, Paul speaks to his children in Christ.

He wants them to be happy.

He’s like a spiritual father to them and they are like spiritual children to him.

As a father to them in Christ, he’s concerned for them.

He knows that there are things that are worrying them.

And so, he tells them a simple message:

“Don’t worry about anything”

You might hear that from a well-meaning friend.

You’re worried about your relationship, your friends say, “don’t worry about it.”

You might have even said it yourself to a friend.

“Why are you so worried? It’s no big deal!”

While it’s a nice phrase to say, it can be hard for the person facing the difficulties to hear.

Do we even understand what our friend is going through?

Or are we just saying it because it makes us feel better because we don’t want to worry about our friend’s worries?

If Paul just tells them, “Don’t worry”, then it might not had been very useful.

But here, he explains why Christians don’t have to worry.

In fact, Christians are the only people who have good reasons to rejoice even when the rest of the world worries.

How?

Stand firm and united in the Lord (4:1-3)

If you have your Bible with me, please open up to Philippians 4:1

Stand firm in the Lord (PPT)

Look with me at 4:1

“So then, my dearly loved and longed for brothers and sisters, my joy and crown, in this manner stand firm in the Lord, dear friends.”

He tells them to stand firm.

In the original language, the word translated to ‘stand firm’ describes soldiers who stand at their post regardless of what happens around them.

Soldiers don’t run away when dangers come; you stay at your post.

Soldiers stand firm.

That’s what Paul tells the Philippians to do.

That’s what Christians are called to do.

We are to stand firm in the Lord.

We don’t get distracted and walk away when life gets busy.

We don’t run away when danger comes.

We stay at our post.

We are to stand firm in the Lord.

Not only do we stand firm in what we believe,

We also stand firm in the way that we live.

We stand firm in our integrity – when we say yes, we mean yes; when we say no, we mean no.

We stand firm in what we believe and in the way we live.

Not only are we to stand firm; we are to stand united. (PPT)

United in the Lord

In the Philippian church, there were these two women, Euodia and Syntyche.

Paul says that both of them have worked really hard with Paul to tell the gospel.

For some reason they had a fall out; they had a disagreement.

And so, Paul pleads with them to agree with each other in the Lord.

They are to reconcile to each other and learn to agree with each other.

They are to stand, united in the Lord.

Even today, disagreements can happen between one Christian and another, between one congregation and another.

Sometimes, it can happen between a minister and another.

Christians are not immune to disagreements and we should not expect to agree on everything.

But even in the midst of our disagreements, Christians should strive to remain united in the Lord.

As a people standing firm in the Lord and united as one in the Lord, we should together rejoice in the Lord. (PPT)

2. Rejoice in the Lord (4:4)

Look with me at verse 4:

‘4 Rejoice in the Lord always. I will say it again: Rejoice!’

This is a theme that we see again and again in Philippians.

The Christian is to be joyful; they are to be happy!

But this is not a superficial happiness.

It’s not putting on a fake smile and pretend to be happy even when things are going bad.

Nor are they to be happy because they are living the good life or because everything is going their way.

No! A Christian is to be happy, to be joyful, because they are in the Lord.

They are to rejoice in the Lord.

The Lord is the ground for their happiness.

It’s because they are in the Lord that the they are joyful even when they face the difficulties, even when they face sadness.

Rejoicing in the Lord in the midst of our struggles is the stuff of faith. (slow)

The one who has faith prays like this:

“Lord, you know the trouble I’m in; you know what worries me; you know the struggles that I’m going through.

I can’t wait to see how you will carry me through them.”

That’s what faith in Jesus looks like.

It’s not seeing everything clearly, it’s not even understanding everything clearly.

But you know that Jesus does see everything clearly, that he’s in control and that he will see you through.

To have faith in Jesus means to have joy in him always.

That’s what it means to rejoice in the LORD.

It means having the confidence that God is able to carry you through.

That’s the secret to the Christian’s joy.

They have Jesus; Jesus is their joy.

You might remember that a few years ago, I went to Jerusalem to attend the GAFCON conference.

At the GAFCON conference, a new Secretary to GAFCON was appointed.

His name is Archbishop Ben Kwashi.

Archbishop Ben is one of Africa’s most senior Church leaders.

Last week, news broke that Archbishop Ben has colon cancer.

Now, because of COVID, he couldn’t attend Church as usual, but he could have a Church service with his family.

Here’s a picture of Archbishop Ben leading the service.

He was jumping, rejoicing in the Lord.

Was he cured? No! Was he in pain? Yes!

But was he happy? Yes!

Why? Because of his joy is in the Lord.

He rejoices in the Lord always, even when he’s in pain, because he trusts in the Lord.

The Lord Jesus has died to save him.

As long as Jesus is Lord, and Jesus is always Lord, Archbishop Ben can rejoice!

His faith is firm in the Lord.

And so, those who stand firm in the Lord stand united in the Lord and stand firm rejoicing in the Lord.

They’ll also be known for their graciousness. (PPT)

3. The Lord is near - Be gracious! (4:5)

Look with me at verse 5, “5 Let your graciousness[b] be known to everyone. The Lord is near.”

Paul says that the Philippians should be famous for being gentle!

The word translated for gentleness here can be translated as graciousness.

It’s the idea of not insisting on getting what’s rightfully yours.

Rather, the person who’s gracious will say that “it’s OK, you take it.”

Gentle people won’t insist on being first.

They will humble themselves for the benefit of the other person.

That’s what Christians are called to do.

Christians are to consider others better than themselves; they’ll let others go first.

Why should they be gracious?

Because the Lord is near.

Do you know that the Lord Jesus is here, right now, in this Church?

Whether you’re sitting there in the pew, or watching online, Jesus is here, for where two or three are gathered in Christ’s name, Christ is there.

Are we really going to insist on how important we are, in the Lord’s presence?

The Lord is near; he’s in here, right now.

Let’s be gentle; let’s be gracious. Even more than that, let’s be famous for being gracious.

When people come to St Paul’s, they should know us as a gracious Church.

Christians are also to be people who don’t worry but pray. (PPT)

4. Don’t worry but pray to the Lord (4:6)

Look with me at verse 6.

“6 Don’t worry about anything, but in everything, through prayer and petition with thanksgiving, present your requests to God.”

We’re not to be anxious about anything.

We are not to be worried about anything, but we’re to come before God, bringing our prayers and our requests with thanksgiving before him.

A Bible commentator by the name of Bengel once said that anxiety and genuine prayer are more opposed than fire and water. (PPT)

You can’t be someone who constantly worries and someone who is fervent in prayer.

The best way to repel anxiety is to pray!

Some of us might say, “you don’t understand what I’m going through.

It can’t be done; not in my case”

But, my dear brothers and sisters, my friends, it can be done.

When the Bible tells us not to worry, it’s not some trite saying aiming to make us feel better about ourselves.

The command to not worry is only half of the command, the other half is to come before God in prayer in everything.

Bring your worries, bring your requests to God!

Tell God your greatest struggles.

Ask him, petition him, for the strength to stand firm in him even during those struggles.

And as you present your prayers and petitions, do it with thanksgiving.

Don’t just tell God about your worries, look for ways to thank him, even in the midst of your worries!

Thank God that he knows your struggles and that he hears your requests and that he’s able to carry you through.

William Cowper was an eighteenth century Christian who had suffered many dark episodes in his life.

And yet, even during those dark times, he trusted God.

As he reflected on the dark periods of his life and how God carried him through, he wrote the following words: (PPT)

‘Ye fearful saints, fresh courage take; The clouds ye so much dread

Are big with mercy and shall break in blessings on your head.

Judge not the Lord by feeble sense, But trust Him for His grace;

Behind a frowning providence He hides a smiling face.’

When the clouds of darkness and doubt come, when the storms of evil and sorrow rain down on us,

They often help Christians shift our eyes from our life situations back to God.

We might not be able to see through the clouds of despair, but God does.

When the clouds come, and they will come, trust him, pray to him.

And thank him that he’s going to take your through.

And as you pray, be sure to know that you’ll be surrounded by the peace of God. (PPT)

4. Be surrounded by the peace of God (4:7)

Verse seven:

“7 And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds in Christ Jesus.”

When Christians stand firm in the Lord, they are guarded, protected by the peace of God.

When Paul wrote to the Philippian church, the city of Philippi was protected by a Roman garrison, or a troop of Roman soldiers.

Paul says that when Christians stand firm in the Lord, they are guarded, or garrisoned by the peace of God.

The peace of God protects them.

That’s how we stand firm in God.

Christians don’t stand firm in God by their own strength; we can never stand firm on our own.

Christians stand firm by faith in God, surrounded by the peace of God.

Because Christians are surrounded by the peace of God, they can stand firm even in the midst of fiery trials.

A pastor by who goes by the name of Pastor Timothy is a Church leader in his 70’s.

One day, he was called to the police station for a ‘chat,’ but they locked him up instead.

When they locked Pastor Timothy up, he just knelt in the cell and prayed.

They told him he was not allowed to pray, so he told them the he was a church leader and a Christian for many years, so all he could do was pray.

God gave Pastor Timothy inner peace and confidence in Him even in the midst of the interrogations.

In the end, the Police just let Pastor Timothy go.

What a wonderful testimony to God’s peace!

When we trust in God, pray to God, the peace of God which passes all understanding will guard our hearts and minds in Christ Jesus.

Finally, they are to think well and follow Paul’s lead. (PPT)

Think well and follow my lead (4:8-9)

Finally brothers and sisters, whatever is true, whatever is honourable, whatever is just, whatever is pure, whatever is lovely, whatever is commendable—if there is any moral excellence and if there is anything praiseworthy—dwell on these things. 9 Do what you have learned and received and heard from me, and seen in me, and the God of peace will be with you.’

Rather than being filled with worrying thoughts, Christians are to be filled with true and noble thoughts.

We are not to let our minds wander and thinking about lies and dishonourable things.

We are to think good thoughts; we are to think about excellent things, praiseworthy things.

Our minds are not vacuums.

If we don’t actively put good thoughts in, sinful, worrying thoughts will fill our minds instead.

If we can somehow look into your minds right now and get a glimpse of how what’s on your mind, what will we see?

Will we see thoughts that you’re shameful of?

Or will we see pure and noble thoughts?

One way that we can influence our thoughts is by what we see on the internet.

What information do you look at when you go on the internet?

Friends, be careful about what you watch on the Internet!

Don’t let them plant impure, shameful thoughts in your mind.

Instead, steer your minds to think good, pure and excellent things.

Paul tells the Philippians not only to think on good things but to do good things.

He tells them to follow his lead.

They’ve seen how Paul leads his life; they’ve heard Paul’s teaching.

Walk in his way as he follows Christ.

That’s what Christian learning looks like.

We listen to a good leader’s talk; we’ll also follow a good leader’s walk.

What does this mean for us?

Firstly, pray.

Are you anxious about COVID? Pray!

Are you worried about your family? Pray!

I’m not talking about a one sentence prayer

I mean real, sustained, truthful prayer

Spend time telling God those things which bother you the most.

Pray to God and let him know what’s really worrying you at the moment.

You might start by praying for that one thing for 5 minutes.

It might not sound like a lot but for many of us making a start, 5 minutes can be a struggle.

But can I ask you to pray for 5 minutes on something that’s causing you worry?

Don’t get distracted but keep praying for that for 5 minutes.

Of course, it will be great if you can pray for longer, but if you’re just starting, try praying hard for 5 minutes.

And as you bring your requests to God, always remember to thank Him.

Second, rejoice.

What is the ground of our joy? What makes us happy?

Don’t let your circumstance be your reason for your happiness.

Don’t let your achievements be the grounds for your happiness.

Let Jesus and Jesus alone be your grounds for happiness.

Jesus loves you, suffered for you, died for you to rescue you from sin and death.

Rejoice because you trust in him and knows that he is Lord.

Don’t ever stop rejoicing for who he is and what he’s done!

Third, think well.

Rather than having worrying thoughts, fill your mind with whatever is true, whatever is honourable and just.

Be careful with what you watch on the internet.

Think holy thoughts and you’ll be holy.

Think garbage thoughts and you’ll be garbage.

One way to keep your thoughts holy is to memorise God’s word.

Hide God’s word in your heart.

One of my farourite verses that I’ve memorized is verse 7.

“And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds in Christ Jesus.”

It’s a verse that we often say at the end of our services.

Hide that verse in your hearts; let it form good and excellent thoughts in your minds.

And so here are the three questions:

How are you praying for?

What’s the ground of your joy?

What are you thinking about?

Friends, in a time such as ours, when there are so many reasons to worry, stand firm in the Lord Jesus Christ.

Stand firm in Jesus for he is the secret to our joy and peace.

Let’s pray,

‘Father, thank you for the Joy that is ours in Christ. We thank you that because of Jesus, we can come before you in prayer, presenting petitions and our requests before you, knowing that you hear us. Father, we pray now that if there’s anyone here today who has anxiety in her heart, or have worries on his mind, that they might bring them before you in prayer. And Father, please surround them with your peace, that they might know the peace that passes all understanding. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.”
