The person who is the Good News

Luke 2.10-12

Hi everyone, my name is David Yung, the minister here at St Paul’s Kogarah

How are you enjoying the night so far?

Has anyone seen what’s changed next door at Kogarah Public School?

All the demountable classrooms were gone!

Oh no! Did someone steal them? No, of course not!

The government is taking them out to make way for a new school building and a new school hall!

To get the demountable classrooms out, they needed not one but two big and massive cranes.

The tradies worked very hard and they got all of them out safely over two days.

It was actually quite cool to see the cranes move the classroom high above the ground.

When I saw the cranes at work, I messaged a young father who comes to our Church and the next thing you know, both he and his son came to the Church see the cranes at work.

After over 150 years, Kogarah Public school is finally getting a school hall.

It’s great news.

That’s what we want to do with great news, don’t we?

We post it on Instagram, or Tik tok or we might send messages.

If you’re old-school, you might use Facebook or, use the phone to call someone.

At Christmas time, that’s what we want to do.

We want to tell everyone about the good news.

It’s not just good news.

It’s the good news, the best news!

We can find out about this great from the pages of the Bible.

I’ve printed a small excerpt for you in our Christmas Concert booklet.

It’s taken from the Gospel of Luke, starting from 2:10.

But the angel said to them, “Don’t be afraid, for look, I proclaim to you good news of great joy that will be for all the people: 11 Today in the city of David a Savior was born for you, who is the Messiah, the Lord.

On that night, the angels brought the Good News, the best news, to the Shepherds.

God has promised that he would send someone who will bring world peace.

The world has seen wars and violence for almost as long as humans have been around.

As long as there’s greed and anger, selfishness and hate, war and turmoil is just around the corner.

And no one is perfectly immune from any of these.

All of us can be tempted to greed and anger and hatred.

All of us have the potential to bring violence and injustice to our world.

But God promised that one day, a person will come to bring peace, true peace.

This person will restore justice to all the nations.

That day arrived when the Jesus was born.

On that night in Bethlehem, the long-awaited Saviour, the Lord, the Messiah, has finally come.

This little baby in a manger will bring about world peace!

There is no other baby born, either before or after Jesus, who is even close to bringing about the kind of peace that Jesus brings.

He is the most significant baby ever born!

But look at how he came.

This will be the sign for you: You will find a baby wrapped tightly in cloth and lying in a manger.”

A little low key, don’t you think?

As to almost emphasise how fragile and vulnerable this baby is, the angels describe him as being wrapped in cloths.

A baby wrapped in clothes doesn’t exactly pose a threat to anybody.

However, if we want to understand how significant Jesus’ birth is, we’ll need to look a little deeper at what it means for Jesus to be the Saviour.

Throughout the Old Testament, God is often portrayed as the Saviour, or the Rescuer.

In Luke, we see Jesus portrayed as the one who saves;

That is, Jesus is portrayed as the Saviour as well.

In fact, with the arrival of Jesus, the Bible portrays Jesus as the God who has come to save.

When Jesus was born in that obscure manger, some two thousand years ago, he wasn’t just a special baby sent by God to save the world.

Jesus was God himself, God incarnate, who came to save the world.

You don’t have to look very far to see how badly the world needs saving.

I’m not talking about saving the environment, as good as that might be.

I’m talking about the world as in the people of the world.

While there are moments of light, our world is often filled with many nights of darkness.

We fight against each other, we lie to each other, we gossip behind one another.

There are people we hate; sometimes, people hate each other so much that they kill one another.

Left to our own, we will destroy ourselves.

But even worse than that, left to our own, we will be destroyed by God.

Those who fail to love and obey God, that is, all of us, deserve the God’s ever-lasting destruction.

The verdict that God gives on us is that we’re guilty of rebelling against him.

The sentence is ever-lasting destruction.

Left to ourselves, we’re hopeless.

But the good news, the best news, is that God’s sent Jesus to come to earth, to die the death that we deserve, so that we don’t have to face God’s judgement anymore.

Instead of a hopeless end, we have an endless hope.

We sing about this at Christmas time.

In the third verse of Hark the Herald Angels Sing, we sing:

Hail the heaven-born Prince of Peace! Hail the Sun of Righteousness! Light and life to all he brings, risen with healing in his wings. Mild he lays his glory by, born that we no more may die, born to raise us from the earth, born to give us second birth.

That’s why Jesus’ birth is so significant; that’s why we celebrate Jesus’ birth.

We celebrate the fact that we have a God who so identifies with us.

We celebrate the fact that our God loves us so much that he became one of us in order to save us.

The famous preacher Charles Spurgeon puts it like this, ‘From henceforth, when God looks upon man, he will remember that his own Son is a man.

From this day forth, when he beholds a sinner, if his wrath should burn, he will remember that his own Son, as man, stood in the sinner’s place, and bore the sinner’s doom.’

This Christmas, remember God’s good news.

Though we were originally doomed for destruction, God sent his Son to rescue those who would believe in him.

Instead of a hopeless end, we have an endless hope.

It’s not just good news; it’s the best news that the world will ever hear.

If you want to find out more about this great news, come and talk with me or Zoe or Ezekiel afterwards.

We would love to talk about it with you!

And so, on behalf of Paul’s Kogarah, have a wonderful Christmas and a blessed New Year.
